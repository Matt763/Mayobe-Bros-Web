Getting facts right matters most when readers use our articles for decisions about money, study or work. This is how we check them.

Our process

  1. Identify claims. Before publishing we list every number, date, name, rule and claim in the article that a reader could act on.
  2. Find the best source. For each claim we look for the original source first, not a copy of a copy.
  3. Check it. We open the source and confirm that it really says what we use it for. We check dates, units (such as TSh and percentages) and arithmetic.
  4. Record it. Sources are listed at the end of the article so you can check them yourself.
  5. Review. A person reads the finished article for accuracy, clarity and fairness before it is published.

Which sources we trust, in order

  1. Official bodies and original documents: government ministries and agencies, regulators, the Bank of Tanzania, universities, courts and gazettes.
  2. The organisation or company the article is about, for facts about itself, such as a product's features or a course's fees.
  3. Reputable news organisations with a record of corrections, for reporting on events.
  4. Peer-reviewed research and recognised institutions, for science and health topics.

Social media posts, anonymous websites and messages forwarded on WhatsApp are not accepted as proof on their own.

Numbers, dates and prices

Every figure needs a source. We do not copy statistics we cannot trace to an original. If a number is an example we made up to explain something, we say it is an example and show the arithmetic. For fees, fares, deadlines and fuel prices, we say when we checked them and link to the official page where the latest figure is published.

When we cannot confirm something

  • We say so in the article, for example "we could not confirm this".
  • We leave out claims that matter but cannot be checked.
  • We report rumours only to explain that they are rumours, and we label them clearly.

Updating and corrections

Facts change. We revisit important articles, update the "updated" date when we change them, and correct errors as described in our Editorial Policy.

Help us check
