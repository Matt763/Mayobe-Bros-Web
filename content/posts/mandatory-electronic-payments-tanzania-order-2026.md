If you pay school fees, take a long-distance bus, book a hotel or sell a plot in Tanzania, a new rule may apply to you. A government order began on 1 July 2026. It says certain payments must be made and received by electronic means, such as mobile money or a bank transfer. This post explains what the order says, who must act and when, and what it does not say. We checked the order's text and news reports in November 2026. In Swahili, people call this *malipo ya kielektroniki*.

## The short answer

- The rule is the Electronic Transactions (Mandatory Electronic Payments for Specified Transactions) Order, 2026, published as Government Notice No. 158C on 30 June 2026.
- It started on 1 July 2026 and covers nine groups of payments listed in a Schedule: transport, malls and filling stations, school fees, hotels, tourism, property, vehicles and some farm sales.
- "Electronic means" includes mobile money, bank transfer, payment card, e-wallet, point of sale (POS) device, internet or mobile banking and government payment systems.
- Anyone who was already receiving such payments on 1 July has six months to set up the means. Reports put the end of that period at the end of December 2026.
- The order does not ban cash in general. It applies only to the listed payments.

## What the order says

The order is short. Section 2 says a person who makes or receives payment for a transaction in the Schedule "shall" do it through an electronic means. So it binds both the person who pays and the person who gets paid.

Section 3 gives a delay. A person who was already receiving payments for a listed transaction just before the order began must put electronic payment means in place within six months. The same section says the order does not change arrangements or contracts made before it came into force.

The order was signed by the Minister for Finance, Khamis Mussa Omar, at Dodoma on 30 June 2026. It was made under section 13(2) of the Electronic Transactions Act.

## The full list of covered payments

The Schedule lists these groups. We have kept the wording close to the official text.

| Group | What is covered |
| --- | --- |
| Public transport | Fees, charges, tolls or fares for bus rapid transit, ferries, bridges, long-distance passenger buses, online taxis, air transport, railways and parking |
| Shopping and leisure | Goods or services in shopping malls, gyms, cinemas, filling stations, conference and event venues, sports arenas, and international trade exhibitions such as Saba Saba and Nane Nane |
| Education | Fees and contributions for pre-primary, primary and secondary schools, tertiary institutions and universities |
| Hotels and food | Accommodation, food and drinks in hotels, restaurants and cafes |
| Tourism | Payments relating to tourism-related services |
| Property | Renting, selling or buying a building, a plot or a farm |
| Vehicles | Buying or selling a motor vehicle |
| Farming through co-operatives | Payments through co-operative unions and AMCOS for strategic crops, including cotton, cashew nuts, coffee, tea, sisal and tobacco, and for farm inputs and pesticides |

Notice what is not named in the Schedule: a roadside vegetable stall, a bodaboda ride or a small kiosk. The order also names no minimum or maximum amount. A report by EY, a tax advisory firm, says the same: no value cut-off is given.

## What counts as "electronic"

The order defines the term widely. It covers a method, system or channel of paying or receiving money through electronic or digital technology. The examples it gives are:

- mobile money,
- bank transfer or electronic funds transfer,
- payment card,
- electronic wallet,
- point of sale device,
- internet or mobile banking,
- a government electronic payment system.

So you do not need a bank card. If you have a mobile money account, you can use it. For a business, the practical tool is a merchant number or a QR code. ## Why the government did this

Government speakers have given two main reasons. Daily News reported on 5 August 2026 that a legal drafting official gave two reasons. Digital payments make transactions easier to track. They also help close loopholes that can be used for money laundering. The finance minister said better payment systems support the effort to discourage cash.

The Citizen reported on 30 July 2026 on the Tanzania Instant Payment System (TIPS). It handled 651 million transactions worth TSh 54.95 trillion in 2025. In 2024 it handled 453 million, worth TSh 29.82 trillion. The paper credited those figures to the finance minister's 2026/27 budget speech. The same report quoted the Governor of the Bank of Tanzania as saying that, over time, there will be places where customers cannot pay with cash.

## What changes for ordinary people

### If you pay school fees or travel

The order covers school and university fees and contributions, so the school or college must be able to receive them electronically. For the transport services in the Schedule, fares, tolls and parking charges must also be paid and received by electronic means. Our post on [Tanzania transport fares in 2026](/post/tanzania-news/tanzania-new-transport-fares/) covers the fares themselves.

### If you rent, buy or sell property or a vehicle

Payments for a building, plot, farm or motor vehicle must go through an electronic means. In practice this means you should pay by bank transfer or another traceable method, and keep the proof. Do not hand over a large sum in cash for a plot or a car and expect it to be seen as correct.

### If you run a shop, hotel, restaurant, gym or fuel station

If you were already trading when the order began, you must be able to receive electronic payments by the end of the six-month period. The delay in section 3 is written for those already receiving payments on 1 July. In our reading, a business that starts later needs the means from day one. Confirm this with TRA or your licensing office.

Daily News also reported that the government amended the Business Licensing Regulations. According to that report, anyone who applies for or renews a business licence must show a digital payment system or device, such as a Lipa Namba or a TANQR code. A business without one cannot renew an expired licence until it complies. We read this in the news report only, so check the exact rule with your licensing office.

### If you sell through a co-operative

If you sell strategic crops through a co-operative union or an AMCOS, payments for the crop and for inputs must move through electronic channels. Ask your AMCOS how and when it will pay.

## What worries people

Not everyone is comfortable with the change. Daily News reported on 11 September 2026 that a tax consultants' leader likes the change because it makes tax collection easier. She asked the government to review transaction charges, and warned that high fees could put people off. The same report said small traders who depend on cash may find the move hardest and that some customers fear fraud and hacking.

EY's note on the order gives three tips. Check that the payment channels you need are available and reliable. Compare charges across banks, cards and mobile money. Think about rural access. It also notes that more payments will be traceable, which may affect record-keeping.

Scams are a real risk when more money moves by phone. ## Common questions

### Is cash now illegal in Tanzania?

No. The order covers only the nine groups in its Schedule. It does not say that cash is banned for every purchase. Nothing in the text of the order says so. If you are not sure whether a payment is covered, ask the bank, your local council or the Tanzania Revenue Authority (TRA).

### Is there a penalty if I pay or receive cash for a listed item?

The order itself does not mention a penalty, and we did not find one in the news reports we read. The licence consequence reported by Daily News is the only one we found. Other laws may apply, so do not assume there is none. Ask a qualified adviser if you run a business.

### When does the six-month delay end?

The order says six months from the day it began, 1 July 2026. Law and tax firm summaries give the end of December 2026. If you were already taking payments for a listed item on 1 July, aim to be ready before then.

### Do I need a smartphone?

Not always. Mobile money works on basic phones through a USSD menu, and bank transfers can be done at a branch or an agent. A business may choose the method that suits its customers, as long as it is on the list of electronic means.

## What to do next

1. If you run a business in one of the listed groups, get a Lipa number, a bank POS or a QR code now, and test that you can receive a payment.
2. Keep records of every electronic payment: the message, the receipt or the bank slip. For property and vehicles, keep them with the sale paper.
3. If you are a parent, ask your school how it wants fees paid, and always use the account or control number the school gives in writing.
4. Read the exact order, and any later notice from the Ministry of Finance, the Bank of Tanzania or TRA, before you act on a news summary.
