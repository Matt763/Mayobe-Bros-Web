You built a website for your shop, tailor business or tour service. Now you want people to find it. Good news: Google itself says that if you run a small local business, you can probably do much of the work yourself.

This post is for owners who already have a website, or plan one. No website yet? A social media page or a Google Business Profile can also help customers find you. Many of the same habits below apply.

## The short answer

SEO (search engine optimisation) means making your pages clear for people and for search engines. Most of it is plain work you can do alone.

- Make sure Google can find your pages.
- Give each page a clear, different title.
- Write useful pages using the words your customers use.
- Describe your pictures with short alt text.
- Use Google Search Console (a tool from Google) to see how your site does.
- Be patient. Results can take weeks or months, and nobody can promise first place.

As checked in November 2026. Tools and menus change, so use the links at the end for the current steps.

## What does SEO mean for a small business?

When someone types "tailor in Mwanza" or "fresh fish delivery Dar es Salaam" into Google, a list of pages appears. SEO is the work that helps your page be understood and shown for the right searches.

Google's guide says there are no secrets that automatically rank your site first. It also says there is no guarantee that any particular site will be added to Google's index (its list of pages). What you can do is make your site easy to understand and genuinely helpful.

## Step 1: Make sure Google can find your site

Google says it mostly finds pages through links from other pages it already knows. So a site that no one links to is harder to find.

Simple ways to help:

- Link your pages to each other. Your home page should link to your main pages, like "Products", "Prices" and "Contact".
- Put your website address on your social media pages, signboard, receipts and WhatsApp business profile. Real people clicking is a good start.
- Optionally, make a sitemap, a file that lists the pages on your site. Google says it is not required.
- Search for your business name on Google. If nothing shows up after a few weeks, check in Search Console (see Step 6).

## Step 2: Write clear page titles and descriptions

The title is the blue line people see in search results. Google says a title should be unique to the page, clear, concise and accurately describe what is on the page.

Bad: "Home" on every page.
Better: "Mama Neema Tailoring, School Uniforms in Mwanza".

A meta description is the short text under the title. Google says a good one is short, unique to the page, and includes the most relevant points. Google may choose other text from your page instead, so do not worry if it changes it.

Quick checklist:

- One title per page, different from every other page.
- Put the business name and the main thing you do or sell.
- Do not repeat the same word again and again. Google calls that keyword stuffing, says it is tiring for readers, and says it goes against its spam policies.
- Google says it does not use the "keywords" meta tag, so you can skip it.

## Step 3: Write useful pages in your customers' words

Google asks for content that is helpful, reliable and written for people first. For a small business this is easy to picture. Answer the questions customers ask you every day.

Ideas for pages you could write:

- What you sell, with clear prices (or "from TSh 5,000") and the date you checked them.
- Where you are, opening hours and how to reach you, written in a way someone new to the area can follow.
- How ordering or delivery works, step by step.
- Common questions: how long does it take, how can I pay, what if something is wrong?

Use the words your customers use. If people say "chips mayai", write "chips mayai", not a fancy name. Write the way you speak, in plain sentences. Keep the page up to date. Old prices or closed branches make visitors leave.

Do not copy another shop's text. Original pages are what Google asks for.

## Step 4: Use good images, alt text and file names

Pictures help customers trust you. Google's image guidance says:

- Put images near text about the same subject.
- Alt text is the most important information about an image. It is a short description of what the picture shows. It also helps people who use screen readers.
- Use short, descriptive file names such as `blue-school-uniform.webp`, not `image1.jpg`.
- Google Search supports several formats, including WebP.
- Sharp pictures get more clicks, but large files make a page slow. Make pictures a sensible size.

A good alt text is plain: "Blue school uniform shirt and shorts on a hanger". A bad one is: "uniform uniform school uniform cheap uniform Mwanza". The second one is stuffing and does not help anyone.

Many customers in Tanzania browse on phones with limited data, so small image files help them too.

## Step 5: Use clear link text

When you link to another page, use words that say what is there. Google says link text should tell users and Google what the linked page contains.

Better: "see our [price list for school uniforms]".
Weaker: "click here".

## Step 6: Check Search Console

Search Console is a service from Google. Google's help page says the only requirement to use it is a Google account. You add your site and verify that you own it. Until you do, you cannot see data for it.

Once it is set up, the reports use these words:

- **Impressions:** how many times people saw a link to your site in Google.
- **Clicks:** how many times people clicked it.
- **CTR (click-through rate):** clicks divided by impressions.

You do not need to study every number. Look once a month: are impressions and clicks slowly going up? Which searches bring people to you? Does anything look strange?

If you hire a helper, Google advises giving only read access at first and not write access.

## Step 7: Be patient

Google says some changes may take effect in a few hours and others could take several months. It suggests waiting a few weeks before judging the effect of a change.

That can feel slow when rent is due. Keep going anyway. Fix one thing a week: a title today, a price page next week, photos after that. Small steps add up.

Anyone who promises you first place on Google is promising something Google says no one can guarantee. Read our post on [fake followers and guaranteed sales offers](/post/business-ideas/marketing/fake-followers-and-guaranteed-sales-scams/) before paying anyone.

## Common questions

### Do I need to pay an agency?

Google says a small local business can probably do much of the work itself. An agency may still help with a bigger site, but ask for examples of past work and what they will do.

### How many keywords should I use on a page?

There is no magic number. Write naturally for your customers. Repeating the same words is keyword stuffing, which Google says to avoid.

### How long until my site shows up?

It varies. Google says there is no guarantee a site will be indexed, and changes can take hours to months. Check Search Console after a few weeks.

### Does my business name need to match everywhere?

It helps people. Use the same name on your sign, social pages and website. The post on [branding basics](/post/business-ideas/marketing/branding-basics-name-logo-colours/) covers this.

## What to do next

1. Search for your business name on Google and write down what you see.
2. Give every page on your site its own clear title, and add a short alt text to each picture.
3. Create a Google account if you do not have one, add your site to Search Console and verify it.
4. Add one useful page this month, answering a question customers ask you.

Your shop deserves to be found. Do the plain work, keep it honest, and give it time.
