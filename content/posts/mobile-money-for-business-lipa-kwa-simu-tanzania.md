Many customers no longer carry much cash. They say "nitume" and expect to pay by phone. If your business can only receive money on your personal line, the shop money, your family money and your friends' messages all end up in one place. That is stressful and hard to track.

This post explains the merchant number idea, what one network asks for, and how to protect yourself. We could only confirm details for some networks, and we say clearly which ones.

## The short answer

A merchant number, called a Lipa number, is a number made for business payments. Customers pay it, you get a message, and the payments are easier to separate from your personal money. Ask your network what you need, keep every payment in your cash book, and never hand over goods before you have checked the payment is real.

- Use a merchant number instead of a personal line where your network offers one.
- Be ready with your NIDA number. Some levels also ask for a TIN certificate and a business licence.
- Check the payment on your own phone or portal, not on the customer's screen.
- Report scam messages to your network and keep the evidence.
- Ask the network about any charges. We do not quote fees because we could not confirm them.

## What is a Lipa (merchant) number and how do customers pay it?

Lipa kwa simu means "pay by phone". A merchant registers with a mobile money network and gets a number that customers use to pay the business.

On its customer page, Mixx by Yas (the network that was formerly Tigo Pesa) says a customer can pay in two ways, as checked in November 2026:

**With USSD (no app):** dial *150*01#, choose Lipa Kwa Simu, choose TanQR, enter the merchant's number, check the merchant details shown, then enter the PIN.

**With the Mixx Super App:** open the app, tap Pay, scan the merchant's QR code or choose Merchant and type the number, enter the amount, check the merchant details and enter the PIN.

Yas says on its FAQ page that payments can come from all networks. Menus change, so ask your network for the current steps before you teach your customers.

## What do the networks ask for?

We opened the Mixx by Yas merchant page, as checked in November 2026. It lists documents by type of merchant:

| Merchant type | What the page says you need |
| --- | --- |
| Low KYC (NIDA only) | A fully registered Yas number and a NIN or NIDA ID |
| Full KYC (individual) | The same, plus a valid TIN with certificate and a valid business licence |
| Companies and institutions | A company TIN, a company business licence, the certificate of incorporation, and an authorisation letter, with extra papers for limited companies and sole proprietors |

KYC means "know your customer": the checks a network makes on who you are. The Yas FAQ says merchants must be registered and that it is typically one line per business. For registration, it says to contact Mixx by Yas.

The page also lists features: several business accounts under one number, instant SMS alerts to several phones, scheduled transfers to a bank account that it says are free, a web portal for sales reports, and the option to add authorised users. It says no refunds are processed without your approval. The page lists no merchant fees, but it links to terms we could not read here, so ask the network before you sign up.

If you still need a TIN or licence, see our post on [registering a small business with BRELA, TIN and licence](/post/business-ideas/startups/register-small-business-tanzania-brela-tin-licence/).

## What about Vodacom M-Pesa, Airtel Money and HaloPesa?

We opened Vodacom's M-Pesa Business onboarding page. It is written for organisations. It says to email m-pesabusiness@Vodacom.co.tz, complete forms and KYC checks, and sign the Services Agreement. The team then creates the organisation account and a 6- or 7-digit short code linked to a bank account. The charging model is agreed during the review, so we cannot say what it costs.

We did not open an official merchant page for Airtel Money or HaloPesa, and we did not find Vodacom's route for an individual small trader. So we will not guess. Visit a shop or agent of your network, or its website, and ask three things: what documents, what charges, and how fast the money arrives.

## How do I protect my till from fake messages?

A merchant number does not protect you from tricks. Sellers are targets because they hand over goods fast.

- Do not rely on a screenshot or on the customer's phone. Look at your own SMS alert, your app or the portal.
- Be careful with messages that arrive while the customer stands at your counter and look almost right but come from an ordinary number.
- Never send money back "because I paid too much" until you have checked your own balance.
- Never share your PIN or any code with anyone, including someone who says they work for the network.

TCRA advises the public never to send money to someone they do not know, not to click links they do not recognise, and to check a message and number before paying (TCRA, August 2022). TCRA also says to dial *106# to see the numbers registered under your national ID and to text the word "Fraud" to 15040 to report scam messages. The Yas FAQ gives the same instruction for fraud reports. Because networks may change the keyword, check your network's current advice.

If you or a customer sends money to the wrong number, the Yas FAQ says to call customer care on 100, and that a reversal request must be made within 30 minutes of the mistaken transaction.

## How do I match mobile money to my cash book?

Every payment is money in. Each evening, add up your SMS alerts or the portal report and write the total in your cash book next to your cash sales. Our post on [simple business records and a cash book](/post/business-ideas/startups/simple-business-records-small-traders-cash-book-tanzania/) shows the columns.

Then compare. Cash in your box plus the merchant balance should equal the book balance. Withdraw a fixed amount for yourself and write it as "owner took". Do not pay household bills straight from the business line without writing it down.

Fees are your job to find. Ask your network for the current tariff for receiving payments and for moving money out, and write the charges in the book as an expense. When you work out prices, include them. Our post on [how to price your products](/post/business-ideas/startups/how-to-price-your-products-small-business-tanzania/) explains how.

## Common questions

### Do I need a merchant number to accept mobile money?

You can receive money on a personal line, but then business and personal money mix and are hard to separate. Ask your network what it allows for businesses.

### Can a customer on a different network pay me?

The Yas FAQ says payments can come from all networks. Ask when you register, and test with a small payment.

### How do I know a payment is real?

Look at your own alert, app or portal. If you cannot see it there, treat it as not paid.

### What does a merchant number cost?

We could not confirm any fee, so we give none. Ask your network for its current tariff in writing.

## What to do next

1. Decide which network most of your customers use.
2. Collect your NIDA number, and your TIN and business licence if you have them.
3. Ask the network what you need, what it charges and when the money reaches you.
4. Teach everyone who serves customers to check payments on the business phone before giving goods.

A small step like this makes your business look more serious to customers, and it gives you clearer numbers every evening.
