Your stomach drops. You pat your pocket, check your bag, and the phone is not there. It holds your photos, your contacts, your chats and maybe your money. That fear is normal. Take one slow breath, because what you do in the next hour matters more than how fast you panic.

This guide gives you an order of steps that works on most phones. It is about the phone, your accounts and your mobile line. It does not claim to know every local rule, and it says where you should ask your network or the police for the exact details.

## The short answer

Stay calm and work in this order: try to find or ring the phone, lock it from another device, protect your important accounts, tell your network and your mobile money provider, report to the police, and erase the phone only if you cannot get it back. Do not chase a thief yourself.

- Use another phone or a computer to see where your phone is.
- Lock it first. Erasing comes last, because it cannot be undone.
- Change the passwords of your email, Google or Apple account, and social media.
- Call your network from another phone and ask what they need from you.
- Tell your bank or mobile money provider.
- Report to the police and keep the number they give you.

## Minutes 1 to 10: try to find it, ring it, lock it

First, retrace your steps. Look in the place you were last. Ask the people near you to call your number. A phone left on a seat or in a shop is often handed to the owner or the staff.

If it is not there, use a second device. A friend's phone, a family member's phone or a computer is enough. You do not need your own phone in your hand.

### If you have an Android phone

Google's tool is called Find Hub. On another Android phone or tablet you can open the Find Hub app. On a computer or any phone you can use the Find Hub website. Sign in with the Google Account that is on your lost phone. If you use a friend's phone, choose the guest sign-in so you do not leave your details there.

From the list, pick your phone. According to Google's help page, you can then:

- See the phone on a map. If it cannot be reached now, the last known location may still show.
- Play a sound. The phone rings at full volume for five minutes, even if it was on silent.
- Mark it as lost. The phone locks with its own PIN, pattern or password. You can add a message and a contact detail on the lock screen.

"Mark as lost" is the step to use first when you think someone else has the phone.

### If you have an iPhone

Apple's tool is Find My. On another Apple device, open Find My, choose Devices, and tap your iPhone. If you have no other Apple device, you can use Find Devices on iCloud.com. Apple's help page says you can see its approximate location, play a sound, and turn on Lost Mode.

Lost Mode locks the iPhone, suspends Apple Pay cards and lets you show a message with a way to contact you. If the phone is offline, Find My can show its last known location for up to seven days, according to Apple.

### A word of caution about seeing the phone on a map

If the map shows your phone in a place you do not know, do not go there alone to get it back. Your own safety matters more than any phone. Tell the police what you see and let them decide what to do.

### The catch: these tools work only if they were switched on before

Google says Find Hub needs the phone to have power, a data or Wi-Fi connection, a signed-in Google Account and Find Hub turned on. Location must be on too. For remote lock or erase, Google also recommends 2-Step Verification on your account.

If you read this and your phone is safe in your hand, check these settings today. That is the best use of this article. More on this at the end.

## Protect your accounts: Google, Apple, WhatsApp, mobile money

A locked phone is safer, but a thief or a stranger may still try to get into your accounts. Do this from a trusted device while you wait.

1. **Change your main password.** Start with the email address you use to reset other accounts. If your Google or Apple account is the key to your phone, change that password next. Use a long password you have never used before.
2. **Change social media passwords.** Facebook, Instagram, TikTok and the like. Sign out of all devices if the app gives you that option.
3. **Secure WhatsApp.** The usual way is to get your number working again on a new SIM and phone and log in there, which moves WhatsApp to the new device. Our guide to [protecting your WhatsApp account with two-step verification](/post/technology-solutions/cyber-security/protect-whatsapp-account-two-step-verification/) explains how to add a PIN so that nobody else can register your number.
4. **Think about your money.** Phone your bank or mobile money provider (M-Pesa, Tigo Pesa, Airtel Money, HaloPesa or any other) from another phone. Tell them the phone is lost and ask what they advise for your account and your line. We do not give you a script or a number here, because each provider handles this in its own way. Use the number printed on your card, your statement or the provider's official website, never a number sent in a message.

Be careful in the days after. Scammers sometimes send a message saying "we found your phone, click here" to steal your login. Only trust Google, Apple or your operator through the official app, website or number you already know.

## Tell your network operator and report it

Your phone number is tied to a SIM card, and the phone itself has an identity number called the IMEI. These are two different things, and both matter.

**The SIM.** Call your network (Vodacom, Airtel, Tigo/Yas, Halotel, TTCL or another) from another phone and say the SIM is lost or stolen. Ask them to block it so nobody can make calls or receive your verification codes. Ask what you must bring to get a replacement SIM with the same number. We could not find an official page that lists the documents, fees or deadlines, so ask your operator directly and write down what they say.

**The phone (IMEI).** Google's help page says your mobile service provider can use the IMEI to disable the device. You can find the IMEI in the phone's settings, or in Find Hub for an Android phone. Many phones also have it on the original box or the receipt. It is worth looking for the box today.

**The police.** Report the loss or theft at a police station. Take your ID, the IMEI if you have it, and your phone number. Ask for a report or reference number, and keep it safe. Your operator or insurer may ask for it later.

Is there one national process for all of this? We could not confirm a single procedure from an official page. Tanzania's regulator, TCRA, does publish news about blocking devices. The Citizen reported in July 2023 that TCRA blocked 108,395 IMEIs between July 2022 and June 2023 for devices linked to theft or crime. That tells us blocking exists, but the article does not explain how an owner asks for it. So the safe advice is simple: ask your operator and the police what they need today, and write it down.

There is also a rule about SIM cards. A law firm's summary of the 2020 SIM card registration regulations says owners should report a lost or stolen SIM and get a loss report, and that this report is needed for a replacement. We did not read the official text, so please treat that as a reason to ask quickly, and confirm the details and the time limit with your operator.

## When to erase the phone, and what that means

Erasing, also called a factory reset, removes everything on the phone. Use it only if you are sure you will not get the phone back, or if it holds information you cannot risk leaving there.

Think about what you lose by erasing:

- **Your data on the phone is deleted for good.** Photos and files that were not backed up are gone.
- **On Android**, Google says the phone no longer appears in Find Hub after the erase. If someone finds it, they need your Google password to use it again.
- **On iPhone**, Apple says erasing cannot be undone, and that it needs two-factor authentication on your Apple Account. If the iPhone runs iOS 15 or later, you can still locate it after an erase.

So the order is: locate, lock, then erase if there is no hope. If you are not sure, lock it and wait a day while you talk to the police and your operator.

## Common questions

### Can I find my phone if it is switched off?

Sometimes you can see its last known location. Google says that on some supported phones the network can help locate it for a few hours after it shuts down. Apple says Find My can show the last known location for up to seven days. Neither promise a live location.

### Will my SIM be blocked automatically if I lock the phone?

No. Locking the phone and blocking the SIM are separate. The lock keeps people out of your phone. Only your network can stop the SIM line. Call them.

### Can someone use my photos and chats if the phone is locked?

A strong screen lock helps a lot. That is why a PIN or password matters more than a simple swipe. Change your account passwords anyway, because other devices may be signed in.

### I did not set up Find Hub or Find My. Is it too late?

You cannot turn them on remotely from nothing. You can still call your network, change passwords, report to the police and tell your bank or mobile money provider. Then set the tools up on your next phone.

## What to do next: be ready before it happens

Do these on a quiet day, when your phone is in your hand.

1. **Set a screen lock.** A 6-digit PIN or a password is stronger than a pattern.
2. **Switch on Find Hub or Find My.** On Android, check Settings, then Security, then Find Hub. On iPhone, check your name, then Find My. Menu names may differ on your phone.
3. **Turn on 2-Step Verification** for your Google or Apple account, and save the backup codes somewhere safe, not only on the phone.
4. **Write down your IMEI** and keep it with your receipt or in a notebook. Also keep a photo of the phone box if you have it.
5. **Back up your photos and contacts** so that a lost phone is a bad day and not a lost life.

Losing a phone hurts, and it is not your fault. A calm first hour, one step after another, gives you the best chance to protect what matters.
