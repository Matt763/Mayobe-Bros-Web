The Tanzania Meteorological Authority (TMA) has warned that a very strong El Niño will shape the rains this season. Many places could get more rain than usual, and the wet spell may run into 2027. This post explains what TMA has said, what "above normal" rain means, and what you can do at home, on the farm and on the road. The facts were checked in November 2026, and the weather changes quickly, so use the latest TMA update before you act.

The Swahili term you will see in the news is *mvua za vuli* (the short rains of October to December). The longer season that TMA announced in October is *mvua za msimu*.

## The short answer

- TMA's August 2026 outlook said the vuli rains (October to December) would be normal to above normal in the northern coast, the north-eastern highlands, the Lake Victoria basin and northern Kigoma, with the heaviest rain expected in November.
- On 23 October, TMA added a second outlook for November 2026 to April 2027. It covers western, central, southern and south-western regions, and expects more rain in the first half of the season (November to January).
- "Above normal" is about the season as a whole. A single storm can still cause flooding in a place with an average season.
- The seasonal forecast covers large areas. TMA also publishes 24-hour, 10-day and monthly forecasts, and these are the ones to check before travel or planting.
- The main risks TMA named are floods, landslides, crop damage, disease and damage to roads, railways, ports and airports.

## What TMA actually forecast

TMA's Director General, Dr Ladislaus Chang'a, said in August that the vuli season would be "substantially influenced by a very strong El Niño", according to The Chanzo. El Niño is a warming of the central Pacific Ocean that changes weather patterns far away, including in East Africa. It is not rain by itself, and its local effect differs from place to place.

The August outlook named these areas for normal to above-normal rain:

- the northern coast: Dar es Salaam, Tanga, Pwani, Mafia, and the islands of Unguja and Pemba
- northern Morogoro
- the north-eastern highlands: Arusha, Manyara and Kilimanjaro
- the Lake Victoria basin: Kagera, Geita, Mwanza, Shinyanga, Simiyu and Mara
- Kakonko and Kibondo districts in northern Kigoma

The rains were expected to start in the Lake Victoria basin in the fourth week of September or the first week of October, then spread. TMA said they could continue into January 2027.

On 23 October, TMA widened the picture. Its November 2026 to April 2027 outlook, reported by The Chanzo on 26 October, expects above-normal to normal rain in Kigoma, Tabora, Katavi, Singida, Dodoma, Rukwa, Songwe, Mbeya, Iringa, Njombe and southern Morogoro. Lindi, Mtwara and Ruvuma are listed as normal to above normal. In most of these regions the season is expected to end between the third and fourth weeks of April 2027.

Two reports use slightly different wording ("above normal to normal" and "normal to above normal"). The meaning is close: a wetter season than usual is more likely than a drier one.

## What "above normal" really means

A Daily News explainer on the vuli forecast makes three points that stay true every season:

1. **It is local.** TMA compares the expected rain in a place with that place's own long-term average (the period 1991 to 2020). There is no single number for the whole country.
2. **It is a probability, not a promise.** A long-range forecast cannot give an exact amount. The real total is known only after the season.
3. **It does not mean rain every day.** A season can finish above average because of a few very wet days, with dry spells in between. Short, heavy bursts can flood a town even when the season total looks normal.

This is why TMA says the seasonal outlook should be used together with shorter forecasts.

## What has happened so far

TMA reported heavy rain in Bukoba on 22 and 23 October, more than 171 mm, against an October average of 165 mm. Tabora had 51 mm against an October average of 26 mm.

The Citizen reported on 13 November that TMA expected rain and thunderstorms in 16 regions between 11 and 20 November, mostly in the Lake Victoria basin, western Tanzania and the south-western highlands. The same 10-day outlook expected relatively little rain on parts of the coast and the north-eastern highlands.

This shows why a single seasonal headline is not enough. In one 10-day period, one region can be soaked while another stays dry.

## Why this matters to ordinary readers

Tanzania has been through a strong El Niño before. TMA's briefing recalled the 2023/24 season, when floods and landslides hit more than 14 regions. The International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies reported at least 155 deaths and more than 200,000 people affected. A TMA officer said some people had ignored warnings that season and suffered badly.

The risks named by TMA this year include:

- **Homes and towns:** flooding, landslides, and blocked drains. The WHO notes that floods can spread cholera, typhoid and malaria, and that drowning causes most flood deaths.
- **Farms:** too much soil water, crop diseases, and losses at harvest. TMA named potatoes, sunflower, beans, sesame, fruits and vegetables as the crops most at risk.
- **Animals:** outbreaks such as Rift Valley fever and foot rot are possible.
- **Travel:** damaged roads and bridges, trouble on the Metre Gauge and Standard Gauge Railways, and delays at ports and airports.

The government has also acted. The Chanzo reports that a four-stage National Emergency Plan for El Niño (preparedness, prevention and mitigation, response, and recovery) was activated in September. The worst-case cost is estimated at Sh730 billion, and Sh10 billion is set aside in the 2026/27 budget.

## How to prepare

These steps come from TMA, the Fire and Rescue Force and local leaders quoted in The Citizen and The Chanzo.

**At home**
- Clear drains and remove rubbish that blocks water, including the drain in front of your house.
- Keep food, some emergency money, torches and charged power banks ready, as TMA's Director General advised flood-prone residents.
- Decide in advance where your family would go if the water rises.
- Move documents and valuables to a high, dry place.
- Remove standing water where mosquitoes can breed.

**On the road**
- Do not cross floodwater unless you can judge how deep and fast it is. The Fire and Rescue Force advises waiting for the level to drop.
- Bus operators are urged to service tyres and brakes before the rains and to delay trips when conditions are dangerous. The Tanzania Bus Owners Association's secretary-general said: "Delaying is better than arriving at an accident."
- If you plan a long trip, check the 10-day forecast and ask the bus company about road conditions.

**On the farm**
- Use weather information when planning land preparation, crop varieties, fertiliser use and harvest time.
- Improve drainage on the plot, and use soil and water conservation methods.
- Livestock keepers should vaccinate early, as the Animal Welfare Society advised.

## Common questions

### Will it flood where I live?

No forecast can say that for one street. TMA's outlooks cover whole regions. Floods depend on local drainage, rivers and the land. If you live near a river, in a valley or in a low-lying area, treat the warning as a reason to prepare, and follow short-term alerts from TMA and your local authority.

### Where do I find the latest forecast?

TMA publishes 24-hour, 10-day and monthly forecasts, severe weather alerts and farming bulletins at meteo.go.tz. TMA has also said it expanded localised forecasts to 86 districts in the vuli regions and 63 districts in the single-season zone. Radio and TV news repeat the alerts.

### Is El Niño the same as rain?

No. El Niño is a pattern of warmer ocean water. It changes the odds of heavy rain in some places, and the effect is not the same everywhere. A strong El Niño raises the chance of a wetter season, but it does not decide the weather on any given day.

### How long will it last?

TMA's November to April outlook expects more rain in the first half of the season, from November to January. The World Meteorological Organization has said El Niño is likely to continue through February 2027. Dates can shift, so check the monthly update.

## What to do next

1. Check the 10-day forecast for your area on the TMA website before any trip, planting day or event.
2. Walk around your home and plot this week and clear the drains.
3. Make a short family plan: where to go, what to carry, and who to call. Keep a charged phone and a power bank.
4. Share the plan with neighbours. Geita leaders advised making sure neighbours can reach each other in an emergency.
