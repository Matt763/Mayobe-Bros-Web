Assignments, CVs, letters, budgets and price lists all need a word processor and a spreadsheet. Microsoft Office costs money, and many students and small offices cannot pay. Some people then install a cracked copy from a friend. That is risky, and you do not need to do it.

There is a free, legal choice called LibreOffice. This guide explains what it is, how to get it safely, and what to expect when you open your Word and Excel files. We state only what the LibreOffice project says on its own website. Menu names may differ slightly on your computer.

## The short answer

Yes. LibreOffice is a free, open source office suite, and you can use it instead of Microsoft Office. It opens Word, Excel and PowerPoint files and can save in those formats too. Download it only from libreoffice.org. Some complex files may look a little different, so check the layout and keep your original.

- It costs nothing to use.
- Writer replaces Word, Calc replaces Excel and Impress replaces PowerPoint.
- It opens .doc, .docx, .xls, .xlsx, .ppt and .pptx files.
- Macros written in Microsoft's language usually need editing.
- The free web versions of Microsoft's apps are another honest choice.

## What LibreOffice is, and why it is free

LibreOffice describes itself as a free, open source office suite built by a community of contributors around the world. "Open source" means the program's code is public, and anyone may study it under a licence. LibreOffice's code is under the Mozilla Public License v2.0. It is linked to a group called The Document Foundation, which holds the project's trademarks.

The suite has six programs:

| Program | What it is for |
| --- | --- |
| Writer | Word processor, from short notes to long documents |
| Calc | Spreadsheets, calculations and charts |
| Impress | Slide presentations |
| Draw | Diagrams and drawings |
| Base | Databases with tables, forms and reports |
| Math | Writing mathematical formulas |

Most people only need Writer, Calc and Impress. You do not pay to use them. The website has a Donate link if you want to support the project, but it is your choice and not a requirement.

## How to download and install it safely

The most important rule: download only from the official website, libreoffice.org. Do not use random download sites, apps that claim to be "LibreOffice Pro", or files sent to you on WhatsApp. Unofficial installers can carry unwanted programs or worse.

### What your computer needs

As checked in November 2026, the LibreOffice page lists these needs for Windows:

- Windows 10 or 11 (some Windows Server versions are also listed).
- At least 256 MB of memory, and 512 MB recommended.
- Up to 1.5 GB of free disk space.
- Administrator rights to install.

If your computer is old, check the page for the current list, because requirements can change.

### Steps

1. Open libreoffice.org in your browser and go to Download.
2. Choose your system, such as Windows, and the right type for your processor. The page offers builds for Windows, macOS and Linux. If you are not sure, most Windows PCs use the x86-64 build.
3. Download the file. It is large, so use Wi-Fi if you can. Our guide on [saving mobile data](/post/technology-solutions/smartphones/save-mobile-data-on-your-phone-settings-that-help/) can help if you only have a bundle.
4. Open the downloaded file and follow the steps on screen. You need administrator rights.
5. Open Writer or Calc from the Start menu to check that it works.

The website shows the current version and says older versions are no longer updated. We do not quote a version number here because it changes. Use what the page shows when you visit.

If you are not sure how to install programs safely on Windows, read our guide to [installing and removing programs on Windows safely](/post/technology-solutions/software/install-and-remove-programs-on-windows-safely/).

## Open and save Word, Excel and PowerPoint files

LibreOffice's help pages explain how it works with Microsoft files.

- **To open a file:** choose File, then Open, and pick the file. Word files (.doc, .docx) open in Writer. Excel files (.xls, .xlsx) open in Calc. PowerPoint files (.ppt, .pptx) open in Impress.
- **To save as a Microsoft file:** choose File, then Save As, and pick an Office format in the file type box. This is useful if your teacher, boss or client uses Word or Excel.
- **To make Office formats the default:** go to Tools, then Options, then Load/Save, then General, and choose the format for each document type. You can still choose another format when you save.
- **To open Office files with LibreOffice by default on Windows:** right-click a file, choose Open with, then choose another app, pick LibreOffice and tick the box to always use it. Windows may word this a little differently.

A good habit: after you save a copy in a Microsoft format, close it and open it again to check that it looks right.

### Macros

A macro is a small program inside a file that automates a task. LibreOffice's help says Microsoft Office and LibreOffice cannot, with a few exceptions, run the same macro code. Office macros use VBA, and LibreOffice uses its own system, so macros usually need editing. If a workbook depends on macros, test it carefully before you rely on it.

## What may look different, and the free web option from Microsoft

We could not find a source that promises that every complex Word file will look exactly the same in LibreOffice. So plan for small differences, especially in files with:

- special fonts that are not on your computer,
- complex tables, text boxes or tracked changes,
- charts and fancy layouts,
- macros.

### How to protect yourself

- **Keep your original.** Copy the file before you edit it, and work on the copy.
- **Check the layout** after opening. Look at page breaks, tables and numbers.
- **Check formulas** in Excel files on Calc before you send them to anyone. Look at totals with your own eyes.
- **For important documents,** such as an assignment due today, open the final file once on another computer or ask a friend to check it.

### Microsoft's free web versions

Microsoft also offers Word, Excel and PowerPoint for the web. The company's page says you can use them free with a free Microsoft account, that you get 5 GB of OneDrive storage, and that files must be saved in and shared from OneDrive. The page we read is written for users in the United States and does not list the limits of the free version in detail. So check what is offered to you in Tanzania before you rely on it.

The web versions need an internet connection. LibreOffice works on your computer without one once it is installed, which matters when data is expensive or the network is slow.

A short note on cracks: pirated Office copies and "activators" are not worth it. Unofficial files can carry harmful software, and they can put your documents and passwords at risk. A free legal option exists, and this is it.

## Common questions

### Is LibreOffice really free?

Yes. The LibreOffice website says it is free to use, with no charge for its office tools. If you pay for a "LibreOffice" download on another website, you may be buying something that is not official.

### Can I use it on a phone?

This guide is about computers. The LibreOffice download page lists builds for Windows, macOS and Linux. We did not check phone versions, so look at the official site for what is available.

### Will my teacher or boss be able to open my file?

If you save in a Microsoft format (File, Save As, then choose the Office type), they can usually open it in Word or Excel. Check how it looks, and ask them to tell you if anything looks wrong.

### Will it slow my computer?

LibreOffice lists low minimum needs, as shown above. An old or crowded computer may still be slow. Close other programs while you work.

## What to do next

1. **Check your computer** against the system needs on the LibreOffice website.
2. **Download LibreOffice** from libreoffice.org only, and install it.
3. **Open an old file** you know well, such as a CV, and compare how it looks to the original.
4. **Save a copy** in a Microsoft format with Save As, and open it again to check.

You do not have to pay or take risks to write a good document. A free, honest tool is enough to start.
