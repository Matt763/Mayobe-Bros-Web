English Language at A-Level is a combination subject, and it is also a language of study for almost every other subject. If you are choosing a combination, or you are already in Form Five, it helps to know what the subject really asks. This guide uses the TIE syllabus and NECTA documents that we opened in November 2026.

Do not mix it up with Academic Communication, which is a different subject that every Form Five and Six student takes. We explain the difference below.

## Key points

- English Language is a principal subject only in combinations that list it, for example HGL, HKL, KLF and KLS.
- The TIE syllabus in force on its site is the "English Language Syllabus for Advanced Secondary Education Form V-VI", dated 29 June 2023.
- It has five main competences: spoken and written skills, understanding texts, communicating in different settings, language services (interpreting, translation, editing) and literature.
- NECTA's latest format that we could open has two papers of three hours each. It dates from 2019 and is based on the 2009 syllabus.
- Literature in English is a separate subject with its own syllabus.

## Two subjects with English in them

The 2023 curriculum places several English-based subjects side by side.

- **English Language** is a combination subject. You take it only if it is in your combination.
- **Literature in English** is a separate combination subject. TIE publishes its own syllabus.
- **Academic Communication** is compulsory for all students in Forms Five and Six, whatever the combination. Its syllabus says the purpose is to strengthen the use of English in academic work. It covers punctuation, paragraphs, searching for information with ICT, class presentations, reports, emails, project proposals and simple charts.

If you see "English" on your selection letter, check which one. English Language sits inside a combination, such as HGL (History, Geography, English Language).

## What the syllabus asks you to master

The syllabus lists five main competences, each with specific ones.

1. **Advanced English skills.** Standard pronunciation in connected speech, vocabulary built from conversation and written texts, and correct grammar.
2. **Understanding oral and written information.** Listening with confidence, reading complex texts closely, and reading critically for meaning beyond what the author says openly.
3. **Communicating in different settings.** Managing a conversation well, writing different kinds of text for different readers, and studying how language works in society.
4. **Language services for the community.** Principles of interpreting, translation, editing and proofreading, and using computer tools for translation and editing projects.
5. **Appreciating and creating literature.** Looking at genres, the setting in which a text was written, and writing your own creative pieces.

TIE gives English Language ten periods of 40 minutes a week.

## Form Five in practice

The Form Five tables start with speaking. You practise sounds, stress and intonation in connected speech, describe events and objects in suitable levels of formality, and join discussions on topical issues. The syllabus names examples such as entrepreneurship, corruption, taxation, child rights, cyber security, drugs and money management.

Next is vocabulary. You learn to find word meanings from context, a dictionary, a thesaurus or an encyclopaedia, and to form new words by compounding, blending and clipping. You then use synonyms, antonyms and correct grammar in speech and writing.

The later Form Five and Form Six content builds on this: reading closely, writing different kinds of text, language and society, interpreting, translation, editing and literature. We read the competence list and the opening of the detailed tables, not every line of every table, so ask your teacher for the full scheme of work.

## How ACSEE tests English Language

The latest NECTA format we could open is the "ACSEE Formats 2019" booklet. It says it follows the 2009 English Language syllabus. As of 24 November 2026 we could not find a newer ACSEE format on NECTA's site, so treat the shape below as the last published one.

- **Paper 122/1, English Language 1:** three hours, 100 marks. Section A has four short-answer questions of 10 marks each, all compulsory. Section B has four essay questions of 20 marks; you answer three, two of them compulsory. Topics are language introduction, word formation, the four language skills (listening, speaking, reading, writing), and interpretation and translation.
- **Paper 122/2, English Language 2:** three hours, 100 marks, the same layout. Topics are appreciating literary works (novels and short stories, plays, poetry) and creative and factual writing such as articles, short stories, plays and poems.

The booklet also lists set readings, for example plays and novels by African writers. The Ministry can change them, so confirm with your teacher.

The 2023 curriculum adds a school-based part: 30% of the final grade comes from continuous assessment, and 70% from the national exam at the end of Form Six. The curriculum says a national exam at the end of Form Six awards the certificate. We could not find an official note on when the first Form Six exam based on the 2023 syllabi will be. Ask your school, and watch NECTA's news page.

The ACSEE page on NECTA's site describes the exam but says nothing about oral or practical papers for English in the format we read. The syllabus does teach speaking, so use it in class even if the written paper tests mostly writing.

## Combinations and careers

English Language is a principal subject in HGL, HKL, KLF, KLS and several creative combinations in the 2023 curriculum. Our guides on [HGL](/post/educational/study-tips/jobs-for-hgl-combination/) and [HKL](/post/educational/study-tips/jobs-for-hkl-combination/) show the 2026/27 TCU entry lines for teaching, law, journalism and public service.

Strong English also helps in translation, editing, tourism, teaching, media and any job with international contact. ## Study advice that works for a language

- Read a little every day: a newspaper article, a chapter of a set book, a TIE textbook page. Note five new words and use each in a sentence.
- Write a short text each week and ask a teacher or classmate to mark it. Writing without feedback improves slowly.
- Practise speaking with a partner. Record yourself on a phone and listen for stress and clear endings.
- For literature, make a one-page summary for each set book: plot, characters, themes, and two short quotes.
- Learn to answer the question that is asked. Underline the task word (describe, explain, discuss) before you write.

## Common questions

### Is English Language compulsory in Form Five and Six?

Not as a combination subject. Academic Communication is compulsory, and it is taught in English. English Language is only for combinations that include it.

### Is Academic Communication the same as General Studies?

No. They are different subjects. See our guide on [General Studies](/post/educational/a-level-subjects/a-level-general-studies-topics-tanzania/).

### Do I need to be perfect in English before Form Five?

No. The syllabus starts with pronunciation, vocabulary and grammar. But daily reading and writing practice matters a lot.

### Where can I find the official syllabus?

On the TIE website, in the list of advanced secondary syllabi. The link is below.

## What to do next

1. Check whether your combination includes English Language, Literature in English or neither.
2. Download the TIE English Language syllabus and read the five competences.
3. Ask your teacher for the current set readings and start the shortest one first.

English improves with steady use. A few honest pages each day beat a big push before the exam.
