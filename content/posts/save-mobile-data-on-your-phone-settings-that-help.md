You buy a bundle, and a day or two later it is almost gone. You did not watch many videos, so where did it go? Often it was not you. Your phone was busy in the background, and apps were downloading things on their own.

The good news is that you can take back control. This guide shows where to look and which settings help most. It works on your phone, whatever network you use. We give no bundle prices here, because operators change them often. Check your own network for current offers.

## The short answer

Check which apps use the most data, switch on your phone's data saving mode, stop WhatsApp from downloading media on mobile data, and do big downloads and updates on Wi-Fi. These steps cost nothing and often make a bundle last noticeably longer.

- Look at your data use by app, then deal with the biggest ones.
- Turn on Data Saver (Android) or Low Data Mode (iPhone).
- Set WhatsApp to download photos and videos only when you choose.
- Update apps and download large files on Wi-Fi.
- Know that some apps may stop updating in the background while saving mode is on.

## Find out which apps use your data

Before you change anything, find out what is eating your bundle. The answer may surprise you.

### On Android

Google's help page for Pixel phones shows these steps: open Settings, tap Network & internet, then SIMs, then App data usage. You can change the time period and then see each app's use below the graph. The same area has Data warning & limit. A warning only sends you a notification when you reach a number you set. A limit turns mobile data off when you reach it, and you can turn it back on from the same menu.

Menus differ on other brands, such as Samsung, Tecno, Infinix or Xiaomi. Look for words like "Data usage", "Mobile data" or "Network" in Settings. If the steps do not match, check your phone maker's help pages.

### On iPhone

Apple's help page says to open Settings and tap Cellular (it can be called Mobile Data). Scroll down to see which apps use cellular data. You can switch off the toggle next to an app so it uses only Wi-Fi. The Reset Statistics button at the bottom lets you start counting from zero, which is useful at the start of a new bundle.

### What to look for

One or two apps often use most of the data. Social media, video apps and app stores are common ones. If an app you rarely open appears near the top, it is probably working in the background. That is the first place to act.

## Android Data Saver and iPhone Low Data Mode

Both phones have a built-in switch for this.

### Android Data Saver

On Google's Pixel page the path is Settings, Network & internet, Data Saver. When it is on, most apps and services get background data only over Wi-Fi. Apps you are using at that moment can still use mobile data. A Data Saver icon shows in the status bar.

If an app you need stops working properly, go to Data Saver, then Unrestricted data, and allow that app. Google warns that some apps may not work as expected without background mobile data. That is the honest trade: you save data, and some apps may be slower to show new messages or updates until you open them.

### iPhone Low Data Mode

Apple's page gives these paths. For 4G/LTE: Settings, Cellular, Cellular Data Options, then turn on Low Data Mode. For 5G, it is under Cellular Data Options, then Data Mode. With two SIMs, tap the number you want to change. You can also turn it on for a single Wi-Fi network from Settings, Wi-Fi, by tapping the info button next to the network.

Apple says Low Data Mode stops apps from using data when you are not using them, turns off Background App Refresh, lowers streaming quality, and turns off automatic downloads and backups. Your carrier may change these settings, so the exact steps can differ.

## WhatsApp: stop media from downloading on mobile data

For many people, WhatsApp is the busiest app of all. Photos, videos and voice notes arrive all day in groups, and if automatic download is on, they all come through your bundle.

WhatsApp has a setting for this. On many phones it is under Settings, then Storage and data, in a section about media auto-download. Names can differ by phone and version, so look around that area. There you can choose which kinds of media download when you use mobile data, and which only on Wi-Fi.

A simple plan:

1. Turn off automatic downloads of videos and documents on mobile data.
2. Keep photos on Wi-Fi only if you can.
3. When you want a file, tap it to download it yourself.

Downloading a photo you care about costs only a little data. Automatic downloading of every video in ten groups costs a lot. A bonus is that you save phone storage too. ## Habits that save data: Wi-Fi for big downloads and updates

Settings help, but habits help even more.

- **Update on Wi-Fi.** App and system updates can be large. Wait until you are on Wi-Fi, such as at home, school or a place that allows it.
- **Download before you leave.** If you need a video, a lesson or a map, download it on Wi-Fi first.
- **Lower video quality.** Watching in a lower quality uses less data, and on a small screen you may not notice. Each app has its own place for this setting, so look in the app's own settings.
- **Close apps you are not using.** Do not rely on this alone. Settings above are stronger.
- **Set a warning.** A data warning in your phone tells you when you are close to your limit, before the bundle is gone.

## Common questions

### Will Data Saver stop my calls and SMS?

No. Data Saver and Low Data Mode control data use by apps. Normal calls and text messages are not data. However, calls made through apps such as WhatsApp do use data.

### Why do I still lose data when I do not use my phone?

Apps can refresh, sync and update in the background. Messages arrive, photos back up and apps check for news. Data Saver or Low Data Mode limits much of this. Your usage screen shows which apps are responsible.

### Is it better to turn mobile data off completely?

It is the strongest option, but then you miss messages when you are away from Wi-Fi. A middle path is to keep data on, use the saving mode, and set a warning. You decide what fits your day.

### Can I trust a message that offers free data?

Be careful. We did not check any operator offers for this guide. Scammers send links that promise free bundles. Use only your network's official app, website or shortcode that you already know, and never share your PIN or password.

## What to do next

1. **Open your data usage screen** and write down the top three apps.
2. **Turn on Data Saver or Low Data Mode** and see how your phone behaves for a day.
3. **Change WhatsApp media download** so that large files wait for Wi-Fi.
4. **Set a data warning** a little below the size of your bundle.

Small changes add up. Many people find that a bundle lasts longer once the background waste is gone, and you did nothing wrong to begin with. You are simply taking back the data you paid for.
