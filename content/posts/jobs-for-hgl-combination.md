HGL means History, Geography and English Language as your three principal subjects in Form Five and Form Six. The government calls these groups of subjects "tahasusi", and students simply say "combination". This guide shows which degrees HGL can open, what the 2026/27 TCU guidebook asks for, and what HGL cannot do. It is written for Form Four leavers who must choose, Form Six students waiting for results, and parents.

We read the Tanzania Commission for Universities (TCU) guidebook for 2026/27 in November 2026. Entry rules change every year and differ between universities, so use this as a map, then confirm the rule for your own university.

## Key points

- HGL is listed by the government as a Social Science combination: History, Geography and English Language.
- It fits law, journalism and public relations, arts teaching, international relations, public administration, heritage and archaeology, and tourism-related degrees.
- Many arts degrees ask for two principal passes and 4.0 points. You study three subjects, so you have a spare one.
- English Language as a principal subject helps a lot. Law, journalism and communication degrees ask about English.
- HGL does not lead to medicine, engineering or most science degrees.
- Nobody can promise you a place or a job. Entry is competitive, and pay differs from one job to the next.

## What HGL means and how to confirm it

The official list of combinations on the TAMISEMI selection site gives HGL as "History, Geography and English Language", in the group of Social Science combinations. We checked that page in November 2026.

Schools sometimes use similar codes with different letters. Before you fill any form, check the exact subject names for your school. Ask the headmaster or the academic master, the teacher in charge of subjects.

## How points work

The TCU guidebook counts points from your principal passes. For results from 2016 onward, A = 5, B = 4, C = 3, D = 2, E = 1 and S = 0.5. The general minimum for most degrees is 4.0 points from two subjects that define the programme.

Say you get B in History and C in English Language. That is 4 + 3 = 7 points. Say another student gets D and D. That is 4 points, which meets the minimum on paper but leaves no room. Universities usually have more applicants than places, so higher marks give you more choice. Our guide on [how to read TCU entry requirements](/post/educational/career-development/how-to-read-tcu-programme-entry-requirements/) explains the wording, and this one on [Form Six results and points](/post/educational/results/how-to-read-acsee-results-points-and-divisions/) shows how divisions and points connect.

The University of Dar es Salaam (UDSM) writes its minimum as "5 from 3 subjects". Read its programme page to see how it counts.

## Law with HGL: what is true and what is not

Law is the dream many HGL students carry. The guidebook shows it is open to you. In the UDSM entry, an applicant without principal passes in both History and English must have subsidiary passes or a "C" at O-Level. A student with HGL already has both as principal subjects. That is a real advantage.

But there are limits. UDOM asks for a "D" grade or better in each of the two subjects it counts. The entries also differ in length: UDSM and UDOM list four years, while Iringa lists three.

A law degree is not the end of the road. The Law School of Tanzania gives practical legal training after the degree. Its Postgraduate Diploma in Legal Practice is for people who hold an undergraduate law degree and plan to practise law in Tanzania. Check the school's own notices for current rules and fees. We did not find official fee figures, so we give none.

## Journalism, communication and public relations

Journalism entries are wide open. UDOM and Iringa accept two principals from a long list of subjects. UDOM adds that if neither principal is English, you need at least a "C" in English at O-Level. Iringa asks for a subsidiary pass in English or a "D" at O-Level in that case. HGL already includes English, so this rule does not trouble you.

Your three subjects train the habits this work needs. History teaches you to check where a claim comes from. Geography teaches you to read maps, places and data. English Language trains you to write clearly. These are skills, not a guarantee of a newsroom job.

## Teaching, public service and heritage

**Teaching.** The Mwalimu Nyerere Memorial Academy lists a Bachelor of Education in Geography and History for two principal passes in those two subjects. HGL gives you exactly this pair. Other universities list BA with Education programmes that accept two principals from History, Geography, Kiswahili and English. Government teacher pay follows official scales, which you should check yourself.

**Public administration and international relations.** UDOM lists both degrees with History, Geography, Kiswahili, English or French among the accepted subjects. Jobs may be in councils, ministries, development groups or private firms. Openings and pay vary.

**Heritage and archaeology.** Archaeology, heritage management and cultural heritage and tourism all accept History, and some accept Geography and English. Few places exist compared with large programmes such as law, so read the capacity column in the guidebook.

## For the student who is afraid

Maybe you hear it in the school corridor or at a family table: "Arts is for the weak." It is not true, and the guidebook proves it. Law, journalism, teaching and public administration all ask for arts subjects. Some of these programmes list hundreds of places.

Maybe your parents sold something, or borrowed, or worked extra hours so you could stay in school. You want to repay that faith, and you are afraid to choose wrong. That fear shows you care.

Here is the honest part. HGL is not easy. History needs reading and long answers. Geography needs maps and data. English needs daily practice. And no combination guarantees a job.

Here is the hopeful part. You can do the work. Three subjects you understand, studied steadily for two years, can take you to a university that matches your goals. Start small. One page of reading every day beats one night of panic.

## Where HGL does not fit

- **Medicine, pharmacy and dentistry** need Physics, Chemistry and Biology. See our [PCB guide](/post/educational/study-tips/jobs-for-pcb-combination/).
- **Engineering** normally needs Advanced Mathematics. Look at the [PCM guide](/post/educational/study-tips/jobs-for-pcm-combination/) if that is your dream.
- **Accounting and finance** usually need Mathematics at some level. HGL students sometimes qualify only with a Mathematics pass at O-Level, and only in some universities. Read each entry.

## What if your points are not enough?

1. **Try a related degree.** If law is out of reach, look at public administration, political science, journalism or sociology. They accept many of the same subjects.
2. **Look at a diploma.** Colleges offer diplomas in law, journalism, tourism and teaching. Check that the college and the course are registered. Our guide on [how to check if a university or college is registered](/post/educational/study-tips/how-to-check-university-or-college-is-registered-tanzania/) shows how.
3. **Try for a different university.** The same degree can ask for different points at different places.
4. **Study again and retake.** Ask your teachers whether it is worth the time and cost for your marks.

## If you are in Form Four: is HGL for you?

Look at your Form Four results and at what you enjoy.

- **History:** Do you like reading, remembering events and writing long answers?
- **Geography:** Do you enjoy maps, climate, population and fieldwork?
- **English:** Can you read and write it well, or are you ready to work hard on it?
- **Dream job:** Search for it in the TCU guidebook and see which subjects it asks for.

If History and English are your weak subjects, HGL will be tough. Talk to a teacher before you pick. A combination should fit your strengths and your goal.

## If you are in Form Six: your plan after results

1. Write down three degrees you want, plus a safe choice and a backup.
2. Check each one in the latest guidebook, at each university you like.
3. Apply only through the official university or TCU channels. Nobody can sell you a place.
4. For fees, read our [HESLB loan guide](/post/educational/study-tips/students-loan-tanzania-complete-guide/).

## A simple plan for Form Five and Form Six

1. **First term:** read the guidebook for three degrees you like.
2. **Every week:** write one short English essay and one History answer.
3. **Before NECTA:** do timed past papers. Our post on [study techniques](/post/educational/study-tips/10-proven-techniques-to-study-effectively-for-students/) can help.
4. **After results:** compare your points with each entry and apply early.

## Common questions

### Is HGL a good combination?

It is good if you want law, journalism, teaching or public service and you are comfortable with reading and writing. It is not suitable for medicine or engineering.

### Can I study law with HGL?

Yes. The 2026/27 guidebook lists History, Geography and English Language among the accepted principal subjects for law at several universities. Check the grade rule at your chosen university.

### Do I need English to study journalism?

You need to be able to work in English. The entries we read ask for a pass or grade in English if it is not one of your principals. HGL already includes it.

### Can HGL students become teachers?

Yes. The Mwalimu Nyerere Memorial Academy lists Education in Geography and History for those two principal subjects. Other universities have BA with Education programmes.

### How much do HGL jobs pay?

It varies a lot, and we could not confirm official figures, so we give none. Check government pay scales and real job adverts.

## What to do next

1. Download the latest TCU guidebook and search for law, journalism and teaching.
2. Write down the subjects, points and years of each entry.
3. Talk to a teacher about your History and English marks.
4. Keep a backup degree or diploma in your plan.
