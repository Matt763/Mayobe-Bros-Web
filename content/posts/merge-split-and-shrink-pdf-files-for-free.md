The application portal says "upload one PDF". You have a CV, a certificate, an ID copy and a letter, all in separate files, and some are large phone scans. It is stressful when the deadline is close. This post shows free ways to make one tidy PDF without sending your private papers to a stranger's website.

The tools below run on your own computer. The steps were checked against the makers' help pages in November 2026. Menu names may differ in your version.

## The short answer

- Make a PDF from Word with **File > Save As** and choose PDF.
- Use PDFsam Basic, a free open source desktop app, to merge and split PDFs on your own computer.
- To make a file smaller, reduce image quality or resolution when you create it. LibreOffice has settings for this.
- Be careful with free PDF websites. Your ID and certificates may be copied, so think before you upload them.
- Read the file size limit on the portal itself. We do not state any limit, because each one sets its own.

## How do I make a PDF from Word or LibreOffice?

Microsoft's help page says that in Word for Windows you go to **File > Save As** (or **Save a Copy**), choose a location, set **Save as type** to **PDF**, and select **Save**. Under **Options** you can set a page range, bookmarks and a password. In Word for the web, the page says to go to **File > Print**, choose **Save as PDF**, and select **Save**.

Microsoft also notes that saving as PDF does not prevent editing, because Word and other programs can open and edit PDFs.

For a CV, a PDF keeps the look the same on any phone or computer. Our post on [writing a CV for your first job](/post/jobs-and-internships/how-to-write-a-cv-for-your-first-job-tanzania/) covers what to put in it.

LibreOffice is a free office program that can also export PDFs. Its help page describes the image settings, which we explain further down.

## How do I merge and split PDFs on my own computer?

PDFsam Basic is described on its own site as a free, open source desktop app that splits and merges PDF files on your own computer. It runs on Windows, macOS and Linux. The page says files are processed locally and are not uploaded.

The page lists six tools:

- Merge
- Split
- Mix (interleaving pages)
- Rotate
- Extract pages
- Insert pages multiple times

Say you have four files: a CV, a degree certificate, a transcript and a reference letter. Open the Merge tool, add the four files, put them in the order the employer asks for, and run it. You get one PDF. Always open the result and scroll through every page before you send it.

Splitting is the opposite. If one PDF has 20 pages and the portal wants only pages 3 to 5, use the Split or Extract tool to make a smaller file with those pages.

We did not find a page describing a compression tool in PDFsam Basic, so do not expect it to shrink files. Use the next section for that.

## How do I make a PDF smaller?

Big PDFs are usually big because of pictures, such as phone photos and scans. LibreOffice's help page on PDF export describes three image settings:

- **Lossless compression** keeps all pixels, so files stay larger.
- **JPEG compression** has a quality setting. A high quality keeps almost all pixels. A low quality loses some detail and adds small flaws, but the file is smaller.
- **Change image resolution** shrinks pictures to fewer pixels per inch, which also reduces size.

So the way to make a smaller PDF is to choose JPEG compression with a moderate quality, and lower the image resolution, when you export. Then open the PDF and check that text and stamps are still easy to read. If a certificate is blurry, raise the quality a little.

If you start from scans, take the scan at a sensible size in the first place. Not every document needs to be a huge photo. Your lecturer, employer or portal may say what quality they need, so read their instructions.

## Is it safe to use free PDF websites?

This part is our own common-sense advice, not from an official source, and we give no statistics.

Many websites let you drag in a PDF and get a merged or smaller file. They may be fine for a menu or a poster. But think before you upload papers with your name, ID number, birth date, signature or address. When you upload a file, a copy goes to a computer you do not control. You often cannot see how long it is kept or who can open it.

A safer habit:

- Use a desktop tool such as PDFsam Basic for private files.
- If you must use a website, use it only for papers that are not sensitive.
- Never upload bank statements or ID scans to a site you do not know.
- Be cautious of sites that ask you to install something or make you pay surprise fees.

## What should I check before I send a PDF?

A few minutes of checking can save a rejected application.

- Open the file from the start and scroll to the last page. Make sure no page is upside down, cut off or missing.
- Zoom in on stamps, signatures and small print. If you cannot read them, the reader cannot either.
- Check the order. Employers often list what they want first, such as a letter, then a CV, then certificates.
- Give the file a plain name without odd symbols, such as "amina-cv.pdf". A clear name helps whoever opens it.
- Look at the file size in your file manager and compare it with the limit on the portal.

If something is wrong, go back to your originals and make the PDF again. This is why you keep the original files.

## Common questions

### Why does my PDF look different on my phone?

Phone viewers draw pages in their own way, and fonts or images can look different. Saving a PDF from Word helps the layout stay the same, but always check on the device that matters.

### How do I put pages in a new order?

Merge the files in the order you want. If pages inside one file are in the wrong order, check what your tool offers, and always read the final file.

### What file size does a portal accept?

We do not know, and it differs for each portal. Read the limit and the accepted format on the portal itself, before you start.

### Can I trust a PDF as proof nothing changed?

No. Microsoft's page itself says saving as PDF does not stop editing, and the same is true of any PDF.

## What to do next

1. Collect your documents in one folder and name them clearly, such as "cv.pdf" and "certificate.pdf".
2. Read the portal's file type and size rule.
3. Merge or split on your own computer, then open the result and check every page.
4. If it is too big, export again with lower image quality or resolution, and check that it is still readable. Keep a copy of the originals, perhaps in a [free cloud backup](/post/technology-solutions/software/back-up-your-computer-files-free-cloud-storage/).

You are doing something careful and sensible for your future, and one tidy file may be all that stands between you and a smooth application.
