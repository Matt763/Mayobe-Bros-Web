Your shop may be well known on your street. But a person who is new to the area, or visiting from another town, cannot ask your neighbours. They open their phone and search. If your shop is not there, they walk to the next one.

A free Google Business Profile is one way to be found. This post explains how to claim or add one, what Google may ask for, and how to fill it in honestly. It is general guidance from Google's own help pages, as checked in November 2026. The steps and menus can change, so use the links at the end for the latest version.

## The short answer

A Business Profile is a free listing that can show your shop on Google Search and Maps, with your name, location, opening hours, phone number and photos. You search for your shop first. If a listing exists, you claim it. If not, you add it at business.google.com/add. Then you verify it, fill in the details truthfully, and add clear photos.

- Adding or claiming a profile costs nothing.
- Google decides how you can verify, and you cannot change the method.
- Use your real shop name and a real address. No keywords, no fake places.
- No one can pay Google for a better local rank, so ignore anyone who sells it.
- A profile can help people find you. It does not promise more customers.

## What is a Business Profile and who can have one?

A Business Profile is the box of information people see when they search for a business by name or look for one on the map. It can show the name, address or area served, hours, phone number, photos and customer reviews.

Google says only businesses that are eligible for a profile can add or claim a location. Read Google's guidelines before you start to see if your business qualifies, and do not try to list a business you do not own or run.

## How do I check if my shop is already listed, and how do I claim it?

Sometimes a shop is already on the map, because Google or a customer added it. Always search first. Two listings for one shop confuse people.

On a computer, open Google Maps, search for your shop name and town, and look for an option to claim or manage the business. On the Google Maps app, find your listing, tap "About", then choose the option to claim and manage it. Then pick a verification method and follow the steps.

Google's help page gives three outcomes:

1. **No listing exists.** Go to business.google.com/add and add your business. Enter your details, then choose a verification method and complete it.
2. **A listing exists but nobody has verified it.** You can claim it from Google Maps and then verify it.
3. **Someone has already verified it.** You cannot claim it directly. You need to ask the current owner for ownership, and Google has a separate guide for that. This can happen if a former worker or an old partner set it up. If you see a second listing for the same shop, Google also has a guide for duplicates.

You will need a Google Account. If you do not have one, you can make one with an email address.

## How do I verify my profile?

Verification is how Google checks that you really run the business. Until you verify, the profile is not fully yours.

Google's help page lists several ways: a phone call or text, an email, a live video call, a postcard sent by mail, and a video recording that you make. Which of these you see depends on your business and location. We cannot tell you which options are offered for a shop in Tanzania. Open your profile and look at what Google offers you. Google also says that it chooses the method for you, and you cannot change it. Some businesses need more than one.

If Google uses a postcard, here is what the page says:

- Most codes arrive within 14 days.
- A code expires after 30 days.
- Asking for a new code makes the old one stop working, which can slow things down.
- Do not change your business name, address or category while you wait, because that can make the code stop working.

After you finish the steps, a review may take up to 5 business days, and sometimes longer.

If you see no option you can use, or it takes a long time, check the Business Profile Help pages for the latest steps. In our opinion, be careful with anyone who asks for money to "verify you fast". Google's own help page offers no paid route.

## How do I fill it in honestly?

The details you enter affect whether people trust you and whether Google keeps your listing. Google's guidelines are clear on the main points.

**Name.** Use your real business name, the one on your sign and receipts. Do not add slogans, phone numbers, opening hours or a list of what you sell. Stuffing the name with words like "cheapest phones Dar es Salaam" breaks the rules and can get a profile suspended.

**Address.** Give the precise, real address of your shop. A P.O. box does not count, and neither does a virtual office. We know that many shops in Tanzania are known by landmarks rather than by a street number. Use the clearest real description you can, and use the map pin to show where the shop stands. Never use a made-up address or someone else's building.

**Service area.** If you travel to customers and do not receive them at a shop, such as a mobile phone repairer or a cleaner, Google says you can set a service area and, in general, hide your address. A business with a storefront and a service area can show both.

**Description.** Describe what you do in plain words. Google says no links of any type are allowed in the description, and it asks you to avoid sales language.

**Category.** Choose the fewest categories that describe your core business, and be as specific as you can. A category says what your business is, not everything it sells.

**Hours.** Enter the regular hours when customers can really find you open. If you close for a holiday, add special hours. Hours that are wrong make people angry, and that is worse than not listing them.

**Phone number.** Use a number you answer. If customers call and nobody picks up, the profile works against you.

## How do I add clear photos?

Photos help a stranger see what to look for. Good ones to take: the front of the shop with the sign, the inside, your best products, and the street so people can find you.

Google's photo guidelines say:

- Use JPG or PNG files between 10 KB and 5 MB.
- The recommended size is 720 by 720 pixels, and the minimum is 250 by 250.
- Photos should be sharp, well lit and lightly edited, so they show the business as it really is.
- Videos can be up to 30 seconds and up to 75 MB, at 720p or higher.

Take photos in daylight, wipe the phone camera lens, and hold the phone steady. Ask people for permission before you include their faces. Only add pictures you took yourself or have the right to use.

## Can I pay for a better position?

No. Google's page on local ranking says there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking. It says results depend mainly on relevance (how well your profile matches the search), distance (how far you are from the person searching) and prominence (how well known your business is).

Google lists things that can help: verify your business, keep information complete and accurate, reply to reviews, and add photos. Be careful with sellers who promise to "put you at the top of Google" for a fee. If a promise sounds too good, it is.

## How do I see whether people use my profile?

Once your profile is verified, Google's performance report can show how many people viewed it and how many tapped to call, ask for directions or visit your website. Google says these reports are only for verified profiles, and you must be signed in to the linked account. Views count unique people, at most once a day.

Check it once a month along with the rest of your plan. These numbers show interest, not sales, so do not read too much into them. Also see how to [ask customers for reviews honestly](/post/business-ideas/marketing/ask-customers-for-reviews-honestly/).

## Common questions

### Is a Google Business Profile really free?

Yes. Google says adding or claiming a profile costs nothing. Beware of anyone who asks for money to create or verify one for you.

### What if I do not have a street address?

Describe the real location as clearly as you can and use the map pin. If you only travel to customers, set a service area instead. Do not invent an address.

### What if someone else has already claimed my shop?

You cannot claim a verified profile directly. Google asks you to request ownership from the current owner, using its guide for that process.

### Will a profile bring me more customers?

It makes it possible for more people to find you, but nobody can promise a number. It works best when your hours, phone number and photos are correct and you reply to reviews.

## What to do next

1. Search for your shop name and town on Google Maps today.
2. Claim the listing or add your business, and write down which verification option Google gives you.
3. Fill in your real name, address or service area, hours, phone number and category.
4. Take five clear daylight photos and upload them.

Being easy to find is a small, honest step. Do it carefully and let the shop speak for itself.
