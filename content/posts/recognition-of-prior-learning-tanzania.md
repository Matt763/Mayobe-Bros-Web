Many people in Tanzania can do skilled work every day without a single paper to show for it. You may have learned to sew, fix engines or weld by watching a master and practising for years. Your hands know the work. This post explains a route that exists for exactly this situation, and what it can and cannot promise.

## The short answer

Recognition of Prior Learning (RPL) is a way to have your skills checked against a national standard, even if you learned them at work or at home. If you meet the standard, you can receive a certificate. It is not a shortcut you can buy, and it is not guaranteed.

- VETA, the Vocational Education and Training Authority, runs an RPL programme.
- It is meant for people who learned informally, for example through apprenticeship or work experience.
- You show your skills through a portfolio of evidence, an interview and an assessment.
- If there are gaps, VETA may offer upgrading training before the exam.

As checked in November 2026, this is what VETA's RPL page says. Fees, dates and centres change, so ask VETA for current details.

## What does recognition of prior learning mean?

Prior learning means skills and knowledge you gained before and outside a classroom. The Tanzania Qualifications Framework, published by the Ministry of Education, Science and Technology, lists RPL among its key terms. It describes the idea as assessing and certifying earlier learning, whether it came from formal schooling, non-formal courses or everyday work.

VETA describes its own version in three parts: an assessment, upgrading that is tailored to your gaps, and certification. The page says assessment happens against prescribed standards, regardless of where or how you learned.

The simple idea is that the standard matters, not the place where you learned.

## Who is RPL meant to help?

According to VETA, RPL is for people who learned skills informally, mostly through apprenticeship or work experience, and were never formally certified. At the vocational level it aims at people working at operational or artisan level. It also serves people who missed formal training or did not qualify for it before.

Think of a tailor in a small shop, a bodaboda mechanic, a welder, or a cook who has fed customers for ten years. These are only examples, not a list from VETA. The point is the kind of person the programme has in mind: someone with real, practised skill and no paper.

### What it may lead to

The VETA page lists several possible benefits. It says RPL can:

- open a pathway to formal training
- help people get better jobs or move into the formal economy
- help people qualify as professionals in their field
- make people eligible to bid for government tenders
- help employers with certified workers qualify for quality marks such as TBS and ISO

Treat these as possibilities, not promises. A certificate is a tool. What you do with it still depends on your work, your market and your effort.

## How does the process work?

The VETA page sets out steps. In short, they are:

1. Fill in an RPL assessment form.
2. A VETA facilitator explains the procedures, who is eligible, what skills are needed and how to build a portfolio of evidence.
3. Submit your application. VETA assessors screen it and interview you.
4. Prepare for the assessment.
5. Be assessed against the standard for the qualification you chose.
6. Attend any upgrading training VETA arranges to fill gaps.
7. Sit the examination.
8. Receive a certificate if you qualify.

Notice that the process includes an exam. The certificate is earned, not given. Anyone who offers you an RPL certificate for money without assessment is selling something false, and it could cause you serious trouble later. Do not accept it.

### What is a portfolio of evidence?

The VETA page says you must show your skills through a portfolio. It does not give a list of what goes in it, so ask the facilitator. In general, evidence of real work might include photographs of items you made, a note from people you worked for, or tools and samples you can show. Ask VETA what it accepts rather than guessing.

## How does it link to the qualification levels?

The Tanzania Qualifications Framework sets out levels of qualifications from basic skills to higher degrees. RPL sits inside that framework. The idea is that a person who gains recognition can step into the system at a level that matches their real skill, and from there move on to further study.

The VETA page says RPL opens a pathway to formal training. We could not confirm from the pages we opened which exact level a given person would receive. That depends on the trade, the standard and the assessment, so ask VETA about your own trade.

## What should I ask VETA or a college?

Before you apply, prepare a short list of questions:

- Is my trade covered by an RPL qualification?
- Where is the nearest centre that does the assessment?
- What does it cost, and how do I pay safely?
- What goes in the portfolio of evidence?
- If I have gaps, how long is the upgrading training?
- Which level does this certificate sit at, and where can I study next?

Write the answers down with the date and name of the person who gave them. Pay only through official channels.

## Common questions

### Can I get a certificate without sitting any test?

According to the VETA page, no. You are assessed, you may attend upgrading, and you sit an exam before a certificate is issued.

### Is RPL only for vocational trades?

The VETA page we read speaks about vocational and artisan level. The national framework treats RPL as a wider idea. Whether universities in Tanzania offer it, we could not confirm. Ask the institution you have in mind.

### Does RPL cost money?

The page we read does not state a fee. Ask VETA directly and ask for the answer in writing.

### Will a certificate guarantee a better job?

No one can promise that. VETA lists better jobs as something RPL can help with. Your skill, your customers and your references still do the heavy lifting.

## What to do next

1. Write down your trade, how many years you have practised it, and who taught you or employed you.
2. Start a simple folder of evidence: clear photographs of your work, names and phone numbers of people who can confirm it, and any papers you already have.
3. Visit the VETA website or your nearest VETA office and ask about RPL for your trade.
4. If you also want to keep learning, read our post on [how to build a career from scratch](/post/educational/career-development/how-to-build-a-career-from-scratch/).

You do not need to start again from zero. The years you already put in are the first piece of evidence.
