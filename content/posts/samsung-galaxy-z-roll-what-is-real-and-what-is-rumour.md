You may have seen videos or articles about the "Samsung Galaxy Z Roll 5G", a phone whose screen rolls out like a scroll. We checked, and as of November 2026 Samsung has not announced or sold a phone with that name. This page explains what a rollable phone is, what Samsung has actually shown, and which claims online are guesses.

Details such as specs, colours and durability figures that you may see online are unconfirmed. We list below only what we could check.

## The short answer

- Samsung has not officially announced a Galaxy Z Roll 5G. You cannot buy one.
- The name "Z Roll" comes from a trademark filing in the European Union in 2021. A trademark does not prove a product is coming.
- Samsung's display company has shown rollable and slidable screen prototypes at trade shows. Those are test screens, not phones for sale.
- Videos and "full specs" pages with prices, cameras and release dates are usually made-up renders or repeated leaks.
- If a shop or website offers you a "Galaxy Z Roll" today, be careful. It may be a fake or a scam.

## What is a rollable phone?

A rollable phone has a flexible screen that is wound around a roller inside the body. A small motor (or your hand) pulls more of the screen out, so a phone-sized display becomes a bigger one. When you are done, the screen winds back in.

A foldable phone, such as Samsung's Galaxy Z Fold series, works differently. The screen bends at a hinge in the middle. That creates a visible line (a crease) where it folds.

## What is confirmed

These points come from documents or statements that were reported by reliable outlets.

- **A trademark.** In May 2021, a filing for "Z Roll" by Samsung Electronics was found at the EU's intellectual property office. The filing covers a broad class that includes mobile phones. It does not describe a product.
- **Patents.** Samsung has filed patents for slidable and rollable devices. Tech Advisor lists a "dual slidable" device patent filed in 2020 and published in January 2021. A patent shows an idea was explored, not that a phone will be made.
- **Display research.** In January 2021, a senior Samsung Display executive said on an earnings call that the company was developing rollable and slideable displays. He did not say whether Samsung's phone division would use them.
- **Prototypes.** Samsung Display has shown rollable concepts at trade shows. The German site netzwelt names two: "Flex Hybrid" (CES 2023) and "Rollable Flex" (SID 2023). These are display demonstrations.

## What is rumour

Everything below is unconfirmed. Treat it as a guess until Samsung says it.

- **Screen size.** One leak, reported by netzwelt in August 2026, claims a 12.4-inch screen when fully rolled out. Earlier reports guessed about 8 inches. These numbers do not agree, which is a sign nobody outside Samsung knows.
- **Release date.** Some websites say the phone will arrive in the second half of 2026. Netzwelt, citing a Korean report, says Samsung may be aiming for 2027 or 2028, and even that is a rumour. As of November 2026, Samsung's latest foldable is the Galaxy Z Fold8, which netzwelt says launched on 22 July 2026.
- **Price, camera, battery, chip, "5G".** We found no reliable source for any of these. Almost every phone sold today supports 5G, so the "5G" in the name tells you little.
- **Durability claims.** Some websites quote a number of roll cycles or years of use for a Z Roll. We found no Samsung source for any such figure, so treat it as unverified.

## Why rollable phones are hard to build

Rolling a screen sounds simple, but a phone has to survive pockets, bags, dust and years of use. Here are the usual problems, based on how the technology works and on what other companies have faced.

- **Moving parts.** A motor, a roller and a sliding frame can wear out or jam. A foldable has fewer moving parts.
- **Dust and water.** Gaps where the screen slides in and out are harder to seal. Many foldables already struggle with dust.
- **Battery space.** The roller and motor take room that a battery could use.
- **Screen wear.** A flexible screen that rolls thousands of times can develop marks or dead spots.
- **Cost.** New parts and low production numbers make early phones expensive.

## Other companies have tried

Samsung is not the only maker with a rollable idea.

- **LG.** LG showed a Rollable phone at CES 2021. In February 2021, reports said it had been scrapped, and LG denied that any decision had been made at that time (Laptop Mag, 23 February 2021). LG later left the phone business, and the Rollable never went on sale.
- **Oppo.** Tech Advisor notes that Oppo unveiled a rollable concept called the Oppo X 2021 in November 2020. It was a concept, not a product.

The lesson is that rollable phones have been shown several times in the last six years, but none has become a normal product you can buy in a shop.

## Would a rollable phone make sense in Tanzania?

Hard to say, and this is our opinion, not a fact. Most buyers here choose affordable phones, and a first-generation rollable would very likely cost much more than a normal one. Foldables already sit at the top of the price range. Repairs would also be harder, since authorised repair for a brand-new design is usually found only in big cities such as Dar es Salaam.

For most people, a solid mid-range phone with a good battery and a screen protector is better value than any new screen format.

## How to spot fake "Galaxy Z Roll" claims

1. Check Samsung's official website for your country. If the phone is not listed there, it is not on sale.
2. Be careful with videos that show "full specs" before any launch event. Many use computer-made images.
3. Do not pay a deposit or "pre-order" fee to a seller on social media. Real launches have official pre-order pages.
4. Compare any claim with at least two trusted tech sites, and look at whether they say "leak" or "rumour".
5. Ask for the IMEI and check the phone before paying for any new model, using the steps for your network.

## Common questions

### Is the Samsung Galaxy Z Roll 5G real?

Not as a product you can buy. Samsung has not announced it. A trademark and some patents exist, and Samsung Display has shown prototype rollable screens.

### When will Samsung release a rollable phone?

Nobody outside Samsung knows. Reports we found give dates from late 2026 to 2028, and none is confirmed by Samsung.

### Is a rollable phone better than a foldable?

Not necessarily. A rollable could avoid the crease, but it has more moving parts. Foldables are on sale now and have a few years of real-world use behind them.

### Should I wait for it before buying a phone?

No. Buy what fits your budget and needs now. A phone that may not exist for years is not a reason to delay.
