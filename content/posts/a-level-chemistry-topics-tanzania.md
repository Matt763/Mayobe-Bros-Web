Chemistry sits in more A-Level combinations than almost any other science subject. PCM, PCB, CBG, CBA and CBN all include it, and medicine, nursing, pharmacy and many laboratory degrees ask for it. If you are choosing a combination or starting Form Five, this guide explains what you will study, how NECTA tests it, and where students usually struggle.

## The short answer

Chemistry (NECTA code 132) has two theory papers and one practical paper. The topics run from atomic structure in Form Five to equilibrium, kinetics and electrochemistry in Form Six, with a lot of organic chemistry in between.

- The practical paper tests chemical analysis: titration, a physical chemistry experiment and qualitative analysis.
- In 2025, almost all candidates passed (97.23%), but two topics had very low scores: Energetics and Selected Compounds of Metals.
- Many health degrees ask for a C in Chemistry, so aim higher than a bare pass.

## The papers

From the NECTA ACSEE formats booklet (revised July 2019):

| Paper | Time | Structure |
|---|---|---|
| 132/1 Chemistry 1 | 3 hours | Seven short questions (10 marks each, all compulsory) and two of three structured questions (15 marks each). 100 marks. |
| 132/2 Chemistry 2 | 3 hours | Five of six questions, 20 marks each. 100 marks. |
| 132/3 Chemistry 3 | 3 hours 20 minutes | Practical. Three questions of 20, 15 and 15 marks. 50 marks. |

The practical comes in three equal versions (3A, 3B, 3C), and your school sits one. The format allows a qualitative analysis guide sheet authorised by NECTA, and non-programmable calculators. For the May 2026 exam, the NECTA timetable put Chemistry 1 and Chemistry 2 on 6 and 8 May, and the practical versions between 13 and 25 May.

The 2025 NECTA report adds that candidates were graded into topics by the share who scored 35% or more. That is a rough measure, but it shows where students struggled.

## Topics by Form Five and Form Six

The TIE Chemistry syllabus (2023) for Form Five and Six organises the work in three strands: concepts and theory, experiments, and a project that begins in Form Five and ends in Form Six. Here is the content, in our own words.

**Form Five**

- **Atomic structure.** Early atomic models, atomic mass, the quantum theory and electron configuration.
- **Chemical bonding.** How atoms join, and what that does to the properties of a substance.
- **Gases.** The gas laws and the kinetic theory, with everyday uses such as tyres, aerosols and fire extinguishers.
- **The periodic table.** Trends across periods and groups: atomic radius, ionisation energy, electron affinity, electronegativity, melting point and metallic character.
- **Colligative properties.** Boiling point rise, freezing point fall, osmotic pressure and Raoult's law.
- **Two-component liquid systems.** Liquids that mix, partly mix or do not mix, and the distribution law.
- **Energetics.** Heat of reaction, enthalpy and Hess's law.
- **Organic chemistry.** Aromatic compounds, haloalkanes, phenols, carbonyl compounds, carboxylic acids and their derivatives, amines and polymers, plus naming with IUPAC rules and making simple products such as soaps and perfumes.

**Form Six**

- **Chemical equilibrium.** Dynamic, ionic and acid-base equilibrium, buffer solutions and acid-base titrations, with industrial uses.
- **Transition elements.** Variable oxidation states, catalysts, magnetic properties and alloys.
- **Complex compounds.** Types of complexes, what affects their formation, and their reactions.
- **Chemical kinetics.** Rate laws, activation energy and catalysis.
- **Electrochemistry.** Cells, electrode potentials, batteries, corrosion and purifying metals.
- **Project report.** You finish and submit the project you began in Form Five.

The syllabus gives Chemistry ten periods of 40 minutes each week.

## A warning about two lists

The NECTA exam format we read (2019) lists some topics that we could not find in the content tables of the 2023 TIE syllabus. Examples are aliphatic hydrocarbons, soil chemistry and environmental chemistry as separate topics. The 2023 syllabus is written for the improved curriculum. NECTA's 2025 report says the 2025 exam followed the 2019 format built on the 2010 syllabus.

In July 2024 the Ministry of Education postponed the improved Form Five curriculum to 2025 (The Citizen, 28 July 2024). We could not find an official page that says which ACSEE year is the first to be examined on the new syllabus, or a NECTA format written for it, as checked on 24 November 2026. So two cohorts may study slightly different lists. Ask your teacher which syllabus applies, and keep both lists in mind. Aliphatic hydrocarbons are also a basis for the organic work above, so you cannot skip them.

## How you are assessed

The 2023 syllabus sets continuous assessment at 30% and the national Form Six exam at 70%. In Form Five, term exams count 5% each. In Form Six, the first term exam is 6%, the project 7% and the mock exam 7%.

## What the 2025 report shows

In 2025, 46,762 candidates sat Chemistry and 45,395 passed (97.23%), a small drop from 2024. The report ranks topics by the share who scored at least 35%:

| Topic | Share who scored 35% or more | Rating |
|---|---|---|
| Environmental Chemistry | 87.47% | Good |
| Aromatic Hydrocarbons | 86.70% | Good |
| Aliphatic Hydrocarbons | 84.65% | Good |
| Chemical Bonding | 72.93% | Good |
| Two-component liquid systems | 51.89% | Average |
| The Atom | 40.30% | Average |
| Carbonyl compounds, carboxylic acids and derivatives | 40.07% | Average |
| Energetics | 21.28% | Weak |
| Selected Compounds of Metals | 6.76% | Weak |

The report says candidates made errors in formulas, equations and calculation methods in the weak topics. The practical paper went well: each of the three practical questions had more than 86% of candidates scoring 35% or above.

Selected Compounds of Metals went from 75.81% in 2024 to 6.76% in 2025, so a topic can swing a lot between years. Do not guess which topic comes next.

## How to revise Chemistry

1. **Write equations, not only notes.** The report links weak results to wrong formulas and equations. Balance every equation and write state symbols.
2. **Practise energetics sums.** Use Hess's law cycles and the Born-Haber cycle (the report names both) until you can set them up without help.
3. **Learn organic chemistry as families.** Make a map: which reaction turns which family into which. Practise IUPAC names every day for a short time.
4. **Master titration.** Do the calculation in steps: moles, ratio, concentration. The report recommends hands-on titration with dilution.
5. **Learn the qualitative analysis scheme.** Know the tests for common ions and gases, write observations exactly as seen, and give conclusions only after the observation.
6. **Use past questions properly.** Attempt first, then compare with the examiners' report. See our guide on [using NECTA examiners' reports to revise](/post/educational/study-tips/use-necta-examiners-reports-to-revise/).

## Combinations and degrees

Chemistry appears in these combinations: **PCM**, **PCB**, **CBG**, **CBA** and **CBN**. Examples from the TCU 2026/27 guidebook:

- Bachelor of Science in Nursing: Chemistry, Biology and either Physics, Advanced Mathematics or Nutrition, at least 6 points, with at least a C in Chemistry.
- Bachelor of Science in Chemistry at the University of Dar es Salaam: Chemistry plus another suitable A-Level subject, and a C or above in Chemistry at O-Level.

## Common questions

### Is Chemistry compulsory for medicine?

Yes. The guidebook asks for Physics, Chemistry and Biology for Medicine, with at least a D in each and 6 points in total. As checked on 24 November 2026, read the latest guidebook before you apply.

### Is Chemistry harder than Physics or Biology?

It depends on you. Chemistry mixes memory (reactions, tests) with calculation (moles, equilibrium). Students who like patterns often do well, but you must practise both parts.

### What if I cannot do the practical well?

Practise titration and qualitative analysis whenever the laboratory is open. In 2025, candidates did well in all three practical questions, so the practical is a place to gain marks.

## What to do next

1. Get the syllabus from TIE and mark topics as strong, medium or weak.
2. Give Energetics, the Atom and Selected Compounds of Metals extra hours.
3. Do one titration calculation and one organic naming drill each week.
4. If you are still choosing, compare the degrees you want against the Chemistry grade they ask for.

You do not need to be perfect in every topic. Small, regular steps add up.
