Two years of Form Five and Form Six end with one result page, and then a new question: is it enough for university? This post shows how the grades on your ACSEE result turn into the points that universities count, so you can check your own position before you apply.

## The short answer

NECTA gives you a grade for each subject and a division. Universities do something a little different. The Tanzania Commission for Universities (TCU) turns your best two principal grades into points, and most degree programmes want at least 4.0 in total.

- The TCU scale for A-Level studies from 2016 onwards is A = 5, B = 4, C = 3, D = 2, E = 1 and S = 0.5.
- Add the points of your two principal passes. For example, a C and a D give 3 + 2 = 5.
- Applicants who do not have two principal passes totalling at least 4.0 are advised by TCU not to apply.
- The NECTA division and the TCU points are not the same number. Do not mix them up.

This was checked in November 2026 against the TCU guidebook for the 2026/27 admission cycle. The scale is printed in each year's guidebook, so check the new one before you apply.

## Principal and subsidiary passes

At A-Level you study subjects at two levels. A principal subject is one of your main combination subjects, for example Physics in a PCM combination. NECTA also requires General Studies of every candidate, and a candidate must have at least three credits in the CSEE to sit for ACSEE.

Universities talk about two kinds of passes.

- **Principal pass:** a pass in a main subject of your combination. Most degree programmes ask for two of these in the right subjects.
- **Subsidiary pass:** a lower-level pass, often asked for as an extra in a subject such as Advanced Mathematics or Basic Applied Mathematics. Some courses in the guidebook, such as architecture, ask for it on top of two principal passes.

Each programme in the guidebook says exactly which subjects it wants. A degree in one field may need Physics and Mathematics, and another may accept History, Geography or Kiswahili. Points are only part of the rule. The subjects must also be the right ones.

## How NECTA shows your result

On the 2025 ACSEE results pages, each school page lists a candidate number, an aggregate (AGGT), a division (DIV) and the grade for each subject. That is the NECTA view of your result.

NECTA does not print a text rule for the divisions on these pages. In the school pages we sampled from the 2025 results, aggregates seen for Division I were 3 to 9, for Division II 10 to 12 and for Division III 13 to 17. We saw too few pages with Division IV or Division 0 to give ranges. These numbers are what we saw in NECTA's 2025 published results. They are not an official rule and they may differ in another year.

The key point is this. The NECTA aggregate is not your TCU points total. They use different scales and add different things. A student can have a good NECTA division and still miss a course because the subjects do not match, and the reverse can also be true. For university entry, the TCU points and the subject rules matter most.

## How TCU turns grades into points

The TCU admission guidebook for holders of secondary school qualifications has a table of minimum entry requirements. For A-Level studies from 2016 onwards, it says the applicant needs 4.0 points in two subjects that define the programme, using this scale:

| Grade | Points |
| --- | --- |
| A | 5 |
| B | 4 |
| C | 3 |
| D | 2 |
| E | 1 |
| S | 0.5 |

The guidebook gives different scales for A-Level studies before 2014 and in 2014 and 2015. If you finished A-Level in an older year, read that row of the table. The scale above is for results from 2016 onwards.

## Reaching the 4.0 minimum

Here are some plain examples. They are made-up combinations to show the sum, not real candidates.

| Two principal grades | Points |
| --- | --- |
| B and C | 4 + 3 = 7 |
| C and D | 3 + 2 = 5 |
| D and D | 2 + 2 = 4 |
| D and E | 2 + 1 = 3 |
| E and E | 1 + 1 = 2 |

In these examples, the first three meet the 4.0 minimum and the last two do not. Remember that 4.0 is the minimum TCU asks for, not a promise of a place. Many programmes set their own higher points, and places are limited. The guidebook says that institutions decide on admission through their own boards and that capacity differs. A programme may list a minimum of 4.0 and still need more in practice because many applicants compete for few places.

If your total is below 4.0, the guidebook says applicants in that position are advised not to apply to degree programmes, since they do not meet the minimum. That can feel heavy, and we will not pretend otherwise. But it is not the only road. Our post on [what to do when you are not selected](/post/educational/study-tips/not-selected-for-university-what-to-do-next/) covers other routes, and some programmes accept an ordinary diploma later.

## Common questions

### Is the NECTA division the same as the TCU points?

No. The division on the NECTA page is NECTA's summary of your result. The TCU points come from the scale in the guidebook and are used only for admission to degree programmes. Always use the TCU scale for university applications.

### Do subsidiary passes count for points?

On the TCU scale, S is worth 0.5 points. Some programmes also list a subsidiary pass in a particular subject as a separate requirement. Read the programme line in the guidebook to see which applies.

### Which year's guidebook should I read?

Read the one for the year you are applying. We cite the 2026/27 edition, published by TCU in August 2026. Scales and programme lists can change, so check the new edition when it appears.

### Where do I learn how to read the guidebook itself?

Our post on [how to read the TCU admission guidebook](/post/educational/study-tips/how-to-read-tcu-admission-guidebook/) walks through the tables step by step.

## What to do next

1. Write down your grades in your two best principal subjects and add the points using the table above.
2. Open the TCU guidebook for the current year and find programmes whose subject rules match your combination.
3. Compare your points with the programme minimum, and keep in mind that many programmes need more than 4.0 to be selected.
4. If you fall short, ask your school's academic office or the institution about other entry routes, such as a diploma.

Reading your own numbers honestly is the first brave step, and it puts you in charge of what comes next.
