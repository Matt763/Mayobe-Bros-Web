Most small traders know roughly how the business is doing. Roughly is the trouble. When someone asks how much you made last month, a guess is not enough for a loan officer, for a tax officer or for you.

The fix is old and simple: write it down the same day. This post shows a cash book you can keep in ten minutes a day, with a made-up week of numbers so you can copy the method.

## The short answer

Keep one notebook. Every day, write each shilling that came in and each shilling that went out, with the date and a few words. Each evening, work out the balance and compare it with the cash and mobile money you really have.

- Write it the same day. Memory fades by the weekend.
- Record sales, expenses, stock bought, loans and your own withdrawals.
- Keep your own money and the business money apart.
- Keep receipts and slips in one envelope or box.
- Once a month, add up and check your profit.

## What should I record?

Five things cover almost every small business.

1. **Sales.** Cash, mobile money and goods given on credit (mark credit clearly, because it is not money in hand yet).
2. **Expenses.** Transport, rent, market fee, airtime, packaging, a helper's pay.
3. **Stock bought.** What you paid the supplier, and what you paid to bring the goods in.
4. **Loans.** Money you borrowed, money you lent to customers, and every repayment.
5. **Money you take for yourself.** Pocket money, school fees, a family emergency. Write each one as "owner took".

Keep every receipt and delivery slip that you can. TRA's own pages carry the line "Whenever you sell, issue receipt. Whenever you buy, demand receipt." (as checked in November 2026). A receipt is proof for you as well as for the tax officer.

## How do I keep a cash book in ten minutes a day?

You need a hard-cover exercise book and a pen. Rule five columns across two pages:

| Date | What happened | Money in | Money out | Balance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Each line is one thing. After each line, work out the new balance: last balance, plus money in, minus money out.

Here is a made-up week for a small shop that starts Monday with TSh 50,000 in cash and mobile money together. The numbers are invented to show the method.

| Day | What happened | In (TSh) | Out (TSh) | Balance (TSh) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mon | Start of week | | | 50,000 |
| Mon | Sales | 40,000 | | 90,000 |
| Mon | Stock bought | | 30,000 | 60,000 |
| Tue | Sales | 35,000 | | 95,000 |
| Tue | Transport | | 3,000 | 92,000 |
| Wed | Sales | 28,000 | | 120,000 |
| Wed | Airtime | | 2,000 | 118,000 |
| Thu | Sales | 45,000 | | 163,000 |
| Thu | Stock bought | | 50,000 | 113,000 |
| Fri | Sales | 52,000 | | 165,000 |
| Fri | Market fee | | 1,000 | 164,000 |

Check the totals. Money in: 40,000 + 35,000 + 28,000 + 45,000 + 52,000 = 200,000. Money out: 30,000 + 3,000 + 2,000 + 50,000 + 1,000 = 86,000. Start 50,000 + 200,000 - 86,000 = 164,000. It matches the last balance.

Each evening, count the cash in your box and look at your mobile money balance. Together they should equal the book balance. If they do not, find the difference today, while you still remember the day.

## Is the balance my profit?

No, and this surprises many people. In the week above, TSh 80,000 went on stock. That stock is not lost. Some of it is still on your shelf, waiting to be sold. The balance is cash, not profit.

To find profit for the month, use this simple line:

**Profit = sales - cost of the goods you sold - other expenses**

Say, in the made-up week, the goods you actually sold cost you TSh 140,000 to buy, and your other expenses were 3,000 + 2,000 + 1,000 = 6,000. Then profit is 200,000 - 140,000 - 6,000 = **TSh 54,000**. To know the cost of goods sold, count your stock on the first and last day of the month. Our post on [how to price your products](/post/business-ideas/startups/how-to-price-your-products-small-business-tanzania/) shows how to turn this into a price.

## How do I keep my money and the business money apart?

When the shop's cash pays for your child's shoes and you do not write it down, the shop looks weaker than it is, and your home budget looks stronger than it is. Both pictures are wrong.

- Decide a fixed amount you take for yourself each week or month, like a salary. Write it in the book as "owner took".
- Use one mobile money line only for the business if you can. Our post on [mobile money for business](/post/business-ideas/startups/mobile-money-for-business-lipa-kwa-simu-tanzania/), explains merchant numbers.
- If you must take extra cash for an emergency, write it down. Later you will know what the business owes you or what you owe it.

## Why does TRA care about my records?

TRA's page on income tax for individuals says that traders under the presumptive system do not have to file audited accounts. But it also shows that the tax depends on whether your records are complete or incomplete. This is as checked in November 2026, so confirm the current table on the TRA website.

Here are two bands from that table, with made-up yearly turnover (turnover means total sales for the year):

| Yearly turnover | Tax if records are incomplete | Tax if records are complete |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Above TSh 4 million, up to TSh 7 million | TSh 100,000 | 3% of the turnover above TSh 4 million |
| Above TSh 7 million, up to TSh 11 million | TSh 250,000 | TSh 90,000 plus 3% of the turnover above TSh 7 million |

Say your turnover is TSh 5,000,000. Incomplete records: TSh 100,000. Complete records: 3% of 1,000,000 = TSh 30,000.

Say your turnover is TSh 9,000,000. Incomplete records: TSh 250,000. Complete records: 90,000 + 3% of 2,000,000 = 90,000 + 60,000 = TSh 150,000.

So, in these two examples, good records mean a smaller tax bill by TSh 70,000 and TSh 100,000. Above TSh 11 million and up to TSh 200 million, the table moves to one rate on turnover, which you can read on the TRA page. For the full picture see our post on [presumptive tax for small business](/post/business-ideas/startups/small-business-tax-tanzania-presumptive-tax-explained/).

For traders outside the presumptive system, TRA says proper records are required, with provisional returns on or before 31 March and a final return on or before 30 June of the following year. TRA also says taxpayers must keep the documents needed to work out tax correctly, for at least five years from the end of the year of income, unless the Commissioner says otherwise in writing. A TRA officer also asked traders to keep proper records so correct tax can be assessed, as reported by the Daily News on 1 November 2023.

This post is general information, not tax or accounting advice. For your own case, ask TRA or a qualified accountant.

## What should I do at the end of each month?

Take thirty minutes.

1. Add up all sales and all expenses for the month.
2. Count your stock and write down its value at cost.
3. Work out profit with the line above.
4. Write down what customers owe you and what you owe suppliers.
5. Put the receipts of the month in one envelope with the month written on it.

Over a few months you will see patterns: which week is slow, which expense keeps growing, which item pays best. That is the real prize of record keeping, more than the tax.

## Common questions

### Do I need a computer or an app?

No. A notebook and a pen work. If you later want an app or a spreadsheet, use the same columns, and make sure you can still export or print your records.

### What if I forget to write for a few days?

Sit down and rebuild it from receipts, mobile money messages and your memory. Mark those lines as "rebuilt". Then go back to daily writing.

### Should credit sales go in the cash book?

Write them in a separate small list, with the customer name, the amount and the date. Move them to the cash book only when the money arrives.

### How long should I keep my records?

TRA's page says at least five years from the end of the relevant year of income for people liable to tax, unless the Commissioner notifies otherwise. Ask TRA if your situation is special.

## What to do next

1. Buy an exercise book today and rule the five columns.
2. Start tomorrow with the cash and mobile money you really have as the opening balance.
3. Keep every receipt in one envelope.
4. Block thirty minutes at the end of this month for your first profit check.

You do not need to be good with numbers. You only need to be honest and regular, and that is something every small trader can do.
