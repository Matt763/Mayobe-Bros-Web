You have your certificates and a CV, and now the advert asks for a letter. Many people copy an old one and change the name. A short letter written for this one job feels very different to the person reading it, and it is not hard to write.

## The short answer

An application letter (also called a cover letter) is a short note that goes with your CV. It tells the employer which job you want, why you fit it, and how to reach you. Keep it to one page, with three to five short paragraphs, and write a fresh one for each job.

- Part 1: say which job you are applying for and where you saw it.
- Part 2: show how your skills and experience match what the job needs.
- Part 3: say why you want this job and this employer.
- Part 4: close politely and ask for an interview.
- Follow the advert. If it asks for a signed letter addressed to a named office, do exactly that.

## What an application letter does that a CV cannot

A CV is a list. A letter lets you connect two or three items on that list to the job in front of you.

The careers service Prospects (UK) says a letter should add context to your CV and not repeat it. It also gives a simple test: take out the employer's name. If the letter could be sent to any other company, it is not specific enough.

The letter also shows how you write. Employers read hundreds of applications, so clear writing with no mistakes helps you stand out. Note that the advice here comes from UK and US university careers services. It is general advice. When a Tanzanian advert gives its own instructions, those come first.

## The four parts of a good letter

Both Prospects and the University of Washington careers centre suggest a short letter of about half to one page. Here is a layout that follows their general advice, written in our own words.

### 1. The opening

Write the job title and say where you saw the advert. Add one line on why you are writing. You do not need to repeat your name and degree, since your CV already has them.

### 2. The match

Pick two or three things the advert asks for. For each, give one example from your life that proves it. Use a real example from school, a project, volunteering or work. Say what you did and what the result was.

### 3. Why you

Say what attracts you to this work and this employer. Show you looked them up: read their website or notice board and mention one thing that is true. Focus on what you can give, not only on what you hope to get.

### 4. The closing

Thank the reader for their time. Say you would be glad to attend an interview. Give your phone number and email once more. If you have a date when you cannot attend, you can mention it.

Prospects says to mention salary only if the advert asks for it.

## A worked example (clearly made up)

The example below is invented. The company, the person and the post do not exist. Use it only to see the shape, and write your own words.

> Your Name
> Phone: 07XX XXX XXX
> Email: yourname@example.com
> Town, Date
>
> The Manager
> Example Hardware Company Ltd
> P.O. Box 000, Town
>
> Dear Sir or Madam,
>
> **Application for the post of Store Assistant**
>
> I am writing to apply for the post of Store Assistant advertised on your notice board in the town centre.
>
> The advert asks for someone who can keep clear records. During school holidays I helped in a family shop, where I wrote down every sale in a book and counted stock each Friday. I am also comfortable using a phone calculator and a basic spreadsheet.
>
> You also ask for someone who works well with customers. In the shop I greeted customers, helped them find items and handled complaints politely. I am used to standing for long hours and being on time.
>
> I would like to work with Example Hardware because you supply builders in this area, and I want to learn more about the products they use. I am ready to learn and to follow your safety rules.
>
> Thank you for your time. I would be glad to attend an interview. You can reach me on the phone number above.
>
> Yours faithfully,
> Your Name

Notice what it does. It is short, it uses the words of the advert, and every claim is something the writer can explain in an interview. Do not copy the sentences. If your example is not true for you, do not use it.

## Letters for public service posts

Public service adverts have their own rules. The vacancy announcement from the Public Service Recruitment Secretariat (PSRS) we read in November 2026 said that a signed application letter should be written in Swahili or English. It named the Secretary of the PSRS in Dodoma as the person to address it to. The address in that advert was a post office box in Dodoma, and we are not printing it here, because adverts can change. Copy it from the advert you are answering.

The same advert said the application itself must be sent through the recruitment portal, and applications sent by other means would not be considered. So the letter is attached with your other documents through the portal. Our post on [applying through the Ajira Portal](/post/jobs-and-internships/ajira-portal-how-to-apply-public-service-jobs/) explains the steps we could confirm.

A good rule is to treat the advert as your checklist. Tick off each requirement before you send.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **One letter for every employer.** Rewrite the opening and the match each time.
- **Repeating your CV.** Add a story or a reason, not a second list.
- **A long letter.** Prospects says shorter is better. Keep to one page.
- **Spelling and grammar errors.** Prospects advises taking a break before you proofread, then asking someone who does not know your work to read it.
- **Wrong name or office.** Check the spelling of the employer's name and the job title.
- **Exaggeration.** Do not claim skills you do not have.
- **Missing signature or contacts.** A signed letter was required in the PSRS advert we read. Sign it by hand if the advert asks.
- **Paying for a letter.** You do not need to buy a "special" letter from anyone. Read [fake job offers: warning signs](/post/jobs-and-internships/fake-job-offers-tanzania-warning-signs-never-pay/) if someone asks for money.

## Common questions

### Can I use the same letter for an internship?

Use the same four parts, but say what you want to learn and when you are free. Change the examples to match the internship.

### Should I write in Swahili or English?

Follow the advert. The PSRS advert we read accepted Swahili or English. Use the language you write best, and keep the sentences simple.

### Is a handwritten letter acceptable?

Only follow the advert. If it says typed, type it. If it asks for a signature, sign it by hand and scan it.

### How long should the letter be?

Half a page to one page. University of Washington advice says no more than one page.

## What to do next

1. Write down two or three real examples from your school, work or volunteering.
2. Draft the four parts for one real advert, and keep each part to a few sentences.
3. Read it aloud, fix mistakes, and ask someone you trust to check it.
4. Save it as a PDF with the right file name, then attach it with your CV.

A short, honest letter shows that you care about this job. That already puts you ahead of many applicants.
