Many job seekers get stuck on one request: "describe yourself". It appears in a CV profile, in an application letter and in almost every interview. If you want to know how to describe yourself without sounding empty or boastful, the answer is the same in all three places. Use a few plain words, and prove each one with something you really did.

## Key points

- A word like "hardworking" costs nothing to write, so employers do not believe it alone. Add one real example.
- A CV profile is short. Prospects, a UK graduate careers service, says no more than 150 words, in one paragraph.
- For "Tell me about yourself", talk about your present, your past and your future in about 60 to 90 seconds.
- Choose three or four qualities that match the job advert. Do not list twenty.
- Say only what is true. You may be asked to show it.

## Why "prove it" matters more than the word

Say you write "I am a good communicator" on your CV. Every other applicant wrote the same line. The reader learns nothing.

Now say you write "I explained lab results to a class of 40 classmates as a group leader" (this is an invented example, not a real person). The reader can picture it, and they can ask you about it in the interview.

Prospects makes the same point. It advises backing claims with specifics and warns against bland, generic claims such as "I work well independently and in a team". It also warns against clichés and overused buzzwords.

There is a local reason to care too. In April 2026 The Citizen reported on a University of Dodoma study of 366 graduates. The study found that stronger soft skills went with a higher chance of employability. The skills named were communication, teamwork, adaptability and problem-solving. So employers do look for these qualities. You still have to show them, not only name them.

## A short word bank, grouped by purpose

You do not need 180 words. You need a few that are true for you. The groups below follow skills that careers services such as Prospects and the University of Washington Career Center list. Pick from them, then attach an example.

**Working style**

- reliable, punctual, organised
- careful with detail
- calm under pressure
- able to learn quickly
- willing to take responsibility

**Working with people**

- clear speaker and listener
- patient with customers
- good at working in a team
- able to lead a small group
- respectful when people disagree

**Thinking and doing**

- good at solving practical problems
- curious and willing to ask questions
- able to plan and finish tasks
- comfortable with spreadsheets, phones or other tools you really use
- adaptable when plans change

### The rule for every word

Next to each word, write one line that answers: "When did I do this, and what happened?" If you cannot answer, drop the word.

Here are two invented examples of the pairing:

- Word: reliable. Proof: "Opened my aunt's shop at 7 a.m. every weekday for two school holidays."
- Word: careful with detail. Proof: "Kept the cash book for a youth group's savings and found a TSh 2,000 counting mistake before the month ended."

Your own examples will be different. Think about school, church or mosque groups, family work, volunteering and projects. Our guide to [writing a CV for your first job](/post/jobs-and-internships/how-to-write-a-cv-for-your-first-job-tanzania/) shows how to turn these into lines.

## How to describe yourself in a CV profile

The profile is the short paragraph under your name. Prospects describes it as a short pitch that tells the employer what you offer and what you want. Its advice:

- Keep it to one paragraph of no more than 150 words.
- Include your education, evidence of what you can do, what you offer the role, and your career goal.
- Change it for each job, using the advert.
- Avoid weak wording such as "trying", "might" or "currently studying".
- Be honest, and read it aloud before you send it.

Here is an invented example for a Form Six leaver:

*"Form Six leaver with Physics, Chemistry and Biology, applying for a laboratory assistant post. Kept accurate records during school practicals and helped younger students set up equipment. Careful, punctual and keen to learn. Looking to build a career in medical laboratory work."*

It is short and every claim can be explained in an interview. Tools such as AI chatbots can help with ideas or proofreading, but Prospects warns against pasting a whole AI-written statement, because it can sound generic. Our post on [using AI for your CV and application emails](/post/technology-solutions/ai-tools/using-ai-for-cv-and-job-application-emails/) explains safe ways to do it.

## How to describe yourself in an application letter

A letter has more room than a profile, but the same rule applies. Pick two qualities the advert asks for. Give one example for each, in two or three sentences. Then say what you can do for the employer.

Do not repeat your CV line by line. Our guide to [writing a job application letter](/post/jobs-and-internships/application-letter-for-a-job-tanzania-how-to-write/) gives a four-part layout you can follow.

## How to answer "Tell me about yourself"

This question feels open, and many people panic. Interviewers use it to hear how you speak and what you choose to say.

Several university career services teach a three-part answer: present, past, future. Tufts University describes it as a story of about 90 seconds, not a reading of your CV. Warwick's careers service uses a nice phrase: give the trailer, not the director's cut.

1. **Present.** Who you are now. Your course, your qualification, or your current work. One sentence.
2. **Past.** Two or three things that prepared you for this job: a project, volunteering, part-time work, a skill you built.
3. **Future.** Why you want this job, and how it fits where you want to go.

An invented example:

*"I am a Diploma in Accountancy graduate from Mwanza. During my final year I kept the records for a small student savings group and learned to use spreadsheets to check totals. I also worked weekends in my uncle's hardware shop, where I dealt with customers and cash. I want to start in an accounts assistant role, and I am keen to learn from a team like yours."*

Said aloud, that takes about 30 seconds, so there is room for more detail where it matters for the job.

### What to avoid

- Reading your life story from childhood.
- Talking about family, travel or social life at length. A little personality is fine, but keep it relevant.
- Vague claims with no detail.
- Asking the interviewer "What do you want to know?", which can sound unprepared.
- Using one answer for every interview. Change it to match each job.

Practise out loud. Record yourself on your phone and listen back. Our guide to [interview questions and answers for graduates](/post/jobs-and-internships/job-interview-questions-and-answers-for-graduates-tanzania/) covers the other common questions.

## Words and claims to avoid

- **Untrue claims.** Do not say you speak a language, use software or hold a certificate if you do not. Prospects notes that lying on a CV is a serious matter, and an employer can check.
- **Empty buzzwords.** "Dynamic", "results-driven" and "passionate" say little unless something follows.
- **Too many words.** Twenty qualities cancel each other out. Three or four with proof are stronger.
- **Copying another person's profile.** Examples, including ours, are for shape only.

We could not find an official Tanzanian rule on how to write a CV profile. This post follows advice from careers services in the UK and the US, so if an advert gives its own instructions, follow the advert first.

## Common questions

### How many words should I use to describe myself?

For a CV profile, no more than 150 words according to Prospects, and many are shorter. For an interview answer, about 60 to 90 seconds.

### What if I have no work experience?

Use education, school projects, volunteering, family work and skills you taught yourself. Prospects lists these as valid evidence. Our guide on [what to do when you cannot find a job after graduation](/post/jobs-and-internships/no-job-after-graduation-volunteering-and-skills-employers-want/) gives ideas for building more.

### Should I describe my weaknesses?

Only if asked. Then name a real one that you are working on and say what you do about it. Avoid stock answers.

### Can I use a list of descriptive words from the internet?

Use it to find the right word, then choose only the ones that are true and that you can prove. A list is a menu, not an answer.

## What to do next

1. Write down three qualities that the job advert asks for.
2. Next to each, write one real example from school, work, volunteering or home.
3. Turn them into a profile of under 150 words, then into a 60 to 90 second spoken answer.
4. Say it out loud to a friend and ask what they would remember.

You already have more to say than you think. A few true sentences, said calmly, are enough to start the conversation.
