You sit down to watch a lesson, and your bundle is gone before the video ends. It is a quiet worry for many students, and it makes online study feel like a luxury. A few settings on your phone can stretch the data you have, so more of it goes to learning.

## The short answer

Most of your data goes to video, and to apps that quietly use data in the background. Turn on Data Saver, lower the video quality, download lessons on Wi-Fi when you can, and write a weekly data budget. Together these steps can make a small bundle last longer.

- Know which apps use the most data.
- Turn on Data Saver on your phone.
- Choose a lower default video quality.
- Download on Wi-Fi, study later.
- Plan your week with a simple budget.

This post is about study. For general ideas on bundles and data, we already have short tips, and we link to them below instead of repeating them. Phone makers use different menu names, so your screens may look a little different from the steps here.

## 1. Know where your data goes

You cannot save what you do not measure. Most Android phones have a data usage page in Settings, often under "Network & internet" or "Connections". It usually shows which apps used the most mobile data in a period. The name of the menu depends on your phone brand, so use the search box in Settings and type "data usage".

Look at the top three apps. For many students it will be video apps, social media and a browser. Once you see the list, you know where to cut.

Tip: reset your view at the start of each bundle, so you can see what one bundle really pays for.

## 2. Turn on Data Saver on Android

Google's help page for Pixel phones explains the Data Saver feature. The steps there are:

1. Open Settings.
2. Tap Network & internet, then Data Saver.
3. Switch Data Saver on.

When it is on, an icon shows in the status bar. Google says Data Saver limits most apps and services to using background data only over Wi-Fi. Apps you have open can still use mobile data. So it does not stop your lesson, but it stops many apps from using data behind your back.

The page is written for Pixel phones. Other brands, such as Samsung, Tecno or Infinix, may use another name or menu, and some call it "data saving mode". We have not verified the steps for iPhone, so we do not give them here.

## 3. Let your study app keep working

Data Saver can stop an app from updating in the background, for example a class app that needs to send you a message. Google's page says you can fix this under Data Saver, in a list called Unrestricted data. Apps you turn on there can use background mobile data even when Data Saver is on.

Use this carefully. Add only the one or two apps that you really need, such as a class messaging app or your school's learning platform. Leave the rest restricted.

## 4. Lower the video quality in YouTube

Video is usually the biggest cost. YouTube lets you choose the quality in two ways, according to its help page.

**For one video:** tap the Settings icon on the player, tap Quality, and choose an option.

**As your default:** tap your profile picture, then Settings, then Video quality preferences. You can choose one level for mobile networks and another for Wi-Fi. The levels are Auto, Higher picture quality, Data saver and Advanced.

Pick "Data saver" for mobile networks and a higher level for Wi-Fi. YouTube says Data saver gives lower picture quality, though videos may start faster. For many lectures, where you mostly listen and watch a board or slides, a lower picture is enough. For a lesson with small text or diagrams, raise the quality only for that moment.

## 5. Download on Wi-Fi, study later

The cheapest data is data you do not buy. If your school, a library, a friend or a relative has Wi-Fi, use it for the heavy work.

- Download PDFs, notes and lessons while on Wi-Fi.
- Check whether the app you use has a download or offline option, and use it where it exists. We do not assume every app has one.
- Update your apps on Wi-Fi.

Large files are the main case. For instance, some official loan guidelines are very large PDFs. Our [student loan guide](/post/educational/study-tips/students-loan-tanzania-complete-guide/) says to use Wi-Fi or a good bundle for them.

If you cannot reach Wi-Fi often, look at a tool that works offline once content is loaded. We explain one in our post on [studying offline with Kolibri](/post/educational/online-learning/study-offline-with-kolibri-free/).

## 6. Keep the camera off when you only need to listen

Video calls use more data than audio. If you join an online class and you are not asked to show your face, you can usually turn your camera off and still follow. If your teacher asks for cameras, follow the class rule. Ask your teacher if there is an audio-only way to join.

## 7. Check what runs in the background

Apps can use data when you are not looking at them. Besides Data Saver, check each of your most used apps in the data usage page. Look for settings such as "restrict background data" or auto-play for videos, and switch off what you do not need. The wording differs by app and phone, so look around the app's own Settings page.

Here is a small rule that works for many people. Before a study session, close the apps you do not need. It takes ten seconds and helps keep data for your lesson.

## 8. Plan a weekly data budget

A budget turns worry into a plan. Say you buy a 6 GB bundle that you want to last four weeks. This is an example, not a real price or a real bundle, so use your own numbers.

6 GB is about 6,000 MB. Divided by four weeks, that is about 1,500 MB a week. You could split it like this:

| Use | Amount per week |
| --- | --- |
| Lessons and video (low quality) | 1,000 MB |
| Notes, search and messages | 300 MB |
| Spare, for surprises | 200 MB |
| Total | 1,500 MB |

Now check your data usage page every few days. If you have used more than half the week's amount by Wednesday, you know to slow down and wait for Wi-Fi.

Remember that nobody can tell you in advance how much one hour of a lesson uses. It depends on the video quality, the app and the network. Test it yourself: note your usage, watch a ten-minute video at low quality, and check again.

## Common questions

### Will Data Saver break my lessons?

It limits background data for most apps, but an app you have open can still use data. If a study app stops sending alerts, add it to Unrestricted data.

### Is a lower video quality bad for learning?

For talking and slides it is often fine. Raise the quality briefly if you cannot read the screen.

## What to do next

1. Open your data usage page and write down your top three apps.
2. Turn on Data Saver, and add only the apps you must keep.
3. Set YouTube's mobile quality to Data saver.
4. Write a simple weekly budget in your notebook, and review it every few days.

Learning does not need a big bundle, only a little care. Start with one setting today.
