In April 2026 the Land Transport Regulatory Authority (LATRA) published new fares for commuter buses (daladala), long-distance buses, ride-hailing taxis and motorcycle taxis. This post explains what was announced, how to work out what you should pay, and where to complain if you are overcharged.

We checked the figures below in November 2026 against news reports and LATRA's website. LATRA's own fare tables were not displayed in full on the pages we could open, so use the fare list at the bus stand or on LATRA's site as the final word.

## The short answer

- LATRA published the new fares in the Government Gazette on 17 April 2026. They took effect 14 days later, around 1 May 2026.
- The minimum daladala fare for trips up to 10 km went from TSh 600 to TSh 700.
- Long-distance ordinary buses are charged TSh 57.93 per km on tarmac and TSh 66.62 per km on gravel. Semi-luxury buses are TSh 74.18 per km on tarmac.
- Students keep a flat subsidised fare of TSh 200, regardless of distance.
- LATRA says the review followed higher fuel prices and the cost of providing transport services.

## What LATRA is and why it sets fares

LATRA regulates road transport in Tanzania. It licenses operators, publishes approved fares and handles complaints. It sets fares under Section 19 of its establishing law, which says fares must be fair to passengers and to service providers.

Before the April 2026 changes, LATRA invited stakeholders to a meeting at Karimjee Hall in Dar es Salaam on 8 April 2026 to give views on reviewing fares. Earlier, in early April, it warned operators not to raise fares on their own.

## Why fares changed

Fuel is the main reason. The EWURA cap price for petrol in Dar es Salaam went from TSh 2,864 per litre in March 2026 to TSh 3,820 in April, about 33 percent higher. Diesel went from TSh 2,858 to TSh 3,806 (Daily News, 14 April 2026).

The same Daily News report quotes LATRA's 2022/23 annual report, which says fuel is about 40 percent of transport costs. Operators also pointed to tyres, spare parts and poor roads.

There was a dispute too. The bus operators' association (TABOA) asked for much larger increases than LATRA approved, and a TABOA strike was suspended after talks with the government. The operators' proposal for a 10 km trip was TSh 850, but the gazetted minimum is TSh 700.

## Daladala fares

The only daladala figures we could confirm are these, from The Citizen and The BizLens (which cites Mwananchi):

| Distance | Old fare | New fare |
|---|---|---|
| Up to 10 km | TSh 600 | TSh 700 |
| 36 to 40 km | TSh 1,400 | TSh 1,500 |

Fares for distances in between rise step by step, but the news reports we found did not print them. Find your exact fare on the LATRA fare table or ask for it at the terminal.

Example, which is only an illustration: if you ride a 10 km daladala twice a day, 22 days a month, the TSh 100 rise adds TSh 4,400 a month.

Students in uniform pay the flat TSh 200. Read the sign inside the daladala, and if a conductor asks for more, ask politely for the LATRA fare.

## Long-distance buses

Long-distance fares are charged per kilometre:

| Bus type | Road | Fare per km |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinary | Tarmac | TSh 57.93 |
| Ordinary | Gravel | TSh 66.62 |
| Semi-luxury | Tarmac | TSh 74.18 |

To estimate a fare, multiply the distance by the rate. As an example, for a 300 km tarmac trip:

- Ordinary: 300 x 57.93 = about TSh 17,379
- Semi-luxury: 300 x 74.18 = about TSh 22,254

The real ticket may be rounded and the route distance may differ from what you expect. This guide does not give a rate for luxury buses or a gravel rate for semi-luxury. Operators with special services may charge differently, so confirm the approved fare for your route before buying.

## Ride-hailing taxis

For apps such as Bolt and Uber, LATRA set ranges, not single prices. According to The Citizen:

- Trips up to 1 km: TSh 4,000 to 5,000 in total
- Base fare: TSh 1,100 to 1,200
- Per km: TSh 1,100 to 1,300
- Per minute: TSh 120 to 150
- Commission taken from drivers: 10 to 20 percent
- LATRA levy: 0.5 to 1 percent

Because these are ranges, the price in your app depends on the company. Always check the quote before you accept the ride.

## Bodaboda and bajaji

The Citizen says the new rules cover motorcycle taxis but printed no figures. We found no mainland bodaboda or bajaji fare table, so we cannot give prices. Bodaboda fares are usually agreed with the rider, so agree the price before you start. Zanzibar has been working on its own regulated fare system for bodaboda, which is separate from LATRA on the Mainland.

## Common questions

### Did student fares go up?

No. Reports say the flat TSh 200 student fare continues regardless of distance.

### Can a conductor charge more because of traffic or rain?

LATRA warned operators against unauthorised increases. The approved fare is the fare unless LATRA announces a change. A 20 percent upward adjustment on unpaved roads was part of LATRA's earlier 2022 order, so ask LATRA what applies to daladala today.

### Where is the official fare list?

LATRA's website has a "Nauli" section covering daladala, long-distance buses, online taxis and the SGR train. Check there for the latest.

### How do I complain about overcharging?

LATRA's website lists a toll-free line, 0800 110 019 or 0800 110 020, and the email info@latra.go.tz. Note the bus number plate, route, time and amount you were charged. If you have a receipt or ticket, keep it.

### Are fares in this post still current?

These fares were announced in April 2026 and were still the latest reported in November 2026, but LATRA can review fares again, so check before you travel.

## What to do next

1. Write down the fare for your usual route from LATRA's table and check what the conductor asks.
2. For a long trip, estimate the cost with the per-km rates above, then compare it with the quote.
3. Keep your ticket, and report overcharging to LATRA.
4. If fuel costs are on your mind, read our post on [Tanzania's fuel security measures](/post/tanzania-news/tanzania-fuel-security-measures-navigating-current-challenges/).
