Geography is one of the most common A-Level subjects in Tanzania. It sits in science, arts and business combinations alike, which makes it a useful link between them. This post explains how ACSEE Geography is examined, which topics you will meet, what the newer TIE syllabus says, and how to study a subject that mixes essays, maps and calculations. It is for Form Five and Form Six students, and for parents and Form Four leavers choosing a combination (tahasusi).

## Key points

- NECTA lists the exam as **113 Geography**, in two papers of three hours each: Paper 1 (practical and physical geography) and Paper 2 (human and world economic geography).
- Paper 1 includes map work and calculation. Question 1, on topographic map interpretation, is compulsory and carries 25 marks.
- There is no separate field-trip exam in the NECTA format we read. Field research is a topic you answer in writing, and a project counts in your school marks.
- Geography appears in HGL, HGK, HGE, PGM, EGM and CBG, among others.
- NECTA's 2025 report said candidates did well in all 13 topics examined, but Simple Survey and Map Making was the lowest.

## What Geography is at A-Level

At O-Level you learn about weather, farming and population in a broad way. At A-Level you study the earth as a system, learn to measure and draw, and write long, organised answers. The TIE syllabus describes it as a subject that builds skills in land surveying, map and photograph reading and research. It gives ten periods of 40 minutes a week.

## How the exam is built

From NECTA's exam format document (revised 2019) and its 2025 report on the subject:

| Paper | Content | What you answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 113/1 | Practical and physical geography. Section A: three questions (topographic map interpretation, simple survey and map making, field research strategies). Section B: four questions (earth structure, water masses, soils, the dynamic earth) | Five of seven. In Section A you answer two, and Question 1 (25 marks) is compulsory. In Section B you answer three, at 20 marks each |
| 113/2 | Population and development, plus regional studies such as manufacturing, livestock, fuel and power, mining and forestry | Five of seven, Question 1 compulsory, 20 marks each |

The 2019 format lists more topics than appeared in 2025, including photograph interpretation, space dynamics, agricultural development, sustainable fishing, environment-friendly tourism, and transport and communication. Any of these can be examined, so cover them all.

### What the 2025 report says

NECTA reported that 73,997 candidates sat Geography in ACSEE 2025, and 73,798 passed. NECTA grouped all 13 examined topics as good. The lowest were Simple Survey and Map Making (72.68%) and Water Masses (78.23%). Its advice to teachers was to give more practical work on topographic maps and survey, and to help students write in clear English. The report also notes that candidates who answered well understood what each question asked, wrote organised essays and could calculate and draw.

Do not read the high pass rate as "easy". It means the skills are learnable. The report lists weaker answers too: too few points, correct points with no explanation, and mistakes in calculation and drawing.

## Topics by form in the 2023 TIE syllabus

The TIE "Geography Syllabus for Advanced Secondary Education Form V-VI", published in 2023, sets out the content below. We have summarised it in our own words.

**Form Five**

- Theories of the earth's structure: continental drift, isostasy and plate tectonics, with the continents, oceans and ridges they explain.
- How the atmosphere, biosphere, hydrosphere and geosphere interact.
- Internal forces that make volcanoes, fold and block mountains, faults and rift valleys, and the study of earthquakes.
- External forces such as waves, wind, gravity and running water, and the landforms they shape.
- Advanced surveying with a plane table, a prismatic compass and levelling.
- Collecting, drawing and reading data with maps, graphs and charts (for example choropleth and dot maps).
- A project that starts by finding a real geographical problem in your community.

**Form Six**

- Rocks and the rock cycle, weathering and soil formation.
- Hydrology and the hydrological cycle.
- Map interpretation and photograph interpretation.
- Finishing and handing in the project.

This list is shorter than the NECTA topic list, which includes population, mining, forestry and so on. We cannot tell you from the documents why the two lists differ. NECTA's 2025 report says the 2025 papers followed the 2010 syllabus, while TIE publishes the 2023 one. As checked on 24 November 2026, ask your school which syllabus your class uses, and check NECTA's current format for your exam year.

## Assessment

The TIE syllabus says school work gives 30% of the final mark and the national Form Six exam gives 70%. The school part is made of first and second term exams in Form Five (5% each), a first term exam (6%), the project (7%) and a mock exam (7%) in Form Six.

## How to study Geography

- **Practise maps with a real sheet.** Measure distances with a ruler, convert scale, find grid references and calculate time and speed. NECTA's 2025 Question 1 asked for the time a bus arrived given its speed, which is plain arithmetic once you know the map scale.
- **Draw by hand.** Diagrams of faults, folds, soil profiles and the water cycle should be neat and labelled. Practise until you can draw each in two minutes.
- **Write essays with points and explanations.** Use a pattern: point, explanation, example from Tanzania or East Africa.
- **Use local examples.** The Rift Valley, the Usambara or Uluguru mountains, Lake Victoria fishing, Mtwara gas and Geita mining can make answers specific. Check each fact in a textbook before you use it.
- **Learn command words.** "Describe", "explain" and "examine" ask for different things.
- **Write calculations in full.** Show formula, numbers and unit.

## Common questions

### Is there a practical exam in A-Level Geography?

The NECTA format we read has two written papers. Map and survey skills are tested on paper, with a ruler, instruments and calculation. The TIE syllabus also asks for a project in school marks.

### Is Geography a good partner for Mathematics or for History?

It works with both. That is why it appears in PGM and EGM as well as HGK and HGL. Choose by what you enjoy and by the degrees you want.

### Why do students lose marks?

NECTA's reports point to too few points, weak explanation, and errors in calculation and drawing, plus language problems.

### Do I need Geography at O-Level to take it at A-Level?

Selection to Form Five uses the passes required by each combination. For the current rules, see the combination list on the TAMISEMI selform site.

## What to do next

1. Get the TIE Geography syllabus and make a topic checklist for Form Five and Form Six.
2. Buy or borrow a topographic map sheet and practise one question each week.
3. Draw five diagrams from memory every weekend and check them against your textbook.
4. Read the NECTA item response report for Geography once and write down what examiners ask.

Geography rewards students who go outside and look. Start with the hill, river or road near your school.
