Every year the government changes some taxes when a new budget year starts on 1 July. For 2026/27 the changes came through the Finance Act 2026. Most are written for big companies, but a few reach ordinary people: people who import a used car, run a small shop, buy cosmetics or own a house. This post lists the ones that matter most, based on the law firms' and newspapers' summaries we read in November 2026. We did not read the full Act, so check the exact text with the Tanzania Revenue Authority (TRA) before you decide anything big. In Swahili, you may hear it called *Sheria ya Fedha ya 2026*.

## The short answer

- The President signed the Finance Bill into law on 30 June 2026. The Finance Act 2026 took effect on 1 July 2026, unless a section says otherwise.
- Excise duty on imported used cars now depends on the car's age: 18% for cars 8 to 10 years old, 35% for 10 to 20 years and 40% for over 20 years.
- Small traders using the presumptive tax can now have yearly turnover of up to TSh 200 million, up from TSh 100 million, and a new business gets a one-year tax holiday.
- Cosmetics, perfumes and some other goods are dearer because of higher excise duty, and the income tax on payments to foreign digital service providers rose from 2% to 3%.
- Property tax is moving from TRA to local councils.

## Used cars: what changed

Importing a used car is one of the clearest effects. The Citizen reported on 1 July 2026 that buyers would "dig deeper" into their pockets, and law firm summaries confirm the new excise rates:

| Age of the imported car | Old rate | New rate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| More than 8 years, up to 10 years | 15% | 18% |
| More than 10 years, up to 20 years | 30% | 35% |
| More than 20 years | none listed | 40% |

The rates in the Finance Bill were higher for some groups, and they were lowered before the Act passed. For example, the 8 to 10 year group was proposed at 20% and ended at 18%.

The Citizen also reported that small cars with engines up to 1,000cc, which had no excise duty before, now pay 5%. The Finance Bill summary by TanzaniaInvest lists the same 5% rate.

Excise duty is only one part of the cost of bringing in a car. Other taxes and charges apply, so ask TRA or a licensed clearing agent for the total before you pay a dealer or an overseas seller. A dealer in Kinondoni told The Citizen that vehicle financing plans, such as one with a 50% deposit, could give buyers some relief. That is one person's view, not a rule.

## Small businesses and the presumptive tax

The presumptive tax is a simple turnover-based tax for small traders. Our guide to [small business tax in Tanzania](/post/business-ideas/startups/small-business-tax-tanzania-presumptive-tax-explained/) explains how it works. The Finance Act 2026 changed three things, as reported by EY and The Citizen:

- The upper limit of yearly turnover for the regime rose from TSh 100 million to TSh 200 million.
- A new individual business under the regime gets a one-year tax holiday, counted from the date it gets a TIN. EY notes this needs the Commissioner General's approval and may depend on conditions set later in regulations.
- Operators of three-wheelers (bajaji) who carry passengers can now be taxed under the regime at a fixed TSh 120,000 a year.

The Citizen reports the rate for turnover between TSh 11 million and TSh 200 million as 4%, after a higher rate of 4.5% was proposed in the Bill.

If your sales are getting close to TSh 200 million, remember that other rules, such as VAT registration, may apply. Ask TRA. Keep good records too; our post on [simple business records](/post/business-ideas/startups/simple-business-records-small-traders-cash-book-tanzania/) shows an easy cash book.

## Things you buy or use

### Cosmetics and some other goods

Excise duty on perfumes, lip and eye make-up, manicure and pedicure items, powders, shampoo and personal deodorants rose from 10% to 15%, according to the EY analysis. The Citizen also reports a new 10% excise on nail-drying machines. A nail technician quoted by The Citizen said prices would have to be adjusted if equipment costs go up. Whether shops raise prices will depend on each seller.

### Drinks, cigarettes and cement

The EY analysis lists an 8% increase in the specific excise duty on a group of goods that includes beer, wine, spirits, cigarettes and cement. From 2026/27 these specific rates are meant to be adjusted every year, using the previous inflation rate plus 2 percentage points, instead of every three years. That means more frequent small changes.

The Citizen reported two more charges linked to health insurance funding: a levy on cigarettes and a charge of TSh 20 per kilogram on imported sugar. We found these in one newspaper only, so check the TRA's published rates before relying on them.

### Cooking oil

A one-year VAT exemption for cooking oil made from locally grown oil seeds was kept, according to The Citizen and TanzaniaInvest. TanzaniaInvest gives the period as 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027.

### Online services from abroad

The income tax on payments to foreign digital service providers went up from 2% to 3%. EY says this may raise the cost of digital services for customers. How much, if any, will be passed on is up to each company.

## Property owners

Property tax collection moves from TRA to local government authorities, according to both The Citizen and TanzaniaInvest. The Bill summary says it is still collected through LUKU electricity tokens. If you pay this tax, watch for notices from your council about how and where to pay. Do not pay anyone who simply claims to be a collector.

## Proposals that were dropped

Some measures in the Bill did not become law. TanzaniaInvest lists the motorcycle, gambling and withholding items below as removed, and The Citizen reports the sugar levy as dropped:

- a planned TSh 10 levy on locally made sugar,
- a planned 5% excise on motorcycles,
- a planned excise on gambling stakes,
- a planned 1% withholding tax on some purchases of food crops, live animals and unprocessed milk and fish.

If you read an old news report from June, check whether it describes the Bill or the final Act.

## Common questions

### Did income tax rates for employees change?

The summaries we read do not list a change to the PAYE rates for employees. If your payslip changed, ask your employer's payroll office or TRA to explain.

### Will the used-car duty change again?

It can. The government sets these rates each year in the Finance Act. The 2026 Act also changed how some specific excise rates are adjusted, so watch the next budget in June 2027.

### Where do I find the real text?

TRA publishes finance acts and notices on its website, and the Ministry of Finance publishes its explanatory notes. Law firms such as EY also publish summaries, but a summary is not the law.

### Is the presumptive tax limit now TSh 200 million for everyone?

It is the upper limit of turnover for the regime, but conditions apply: the person must be a resident individual whose business income falls under the rules. Ask TRA before you choose a tax method.

## What to do next

1. If you plan to import a used car, get a written total cost from a clearing agent that includes every tax, and compare the age bands above before you pick a car.
2. If you run a small business, check which tax band your sales fall in and whether you qualify for the one-year holiday, then keep monthly records.
3. If you own a house or land, find out from your local council how property tax will be collected in your area.
4. Read TRA's own notices and, if you can, the Act itself before you act on any summary, including this one.
