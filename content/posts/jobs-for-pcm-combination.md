PCM means Physics, Chemistry and Mathematics (Advanced Mathematics) as your three principal subjects in Form Five and Form Six. Government selection lists call this kind of choice a combination, or *tahasusi* in Kiswahili. PCM is the combination most engineering degrees are built around, and it also leads to geology, meteorology, computing and science teaching. This guide is for Form Four leavers choosing a combination, Form Six students waiting for results, and parents who want to understand the choice.

We read the entry rules below in the TCU admission guidebook for the 2026/27 cycle in November 2026. Rules change every year and differ between universities, so use this as a map and check the latest guidebook before you apply.

## Key points

- PCM gives you the two subjects that engineering degrees ask for most: Physics and Advanced Mathematics. Chemistry is a third strong subject that helps in geology, chemical engineering and science teaching.
- Most engineering degrees in the guidebook ask for two principal passes in Advanced Mathematics and Physics, with 4.0 as the general minimum points.
- PCM does not lead to medicine, pharmacy, dentistry or nursing. Those degrees ask for Biology.
- A place is never guaranteed. Universities get more applicants than places, so grades well above the minimum matter.
- Pay and job openings differ a lot between careers. We could not confirm salary figures from an official source, so we give none.

## How the points work

The Tanzania Commission for Universities (TCU) publishes a guidebook every year for Form Six leavers. For each degree it lists the subjects asked for, the minimum points, the number of places and the years of study.

For results from 2016 onward, the guidebook counts A = 5, B = 4, C = 3, D = 2, E = 1 and S = 0.5. The general minimum is two principal passes with a total of 4.0 points in the two subjects that define the programme. For engineering, those two subjects are usually Physics and Advanced Mathematics.

Here is a hypothetical example. Say you get B in Physics, C in Chemistry and C in Advanced Mathematics. That is 4 + 3 + 3 = 10 points in total, and 7 points from Physics and Mathematics. You are well above 4.0. Another student gets D in both subjects. That is exactly 4.0, which meets the minimum on paper but leaves no room, because applicants compete for a fixed number of places.

## Degrees and subjects the guidebook lists for PCM

The table shows examples from the 2026/27 guidebook. Entry lines differ by university, and some add conditions such as a credit in Chemistry at O-Level, so read the full entry for your chosen university.

| Degree | Subjects asked for | Minimum points | Years |
|---|---|---|---|
| Civil Engineering (UDSM) | Advanced Mathematics and Physics | 4.0 | 4 |
| Electrical Engineering and Mechanical Engineering (DIT, Dar es Salaam) | Physics and Advanced Mathematics | 4.0 | 4 |
| Chemical and Process Engineering (UDSM) | Advanced Mathematics and Physics, at least D in Mathematics | 4.0 | 4 |
| Mining Engineering (UDSM) | Advanced Mathematics and Physics | 4.0 | 4 |
| Petroleum Engineering (UDSM) | Advanced Mathematics and Physics, and at least a subsidiary pass in Chemistry | 4.5 | 4 |
| Geology (UDSM) | Principal passes in Advanced Mathematics, Physics and Chemistry | 4.0 | 4 |
| Meteorology (UDSM) | Advanced Mathematics and Physics | 4.0 | 3 |
| Computer Science (UDSM) | Advanced Mathematics and Physics or Computer Science | 4.0 | 3 |
| Bachelor of Science with Education (UDSM) | Two from Physics, Chemistry, Advanced Mathematics, Biology, Economics, Computer Science or Geography | 4.0 | 3 |
| Architecture (Ardhi University) | Two from Advanced Mathematics, Physics, Chemistry, Biology, Geography, Computer Science or Fine Arts, plus a mathematics pass | 4.0 | 5 |

Two details are worth knowing. First, several engineering entries add that an applicant without a subsidiary pass in Chemistry needs a credit in Chemistry at O-Level. PCM students usually meet this because Chemistry is a principal subject for them. Second, programmes such as mining, geology and petroleum list "physical fitness" as a special requirement. Ask the university what that means in practice.

DIT, the Dar es Salaam Institute of Technology, lists the same Physics and Advanced Mathematics rule for civil, computer, electronics and telecommunication, oil and gas, and biomedical engineering. It also asks for a pass in Basic Mathematics and Physics at O-Level.

## What each PCM subject does for you

- **Advanced Mathematics** is the key. It appears in almost every engineering, computing and statistics entry. If you are weak in Mathematics, no other subject will rescue an engineering plan.
- **Physics** is named beside it in nearly every engineering entry. Electricity, forces, heat and waves are the base of civil, electrical, mechanical and telecommunication work.
- **Chemistry** is required as a principal pass for geology and helps in chemical and petroleum engineering, and in science teaching. It is also the subject that keeps health science degrees partly in view, such as laboratory courses that ask for Chemistry and Physics.

## Jobs these degrees can lead to

We list the kinds of work, not salaries or job numbers. Pay varies between government, private and mission employers, and we could not confirm figures from an official source.

- **Civil and construction work.** Roads, bridges, water systems and buildings. Civil engineers work for government agencies, contractors and consulting firms.
- **Electrical, electronics and telecommunication work.** Power networks, solar installations, phone and internet systems.
- **Mining, geology and petroleum.** Mineral exploration, mine planning and energy projects. These degrees are small, with a few dozen places each at some universities, so competition can be stiff.
- **Weather and climate.** The meteorology degree in the guidebook prepares you for work that depends on weather data.
- **Computing and data.** Computer Science and related degrees accept Advanced Mathematics with Physics.
- **Teaching.** A Bachelor of Science with Education is one of the largest programmes in the guidebook. Some universities list hundreds of places.

Engineering is a regulated profession. The Engineers Registration Board (ERB) says that everyone who practises engineering in Tanzania must register with it, and that registration is the only legal authorisation to practise. A graduate engineer is the entry level for citizens with a recognised degree. Professional engineers need a practising licence. Ask your university about the steps after graduation.


## A message for the student who is afraid of choosing wrong

Imagine you are in Form Four and your classmates are already arguing about combinations. You are not sure. Maybe your parents have sold something, borrowed something or gone without something to pay your fees. You do not want to waste it.

That fear is a sign you take your future seriously. It does not mean you will fail.

PCM is hard. Mathematics and Physics ask for steady practice, not last-month panic. But a student who works at them every week often finds the questions get friendlier. Pick PCM because you like building, solving and working things out, not because a friend did. Then give it your best, week by week.

No guide can promise you a place or a job. What we can say is that the subjects you choose in Form Five really do decide which doors the guidebook shows you. Choose with your eyes open, and your effort has somewhere to go.

## If you are in Form Four: is PCM right for you?

Check these honestly before you list PCM as a first choice.

1. **Mathematics.** Do you enjoy it and score well? PCM needs Advanced Mathematics, which is a step up from O-Level.
2. **Physics.** Do you understand it or only memorise it? Engineering uses it every day.
3. **Chemistry.** A credit helps, because it is the third subject.
4. **Your goal.** Write down three careers. Search each degree in the TCU guidebook and see if PCM appears.
5. **Selection rules.** The official TAMISEMI list of combinations shows PCM (number 28) and says the student must have passed the subjects of the combination. Ask your school what grades Form Five selection needs in your year.

If Biology is your strongest subject and you dream of being a doctor or pharmacist, read our [PCB guide](/post/educational/study-tips/jobs-for-pcb-combination/) instead.

## If you are in Form Six: choosing a degree

When results come out, use our guide on [how to read your Form Six results for university entry](/post/educational/results/how-to-read-acsee-results-points-and-divisions/) to work out your points. Then:

1. Put your points next to each degree you like and the minimum in the guidebook.
2. List the universities that offer it. Entries differ between universities.
3. Apply through the official university and TCU systems only. Nobody can sell you a place.
4. Look at how you will pay. Our guide on [HESLB student loans](/post/educational/study-tips/students-loan-tanzania-complete-guide/) explains the process.

## Where PCM does not fit

Health degrees are the main example. Medicine, dentistry, pharmacy and medical laboratory sciences list Biology in the harmonised table, with at least 6 points. Nursing asks for Chemistry, Biology and one of Physics, Advanced Mathematics or Nutrition. A PCM student has no Biology principal pass, so these routes are closed.

Some other degrees are more open than people expect. The guidebook lists law and business programmes that accept two principal passes from a long list including Physics, Chemistry and Advanced Mathematics. They may add a subsidiary pass in English. PCM does not block you, but it is not built for these paths.

## What if your points are not enough?

Many students miss their first choice. You still have roads.

1. **Apply to a related degree.** Engineering may be full, but Mathematics, Statistics or Information Technology at some universities ask for Advanced Mathematics with a minimum D. Read each entry.
2. **Take the diploma route.** Technical colleges offer engineering diplomas under NACTVET. Our post on [NTA levels](/post/educational/career-development/nta-levels-explained-tanzania/) explains how they work, and the guide on [going from diploma to degree](/post/educational/career-development/diploma-to-degree-tanzania-entry-requirements/) explains how you can join a degree later. Check that the college and programme are accredited before you pay.
3. **Retake.** Some students sit examinations again. This costs time and money, so ask your teachers whether it makes sense for your results.

## Common questions

### Can a PCM student study medicine?

No. The guidebook lists Physics, Chemistry and Biology for medicine, and PCM has no Biology principal pass.

### Is PCM only for engineering?

No. It also leads to geology, meteorology, computer science, statistics, architecture and science teaching, depending on the university.

### How many points do I need for engineering?

The general minimum in the guidebook is 4.0 from two principal passes, usually Advanced Mathematics and Physics. Real competition is higher, so aim well above it.

### Do I need Chemistry for civil engineering?

Usually a subsidiary pass or a credit in Chemistry at O-Level, as some entries say. PCM students have Chemistry as a principal subject.

### Which is better, PCM or PGM?

Neither is better. PCM suits engineering, geology and chemistry-linked work. PGM suits land, mapping and planning. Choose by the careers you want and the subjects you do well in.

## What to do next

1. Download the latest TCU guidebook for Form Six holders and search for three degrees you like.
2. Write down the subjects, points and years of study for each, and the universities that offer them.
3. Check your marks in Mathematics and Physics. If one is low, start extra practice now.
4. Keep a backup plan, such as a related degree or an accredited diploma.
