It is late, the deadline is close, and your work is finally written. Then the page numbers jump around and the contents page is wrong. Many students know this feeling. The good news is that a handful of Word features fix most of it, and you can learn them in one evening.

This post shows those features: heading styles, page numbers, an automatic table of contents, and saving a PDF. It also shows the same ideas in Google Docs, which is free with a Google account. The steps were checked against Microsoft and Google help pages in November 2026. Menu names may differ in your version of Word, in Word for the web or on a Mac, so look for the closest match.

## The short answer

Do the formatting in this order, and it goes smoothly:

- Write your text first, then format it.
- Mark each heading with a heading style from the Styles gallery, not with bold and a bigger font.
- Add page numbers through the header or footer.
- Insert an automatic table of contents, and update it at the end.
- Save a PDF copy if your lecturer asks for one, and keep the Word file too.

Fonts, margins, line spacing and referencing style are different at every school. We do not give those numbers here. Follow your lecturer's or department's written instructions.

## Why use heading styles, not just bold text?

A heading style is a label that tells Word "this line is a heading". Bold text only changes how the line looks. Word cannot tell that a bold line is a heading, so it cannot put it in a contents page.

Microsoft's help page gives the steps. Select the heading text, go to the **Home** tab, and in the **Styles** section choose the heading style you want. If you do not see the style, click the down arrow next to the Styles gallery to open more choices.

Use **Heading 1** for main sections. Use **Heading 2** for parts inside a section, and **Heading 3** if you need one more level. Microsoft says to repeat this for each heading. If entries are missing from your contents page later, the usual cause is that those headings were not formatted as headings.

Say your assignment is about a small shop. Your main sections might be "Introduction", "The shop and its customers", "Problems and solutions" and "Conclusion". Each of those gets Heading 1. A part such as "Prices" inside the second section gets Heading 2.

Styles also save time. If your lecturer wants all headings in one font or size, you can change the style once instead of changing every heading by hand.

## How do I add page numbers in Word?

Page numbers live in the header or footer, the space at the top and bottom of each page. Microsoft's page on inserting page numbers (it applies to Word for Microsoft 365, Word 2024, 2021, 2019 and 2016) gives these steps for Windows:

1. On the **Insert** tab, select **Page Number**, then choose a location and style.
2. If you want no number on the first page, such as a title page, select **Different First Page** on the **Header & Footer** tab.
3. When you finish, select **Close Header and Footer** or press Esc.

To start numbering at 1 on the second page, Microsoft says to go to **Page Number > Format Page Numbers** and set **Start at** to 0. This works because the cover page then counts as page 0.

To edit a header or footer later, double-click in that area.

The Mac steps are a little different. Microsoft says to select **Insert > Page Number > Page Number**, then choose a location and alignment, and use **Format** to change the number style. In Word for the web, Microsoft's header and footer page points to **Insert > Header & Footer**.

You can also start from the header or footer itself. On Windows, Microsoft says to go to **Insert > Header** or **Footer** and choose a built-in style, because some designs already include page numbers. To take a header away, go to **Insert > Header** (or **Footer**) and then **Remove Header** (or **Remove Footer**).

## How do I make an automatic table of contents?

An automatic table of contents is built from your headings, and Word can refresh it when your work changes. Microsoft gives these steps:

1. Click where you want the table, usually near the start of the document.
2. Select the **References** tab, then **Table of Contents**.
3. Choose an **Automatic Table of Contents** style.

Microsoft warns you to avoid the manual styles. Word cannot build those from your headings and cannot update them for you.

Your headings and page numbers will change as you edit. So update the table at the end. On Windows, right-click the table and choose **Update Field**. On a Mac or in Word for the web, right-click it and choose **Update Table of Contents**. Do this after you change heading text, heading order or heading levels, and again just before you submit.

Update the contents page last, after your final edit, because adding one paragraph can move a heading to the next page.

## Can I do the same in Google Docs?

Yes. Google's help page says page numbers and headers or footers are not available in pageless format, so make sure your document is in pages format first.

For headers and footers, click **Insert > Page elements > Header** or **Footer**, then type your text. For page numbers, click **Insert > Page elements > Page number**, then pick a location. You can also choose **Page count**.

To start numbering on a later page, click **Insert > Page elements > Page number > More options**. Under "Apply to", choose where the change should start, then click **Apply**. If you do not see "Apply to", add a section break first. To use a different first page, click a header or footer and tick **Different first page**.

We did not confirm Google's contents page steps, so check Google's own help for that part.

## How do I save my assignment as a PDF?

A PDF keeps your layout the same on every phone and computer. This matters if your lecturer opens it on a different device.

In Word for Windows, Microsoft says to go to **File > Save As** (or **Save a Copy**), choose a location, set **Save as type** to **PDF**, and select **Save**. Under **Options** you can choose a page range, make bookmarks from your headings, and set a password.

In Word for the web, Microsoft says to go to **File > Print**, choose **Save as PDF** as the printer, and select **Save**. On a Mac, use **File > Save As**, then choose PDF as the file format.

One honest warning from Microsoft: saving as PDF does not stop editing. Word can open and edit PDFs, and so can other programs. Do not treat a PDF as a lock.

Always keep the original Word file. If your lecturer asks for a correction, you will need it.

## Common questions

### Should I type the contents page by hand?

No. A hand-typed page will be wrong the moment a heading moves. Use the automatic table and update it.

### Why are some of my headings missing from the contents page?

Most often, those lines were never given a heading style. Select the line, apply Heading 1 or Heading 2 from the Styles gallery, then update the table.

### Which font and margins should I use?

Use what your school says. These rules change from one college to another, and a lecturer's written instructions always win.

### Does Word cost money?

Word for Windows and Mac is part of Microsoft's paid software, so check Microsoft's own page for what your account includes. Google Docs is free with a Google account, and it covers all the ideas in this post.

## What to do next

1. Open your current assignment and turn each heading into a real heading style.
2. Add page numbers, and decide with your lecturer's rules whether the title page shows one.
3. Insert an automatic table of contents, then update it after your last edit.
4. Save a PDF copy only if it is asked for, and keep the Word file safe. A [free cloud backup](/post/technology-solutions/software/back-up-your-computer-files-free-cloud-storage/) means a broken laptop will not take your work with it.

Tidy formatting will not write the assignment for you, but it lets your good work be read without distraction. That is a fair reward for an hour of learning.
