Many students in Form Two and Form Four hear the same advice: "Take science." Others hear the opposite from a friend. This guide gives a fair picture. It explains what science subjects are, which doors they open in Tanzanian universities, what they do not guarantee, and how to choose using your own strengths.

## Key points

- In the TCU 2026/27 guidebook, medicine, pharmacy, dental surgery, nursing and engineering all ask for science subjects at A-Level.
- Many other degrees, including law, education, business and economics, accept arts, business or mixed subjects. Science is not the only strong path.
- Tanzania's Development Vision 2050 puts science, technology, engineering and mathematics (STEM) among its priorities, but it also lists weak infrastructure and skills gaps.
- Mathematics is the subject that opens the most doors, and it is where many students struggle.
- Choose with your strengths, your interests and the entry rules in front of you. No combination guarantees a place or a job.

## What counts as a science subject

At O-Level, the NECTA Form Four examination formats for 2022 list Physics, Chemistry, Biology, Basic Mathematics and Additional Mathematics. They also list Agriculture, Engineering Science, and Information and Computer Studies. Many schools treat Physics, Chemistry, Biology and Mathematics as the core sciences.

At A-Level, the TAMISEMI selection site (Selform) lists 65 combinations in groups. The science group, section D, includes PCM (Physics, Chemistry, Mathematics), PCB (Physics, Chemistry, Biology), PGM (Physics, Geography, Mathematics), CBG (Chemistry, Biology, Geography), PMCs (Physics, Mathematics, Computer Science), CBA (Chemistry, Biology, Agriculture) and CBN (Chemistry, Biology, Food and Human Nutrition). Other groups cover social science, languages, business studies, sports, arts and religious education.

## Why science matters: what the sources say

### What universities ask for

The TCU admission guidebook for 2026/27 is the best place to see real entry rules. We read it in November 2026. Some examples:

- **Doctor of Medicine, Dental Surgery, Pharmacy:** three principal passes in Physics, Chemistry and Biology, with a minimum of 6 points in the harmonised table for health programmes, and set minimum grades in each subject.
- **Nursing (BScN):** three principal passes in Chemistry, Biology and either Physics, Advanced Mathematics or Nutrition.
- **Engineering:** for example, UDSM's Chemical and Process Engineering asks for principal passes in Advanced Mathematics and Physics, with at least a D in Mathematics.

Rules differ by university and change every year, so check the current guidebook. Our guide on [how to read the TCU guidebook](/post/educational/study-tips/how-to-read-tcu-admission-guidebook/) shows how.

### What the national plan says

Tanzania Development Vision 2050 (Dira 2050) says its second pillar prioritises quality education and skills, "especially in" STEM. A section on science and technology says these are important drivers of productivity and growth. It also names problems: limited infrastructure, funding shortages, skills gaps, and gender disparities in access to quality STEM education.

This is a policy statement about goals. It does not promise a job to every science graduate.

### What exam results show

Science at school is not easy for everyone. Reporting by The Citizen on the 2025 Form Four results (released 31 January 2026) gave these pass rates: Basic Mathematics 26.45%, Biology 65.68%, Physics 84.04% and Chemistry 96.88%. The article does not define "pass", and an education analyst quoted in it linked weak results to teaching quality, learning time and foundations. So mathematics is a real hurdle, and you should plan for it.

## What science opens, and what it does not

**What it opens**

- Health courses such as medicine, nursing and pharmacy, which need Physics, Chemistry and Biology or Chemistry and Biology plus another science.
- Engineering and technology degrees, which usually need Advanced Mathematics and Physics.
- Science teaching, laboratory work, agriculture, food science and environment courses.
- Many degrees that accept both science and arts subjects, so a science combination keeps more doors open.

**What it does not do**

- It does not guarantee admission. Places are limited, and points matter.
- It does not guarantee a job. Openings and pay vary.
- It does not suit everyone. A student who dislikes the work may do better in another field.

## Arts and business: a fair comparison

A push for science should not shame other subjects. The guidebook shows real strength elsewhere:

- **Law.** Bachelor of Laws entries we read, for example at UDSM, list History, Geography, Kiswahili, English, Economics and other arts and business subjects, and also Physics, Chemistry, Biology and Advanced Mathematics. They usually also ask for English or History. One university we checked asks only for "any two subjects".
- **Education.** The Bachelor of Arts with Education at UDSM lists 1,386 places, and asks for arts subjects, Economics or Advanced Mathematics.
- **Business and economics.** Combinations such as EGM, ECA and HGE lead to economics, finance, planning and accounting. See our guides to [EGM](/post/educational/study-tips/jobs-for-egm-combination/), [ECA](/post/educational/study-tips/jobs-for-eca-combination/) and [HGE](/post/educational/study-tips/jobs-for-hge-combination/).
- **Languages and humanities.** [HGL](/post/educational/study-tips/jobs-for-hgl-combination/), [HGK](/post/educational/study-tips/jobs-for-hgk-combination/) and [HKL](/post/educational/study-tips/jobs-for-hkl-combination/) lead to teaching, law, public service and communication.

Teachers, lawyers, accountants and planners are needed too. The right question is not "science or arts?" but "which doors do I want open?"

## If you feel weak in science

You can improve. Here is honest, practical advice.

1. **Start with mathematics.** Most science subjects use it. Fix basics first: fractions, algebra, units.
2. **Practise, do not only read.** Quiz yourself with questions. Our guide to [study techniques](/post/educational/study-tips/10-proven-techniques-to-study-effectively-for-students/) explains how.
3. **Use past papers and examiners' reports.** NECTA publishes reports on how candidates answered. Our post on [using examiners' reports to revise](/post/educational/study-tips/use-necta-examiners-reports-to-revise/) shows how.
4. **Ask questions early.** Ask a teacher or a stronger classmate when you are stuck, not at the end of term.
5. **Be patient.** If your results are low, read your options in [what to do after poor Form Four results](/post/educational/results/what-to-do-after-poor-form-four-results/).

Remember that A-Level science is harder than O-Level. If you are unsure, try honest practice questions in the A-Level subjects before choosing.

## How to choose using your own strengths

1. List the subjects where you score well and enjoy the work.
2. Write down three careers that interest you.
3. For each, search the TCU guidebook to see which subjects it asks for.
4. Compare with the combinations your school offers.
5. Talk to a teacher, a parent and, if possible, someone working in the field.

Our overview of [how your A-Level combination affects your career](/post/educational/combination-na-aina-ya-kazi-utakayofanya/) has a table you can use for this.

## Common questions

### Is science better than arts?

No. They lead to different doors. Choose the one that fits your strengths and goals.

### Can I study medicine with an arts combination?

Not in the entries we read. They need Physics, Chemistry and Biology, or Chemistry and Biology plus another science. Check the current guidebook.

### Do I need Mathematics for every science course?

No, but it appears in many. PCB-type health courses may not require Advanced Mathematics, while engineering usually does.

### What if my school does not offer the combination I want?

Ask your teachers about other schools and how selection works. Do not pay anyone for a place.

## What to do next

1. Write down the career you want and look it up in the TCU guidebook.
2. Check your mathematics and science results honestly, and make a plan to improve.
3. Read the combination guide that matches your shortlist.

Choose carefully, and know that effort in the right place matters more than any label.
