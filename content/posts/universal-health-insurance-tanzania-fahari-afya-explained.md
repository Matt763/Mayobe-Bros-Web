Tanzania's Universal Health Insurance (bima ya afya kwa wote) started on 26 January 2026. On 22 October 2026 the government opened its second phase, which lets people who work for themselves, such as farmers, traders and bodaboda riders, join as well. This post explains what the scheme is, what the main package costs, how to join and what to check before you pay. The facts were checked in November 2026 and some details are still being settled, so we say where reports differ.

## The short answer

- A law passed in 2023 makes health insurance compulsory. The government pays for households it classes as unable to pay.
- The main package for people who pay for themselves is called Fahari Afya. It costs TSh 150,000 a year for a household of up to six people.
- A household here means the head, a spouse and up to four dependants. A person living alone counts as a household.
- You join through the NHIF self-service website, an agent, a bank, a mobile network or an NHIF office. You need your NIDA number.
- Insurance does not mean every treatment is free everywhere. You use hospitals that work with the scheme and follow the referral steps.

## What the scheme is and where it came from

The Universal Health Insurance Act was passed in 2023. The Citizen reported that Parliament passed it on 1 November 2023, the President signed it on 19 November 2023 and it was published on 1 December 2023. The scheme began on 26 January 2026. The National Health Insurance Fund (NHIF) leads it, and the Tanzania Insurance Regulatory Authority (TIRA) regulates health insurance.

The idea is simple. Many families pay for care out of their own pocket on the day someone falls ill, and a big bill can wipe out savings. Insurance spreads that risk. Everyone pays a share in advance, and the money pays for care when someone needs it.

Health Minister Mohamed Mchengerwa said at the launch that people who can afford insurance should pay towards their own care, while the government keeps supporting vulnerable households. The WHO's Tanzania office reported this in its news release of 22 October 2026.

## Phase one and phase two

**Phase one** began in January 2026 and was aimed at households the government classes as unable to pay. The Chanzo reported a budget of TSh 48.8 billion and a target of 276,004 households, about 1.2 million people. The Citizen reported on 23 October that 198,709 of those households had been verified and had received insurance cards, which is 72% of the target.

**Phase two** was launched nationally at Sabasaba grounds in Mtwara on 22 October 2026, with similar events in other regions. It does two things. It adds more households the government will pay for, and it opens the scheme to people who pay for themselves, including those in informal work.

The reports do not agree on the size of the second phase. The Citizen and Daily News gave a target of 625,000 households. The Chanzo gave 589,772 households that the government will pay for, plus people who fund their own membership. An earlier Citizen report from May 2026 said 589,000. We could not find one official figure, so treat the number as "roughly 600,000 households".

The Health Minister has also said the aim is 25 million people covered by June 2027, according to Daily News. That is a target, not a result.

## Fahari Afya: price, size and services

For people who pay for themselves, the main package is Fahari Afya. WHO's news release and the Citizen both give the price as TSh 150,000 a year for a household of up to six people.

Daily News described who can be in the household:

- the household head and a spouse
- up to four dependants, which can include a parent or a parent-in-law
- a child or a brother or sister under 21

A child or sibling who turns 21 must join as a separate household. A household with more than six people can form a second household. If you live alone, you can register as one household.

The package covers what the reports call 372 essential services. Daily News listed consultations, laboratory and X-ray tests, medicines, supplies, surgery, hospital stays, eye care, and oral and dental care. Medicines follow the National Essential Medicines List, so a medicine outside that list may not be covered. Benefits also depend on the level of the facility.

One detail changed along the way. In January the Citizen reported a package of 277 services. The later reports from The Chanzo and Daily News say 372. If this matters to you, ask NHIF which list applies when you register.

The premium is for the whole year. Daily News reported that you can pay in a lump sum, and the Citizen reported in January that payments through mobile money could start from TSh 14,000 and be added up until the TSh 150,000 is complete.

Here is a simple example with made-up numbers to show the idea. If a family of five pays TSh 150,000 for the year, that is TSh 12,500 a month on average. The same family could pay TSh 30,000 for one clinic visit with tests and medicine, and a hospital stay would cost far more. Your own costs will differ, so use this only to compare.

## How to join

According to Daily News, there are five ways to register:

1. The NHIF self-service website, selfservice.nhif.or.tz
2. Authorised insurance agents
3. Participating banks and mobile network operators
4. Community health workers
5. NHIF offices

You will need your NIDA number and proof of your relationship with each dependant, such as a marriage or birth certificate. After you pay, you receive a membership number and a digital card. If you do not yet have a NIDA number, read our guide on [how to get your national ID and the new 2026 card](/post/tanzania-news/nida-national-id-new-card-2026-how-to-get-yours/).

Check the address before you type anything. NHIF's own website is nhif.or.tz. Scammers copy health and money services, so never send money to a personal number because someone says it is for "insurance". Our post on [spotting fake SMS links and login pages](/post/technology-solutions/cyber-security/spot-fake-sms-links-and-login-pages/) shows the warning signs.

## Waiting time, renewal and fraud

Daily News reported four rules worth knowing. We could not find them on an official NHIF page, so confirm them when you register.

- Services start 30 days after you register and pay. Some groups are exempt, such as people the government pays for and public and formal private-sector employees.
- Membership lasts 12 months from the day you pay.
- You get a text reminder 30 days before it ends. If you renew within 30 days after it ends, you keep your status. After that you are treated as a new member and wait again.
- Using someone else's card or giving false details can lead to legal or administrative action. The Citizen reported the Health Minister warning against fraud, including using other people's cards and claiming for services that were not provided.

## People who work for pay and people the government covers

Formal workers are covered differently. The Citizen reported that in the formal sector the employee and the employer each pay 3% of pay, 6% in total. If you are starting your first job, our guides on [your employment contract](/post/jobs-and-internships/employment-contract-and-your-rights-first-job-tanzania/) and [NSSF paperwork](/post/educational/career-development/first-job-in-tanzania-nssf-paperwork-and-nerves/) explain what to ask your employer.

For households the government pays for, Daily News said the families are listed in a database using work by the National Bureau of Statistics and the Tanzania Social Action Fund (TASAF), and eligibility is checked again every three years. You do not buy anything. If you think your household qualifies, ask your ward or village office how the list is made.

## What to watch for

Having a card is not the same as getting good care. The WHO's Tanzania representative, Dr Alex Gasasira, said success depends on whether people who enrol can really get quality care, and he called for more medicines, health workers and facilities. The Chanzo reported a WHO review from March 2026 that found shortages of staff and medicines.

The Chanzo also reported that of the phase-one beneficiaries, about 495,000 cards had been collected and about 502,000 had not. If you are enrolled by the government, collect your card and keep your details up to date.

The Health Minister has said that nobody should be turned away only because they have no cash or no card, according to The Chanzo. How well that works in practice will vary between hospitals, so keep your receipts and your membership number.

## Common questions

### Is health insurance now compulsory in Tanzania?

The law makes it compulsory, and employers must register their workers. The reports we read did not describe a penalty for a household that has not joined. Enrolment for people in informal work is being opened in phase two. Ask NHIF or your local health office about the rules for your situation.

### Does TSh 150,000 cover my whole extended family?

No. It covers up to six people: the head, a spouse and up to four dependants, with age limits for children and siblings. A larger family can form a second household.

### Can I choose any hospital?

No. Daily News reported that you must use facilities contracted by the scheme and follow the referral procedure. Skipping the referral steps can leave you paying for treatment. Ask NHIF or the facility before you go.

### Who do I call with a complaint?

Daily News listed the Ministry of Health toll-free line 199 and NHIF on 0800 787800. Check these numbers on the official sites before relying on them. NHIF's own site lists +255 26 2963887 and info@nhif.or.tz.

## What to do next

1. Count who is in your household and check each person's age and relationship against the rules above.
2. Make sure each adult has a NIDA number, then visit the NHIF self-service site or an NHIF office to register.
3. Ask which hospitals near you accept the scheme and what the referral steps are.
4. Note your renewal date, and keep your phone number current so the reminder reaches you.
