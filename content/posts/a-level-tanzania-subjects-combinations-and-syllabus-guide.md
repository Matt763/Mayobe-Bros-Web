A-Level in Tanzania means Form Five and Form Six, called *kidato cha tano* and *kidato cha sita*. At the end, you sit the national exam that decides your university and college options. This guide explains how the system fits together: the exam, the combinations (called *tahasusi* in Kiswahili), the syllabi, and how to pick a path. Each subject has its own detailed guide, linked below.

It is written for Form Four leavers, Form Five and Six students, and parents.

## Key points

- The Advanced Certificate of Secondary Education Examination (ACSEE) is run by NECTA, the National Examinations Council of Tanzania. It comes after two years of advanced level study.
- You do not study every subject. You study one combination, usually three principal subjects such as PCB or HGL, and the combination decides which degrees you can apply for.
- NECTA says General Studies is compulsory for all ACSEE candidates. The 2023 TIE curriculum names two different compulsory subjects. The two descriptions are not yet explained together by any official page we found.
- The 2023 syllabi from the Tanzania Institute of Education (TIE) are the newest published, but the NECTA exam formats we could open still date from 2019. Always ask your school which one your class follows.
- Official syllabi are free on the TIE website. You do not need to download notes from unofficial sites.

## What A-Level and the ACSEE are

After Form Four and the CSEE (the O-Level exam), students who qualify join Form Five. NECTA's ACSEE page says candidates are those who have completed two years of advanced level secondary education and had at least three credits in their CSEE. It also says the exam is held each year in the first week of May, and that its purpose is to assess whether learners are ready for diploma and degree courses.

How you get into Form Five is a separate process. Our guide on [Form Five selection](/post/educational/study-tips/form-five-selection-tanzania-how-to-check-and-join/) shows how to check and join. The ministry's selection list (*selection ya kidato cha tano*) places you in a school and a combination.

When results come out, they are given as grades per subject, with points added up. Our guide on [reading Form Six results](/post/educational/results/how-to-read-acsee-results-points-and-divisions/) explains the points and divisions.

## How a combination works

A combination is the fixed group of subjects you study for two years. Your school teaches it as a package, so you cannot mix and match freely once you join.

Most combinations have three principal subjects. The name is a short code made from the first letters. PCB is Physics, Chemistry and Biology. HGL is History, Geography and English Language. NECTA's ACSEE page lists these combinations:

| Group on NECTA's page | Combinations |
|---|---|
| Natural science | PCM, PCB, PGM, EGM, CBG, CBA, CBN |
| Arts | HGL, HGK, HKL, KLF, ECA, HGE |

The letters are:

- **PCM**: Physics, Chemistry, Mathematics
- **PCB**: Physics, Chemistry, Biology
- **PGM**: Physics, Geography, Mathematics
- **EGM**: Economics, Geography, Mathematics
- **CBG**: Chemistry, Biology, Geography
- **CBA**: Chemistry, Biology, Agriculture
- **CBN**: Chemistry, Biology, Food and Human Nutrition
- **HGL**: History, Geography, English Language
- **HGK**: History, Geography, Kiswahili
- **HKL**: History, Kiswahili, English Language
- **KLF**: Kiswahili, English Language, French
- **ECA**: Economics, Commerce, Accountancy
- **HGE**: History, Geography, Economics

NECTA's list is not the whole picture. The TAMISEMI Form Five selection website lists 65 combinations in seven groups, as checked on 24 November 2026. It names the ECA group as ECAc, and it also lists newer ones. Your school offers only some of them. Ask the headmaster or academic master which ones it runs.

Some combinations have entry conditions. For example, TAMISEMI says EGM needs a pass in Geography and Mathematics, and ECAc needs a pass in Commerce and Book Keeping. Check your own combination's line on the official list.

## Principal and subsidiary subjects

University entry rules use the word "principal". The TCU admission guidebook for 2026/27 says applicants need at least two principal passes at ACSEE with a total of at least 4.0 points, and it says those who do not should not apply. For those who finished A-Level from 2016 onwards, the guidebook gives these points: A = 5, B = 4, C = 3, D = 2, E = 1, S = 0.5.

Two principal passes are the minimum, not the whole story. Each programme names the subjects it wants, so a pass in the wrong subjects does not count. Our guide on [reading the TCU admission guidebook](/post/educational/study-tips/how-to-read-tcu-admission-guidebook/) shows how to find your programme line.

A subsidiary subject is a lighter subject that sits beside the principals. Some programmes ask for one, for example a mathematics or English pass. Some other compulsory subjects sit outside your combination altogether.

## The extra subjects everyone meets

Two descriptions are in print, and they do not match yet.

**NECTA's version.** The ACSEE page says General Studies is compulsory for all candidates, and other subjects depend on the combination. The 2019 NECTA format describes one General Studies paper. Our [General Studies guide](/post/educational/a-level-subjects/a-level-general-studies-topics-tanzania/) has the details.

**TIE's 2023 version.** The upper secondary curriculum for Forms Five and Six (TIE, dated 29 June 2023) lists two compulsory subjects outside the combination: Academic Communication (taught in English) and Historia ya Tanzania na Maadili (History of Tanzania and Ethics). The same curriculum says students in science, economics or accounting combinations without Mathematics must take Basic Applied Mathematics, unless they passed Additional Mathematics at O-Level. Our guides on [Advanced Mathematics](/post/educational/a-level-subjects/a-level-advanced-mathematics-topics-tanzania/) and [Physics](/post/educational/a-level-subjects/a-level-physics-topics-tanzania/) explain this in more detail.

We could not find an official page that says which Form Six year is first examined under the 2023 subject list. Ask your school and check the NECTA website.

## Two sets of documents: syllabi and exam formats

Two organisations shape what you study.

- **TIE** writes the syllabi, the list of topics for each subject. Its upper secondary syllabi page lists 2023 syllabi for subjects such as Physics, History, Geography, Kiswahili, Literature in English and French, as checked on 24 November 2026. The page has more than one page of results, so look through all of it for your subject.
- **NECTA** writes the exam formats, which say how many papers there are, how long they last and how marks are given. NECTA's ACSEE page links the 2019 formats booklet.

Here is the part that confuses people. The newest TIE syllabi are from 2023, but the formats on NECTA's site are from 2019, and our subject guides found that the 2019 formats are built on older syllabi (2009 or 2010 depending on the subject). We found no published NECTA format for the 2023 syllabi.

What to do with this:

1. Study from the syllabus your school gives you.
2. Use the NECTA format for the exam year you will sit.
3. Do not skip a topic only because it appears in one list and not the other. Topic names overlap strongly.
4. If a teacher says the format has changed, ask for the document and check it on NECTA's site.

## How to choose a combination

Start from the end, not from the start. Choose the degree or career you might want, then see which combinations lead to it. Do not choose only because friends or a school's most popular group.

Use this order:

1. **Write down two or three possible careers.** They can change later.
2. **Open the TCU guidebook entry for each degree.** Note the subjects it asks for. Some ask for two named principals. Some ask for three.
3. **Compare with the combinations.** Engineering usually points to a combination with Physics and Advanced Mathematics. Medicine points to Physics, Chemistry and Biology. Many arts, law and education degrees accept a wide range of subjects.
4. **Check your O-Level results.** A combination is easier when you did well in its subjects in Form Four. Basic Applied Mathematics or Advanced Mathematics is harder if mathematics was a weak point.
5. **Ask what your school offers.** A good combination on paper means little if your school has no teachers or laboratory for it.
6. **Keep a second option.** Many students end up with a different degree than they first planned.

## Where to find the official syllabi and formats

- **TIE syllabi:** the TIE website has a page for upper secondary syllabi (*Muhtasari Elimu ya Sekondari ya Juu*). The page is in Kiswahili and lists subject syllabi by year. Open the one for your subject and check the year printed on it.
- **TIE curriculum:** the upper secondary curriculum (*Mtaala wa Elimu ya Sekondari Kidato cha V-VI*) is also on the TIE site. It is the document that names the compulsory subjects and the combination list.
- **NECTA formats:** the ACSEE page on the NECTA site links the formats booklet. NECTA also publishes examiners' reports for free, which show common mistakes.
- **Your teacher and school library:** TIE books and the school's copies match your class.

Official sources are free. Avoid sites that sell or give away unofficial copies of notes and books, because they may copy work without permission and their files can be unsafe.

## Common questions

### Can I change my combination after I join Form Five?

We could not find an official rule that guarantees this. Schools and the ministry set their own procedures, and a change usually needs the school's approval and a place in the new group. Ask your academic master early, because the sooner you ask, the fewer topics you miss.

### How many subjects do I study at A-Level?

Your combination has three principal subjects. The 2023 curriculum adds compulsory subjects outside it, and some students must take Basic Applied Mathematics. NECTA also says General Studies is compulsory. Ask your school which subjects it will enter you for.

### Do I need the same subjects as at O-Level?

Not always, but the combination gives the best results when you were already comfortable with its subjects. TAMISEMI lists entry conditions for some combinations, such as a pass in Geography and Mathematics for EGM.

### Which combination is best?

There is no single best one. The best combination is the one that leads to degrees you want and that you can pass with good grades. Check the TCU entry line for those degrees.

### Which syllabus is my exam based on?

This depends on your exam year. NECTA's latest published formats we could open are from 2019. TIE's newest syllabi are from 2023. Ask your teacher and check NECTA's website.

## What to do next

1. Pick two or three degrees you might want, and read their entry lines in the TCU guidebook.
2. Match the lines to combinations using the table above and the guides linked in it.
3. Open the TIE syllabus for your main subjects and read the topic list once.
4. Ask your school which combinations it runs, which syllabus it follows, and which compulsory subjects it will teach.
