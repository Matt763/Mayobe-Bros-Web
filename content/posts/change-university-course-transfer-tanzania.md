Maybe the first weeks have gone by and a quiet voice keeps asking: is this really the course for me? Many first-year students feel that. It is not a failure, and in Tanzania there are official ways to change. This post explains them using TCU's own notices.

## The short answer

You can ask to move to another programme or university, but you must follow the rules, and there are limits. For first-year students, TCU opens a transfer window each year. For students who have already completed courses, there is a separate credit transfer process. In both cases, the university you want to join decides.

- A first-year transfer needs a written request to the university you want to join.
- The programme must have free places, and you must meet its entry requirements.
- Credit transfer is for students who have already passed courses or modules.
- To apply again in a later admission round, TCU's FAQ says you should first cancel your current admission.
- Do not treat a transfer as granted until you get official notice.

## Feeling stuck in the wrong course

Take a breath first. Picking a course at 18 or 19, often with little information, is hard. Wanting to change does not mean you are lazy or ungrateful. It means you are paying attention.

Before you act, ask yourself two honest questions. Is the problem the subject itself, or only a hard first month? And what do you want to study instead? A clear answer helps when you speak to a university office. Talk to a lecturer or student adviser at your current university as well.

## How does a first-year transfer work?

TCU published a notice on 19 October 2026 about first-year transfers from one higher education institution to another for 2026/27. We read it in full. It says the window was open from 19 October to 2 November 2026. Dates will be different in other years, so check TCU's current notice and almanac.

The procedure in the notice is:

1. Write an application to the institution you want to join.
2. You must have been admitted to a degree programme in the current academic year.
3. The programme you want must have slots for extra students.
4. You must meet the minimum entry requirements for that programme.
5. The receiving institution processes your application.
6. The receiving institution's Senate or Board must approve the transfer and send the approved list to TCU before the deadline.

So you apply to the new place, not to TCU. TCU's FAQ says the same: different institutions have different ways of receiving transfer requests, so ask the institution you want to join.

Say you were admitted to a programme at one university but you really want a different programme at another. You would write to the second university inside the window, show you meet its entry requirements, and wait to see if there is a free slot. Nothing is certain.

## Credit transfer for students who are further along

If you have already completed courses or modules, another process may fit you. TCU's guide "Your Guide to Transferring Academic Credits" (June 2026) says continuing students at all levels who have passed courses may apply to move those credits to another university in Tanzania, or from accredited universities elsewhere.

The guide says you start with a formal request to the receiving university, with:

- an official release letter from your current university
- certified copies of the certificates you used to get admitted
- course or module descriptions that show content, contact hours, assessment methods and grading
- an official transcript and anything else the receiving university requires

It also lists conditions. Some of the main ones:

- Ask at least one month before the new academic year opens.
- You must meet the entry requirements and the cut-off point for the new programme, based on the admission cycle in which you were first admitted.
- The programme must have empty slots.
- You must be registered at your current university. A student who was discontinued is not eligible.
- Credits must be transferred within five years of being earned.
- To graduate, at least 50% of core credits must be earned at the receiving university.
- Do not consider credit transfer granted until the University Senate sends official notice.

The guide also says a course that was core at your old university may not be core at the new one. Expect that some of your credits may not count the way you hoped.

TCU's 2026/27 admission guidebook lists a credit transfer window of 8 to 21 September 2026. Treat that as an example from one year and check the current almanac.

## Cancelling an admission to apply again

Sometimes the better route is to start the application again in a later window. TCU's FAQ says that if you want to change programme in a later admission window, you should first cancel the previous admission or selection, and then apply for the other programme or institution.

This is a big step. If you cancel and then do not get another place, you may have nothing. Before you cancel, ask the university and TCU what it means for your case, and be sure you meet the entry requirements for the new programme. Our post on [how to read the TCU admission guidebook](/post/educational/study-tips/how-to-read-tcu-admission-guidebook/) can help you check requirements.

For postponing studies, the FAQ says to consult your institution, because procedures differ.

## Common questions

### Can I change my course inside the same university?

The TCU pages we read speak about moving between institutions and about cancelling an admission. They do not describe a change within one university. Ask your university's registrar office.

### What if the window has closed?

The FAQ route is to cancel and apply again in a later window. Windows repeat each year, so check the TCU almanac for the next one.

### Will TCU approve my transfer?

TCU's notice says the receiving institution processes and approves transfers, and then sends them to TCU. You deal with the institution first.

### Will my loan or fees move with me?

We did not find this on the pages we opened. Ask your university and, if you have a loan, the loans board. Our post on the [student loan in Tanzania](/post/educational/study-tips/students-loan-tanzania-complete-guide/) is a starting point.

## What to do next

1. Write down why you want to change and what you want instead.
2. Read the entry requirements of the programme you want and check you meet them.
3. Find the current TCU notice or almanac for transfer dates, and contact the receiving institution before the window closes.
4. Do not cancel anything until you have an answer in writing.

Choosing again is not starting over. You have already learned what does not fit, and that is useful to know.
