A phone that lasts three years instead of two saves you real money. The good news is that most battery habits cost nothing. You do not need special tools, only a few small changes in how and where you charge.

This post repeats only what the phone makers say on their own help pages, and it says clearly where we could not confirm something.

## The short answer

Batteries wear out with age and use. You cannot stop that, but you can slow it. Heat is the biggest enemy, so keep your phone cool. Use a good charger. Turn on a charging-limit feature if your phone has one.

- Keep the phone out of hot places, such as a car in the sun.
- If the phone gets hot while charging, take off a thick case.
- Use the charger that came with the phone, or a good one made for it.
- Look in Battery settings for a feature that limits or delays charging to 100 percent.
- You do not need to drain the battery to zero before charging.

## Why heat matters more than most people think

Apple says heat can permanently reduce a battery's capacity, and charging in the heat adds more damage. Apple advises keeping iPhones out of ambient temperatures above 35 degrees Celsius, and says some cases trap heat while charging. If your phone gets warm while charging, take it out of the case first (Apple, as checked in November 2026).

Google says the same in plain words. A battery drains faster when it is hot, even when you are not using the phone, and heat can damage it. The phone also warms up while it charges, so Google advises not leaving it plugged in longer than needed (Google Pixel Help, as checked in November 2026).

What does this mean on an ordinary hot day?

- Do not leave your phone on a car seat or dashboard.
- Do not charge it under a pillow or in direct sun.
- Avoid playing a heavy game while it charges.
- Give it some air when it is warm.

## Charging habits that help (and the charger you use)

### Use the right charger

Google says to use the power adapter that came with your phone. Other adapters and cables can charge slowly, fail to charge, or damage the phone or battery. A cheap unknown charger is a risk. If you need a new one, buy a good one from a trusted shop and check that it suits your phone.

We did not find official proof that fast chargers or power banks harm batteries, so we make no claim about them either way. Follow your phone maker's advice.

### You do not need to drain it first

Google says you do not need to fully drain or fully charge the battery to teach the phone its capacity. Charge as much or as little as you need. Charging in short top-ups is fine.

### Do not leave it hot and full for no reason

Apple's advice for a phone you will store for a long time is to charge it to about 50 percent, switch it off and keep it cool and dry. Apple also says to avoid storing it fully charged or fully empty. This is for storage, not daily use. We do not state a fixed 20 to 80 percent rule, because the makers' pages do not set one.

## Battery-saving features to look for

Phones now have settings that protect the battery for you.

### On iPhone

Apple's Optimized Battery Charging learns your daily routine. It may wait to finish charging to 100 percent until shortly before you usually need the phone. Apple says it is available on iOS 13 and later (Apple, as checked in November 2026).

### On Google Pixel

Google's help page says you can go to Settings, then Battery, then Battery health, then Charging optimization. You can pick Adaptive Charging, which learns your habits over about 14 days and finishes charging near the time you normally unplug. On Pixel 6a and later, you can choose Limit to 80 percent instead. Google says this works best if you usually end the day with charge left, and you can switch it off when you need the longest battery life. A feature called Protecting your battery can also limit charging to about 70 to 80 percent in some hot or very long charging cases (Google Pixel Help, as checked in November 2026).

### On other Android phones

Other brands name these features differently, and we did not check them one by one. Open Settings, tap Battery, and look for a setting about charging limit, protecting the battery or optimised charging. Menu names may differ by brand and Android version.

## How to tell when a battery is worn out

Look for these signs:

- The phone drops from full to low much faster than it used to.
- It shuts off suddenly when the screen still shows some charge.
- It gets hot while doing simple things.
- The phone slows down or restarts more often.
- The back or screen is lifting, which can mean a swollen battery. Stop using and charging it, and take it to a repair person.

On an iPhone, open Settings, then Battery, then Battery Health. Apple says maximum capacity compares the battery with when it was new, and that it falls as the battery ages. Apple says iPhone 14 models and earlier are designed to keep 80 percent of original capacity at 500 complete charge cycles under ideal conditions, and iPhone 15 models at 1,000 cycles. These figures are as checked in November 2026 and real results depend on how you use and charge the phone (Apple Support).

If Battery Health says the battery is significantly degraded, Apple says an authorised service provider can replace it, and that this message is not a safety issue. A worn battery on any phone can often be replaced. Ask a trusted repair person for the price and the quality of the part, and be careful with very cheap batteries. ## Common questions

### Should I charge my phone overnight?

Phone makers have built features for this, like Optimized Battery Charging and Adaptive Charging. If your phone has one, turn it on. Also make sure the phone is not covered and does not get hot.

### Do I have to let the battery run to zero?

No. Google says you do not need to drain it fully to calibrate it.

### Is it safe to use my phone while it charges?

The makers' pages we read do not forbid it. Heat is the concern, so avoid heavy games or video while charging, and take off a case that gets hot.

### How long should a battery last?

It depends on the phone and on how you use it. Apple gives cycle figures for iPhones, and other makers may give their own. Check your maker's website.

### Does a battery saver mode wear the battery less?

We did not verify this, so we make no claim. It does help you get through a day.

## What to do next

1. Look in Settings, then Battery, for a charging optimisation or limit feature, and turn it on.
2. Move your charging spot to a cool, shaded place and remove any case that traps heat.
3. Check that your charger is the one made for your phone.
4. Watch the signs of a worn battery and plan a replacement before it fails.

Small habits repeated every day are what keep a phone going. You already have what you need to start.
