On your phone are the faces of the people you love, and the messages you may want to read again. Losing them hurts more than losing the phone. A new phone should never mean a fresh start you did not choose.

This post helps you save what matters first, then move it across with three clear paths.

## The short answer

Back up your old phone to your account, sign in on the new phone, and restore during setup. Keep both phones charged and on Wi-Fi. Do not erase or sell the old phone until you have checked that your photos, contacts and chats are on the new one.

- Back up first, even if you think you are safe.
- Check that you have enough free storage in your account and on the new phone.
- Use Wi-Fi, not mobile data, because backups can be large.
- Some things do not move. Check them one by one.
- Moving a SIM card or mobile-money line is a separate matter for your operator.

## Before you do anything: back up and check your storage

1. Charge the old phone, and connect it to Wi-Fi.
2. Write down your account details: your Google Account or Apple Account and its password. You will need them on the new phone.
3. Check your storage. A backup needs space in your account, and the new phone needs room for everything you move. Google says an account has 15 GB of free backup storage, with more available if you pay (Google Android Help, as checked in November 2026).
4. Back up your WhatsApp chats separately (see below).
5. Keep the old phone until you finish.

If your storage is almost full, our post on [how much RAM and storage you need](/post/technology-solutions/smartphones/how-much-ram-and-storage-do-you-need-in-a-phone/) explains how to see what uses the space.

## Android to Android: Google backup and restore

Google says Android can back up apps and app data, call history, contacts, device settings and SMS and MMS messages. Photos and videos are kept by Google Photos if you use it (Google Android Help, as checked in November 2026).

To back up now, open Settings, tap Google, then All services. Under Backup and restore, tap Backup, then Back up now. Menu names may differ by brand and Android version.

To restore, turn on the new phone and follow the setup screens. When you add your Google Account, you can choose the earlier backup. Google says the process can take up to 24 hours to complete fully, so be patient.

Two limits to know:

- A backup made on a newer Android cannot be restored onto a phone that runs a lower Android. If the new phone is behind the old one, the transfer may be incomplete.
- Contacts saved only on the SIM card may need to be restored by hand. Google links to a separate guide for this.

## iPhone to iPhone, and Android to iPhone

### iPhone to iPhone with Quick Start

Apple says the other phone must be an iPhone or iPad with iOS 11 or iPadOS 13 or later, and both should be updated. Put the new phone near the old one. The old phone must be unlocked, on the Home Screen, on Wi-Fi with Bluetooth on. Follow the prompts, then choose a transfer method.

There are two main ways. With iCloud download, apps and data come down in the background and you can start using the new phone right away. With direct transfer, you must wait until the transfer finishes on both phones. Keep both phones near each other and plugged in until it is done (Apple Support, as checked in November 2026).

### Android to iPhone with Move to iOS

Apple says the iPhone must be new or erased. On the new iPhone, choose to set up without another Apple device, then choose to transfer from Android. On the Android phone, open the Move to iOS app and enter the code the iPhone shows. Keep both phones plugged in and close to each other until the loading bar ends, and do not use other apps on the Android phone meanwhile.

Apple lists what can move: contacts, messages, camera photos and videos, files, bookmarks, mail accounts, WhatsApp messages and media, call history and calendars. Some free apps move if they exist on both stores. All of it must fit on the iPhone (Apple Support, as checked in November 2026). Newer versions may also offer a method without the app. Read Apple's page for the current steps.

## WhatsApp: back it up on its own

WhatsApp's help page says that on Android you open WhatsApp, tap Settings, then Chats, then Chat backup, and tap Back up. Connect to Wi-Fi first, because backup files can be large and may use mobile data. You can also turn on an end-to-end encrypted backup. If you do, keep the password or key safe, because without it you cannot open the backup later (WhatsApp Help Center, as checked in November 2026).

For the restore itself, we did not confirm the details. Follow the WhatsApp restore screen on the new phone and read WhatsApp's own help page.

## What does not move, and how to check

Do not promise yourself that everything will move. Plan to check.

- **Music, books and PDFs** do not move from Android to iPhone with Move to iOS. Copy them yourself.
- **Most apps** need to be installed again, and some need you to sign in again.
- **SIM-only contacts** may need to be restored by hand on Android.
- **Chats in other apps** may not move. Look inside each app for its own backup option.
- **Your phone number and mobile-money line** are tied to your SIM and your operator. We did not find a source to describe the steps, so ask your operator.

After the move, spend ten minutes checking:

1. Open your photos and scroll to the oldest ones.
2. Open your contacts and compare the count with the old phone.
3. Open your most important chats.
4. Open the apps you use daily and sign in.
5. Check your files and downloads.

Only when all of this looks right should you erase the old phone. Keep it a few more days if you can.

## Common questions

### How long does it take?

It depends on how much you have and on your network. Google says a restore can take up to 24 hours to complete fully. Apple says transfer time depends on the network and the amount of data.

### Will mobile data be used?

Backups can be large. Use Wi-Fi where you can, as WhatsApp itself advises.

### What if my backup account is full?

Free up space, buy more storage, or move your photos to a computer. Do not delete anything until you have a copy.

### Can I move from iPhone to Android?

We did not check a source for this path, so we do not cover it here. Look at the maker's official pages.

## What to do next

1. Back up your phone today, before anything goes wrong.
2. Write down your account passwords somewhere safe.
3. When you get the new phone, choose the path above that fits and keep both phones plugged in.
4. Check your photos, contacts and chats before you erase the old phone.

Your memories are worth ten careful minutes. Take them slowly and they will be waiting for you on the new phone.
