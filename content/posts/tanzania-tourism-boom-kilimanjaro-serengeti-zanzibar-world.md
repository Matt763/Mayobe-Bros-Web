Tanzania's tourism income reached a record in 2025, according to the Bank of Tanzania's visitor survey. This post sets out the official numbers, explains what they mean and what they do not, and gives practical advice if you plan to visit Kilimanjaro, the Serengeti or Zanzibar yourself.

The figures here were checked in November 2026. Some figures that circulate online, such as tourism's exact share of GDP, do not trace back to an official page, so we leave them out.

## Key points

- Tourism earnings in 2025 were a record US$4.41 billion (about TSh 11.6 trillion), up 13 percent from US$3.90 billion in 2024.
- International arrivals were 2,294,495, up 7.1 percent from 2,141,895.
- Visitors spent more per day. Average spending on the Mainland rose 19 percent to US$289 per person per night.
- Zanzibar earned US$1,190.8 million from 654,880 arrivals.
- The government says it is aiming for 7 million tourist arrivals in 2026. It is not clear that it counts the same visitors as the survey above (see below).

## What the numbers say

These figures come from the Bank of Tanzania's International Visitors' Exit Survey for 2025. The survey asks visitors about their trip as they leave the country. The results were reported by the Daily News on 15 July 2026 and by TanzaniaInvest.

| Measure | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| International arrivals | 2,141,895 | 2,294,495 |
| Tourism earnings | US$3,903.1 million | US$4,410.6 million |
| Average spend per person per night (Mainland) | US$243 | US$289 |

Earnings grew faster than arrivals. In other words, the 2025 record came more from each visitor spending more than from many more people arriving.

Daily News also reported that arrivals were above 150 percent of 2019 levels, and that holidaymakers produced about 96 percent of receipts. Package tourists, whose trips are booked as a bundle, made up 58.8 percent of Mainland visitors and 75.2 percent of Mainland earnings (TanzaniaInvest).

### Where visitors come from

On the Mainland, the biggest shares of arrivals were the United States (12.4 percent), Italy (11.8), France (7.0), Kenya (6.4) and the United Kingdom (6.0). In Zanzibar, the leaders were Italy (18.8 percent), France (10.6), the United Kingdom (7.7), the United States (6.6) and Germany (6.4).

### Why 2.29 million and 7 million are different

You may see different totals in the news. Officials sometimes quote larger totals that may include other kinds of visitors, and such a figure does not trace back to a primary page, so we do not use one. The 7 million target for 2026 was stated by Minister Ashatu Kijaji (AllAfrica, 5 August 2026), who said "we are going to hit seven million this year". It is not clear exactly which visitors the target counts, so do not compare it directly with the 2.29 million figure. Ask the Ministry of Natural Resources and Tourism or NBS if you need a precise comparison.

## Zanzibar

Zanzibar had 654,880 arrivals in 2025, up 9 percent from 601,006, and earnings of US$1,190.8 million, up 19.3 percent. Spending per visitor per night was US$274, up from US$251.

Zanzibar's mix of visitors is more European than the Mainland, with Italy first. That matters for hotels and tour operators, who plan seasons and languages around their main markets.

## Kilimanjaro and the Serengeti

Kilimanjaro is the highest mountain in Africa, at roughly 5,900 metres. The Serengeti is famous for its large wildlife herds and for the wildebeest migration. Both draw international visitors. Safari trips often combine northern parks such as the Serengeti, Ngorongoro and Tarangire.

For practical rules and current park fees, use the official Tanzania National Parks Authority (TANAPA) and Ngorongoro Conservation Area Authority (NCAA) notices. Fees and rules change, so do not rely on a blog, including this one, for the final price.

Two things are widely stated but worth confirming before you book:

- Climbing Kilimanjaro needs a licensed guide. Ask your operator for proof of licence.
- Parks have different fees for foreign visitors, East African citizens and Tanzanian residents. Ask TANAPA for the current resident rate and what ID it needs.

## Planning a trip

Here is a simple plan if you are a Tanzanian resident or a local traveller:

1. Pick one area. Do not try to see the Serengeti, Kilimanjaro and Zanzibar in one short trip.
2. Ask at least three licensed operators for a written price. Check that the quote lists park fees, transport, accommodation and meals, and what is not included.
3. Pay only to a business name you can verify. Be careful with offers on social media that ask you to send money by mobile money to a personal number.
4. Check the weather and the migration season with your operator, because wildlife movements change from year to year.
5. Read and follow park rules about distances from animals and about plastic.

## What it means for Tanzanians

More visitor spending can mean more income for hotels, guides, drivers, cooks and craft sellers. It does not mean every worker earns more. Package tourism, where most of the money is paid up front to a travel company, may leave less in the local area than the headline figure suggests. We did not find a source that shows how much of the US$4.41 billion stays in local communities.

If you want to work in tourism, look at the Mainland's licensed guide training, hotel and hospitality training colleges, and language skills. Our post on [how to build a career from scratch](/post/educational/career-development/how-to-build-a-career-from-scratch/) has general steps. We do not promise any income figure.

## Common questions

### Is it true that tourism is 17 percent of Tanzania's GDP?

That figure does not trace back to an official source, so we do not use it. Daily News via AllAfrica said tourism contributes about 25 percent of export earnings, which is a different measure.

### Does the record mean tourism is safe from shocks?

No. Tourism depends on world travel, air routes and the economy of visitors' countries. Fuel and currency changes can affect costs too. Our post on [Tanzania's fuel security](/post/tanzania-news/tanzania-fuel-security-measures-navigating-current-challenges/) explains one part of that.

### Where can I find official statistics?

The Bank of Tanzania publishes the Exit Survey, and the National Bureau of Statistics publishes arrivals and economic data. TANAPA and NCAA publish park information.

## What to do next

1. Before booking, ask TANAPA or NCAA for the current fee list, and confirm it with your operator.
2. If you work in tourism, keep your licences up to date and ask your association about training.
3. If you are researching for school or business, use the Bank of Tanzania report and mention which year and survey you used.
