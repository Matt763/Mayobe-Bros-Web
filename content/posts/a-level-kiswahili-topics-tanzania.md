Kiswahili in kidato cha tano na sita is not the Kiswahili you studied in Form Four. It goes deep into how the language is built, how it spread, how to edit, translate and interpret, and how to judge a poem, a novel or a play. This guide is for Form Four leavers choosing a combination, Form Five and Form Six students, and parents who want to know what the subject asks of a child.

Everything below comes from the Tanzania Institute of Education (TIE) syllabus and from NECTA pages that we opened in November 2026. The topic names are Kiswahili terms, explained here in English.

## Key points

- Kiswahili is a combination subject. You study it only if your combination includes it, for example HGK, HKL or KLF. It is not taught to everyone.
- The syllabus we read is the TIE "Muhtasari wa Kiswahili Elimu ya Sekondari Kidato cha V-VI", first edition 2023. It has five big competences, from language structure to writing literature.
- Literature has its own subject called Fasihi ya Kiswahili. Some combinations use that instead of plain Kiswahili.
- NECTA's older exam format has two papers of three hours each, with short questions and essays. A newer format could not be found online.
- Passing is easy, but top grades are rare. In the 2025 Form Six exam, 99.98% passed Kiswahili, yet only 0.56% got an A.

## What the subject is, and who takes it

The 2023 syllabus says Kiswahili is a compulsory subject for a student whose combination contains it. Its aim is to build advanced language skills, respect for Kiswahili as the national language, editing, translation and interpreting, and a taste for literature.

The TIE upper secondary curriculum lists Kiswahili, English, Fasihi ya Kiswahili and Literature in English as separate subjects in the "Lugha na Fasihi" area. Kiswahili gets ten periods of 40 minutes each week. That is the same as any other main combination subject.

On NECTA's own ACSEE page, the arts combinations include HGK (History, Geography, Kiswahili), HKL (History, Kiswahili, English Language) and KLF (Kiswahili, English Language, French). The 2023 curriculum adds many more, such as KLS (Kiswahili, English Language, Sports) and some with Theatre Arts, Music or Fine Art.

## The five big areas of the syllabus

The syllabus sets out five main competences. Each one is a block of topics.

| Area | What you learn |
|---|---|
| Advanced Kiswahili skills | Sounds (fonimu and alofoni), syllables, word parts, sentence analysis, new vocabulary, meaning and ambiguity |
| Kiswahili as a national language | Origin theories, Bantu roots, spread before and after independence, first, second and official language, standardisation and dialects |
| Editing | Rules and marks of editing, correcting texts |
| Translation and interpreting | Translation methods, note-taking, interpreting, judging other people's translations |
| Literature | Theory of literature, how it developed, judging novels, plays and poems, writing your own |

## Form Five topics

In Form Five the syllabus starts with the building blocks of the language. You describe sounds and their groups, the structure of syllables, and stress and intonation. You then study word parts (mofu and alomofu) and how they work in a word.

Sentence analysis comes next. You break down complex and compound sentences in more than one way, for example with tree diagrams and tables. You also build your vocabulary from speech and written texts, and learn how context and pragmatics shape meaning, including why a sentence can be ambiguous.

The second block is about Kiswahili itself. You discuss theories about where the language began, test the claim that it is a Bantu language by comparing words and sentences, and trace how it spread in East Africa before and after independence. Science and technology also appear, for example how radio, phones and social media spread the language. You also meet ideas such as first language, second language, official language, national language and international language.

The remaining Form Five work is practical. You learn the rules of editing and practise on texts with mistakes. You learn translation methods, translate ordinary texts, and begin interpreting. In literature you study what literature is, how it grew before and after foreign contact, and how to judge a novel and a play. You also write a short novel or play yourself.

## Form Six topics

Form Six goes deeper. Semantics (the meaning of words) returns with more depth, and so does vocabulary work. You study the standardisation of Kiswahili and the role of dialects. There are also lessons on correct and polite language, and on common errors.

Editing moves to harder texts, including technical ones. Translation and interpreting now include judging quality: you assess translated texts and interpreted speech, and study the problems interpreters face. The syllabus also asks you to look at job chances in these fields.

In literature the focus shifts to poetry. You study how poems are built and what they say, and how literature changed in the period around independence. You also write poems and discuss the work of a chosen writer.

## How Kiswahili is marked

The 2023 curriculum says that 30% of the final mark comes from continuous assessment and 70% from the national Form Six exam. In Form V the two term exams count 5% each. In Form VI the first term exam counts 6%, a project 7% and a completion test 7%. The same split appears in the Kiswahili syllabus.

The latest NECTA exam format we could open is the 2019 booklet, which is built on the 2009 syllabus. As of 24 November 2026 we could not find a newer ACSEE format on NECTA's website. In that booklet, Kiswahili has two papers of three hours. Each paper has four short questions worth 10 marks each, all compulsory, and four essay questions of 20 marks, where you answer three and one is compulsory. Paper 1 covers language topics. Paper 2 covers literature, with set poetry, novels and plays.

NECTA's report on the 2025 Form Six paper confirms the same two-paper shape, and the topics tested: comprehension and summary, grammar, translation and interpreting, composition, development of Kiswahili, language use, oral literature, general literature, text design, and criticism of poetry, novels and plays. Set books can change, so ask your teacher for the current list.

## What 2025 results tell you

NECTA published an analysis of the 2025 Form Six Kiswahili paper. A total of 40,689 candidates sat it. Of them, 99.98% passed with grades A to S, and 54.39% got a C. The A share was 0.56% and B was 24.94%.

The weakest topics were language use (37.85% of marks), general literature (47.60%), translation and interpreting (50.31%) and development of oral literature (51.68%). NECTA's own reasons were that students did not follow what the question asked and had a thin grasp of some topics. This is useful for planning. Do not spend all your time on grammar drills if your language use and literature theory are thin.

## Study advice that fits this subject

- Read the poems, novels and plays for the year early. A set book read for the first time in Form Six is a heavy load.
- Practise answering the exact task. Many marks are lost by writing about a topic instead of answering the question.
- Write something every week: a summary, a short translation, a letter. Ask your teacher to correct it.
- Make a list of terms (fonimu, alofoni, mofu, virai, vishazi) with one example sentence of your own for each. Terms are easy marks.
- Practise short translations both ways, Kiswahili to English and English to Kiswahili.

We do not link to past papers here. Ask your school for the papers it holds.

## What this subject opens

Kiswahili is often paired with History, English or Geography. Our guides on [HGK](/post/educational/study-tips/jobs-for-hgk-combination/) and [HKL](/post/educational/study-tips/jobs-for-hkl-combination/) show degrees such as education, law, social work and public administration, using the TCU guidebook for 2026/27. Entry rules differ for each programme, so read the line for your chosen course. Our guide on [reading TCU entry requirements](/post/educational/career-development/how-to-read-tcu-programme-entry-requirements/) shows how.

Kiswahili also leads to teaching, translation, editing, broadcasting and writing. The syllabus itself says the subject aims to help students find work or self-employment. That is the goal, not a promise.

## Common questions

### Is Kiswahili the same as Fasihi ya Kiswahili?

No. They are two separate subjects in the 2023 curriculum. Kiswahili covers language and also some literature. Fasihi ya Kiswahili is about literature only. Your combination tells you which one you take.

### Do I need Kiswahili to join a degree?

Only if the programme you want lists it. Many arts programmes accept two principal passes from a long list that includes Kiswahili. Check the exact line in the TCU guidebook.

### Is the 2023 syllabus already examined?

We could not confirm from NECTA which Form Six year first follows the 2023 syllabi. NECTA's 2025 report refers to the Kiswahili syllabus for Forms Five and Six without a year. Ask your school or check NECTA's news page.

### Is Kiswahili an easy subject because it is our language?

The pass rate is very high, but A grades are rare. The subject is academic and needs practice.

## What to do next

1. Ask your teacher which syllabus your class follows and which set books you need.
2. Download the Kiswahili syllabus from TIE and read the topic list for your form.
3. Choose one weak area from the 2025 results list and practise it this week.

You already speak the language, so you start ahead. Steady practice does the rest.
