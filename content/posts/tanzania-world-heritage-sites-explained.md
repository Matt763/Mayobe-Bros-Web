Tanzania has seven places on UNESCO's World Heritage List. The list names sites that UNESCO's member countries agree have value for all people, not only for the country where they stand. Many of us know the names (Kilimanjaro, Serengeti, Stone Town) without knowing what the listing means. This post explains the seven sites, what each one protects, and what UNESCO has said about their condition.

Everything here was checked in November 2026 on UNESCO's own World Heritage pages. The status of a site can change at the yearly meeting of the World Heritage Committee, so check UNESCO's site for the latest.

## The short answer

- UNESCO's page for Tanzania lists seven properties: three natural, three cultural and one mixed.
- The natural sites are Kilimanjaro National Park (1987), Selous Game Reserve (1982) and Serengeti National Park (1981).
- The cultural sites are Kondoa Rock-Art Sites (2006), the Ruins of Kilwa Kisiwani and Songo Mnara (1981) and the Stone Town of Zanzibar (2000).
- Ngorongoro Conservation Area (1979) is the mixed site, listed for both nature and culture.
- Selous Game Reserve has been on UNESCO's List of World Heritage in Danger since 2014.
- Being on the list is not the same as being a national park. It is an agreement to protect the place, and UNESCO checks on it.

## What "World Heritage" means

A country first puts a place on its "tentative list". It then sends a full proposal, and the World Heritage Committee decides. A listed place must have "outstanding universal value". UNESCO writes a short text for each site to explain this.

Listing brings attention and some support, but the country still runs the place. UNESCO asks for "state of conservation" reports and can place a site on the danger list when it thinks the site faces serious threats. Tanzania's page shows reports for Ngorongoro, Selous, Serengeti and Stone Town in 2026.

## The natural sites

### Serengeti National Park

Listed in 1981, the Serengeti covers about 1.48 million hectares. UNESCO says its plains hold the largest remaining unaltered animal migration in the world. More than a million wildebeest, plus hundreds of thousands of gazelles and zebras, travel a circular route of about 1,000 kilometres that crosses into Kenya and back. UNESCO also notes some of the highest concentrations of large predators in the world.

The migration depends on rain and grass. It does not follow a fixed calendar, so ask a licensed operator rather than trusting a date on a website.

### Kilimanjaro National Park

Listed in 1987, the park covers about 75,575 hectares. UNESCO describes Kilimanjaro as the largest free-standing volcanic mass in the world and the highest mountain in Africa, at 5,895 metres. It has three volcanic peaks, Kibo, Mawenzi and Shira, and five vegetation zones from the lower slopes to the summit.

### Selous Game Reserve

Listed in 1982, Selous covers about 5.12 million hectares in the south of the country. UNESCO describes it as among the largest protected areas in Africa and relatively undisturbed by people. UNESCO's text names many elephants, black rhinoceros, hippos and buffalo.

UNESCO shows Selous on the danger list from 2014 to the present. A UNESCO news item from June 2014 links the listing to poaching, and later items from 2018 and 2019 express concern about a planned dam and its environmental assessment. We found no UNESCO page showing that Selous has left the danger list.

## The mixed site

### Ngorongoro Conservation Area

Listed in 1979, with boundary changes in 2010, Ngorongoro covers about 809,440 hectares. UNESCO says it was set up in 1959 as a multiple land use area, where wildlife and semi-nomadic Maasai herders share the land. It includes the Ngorongoro Crater, described as the world's largest caldera, and Olduvai Gorge. UNESCO notes evidence of human evolution reaching back almost four million years, including fossil footprints at Laetoli.

Ngorongoro is often in the news. In September 2026 the authority (NCAA) said ten lions were found dead in the Ndutu area on 18 September, with preliminary findings pointing to poison and an investigation under way (CBS News). In November 2026 a news site reported that four NCAA staff trained at a private reserve in South Africa on rhino conservation. Ngorongoro is also the centre of a long public debate about people living inside the area. We leave that debate out here, because it needs more space and the official positions are still moving. Read UNESCO's state of conservation page for the formal record.

## The cultural sites

### Ruins of Kilwa Kisiwani and Songo Mnara

Listed in 1981, these ruins lie on two islands off the southern coast, about 300 kilometres south of Dar es Salaam. UNESCO says Kilwa Kisiwani was lived in from the 9th to the 19th century, and reached its peak in the 13th and 14th centuries. It grew rich from Indian Ocean trade in gold and ivory. UNESCO says the traveller Ibn Battuta stopped there in 1331 to 1332. The Great Mosque dates to the 11th century. The site was on the danger list from 2004 to 2014.

For the story of the city, read our post on [Kilwa Kisiwani, the Swahili trade city](/post/world-histories/kilwa-kisiwani-swahili-trade-city/).

### Stone Town of Zanzibar

Listed in 2000, Stone Town covers about 96 hectares on the west side of Unguja island. UNESCO calls it an outstanding example of a Swahili trading town, with a mix of African, Arab, Indian and European influences. The houses are built of coral stone and lime, and many have carved wooden doors. Landmarks named by UNESCO include the Old Fort, the House of Wonders and Christ Church Cathedral, which UNESCO says stands on the site of the last slave market. You can read more in our post on [how the Zanzibar slave trade ended](/post/world-histories/zanzibar-slave-trade-and-how-it-ended/).

### Kondoa Rock-Art Sites

Listed in 2006 in the Dodoma Region, Kondoa has natural rock shelters on the slopes of the Masai escarpment, with paintings made over at least two thousand years. UNESCO says between 150 and 450 decorated shelters are estimated, though the exact number is not known. Some shelters are still used by nearby communities for rituals, which UNESCO says keeps a living tradition connected to the paintings.

## What is next for the list

UNESCO's page shows seven places on Tanzania's tentative list, the first step towards a new listing. They are Oldonyo Murwak, Gombe National Park, Jozani and Chwaka Bay, the Eastern Arc Mountains Forests, the Central Slave and Ivory Trade Route, Tendaguru Paleontological Site, and a shared rock art entry with Kenya and Uganda. Being on that list does not mean a listing will follow.

## Common questions

### Is Mount Kilimanjaro a World Heritage Site?

Yes. UNESCO lists Kilimanjaro National Park, inscribed in 1987.

### Which Tanzanian site is on the danger list?

On UNESCO's page, Selous Game Reserve shows as on the danger list from 2014 to the present. Kilwa was on it from 2004 to 2014, and Ngorongoro from 1984 to 1989, but both have since been removed.

### Does the listing give me free entry?

No. Entry fees and rules are set by the park or site authority, such as TANAPA or NCAA. Ask them for the current fee for citizens and residents.

### Can a site lose its status?

UNESCO can remove a site from the list, but this is rare. Usually the Committee asks for reports and changes to protect the place.

## What to do next

1. Read UNESCO's own page for any site you plan to visit. It is free and written in plain language.
2. Check TANAPA, NCAA or the Zanzibar tourism office for current fees and rules before you travel.
3. If you visit Kilwa, Stone Town or Kondoa, use a licensed local guide and follow the rules about not touching painted or old walls.
4. Teachers can use this post as a short lesson on the sites. For the history, read our posts on Kilwa and Zanzibar.
