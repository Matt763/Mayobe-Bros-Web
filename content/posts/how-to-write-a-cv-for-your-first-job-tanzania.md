Writing your first CV can feel strange. You may think you have nothing to put on it. You have more than you think, and this post shows where to find it and how to set it out clearly.

## The short answer

A CV (curriculum vitae) is a short document that tells an employer who you are, what you have studied, what you can do and how to reach you. For a first job, keep it to one or two pages, use clear headings, and fit it to the job advert.

- Put your contact details at the top, then a short profile, education, skills, experience and referees.
- Use school, volunteering, projects and community work as proof of what you can do.
- Say only what is true. You may be asked to prove it.
- Ask your referees before you list them.
- Check the advert. Some Tanzanian employers ask for things a general CV guide does not mention.

## What a CV is and what it is for

A CV does one job: it helps a stranger decide in a minute or two whether to invite you for an interview. It does not need to tell your life story.

The advice in this post comes from university careers services in the UK and the US. We say so plainly: it is general advice, and Tanzanian employers differ. We did not find one official Tanzanian CV standard. Online blogs disagree, for example on whether a CV should be one page or two. When an advert gives instructions, follow the advert first. Use the general advice to fill the gaps.

One example is public service jobs. The vacancy announcement we read in November 2026 asked for three reputable referees with reliable contacts. It also said Form IV and Form VI result slips and testimonials should not be attached, and that a recent passport-size photo must be uploaded to the recruitment portal. Rules like these can change from advert to advert. Our guide to [applying through the Ajira Portal](/post/jobs-and-internships/ajira-portal-how-to-apply-public-service-jobs/) explains more.

## The sections to include, in order

The UK careers service Prospects suggests a simple order: contact details, a short personal statement, work experience, education, skills and achievements. If you have little work experience, put education first and make it strong. Use the same font and the same heading style all the way through.

1. **Name and contact details.** Your full name, phone number, email address and town (for example, Mwanza or Dodoma). Use a plain email address such as your name, not a joke name. Make sure your voicemail and your phone are working.
2. **Profile.** Two or three sentences. Say what you studied, what kind of work you want and one real strength. For example: "Form Six leaver with Physics, Chemistry and Biology, looking for a laboratory assistant post. Careful with records and used to working in a team."
3. **Education.** List your most recent level first: school or college, years, the award, and your combination or course. Add results if they help you.
4. **Skills.** Pick skills that match the job. Computer use, a language you speak, driving, typing, bookkeeping, basic first aid. Only list what you can do today.
5. **Experience.** Paid work, but also volunteering, internships and projects (see below).
6. **Referees.** Two or three people, with name, role, phone and email.

Prospects also says to match your CV to the advert and use the words it uses where they are true for you. If an advert asks for "customer service" and you handled customers in your family shop, write that.

## How to write about experience when you have none

You do not have none. Prospects makes the point that school projects, volunteering, hobbies and personal projects all show skills employers want, such as communication, organisation, problem-solving and teamwork.

Think about these questions and write down the answers:

- Have you helped run a family shop, a stall or a farm?
- Have you led a class group, a club or a youth group at church or the mosque?
- Have you volunteered at a school, clinic, sports event or community clean-up?
- Did you do a final-year project, field practice or an attachment?
- Have you taught younger children or friends?
- Have you built a skill on your own, such as repairing phones, sewing or using a spreadsheet?

Then turn each answer into one short line that says what you did and what happened.

- Weak: "Helped in shop."
- Better: "Served customers and kept daily sales records in a family shop during school holidays."

Add a number only if it is true and you can explain it, such as "kept records for about 30 customers a day." Never invent figures to look better.

Prospects also suggests volunteering and personal projects as ways to build experience and to meet people who can later act as referees. That is useful if your CV is thin today. Choose one small activity you can do over the next month and add it to your CV when it is done.

## What to leave out

Prospects lists several things to remove. Some of it depends on the country, so use your judgement and the advert.

- **A long list of hobbies.** Keep only those that show a skill the job needs.
- **Anything you cannot back up.** Prospects warns that lying on a CV can even be a criminal offence. Public adverts in Tanzania also warn that forged certificates lead to legal action.
- **Details that are not needed.** The Prospects guide says to leave out date of birth and a photo, which is the UK custom. In Tanzania, some adverts ask for a passport photo or for school results. If the advert asks, include it. If it does not, you can leave it out.
- **The line "References available on request".** Just list your referees, or say nothing if the advert does not ask.
- **Long paragraphs.** Short bullet points are easier to read on a phone.
- **Spelling errors.** One mistake can make an employer doubt your care.

## Referees: who to ask and how

A referee is a person who agrees to tell an employer what you are like. The Purdue University writing lab gives clear advice: choose people who know your work and can describe your strengths, and always ask before you list them.

Good choices can be a teacher or lecturer, a head of department, a supervisor from an attachment, or a community leader who has seen you work. A close friend or a relative is a weaker choice, because an employer will not think they are neutral.

Here is how to ask:

1. Contact the person politely, in person or by message.
2. Say which job you are applying for and why you think they can speak for you.
3. Send them your CV so their answers match it.
4. Confirm their best phone number and email.
5. Thank them, and tell them if you get the job.

Public service adverts have asked for three referees, so it helps to have three people ready before you need them.

## Check, save and send it

Before you send your CV:

- Read it slowly, then read it aloud. Prospects suggests changing the font or reading from the bottom up to spot mistakes.
- Ask one person you trust to read it for spelling and clarity.
- Check your phone number and email one more time.
- Save it as a PDF so the layout does not change on another phone or computer. Name it clearly, for example `Your-Name-CV.pdf`.
- Keep an editable version so you can change it for each job.

Never pay anyone to "fix" your CV in exchange for a promise of a job. Read about [fake job offers](/post/jobs-and-internships/fake-job-offers-tanzania-warning-signs-never-pay/) if someone asks you for money.

## Common questions

### How long should my first CV be?

Prospects says to aim for about two pages and to keep one page for student or part-time roles. For a first job, one page is often enough if it is clear.

### Should I put a photo on my CV?

UK advice says no, but some Tanzanian adverts ask for a passport photo. Follow the advert.

### What if my results are not good?

Do not hide or change them. Focus on what you can do, add skills and projects, and keep learning. Honest records last longer than clever ones.

### Do I need a different CV for every job?

You need a base CV and small changes for each job. Move the most relevant points to the top, and use words from the advert where they are true.

## What to do next

1. Write down your education, skills and any experience, using the questions above.
2. Draft the CV on one page and ask someone you trust to read it.
3. Ask three referees for permission and write down their contacts.
4. Save it as a PDF, then read the next advert you want and fit your CV to it.

Everyone starts with a first CV. Yours will look better after each job you apply for, so begin now.
