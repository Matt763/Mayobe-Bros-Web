Accountancy is the subject for students who like numbers that must balance. In Tanzania it is a principal subject in business combinations, and it feeds degrees in accounting, banking, tax and business. This guide is for Form Four leavers choosing a combination, Form Five and Six students, and parents.

We read the TIE Accountancy syllabus and NECTA's exam format in November 2026. Where something could not be confirmed, we say so.

## Key points

- NECTA's subject name is Accountancy, code 153. Commerce is a different subject, code 152, in NECTA's older format.
- In the 2023 TIE curriculum the business subjects are Economics, Business Studies and Accountancy. Commerce does not appear in that subject list.
- The 2023 TIE syllabus runs for ten periods of 40 minutes a week and covers theory, records, financial statements, cost topics, ethics and accounting software.
- NECTA's latest format that we could open has two papers of three hours, each with short questions and long problems. The calculator rule is stated in it.
- Students who take Accountancy and do not take Mathematics must also take Basic Applied Mathematics, unless they passed Additional Mathematics in Form Four.

## Accountancy, Commerce, Book-keeping: the names

Older books and school talk use three names, so here is how they fit.

- **Accountancy** is the A-Level subject. NECTA's ACSEE page lists the ECA combination as Economics, Commerce and Accountancy.
- **Commerce** is about trade, banking, insurance and transport. NECTA's older format has a separate paper pair for it.
- **Book-keeping** is the Form Four subject that teaches the first record steps. TCU degree lines often ask for it at O-Level.

The 2023 curriculum renames the business stream. Its combination list has EBuAc (Economics, Business Studies, Accountancy), BuAcM (Business Studies, Accountancy, Mathematics) and BuAcCs (Business Studies, Accountancy, Computer Science). The TAMISEMI Form Five list for 2026 shows "EBuAc(ECA)" as a combination name, so both labels are in use right now.

The curriculum also says that students in a combination marked with two stars must take Basic Applied Mathematics, or have passed Additional Mathematics. EBuAc and BuAcCs are marked this way.

## What the 2023 syllabus asks you to do

TIE's Accountancy syllabus gives four main competences:

1. Understand the concepts, theories and principles of accounting, and make an accounting plan.
2. Prepare accounting reports: keep records, adjust them for financial statements, and use the principles.
3. Show a good grasp of professional ethics in accounting.
4. Use ICT to analyse financial statements.

Learning is meant to be active. The syllabus suggests problem solving, field trips to a nearby office, guest speakers, case studies and skills labs.

## Form Five topics

In Form Five you meet the large pieces of the subject. From the syllabus tables:

| Area | What you do |
|---|---|
| Conceptual framework | The aims of general-purpose financial statements, who uses them, and what makes information useful |
| Inventories (stock) | Types, valuation methods such as FIFO, LIFO and average cost, estimating stock, insurance claims |
| Payroll | Forms and methods of paying workers, gross pay and deductions |
| Investments | Meaning, types and terms; shares and the stock exchange |
| Branch accounting | Businesses with branches, in the books of the head office and the branch |
| Royalties | Meaning, types and the books of the lessor and the lessee |
| Non-current assets | Nature, valuation and depreciation |
| Hire purchase | Meaning and records for buyer and seller |
| Correcting errors | Finding and correcting different types of error, and how they affect the trial balance |

The Form Five tables give the largest share of time to preparing and keeping records, and a smaller share to the concepts and the action plan. The syllabus also asks you to compare how different stock methods change reported profit.

## Form Six topics

Form Six moves to the business as a whole:

- Partnerships that end or merge, and the records for dissolution.
- Company records and how a company gets its capital.
- Cost and management accounting: classes of cost, cost behaviour, cost-volume-profit analysis and budgets such as sales, production, purchases and a master budget.
- Reading financial statements with ratios of profit, liquidity and efficiency.
- The professional code of ethics for accountants: honesty, fairness, skill, confidentiality and professional behaviour.
- Using accounting software to analyse statements.

## How NECTA tests Accountancy

The newest ACSEE format we could open is the 2019 booklet, built on the 2009 syllabus. We could not find a newer one on NECTA's site as of 24 November 2026. In that booklet:

- There are two papers, 153/1 and 153/2, each three hours and 100 marks.
- Section A has four short-answer questions of 10 marks, all compulsory.
- Section B has four structured problem questions of 20 marks; you answer three.
- Candidates must show every step of the working, and non-programmable calculators are allowed.

The old topic list in that booklet differs from the 2023 syllabus, so ask your teacher which one your exam will follow. We could not confirm from NECTA which Form Six year first follows the 2023 syllabi.

The 2023 curriculum splits marks as 30% continuous assessment and 70% national exam in Form Six.

## Which combinations include it

On NECTA's page, ECA (Economics, Commerce, Accountancy) is the best known. The 2023 curriculum lists EBuAc, BuAcM and BuAcCs among others. Read our [ECA combination guide](/post/educational/study-tips/jobs-for-eca-combination/) for degrees and entry rules from the TCU 2026/27 guidebook.

## Careers it can open

Accountancy points to degrees in accounting, banking and finance, tax, procurement, business and economics. Jobs in firms, banks, schools, shops and public offices keep accounts. These are directions, not promises. Our ECA guide covers the professional-exam route. We do not repeat professional rules here, because they change, so check the official body before you plan. ## Study advice that fits Accountancy

- Practise with a pencil, every week. Accounting is learned by doing problems, not by reading about them.
- Learn the layout of each statement and account until you can write it from memory, then focus on the numbers.
- Check your work: does the trial balance agree, do totals carry over? Most lost marks come from small slips.
- Write down the working. Marks are given for steps even when the final figure is wrong.
- Keep a notebook of formulas and rules (depreciation, stock methods, ratios) and revisit it weekly.
- Work through the problems at the end of each chapter in a TIE textbook before moving on.

## Common questions

### Is Accountancy hard?

It is steady, not mysterious. It rewards neat habits and practice. Students who skip problems find it hard.

### Do I need Mathematics?

You need comfortable arithmetic and algebra. The curriculum asks students in some business combinations to take Basic Applied Mathematics or have Additional Mathematics.

### Is Commerce part of the 2023 syllabus list?

The 2023 subject list names Economics, Business Studies and Accountancy. We found Commerce only in NECTA's older format and in combination names such as ECA.

### Can I become an accountant with Accountancy only at A-Level?

Accounting careers usually need more study, such as a degree or professional exams. Check the official bodies for current rules.

## What to do next

1. Ask your school which combination name it uses and which Mathematics support it offers.
2. Read the Accountancy syllabus from TIE and tick the topics you have met in Book-keeping.
3. Start a weekly set of problems and time yourself.

Careful work builds trust, in an exam and in a job.
