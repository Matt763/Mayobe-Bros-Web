You worked hard for your diploma, and maybe you have wondered whether it can still lead to a degree. For many people in Tanzania it can. There is an official route for it, and this post shows you the grades it asks for.

All figures below are as checked in November 2026, in the TCU guidebook for the 2026/27 admission year. Requirements change from year to year, so always read the current edition.

## The short answer

Yes, you can apply for a bachelor's degree with an Ordinary Diploma (NTA Level 6). The Tanzania Commission for Universities (TCU) calls this "equivalent entry". The usual minimum is a Grade Point Average (GPA) of 3.0, plus enough O-Level passes.

- At least four O-Level passes, each grade D or better.
- A GPA of at least 3.0 for an Ordinary Diploma (NTA Level 6).
- Diploma in Teacher Education: an average of B.
- Health-related courses: an average of B or a GPA of 3.0, and at least five O-Level passes in non-religious subjects.
- Nutrition and dietetics degrees: an average of B+ or a GPA of 3.5, and five O-Level passes.

Meeting the minimum does not promise a place. Each programme can ask for more, and places are limited.

## What does "equivalent entry" mean?

TCU describes two kinds of entry to a degree. Direct entry is for people with Form Six or a foreign secondary certificate. Equivalent entry is for people who come through another recognised qualification, such as an Ordinary Diploma or the Foundation Certificate of the Open University of Tanzania.

So your diploma is not a second-class ticket. It is a named route in the official guidebook, with its own book. If you want to know where a diploma sits in the national ladder, read our post on [NTA levels explained](/post/educational/career-development/nta-levels-explained-tanzania/).

## What are the minimum requirements for an Ordinary Diploma?

The guidebook for holders of an Ordinary Diploma has a table of general minimum entry requirements. For an Ordinary Diploma, FTC and equivalent qualifications, it asks for two things together.

**First, O-Level.** At least four passes (grade D and above) at O-Level, or NVA Level III with fewer than four O-Level passes, or equivalent foreign qualifications as established by NECTA or NACTVET.

**Second, your diploma result.** The table lists these by type of award:

| Your award | Minimum in the guidebook |
| --- | --- |
| Ordinary Diploma (NTA Level 6) | GPA of at least 3.0 |
| Full Technician Certificate (FTC) | Average of C (where A=5, B=4, C=3, D=2) |
| Diploma in Teacher Education | Average of B |
| Health-related awards such as Clinical Medicine | Average of B |
| Unclassified diplomas and certificates | A Distinction |
| Classified non-NTA diplomas | Upper Second Class |

The guidebook also says that applicants who do not have at least 3.0 in the diploma or the Foundation Programme should not apply, because they do not meet the minimum.

Say your diploma GPA is 3.4 and you have five O-Level passes at D or better. That example is above both minimums in the general table. Say your GPA is 2.8. The guidebook advises you not to apply for a degree through this route.

## What are the higher bars for health programmes?

Health courses have their own table, and it asks for more.

- **All health-related programmes:** an appropriate Diploma or Advanced Diploma with an average of B or a GPA of at least 3.0, and at least five passes in non-religious subjects at O-Level.
- **Clinical Nutrition and Dietetics, and Food, Nutrition and Dietetics:** a Diploma in Clinical Medicine, Agriculture, Nutrition, Environmental Health, Nursing or Midwifery with an average of B+ or a GPA of at least 3.5, plus five O-Level passes in non-religious subjects.

The word "appropriate" matters. Your diploma subject should fit the degree you want. Each institution's own page lists exactly which diplomas it accepts for each course.

## How do I find my programme in the guidebook?

The guidebook is long, about 570 pages in the edition we read. Most of it is a list of institutions and their programmes. Here is a simple way to work.

1. Open the guidebook for diploma holders, not the one for Form Six leavers. They are two different books.
2. Read the general tables first (Table 1 for general courses, Table 2 for health).
3. Use the search tool in your PDF reader. Type the name of the institution, then the programme.
4. Read the whole row. Programmes can list the diplomas they accept, a minimum GPA or average, and sometimes a grade in a particular O-Level subject. One programme in the guidebook, for example, also asks for a "C" in Mathematics at O-Level.
5. Note the programme's capacity, which is the number of places. Fewer places mean more competition.

Our post on [how to read the TCU admission guidebook](/post/educational/study-tips/how-to-read-tcu-admission-guidebook/) goes deeper into the tables.

## What should I do if my GPA is just below 3.0?

This is a hard moment, and it is fine to feel disappointed. The guidebook is clear that applicants below the minimum are advised not to apply this way, so the honest answer is that this route is closed for now.

You still have options. Our post on [what to do if you are not selected for university](/post/educational/study-tips/not-selected-for-university-what-to-do-next/) lists some. You can also ask your college whether a higher diploma or other course is open to you. Check that anything you pay for is at a registered institution first.

## How do I apply?

TCU's undergraduate admissions page says you apply directly to the institution you choose. Each institution runs its own admission and announces its own successful applicants. The guidebook adds three useful rules:

- Make sure the names and index numbers in your application match those on your exam records.
- Questions about your application go to the institution you applied to.
- If you are admitted to more than one institution, you must confirm the one you prefer.

## Common questions

### Is a GPA of 3.0 enough to be admitted?

It is the minimum to be eligible. It is not a guarantee. The programme may ask for more, and the number of applicants can be higher than the number of places.

### What if I have fewer than four O-Level passes?

The table gives one other option: NVA Level III for those with fewer than four O-Level passes. Equivalent foreign qualifications are also listed. Ask your institution or NACTVET which applies to you.

### Do these numbers stay the same every year?

Not always. That is why we say "as checked in November 2026". Read the guidebook for the year you want to apply.

### Can I go from diploma to degree without Form Six?

The guidebook lists the diploma as its own route. Whether your own file qualifies depends on your O-Level passes and your diploma result, so check the table against your certificates.

## What to do next

1. Find your diploma result (GPA or average) and your O-Level results, and put them side by side with Table 1 or Table 2.
2. Download the current guidebook for diploma holders from the TCU website.
3. Make a short list of programmes and read each row in full.
4. Check each institution's website for its application dates and portal.

You already finished one hard step. Taking the next one carefully is a good way to keep going.
