You have clips on your phone: a wedding, a shop opening, a school event, or a short video to promote your business. Now you want to cut them together. Many people pick the editor that looks easiest and only later learn what "free" really allowed. This post helps you choose with your eyes open.

We compare three free choices, DaVinci Resolve, CapCut and Shotcut, using each maker's own pages. Everything below is as checked in November 2026. We do not list prices or version numbers, because they change. Always read the maker's page before you decide.

## The short answer

All three can be used without paying, but "free" means different things.

- **Shotcut** describes itself as a free, open source, cross-platform video editor.
- **DaVinci Resolve** has a free version, and a paid Studio version with extra tools.
- **CapCut** has free features and paid ones, and its terms have rules about music, templates and commercial use.
- If you will post only for fun, any of them can work.
- If you will earn money from the video, read the licence for every music track, effect and template first.

## What can "free" mean?

There are three common meanings.

**Open source.** Anyone can see and share the program's code. It is usually free to use. Shotcut says it is open source. Its page does not give more detail on the exact licence, so we say only that.

**A free version of a paid product.** The maker gives you a version with fewer tools and hopes some people will upgrade. DaVinci Resolve is like this.

**Free with paid parts.** You can start without paying, but some features, effects or sounds need a subscription. CapCut's terms say some features are free and others need payment.

None of these means every file you can click on is free to use in your work. The editor and the music inside it are separate questions.

## DaVinci Resolve: what the free version offers

Blackmagic Design's page says DaVinci Resolve is available for Mac, Windows and Linux. About the free version, the page says it works with virtually all 8-bit video formats at up to 60 frames per second, and handles resolutions as high as Ultra HD (3840 x 2160). It also says the free version includes multi-user collaboration and HDR grading.

Studio is the paid upgrade. The page says it includes everything in the free version plus the DaVinci AI Neural Engine and extra tools such as more effects and noise reduction. It also lists 10-bit video up to 120 frames per second and resolutions beyond 4K.

What does that mean for you? Most phone videos are fine at these limits. Phones often record at 30 or 60 frames per second. If your camera records in a more advanced format than the free version supports, check Blackmagic's page before you start.

Resolve is a professional tool, so expect more to learn than in a phone app. We did not find its computer requirements on the page we opened. It links to a separate "Tech Specs" page, so check that page before you download, especially on an older computer.

## CapCut: what the terms say about your videos, music and templates

CapCut is popular because it is quick. Its legal terms are worth reading, so here is what two of its pages say. The Terms of Service were last updated on 15 April 2026. The Materials License Agreement was last updated on 22 January 2026. Terms can change, so open them yourself.

**Your videos.** The terms say the company does not own your content. But you give it a non-exclusive, royalty-free, transferable, sub-licensable, perpetual and worldwide licence to use your content. That is a wide permission. Read it before you upload anything private or anything for a client.

**Free and paid.** The terms say some features are free and others need payment. Subscriptions renew automatically unless you cancel, and after you cancel you are moved to the free services.

**Age.** The general rule is 13 years or older. People under 18 need a parent's or guardian's consent. Some countries set different ages.

**Music.** The terms say no rights are licensed for sound recordings and musical works made available through the service. If you upload your own music, you must own it or have the rights.

**Templates and effects.** The Materials License says whether you can use a template or effect commercially depends on a mark. Items marked "Dual Use" can be used in certain commercial ways, such as promotional material for goods or services on e-commerce platforms. Items without that mark are for personal and non-commercial use. If a video mixes both kinds, you can use it only for personal and non-commercial purposes.

**Sounds.** The standard sound library is licensed for personal, non-commercial use. Using a sound to promote a brand or business needs separate rights. "Commercial Sounds" are offered separately to businesses, for use on the platforms named in the agreement.

The terms we read were in English. CapCut's features and rules may differ by country and app version, so we do not claim anything about watermarks. Check what the app shows you.

## Shotcut: simple and open

Shotcut's own page says it supports resolutions up to 4K and hundreds of audio and video formats and codecs. It runs on Windows, Mac and Linux. It does not mention paid tiers on the page we read.

That makes it a simple choice if you want a desktop editor with no account story to worry about. It is a desktop program, so you install it on a computer. It does not remove the music question. Shotcut cannot give you rights to a song that you do not own.

## How do I choose?

| Your situation | Worth a look |
| --- | --- |
| Quick clips for family and friends | CapCut, with its terms in mind |
| You want an open source desktop editor | Shotcut |
| You want professional colour and audio tools and have a capable computer | DaVinci Resolve (free) |
| Videos for a paying client or your shop's adverts | Any editor, but only with music and effects you have rights to |

Ask three questions. What computer or phone do I have? Will money be involved? Where will I post? A video for your shop's WhatsApp status is different from one for a client's ad.

## Common questions

### Is it legal to use a popular song in my edited video?

Not automatically. Owning a copy of a song does not give you the right to use it in a video you share. Use music you made, or music whose licence clearly allows your use. The maker's terms, as with CapCut's, may restrict commercial use.

### Can I sell videos I edit with a free editor?

The editor's licence is only part of the answer. Read its terms, and check every effect, template and sound inside the video. If a client pays you, you must have the rights to everything in it.

### Should I download a cracked version of paid software?

No. Cracked software is illegal to use, and it is a common way for viruses to reach your computer. The free versions above are the safe route.

### Which one is easiest?

That depends on you. Try a short clip in two editors and see which feels clear. Opinions differ, and we have not ranked them.

## What to do next

1. Write down your goal: for fun, for your shop, or for a client.
2. Open each maker's page, read the terms that matter, and check the date at the top.
3. Make a one-minute test video with your own clips and your own music, or music you have clear rights to.
4. If your videos will help a shop, our post on a [one-page marketing plan](/post/business-ideas/marketing/one-page-marketing-plan-small-business/) can help you decide what to say.

Your first edit will not be perfect. Every editor you admire began with a rough first cut, and you can learn this one clip at a time.
