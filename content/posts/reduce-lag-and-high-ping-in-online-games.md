You are one shot from winning, and the screen freezes. A moment later you are out. That feeling is hard to forget, and it is usually lag. The good news is that you can fix many causes yourself, for free.

This post explains the three words you will see in game menus and help pages, then gives you checks in a sensible order.

## The short answer

Lag means the game receives your moves, or sends back the result, too late. The most common causes are a weak Wi-Fi signal, other apps using the connection, and a busy network. Fix the things you control first, and then check whether the game server or your provider is the problem.

- Restart your phone or computer, and the router.
- Move closer to the router, or use a cable on a PC.
- Stop downloads, streaming and other heavy apps while you play.
- Do not use a VPN to play unless the game's own support says it is fine.
- If everything else is fine, the problem may be the game server or your provider.

## What are ping, packet loss and jitter?

Riot Games, the maker of League of Legends, explains these words on its support page. League is a PC game, and we use the page only for the definitions, which apply to any online game. We do not recommend any game here, so check the age rating of whatever you play.

- **Latency, or ping.** The time data takes to travel from your device to the game's servers and back, counted in milliseconds. Lower is better. When your connection works normally, your physical distance from the servers is the biggest factor.
- **Packet loss.** Some data never arrives. The missing data must be sent again, and the wait causes lag. Riot says it should be as close to 0% as possible.
- **Jitter.** A change in latency over time. A game expects a steady flow of data, and when pieces arrive late or out of order, you see lag. Riot says it should be as close to 0 ms as possible.

We do not give a "good ping" number here. The right level depends on the game, and we could not confirm one from an official page. As a simple rule, a steady connection matters as much as a low one.

## What quick checks fix many lag problems?

Work down this list in order. Test the game after each step so that you learn what helped.

1. **Restart.** Restart your phone or computer, then the router. It is a small step that clears many small faults.
2. **Close other apps.** Close apps and browser tabs you do not need. Microsoft's help page says to close programs you do not need when a connection feels slow, because they compete for memory and processing power.
3. **Move closer to the router.** Microsoft says distance and obstacles such as walls weaken a Wi-Fi signal. Sit in the same room as the router if you can, with fewer walls in between.
4. **Look for interference.** Microsoft names 2.4 GHz cordless phones as a source of Wi-Fi interference. Keep such devices away from the router.
5. **Run a security scan.** Spyware and viruses can use your connection. Keep your antivirus running and scan the device.
6. **Update the game and the device.** Install updates when you are on Wi-Fi, and before you start a match, not in the middle of one.

## Wi-Fi, mobile data or cable: which is steadier?

Riot recommends a wired (Ethernet) connection when you can use one. It says wireless connections are hard to troubleshoot, because the environment and other radio signals can cause lag spikes and packet loss. On a laptop or a desktop PC, a cable from the router is the first thing to try if lag continues.

Most readers play on a phone, where a cable is not an option. For phones, here is general advice. It is not from an official page, so treat it as common sense and test it yourself:

- Wi-Fi in the same room as the router is often steadier than a weak signal from the next room.
- Mobile data depends on your signal. If the bars drop, move to a spot with a better signal, such as near a window.
- If one connection lags, try the other for a few minutes and compare.
- Playing at a quiet time of day can help when many people share the same network.

Mobile data also costs money, and online games use it all the time. Our post on [saving mobile data while playing games](/post/video-games-updates/save-mobile-data-while-playing-games/) shows how to set a warning and a limit.

## What eats your connection in the background?

Your game shares the connection with everything else on it. Riot lists common causes of raised buffering: Discord, Spotify, Hamachi, anything that streams HD video such as YouTube or Twitch, torrent programs, and active downloads such as Steam.

At home, the others in the house count too. If someone is watching video or downloading a large file on the same Wi-Fi, your game can suffer. Agree on times, for example: downloads and updates at night, matches in the evening when others are not streaming.

On a phone, check for updates running in the Play Store, cloud backups and video calls. Pausing them during a match costs nothing.

## Should I use a VPN or a game booster?

We do not recommend VPN "game boosters" or paid tools that promise lower ping. Riot's page says plainly not to use a VPN or proxy to connect to League of Legends, because they can cause connection problems that are impossible to fix. It says a VPN should be used only to test the routing path in specific cases, when its technical staff advise it. Other games may have other rules, so read the support page of your own game.

## When is the problem not on your side?

Sometimes you did nothing wrong. Riot says there are three possible sources of latency, packet loss and jitter: something at the game company's end (such as server problems or an outage), your internet service provider, or something in your own equipment and settings.

You can tell them apart with a few questions.

- **Is it only one game?** Try another online game or a video. If only one game lags, check the game's status page or social media for outage news.
- **Is it every app?** Then it is probably your network or provider. Restart the router, and if it still fails, contact your provider.
- **Is it only at certain times?** Busy hours can slow a shared network. Microsoft lists local congestion as an outside cause, with no fix on your side, other than trying at another time.

For a complaint to your provider, note the times, the device and what happened. It makes the call shorter and more useful.

## Common questions

### Why is my ping high even with fast internet?

Speed and ping are different things. Riot says that on a normal connection, distance to the game servers is the largest factor in latency. A fast line far from the server can still show a high ping.

### Is Wi-Fi or a cable better for gaming?

Riot recommends a cable where possible, because wireless can add lag spikes and packet loss. If you only have Wi-Fi, sit close to the router.

### Can a virus cause lag?

Microsoft says spyware and some viruses can slow your connection. Keep your security software on and scan if you are unsure.

### Are online games safe for children?

Age ratings and chat features differ between games. Check the rating and the chat settings first. Our post on [game age ratings and parental controls](/post/video-games-updates/game-age-ratings-esrb-pegi-and-parental-controls/) explains how.

## What to do next

1. Restart your device and router, then move closer to the router and test again.
2. Close downloads, streaming and extra apps before you start a match.
3. On a PC or laptop, try a cable, and compare.
4. If every app is slow, tell your provider the times it happens. If only one game is slow, check its status page.

Lag can ruin a good match, but a few calm checks will fix more than you expect.
