Starting a business is a big step, and forms and government offices can make it feel bigger. Take it one agency at a time. This post shows the order for a small business with one owner, what to prepare, and where people lose time. It is general information, not legal or tax advice. Every fee and step below is as checked in November 2026 on official pages, and the pages can change.

## The short answer

For a one-owner business the usual order is: business name, then TIN, then tax clearance, then the business licence. Each step needs papers from the one before it.

- BRELA registers your business name online. This step is optional for a sole owner, but it gives your business a recognised name.
- TRA gives you a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN). TRA says to apply within 15 days of starting the business.
- After TRA assesses you and you meet your tax duties, you get a tax clearance.
- With the tax clearance you ask for your business licence. Small local businesses are usually licensed by the council trade office.
- You never need to pay a middleman. Pay only through the official system and keep every receipt.

## What to prepare before you start

Gather these first, so you do not stop halfway through a form.

- Your National Identification Number (NIN) from NIDA.
- A phone number registered in your own name, and your own active email address. BRELA asks for both.
- The address of the business place and your home address.
- A lease agreement or a title deed for the business place.
- A letter from your Local Government Authority (TRA asks for it with the TIN application).
- A name for the business that is acceptable by law. Pick two or three choices in case your first one is taken.

If you still have not decided what to sell, read our post on [low-cost online business ideas in Tanzania](/post/business-ideas/e-commerce/low-cost-online-business-ideas-tanzania/). It also has a short answer on whether you need to register.

## Step 1: Register your business name with BRELA

BRELA is the Business Registrations and Licensing Agency. Its page explains that a business name can belong to one person or to a group of up to 20 people. Examples on the page are names like "Rose Beauty Salon".

The BRELA page says the applicant must be Tanzanian, at least 18 years old, and have a NIN, a phone in their own name and an active email. The online system is called ORS. The steps on the page are, in short:

1. Go to brela.go.tz or ors.brela.go.tz.
2. Log in to your ORS account, or create one.
3. Choose the online service, then the business name service.
4. Choose the type of service and the type of business name.
5. Fill in the business details.
6. Upload the attachments the system asks for.
7. Pay by mobile money or by bank.

BRELA says the application is processed after your payment arrives. The page gives no processing time, so we do not give one either. Be careful with numbers you see on forums or social media.

## Step 2: Get your TIN from TRA

TRA is the Tanzania Revenue Authority. Its "Starting Business" page says an individual applies online at taxpayerportal.tra.go.tz. You need an official ID, such as a National ID, passport or voter's card. The portal tells you which documents to upload.

For a business TIN, TRA says you should apply within 15 days of the day you start the business. It asks you to attach a letter from the Local Government Authority and a lease agreement or title deed for the business place.

If you registered a business name with BRELA, TRA says the name shows on your TIN certificate next to your own name, written as "T/A" (trading as).

TRA may change how the TIN application works, for example the portal or the IDRAS system it mentions. If a screen looks different from what we describe, follow the screen and the TRA page.

## Step 3: Tax clearance and your business licence

Once you have your TIN certificate, TRA says you will go through an assessment interview. This is where TRA works out your tax duties. After you meet them, you receive a tax clearance. TRA says you need it to get a business licence from the trade office of the district, municipal or city council, or from the Ministry of Industry and Trade, depending on the type of business.

BRELA's licence page describes two classes:

- **Class A** is for businesses of a national or international nature. BRELA issues these.
- **Class B** is for businesses that are not of that nature. Local councils (halmashauri or manispaa) issue these.

Most one-person shops, salons and small service businesses are local, so the council is the place to ask. If you are unsure which class fits you, ask the council trade office. For a first-time owner this is the safest question to ask in person.

For an individual, BRELA lists these documents for a licence: your NIN, your TIN, proof of the business place (a lease, a residence licence or a title deed), a tax clearance certificate, and a permit from the regulator if your type of business needs one. Food, cosmetics and medicines are examples where another regulator may be involved. We cover shops in our post on [opening a small duka](/post/business-ideas/startups/start-small-duka-tanzania-licence-stock-costs-risks/).

## What it costs

Fees are the part that changes most, so treat this as a snapshot. As checked in November 2026, BRELA's business-name fee page lists:

| Service | Fee (TSh) |
| --- | --- |
| Application to register a business name | 15,000 |
| Operating fee, paid every year | 5,000 |
| Registering a change to any registered detail | 15,000 |
| Notice that the business has ended | 10,000 |
| Inspecting the register | 2,000 |

So the first cost is the TSh 15,000 application, and TSh 5,000 is paid each year to keep the name running. The page does not say when the first yearly fee falls due, so check that on the BRELA page or ask BRELA before you pay.

We could not find licence fee amounts on BRELA's licence page, and council licence fees are set by each council. We do not give a number for them. Ask the council trade office, and ask for the fee in writing or on an official payment slip. TRA does not list a fee for a TIN on the pages we read.

## Mistakes that slow you down

- **A name that does not match.** If the name on your NIDA ID is different from the name on your TRA TIN, BRELA's FAQ says to correct the TIN through TRA so it matches NIDA. Check the spelling before you submit anything.
- **A phone or email that is not yours.** BRELA asks for a phone registered in your own name and your own email. A shop owner's phone or a cybercafe email can lock you out later.
- **Paying without a receipt.** Pay through the official payment method and keep the receipt or control number. If someone asks for cash to "speed things up", walk away.
- **Starting trade and forgetting the 15 days.** Note the date you start selling. TRA counts from it.
- **Losing your ORS login.** BRELA's FAQ says one NIDA number can have several ORS accounts if each uses a different email. Write down which email you used.

## Common questions

### Do I have to register a business name?
TRA says an individual "may opt" to register the business name with BRELA. It is a choice for a sole owner. You still need a TIN if you carry on a business, and you usually need a licence from the council.

### Can I do this myself without an agent?
Yes. The BRELA and TRA steps are online, and BRELA lists a call centre on its page. You can also ask at the council trade office. Being careful with names and documents matters more than paying someone.

### How long will it all take?
We do not know, and no official page we read gives a time. Plan for it to take longer than you hope and do not sign a lease or buy stock that you cannot return before your papers are ready.

### Will registering mean I pay a lot of tax straight away?
Not necessarily. Small traders may pay a turnover-based tax, and TRA describes a relief for some new traders. Read our post on [small business tax in Tanzania](/post/business-ideas/startups/small-business-tax-tanzania-presumptive-tax-explained/).

## What to do next

1. Write down your business name choices, your NIN, and check that your phone and email are in your own name.
2. Open the BRELA page and the TRA "Starting Business" page, and compare them with this post. If anything differs, trust the official page.
3. Visit your council trade office with your list of papers and ask which licence class and which permits apply to your business.
4. Keep a folder, paper or phone photos, with every certificate and receipt.

You do not need to know everything today. One honest step, done carefully, is a good start.
