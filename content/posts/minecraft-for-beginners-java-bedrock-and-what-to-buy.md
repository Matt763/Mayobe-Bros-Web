Minecraft is a mchezo (game) where you start with an empty world and build whatever you can imagine. A hut, a farm, a castle or a working machine, all made of blocks. This guide is for students and parents who are new to it. It explains the two main versions, how to try the game for free, what kind of computer or phone you need, and how to keep a child safe online.

Everything about versions, the trial, requirements and rules was checked on official Minecraft and Mojang pages on 19 November 2026. Prices and offers change, so check the store page before you pay.

## The short answer

- Minecraft is a sandbox game. You make your own goals, so there is no single "right" way to play.
- On a PC you buy one product, **Minecraft: Java & Bedrock Edition**, and it includes both editions. On consoles and phones you get Bedrock Edition.
- There is an official free trial of about 100 minutes of in-game time on both editions.
- Java asks for a stronger computer than Bedrock. For a weak PC or phone, start with the trial.
- Child accounts have online multiplayer and chat switched off until a parent turns them on.

## What Minecraft is

Mojang's own parents' guide calls Minecraft a sandbox game where players create their own adventures inside worlds made of blocks. The same guide says more than 150 million people play it. We could not find a more detailed breakdown of that number, so treat it as Mojang's own claim.

You can dig, collect materials, craft tools and place blocks to build. Nobody gives you a mission. That is why a younger child can decorate a small house while an older sibling digs deep underground for rare materials, in the same game.

## Java Edition and Bedrock Edition

Minecraft comes in two main editions. They share the same idea but differ in where they run and what you can add. This table follows Minecraft's own comparison on its store page.

| | Java Edition | Bedrock Edition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Runs on | Windows, macOS and Linux only | Consoles, mobile, Chromebook and Windows PC |
| Play with friends on | Other Java players | Bedrock players on other devices (cross-play) |
| Mods (changes made by other people) | Yes | No |
| Marketplace add-ons and skins | No | Yes |
| Touch and controller support | No | Yes |
| Needs a strong computer | Yes, more resource-heavy | Often runs more smoothly |
| Microsoft parental controls | No | Yes |

A few plain-language notes:

- **Mods** are independent changes made by the community. Mojang says they are not affiliated with it, and some are not suitable for younger players. A parent should look at them first.
- **Marketplace** is the in-game shop for worlds, skins and add-ons. Some of it costs money. Mojang says this content follows the game's age ratings.
- **Cross-play** means a friend on a phone can join your world on a PC, if you both play Bedrock.

Mojang calls Bedrock the most-played edition and recommends it for young players. That is the company's advice, and a keen Java fan may disagree. For a first-time player on a basic device, the advice is reasonable.

## Is there a free trial?

Yes. Minecraft has an official free trial page. As checked on 19 November 2026, here is what it says:

- Both the Java and Bedrock trials last about 100 minutes of in-game time, which is about five in-game days. It may vary a little by device.
- You can play in Survival mode, with crafting, exploring and combat.
- You can play offline, but online multiplayer servers are disabled. Playing on the same local network (LAN) is allowed.
- Bedrock trial downloads are listed for Windows 10/11, PlayStation and Android. Java trial downloads are listed for Windows, Linux and Mac.
- Realms, the private servers run by Minecraft, have a separate free 30-day trial for first-time users who sign up for a 10-user plan.

Use the trial to answer one question: does the game run well on this device, and does the player enjoy it? Download the trial only from the official Minecraft page, never from a site offering a "free full version". ## What to buy

Mojang's parents' guide explains the options:

- **On PC:** you buy Minecraft: Java & Bedrock Edition on Minecraft.net, in the Microsoft Store or through the Minecraft Launcher. It is also included with PC Game Pass Premium. You need a Microsoft account.
- **On consoles and phones:** you buy Bedrock Edition in that platform's own store (Xbox, PlayStation, Nintendo eShop, App Store, Google Play, Amazon Appstore, and so on).
- **One copy does not cover every device.** A copy bought on one platform does not work on another, even with the same Microsoft account. Buy it for the device the child will really use.
- **Standard, Deluxe and Ultimate** are the same game. The larger versions only add Minecoins and Marketplace add-ons, which work in Bedrock only.

For most families, the Standard version is enough. We did not confirm a current price in TSh, because it depends on the store and your country, so check the store page you plan to use. Never pay through an unknown person or a link in a message.

## Will it run on a low-end PC or phone?

Here are the official minimum figures from Minecraft's store page, as checked on 19 November 2026.

| | Memory (RAM) | Processor and graphics |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bedrock on Windows | 4 GB minimum | Intel Celeron J4105 or AMD FX-4100; Intel HD Graphics 4000 or similar |
| Java | 8 GB with a separate graphics card, or 12 GB with built-in graphics | Intel Core i3-10100 or Ryzen 3 3100 (4 cores minimum); a Vulkan 1.3 graphics card with 2 GB of video memory |

Bedrock needs Windows 10 (version 19041 or higher) on the minimum list. Java needs Windows 10 64-bit, macOS 12 or newer, or 64-bit Linux.

The message is simple. If your computer has 4 GB of RAM and an old processor, Bedrock is the realistic choice, and even then you should try the trial first. Java asks for much more than it used to.

We could not confirm the official minimum for Bedrock on Android phones from Minecraft's own pages, so we are not giving a number. Try the free Android trial on the phone itself. ## Creative mode and Survival mode

Minecraft has two main ways to play, and the parents' guide describes them well.

**Creative mode** gives you an endless supply of blocks and items, and lets you fly. It is calm and good for pure building. There is nothing to fear.

**Survival mode** asks you to gather your own materials, make tools and shelter, and protect yourself from creatures that come out, especially at night. A Creeper, for example, hisses and then makes a small explosion. The guide says Minecraft has no blood or gore, but players can fight mobs and be attacked, and some moments can feel eerie for small children.

Two useful details for parents:

- Hostile creatures appear only in Survival mode, and they can be turned off.
- Setting the world to the **Peaceful** difficulty means no hostile creatures at all, so a child can explore, build and meet animals and villagers without scares.

Hardcore mode is the hardest and is not recommended for new players.

## What Minecraft Education is

Minecraft Education is a separate version made for classrooms. It is not the same as buying Minecraft at home. According to Minecraft Education's parents' page:

- It is licensed through Microsoft 365 and used on school-managed devices.
- It has a lesson library, classroom tools, and a coding tool called Code Builder that uses MakeCode.
- Students play with classmates, not on public servers.
- Your child may already have access through a school account, so ask the school before buying anything.

The licensing page says anyone with a Microsoft 365 account can purchase and use it. As checked on 19 November 2026, qualified schools could buy per-user subscriptions at $5.04 per user per year, and camps, clubs and other organisations at $36 per user per year. Prices may differ for your country, so confirm with the official page or through your school's IT staff.

It also has low requirements. For Windows, the page lists 2 GB of RAM, and for iPhone and iPad, 1 GB. That can suit schools with older equipment. If you are a teacher or a camp leader, start with the licensing page.

## Can it help with creativity and learning?

Here we stay with what we can source. Mojang's parents' guide says players practise creativity, planning, critical thinking and problem-solving, and that building together teaches teamwork and compromise. That is the maker's description, so read it as such.

What the game clearly does is give you a reason to plan. You decide what to build, work out the materials and fix mistakes. If a build is ruined, you can start again, which is a useful habit in any school subject.

## Safe multiplayer for children

Playing with others is a big part of Minecraft, and it is also where parents should pay attention. The steps below follow Mojang's parents' guide.

1. A parent creates an adult Microsoft account.
2. The child's account is added to a Microsoft family group. Child accounts must be linked to an adult account, and only adults can change family settings.
3. Child accounts have online multiplayer and chat turned off by default.
4. To allow online play, the parent opens account.xbox.com/settings, selects the child's account and allows them to join multiplayer games.
5. Under Privacy, the parent chooses who can communicate with the child. Mojang suggests Friends as a starting point. Choosing Block hides chat and invitations without turning off multiplayer.
6. Mojang recommends the Xbox Family Settings app to manage the child's account from a phone.

For servers, a Realm is a private server where you decide who joins. Partner Servers in Bedrock use chat filters and in-game reporting. For other servers, Mojang points to its official server list.

Talk with your child about not sharing personal details, and about using mute, block and report. For more, read our guide to [game age ratings and parental controls](/post/video-games-updates/game-age-ratings-esrb-pegi-and-parental-controls/). The guide says Minecraft has a PEGI rating of 7 and an ESRB rating of Everyone 10+.

## Common questions

### Is Minecraft free?

The full game is not free, but the official trial gives about 100 minutes of in-game time on Java or Bedrock. Minecraft Education has its own demo download, and your school may already have access, so ask your teacher.

### Which edition should a beginner choose?

On a phone or console you will have Bedrock. On a PC, the Java & Bedrock product includes both, so you can try each. Mojang recommends Bedrock for young players.

### Can I play with friends on a different device?

Bedrock players can play together across Windows, Xbox, Nintendo Switch, PlayStation and mobile. Java players can play only with other Java players on Windows, Mac and Linux.

### Is Minecraft safe for children?

The game is rated PEGI 7 and ESRB Everyone 10+, and Peaceful mode removes hostile creatures. Online safety depends on the settings you choose, so set up a family group first.

## What to do next

1. Download the official free trial on the device you plan to use, and note how well it runs.
2. Try Creative mode for a first build, such as a small house with a door and a window.
3. If a child will play, set up the Microsoft family group before turning on multiplayer.
4. Ask the school if it already has Minecraft Education before buying a separate copy.

## First-hour checklist

- Sign in with a Microsoft account (an adult's, or a child account in a family group).
- Pick Peaceful difficulty if the player is young.
- Choose Creative mode and build a small shelter.
- Switch to Survival once you are comfortable and gather wood for your first tools.
- Find the settings and note where to adjust the controls.
- Stop after about an hour, and plan the next build.
