Advanced Mathematics is the subject that opens engineering, statistics, actuarial science and many science degrees, and it is also the one students fear most. This post explains what it is in Tanzania's ACSEE, which topics you meet in Form Five and Form Six, how the exam is built, and what Basic Applied Mathematics is. It helps students already in Form Five, and parents or Form Four leavers who are choosing a combination (kuchagua tahasusi).

## Key points

- NECTA lists the exam as **142 Advanced Mathematics**, in two papers (142/1 and 142/2). TIE titles its syllabus "Mathematics Syllabus for Advanced Secondary Education Form V-VI".
- **Basic Applied Mathematics** (NECTA code 141) is a lighter subject, one paper, for students whose combination does not include Mathematics.
- The TIE syllabus dated 2023 gives 30% of your final mark to school work (tests, a project and a mock exam) and 70% to the national exam.
- In ACSEE 2025, NECTA's own report says Integration was the weakest topic in Advanced Mathematics.
- Which syllabus your class follows depends on your cohort. Ask your school and check the TIE page (see below).

## What Advanced Mathematics is in the ACSEE

At A-Level you do not study "Mathematics" in general. Students whose combination has Mathematics, such as PCM, PGM and EGM, study Advanced Mathematics. NECTA's exam format document says the Advanced Mathematics exam tests thinking, problem solving, use of a scientific calculator and the use of computer packages. A non-programmable calculator is allowed in the exam room, so check what kind your school accepts.

TIE gives ten periods of 40 minutes a week to the subject, the same as other main subjects. That is a lot of time, and you will need it.

## Topics in the 2023 TIE syllabus

The syllabus we read is titled "Mathematics Syllabus for Advanced Secondary Education Form V-VI", published by the Tanzania Institute of Education in 2023. Its content is set out by form. The list below is our own summary of its headings.

| Form | Main areas |
| --- | --- |
| Form Five | Functions (polynomial, rational, composite, exponential, logarithmic); linear programming (transport problems); coordinate geometry (angle between lines, locus, circles); trigonometry (compound angles, inverse functions); differentiation; stationary points and series expansions; integration (by parts, substitution, partial fractions), area and volume; sets and logic; algebra (roots, inequalities, 3 by 3 matrices, partial fractions, induction, binomial theorem); a project |
| Form Six | Hyperbolic functions; probability and probability distributions; first and second order differential equations and their uses; numerical methods (Newton-Raphson, trapezoidal and Simpson's rules); parabola, ellipse, hyperbola and polar coordinates; vectors; complex numbers; statistics (mean, variance, quartiles, percentiles); the project is finished and handed in |

Two things stand out. First, calculus is spread over both years, so a weak start in Form Five costs you later. Second, the syllabus expects a project in mathematics, which counts towards your school marks.

## How the exam works

NECTA's format document (revised 2019) and its 2025 report describe two papers, each three hours.

- **142/1:** ten compulsory questions, ten marks each.
- **142/2:** Section A has four compulsory questions of 15 marks. Section B has four questions of 20 marks, and you answer two.

Topics in Paper 1 include calculating devices, hyperbolic functions, linear programming, statistics, sets, functions, numerical methods, coordinate geometry I, integration and differentiation. Paper 2 covers probability, logic, vectors, complex numbers, trigonometry, algebra, differential equations and coordinate geometry II.

Notice that nearly every topic can appear every year, and Paper 1 questions are all compulsory. You cannot skip a topic and hope for the best.

### What the 2025 report says

In ACSEE 2025, NECTA reported that 18,136 candidates sat Advanced Mathematics and 97.51% passed. Pass here means any pass grade, not an A. The report also gives the share of candidates who did well in each topic. Logic, functions and linear programming went best. Integration was weak, and complex numbers, coordinate geometry II and differential equations were only average. NECTA says many candidates did not understand the idea of integration or how to use it.

Read that as a study plan. The topics that most students find hard are the ones where extra practice pays most.

## Which syllabus applies to you?

Here the picture is mixed, and we do not want to guess. NECTA's 2025 report says that year's Advanced Mathematics papers were set from the syllabus issued in 2010. TIE's website lists the 2023 syllabus, and the 2023 secondary curriculum for Form Five and Six adds two compulsory subjects, Academic Communication and Historia ya Tanzania na Maadili. News reports from July 2024 said the ministry postponed the new Form Five curriculum to 2025 because textbooks were not ready. A TIE statement quoted in July 2025 said rollout had been postponed for reasons given to the public. A distribution of Form Five books for the improved curriculum started on 24 June 2025.

So we can say the 2023 syllabus exists and Form Five books for it were being distributed in 2025. We could not find an official page that says exactly which Form Six exam year is the first on the new syllabus. As checked on 24 November 2026, ask your school which syllabus your class follows, and read NECTA's current exam format for your exam year at necta.go.tz.

## Basic Applied Mathematics: the other maths subject

The 2023 curriculum says a student who takes a science, economics or accounting combination without Mathematics must also study Basic Applied Mathematics. The exception is a student who passed Additional Mathematics at O-Level. TIE's syllabus gives it four periods a week.

Its NECTA exam, code 141, is one paper of ten compulsory questions with ten marks each. It covers calculating devices, functions, algebra, differentiation, integration, statistics, probability, trigonometry, exponential and logarithmic functions, and matrices with linear programming. In ACSEE 2025, 46,790 candidates sat and 72.66% passed. NECTA named trigonometry, probability, exponential and logarithmic functions and calculating devices as weak topics.

Do not treat it as a free pass. A quarter of candidates did not pass.

## Study advice that fits this subject

- **Practise every day.** Mathematics is a skill, like football. Reading a worked solution is not the same as solving the question yourself.
- **Fix calculus first.** Integration is where NECTA says many candidates lose marks. Redo differentiation until it is automatic, then move to integration by parts and substitution.
- **Keep an error book.** Write each mistake and the reason. NECTA's report names repeated errors, such as forgetting to divide the angle by n when finding roots with De Moivre's theorem.
- **Show every step.** NECTA's format says candidates must show all steps. Marks go to method.
- **Learn your calculator.** Set it to radians or degrees correctly before each question. Wrong mode ruins trigonometry answers.
- **Work in a small group.** Explaining a solution to a friend shows what you do not yet understand.

## Common questions

### Is Advanced Mathematics the same as Pure Mathematics?

Many students still use the old name "Pure Mathematics". The NECTA subject name in the current exam format is Advanced Mathematics, code 142. TIE titles its syllabus simply Mathematics for Advanced Secondary Education.

### Can I do an A-Level science combination without Advanced Mathematics?

Yes, in some combinations, such as PCB. You then study Basic Applied Mathematics, unless you passed Additional Mathematics at O-Level. Some university courses still ask for Advanced Mathematics, so check before you choose.

### Is there a practical exam?

The NECTA format we read lists two written papers and no practical paper. There is a project in the school-work marks under the TIE syllabus.

### Are the exam questions the same every year?

No, but the topics are the same list. Learn the whole syllabus.

## What to do next

1. Ask your school which syllabus your class follows and get a copy from the TIE publications page.
2. Make a checklist of the 18 exam topics and mark each one weak, medium or strong.
3. Spend your first extra hours on integration, complex numbers and differential equations.
4. If you are still choosing a combination, read the guide for each one you are considering.

Mathematics rewards patience. Small daily practice beats a big rush before the exam.
