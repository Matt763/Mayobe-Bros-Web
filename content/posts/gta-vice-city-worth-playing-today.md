Grand Theft Auto: Vice City, often shortened to GTA Vice City, came out in 2002 and many players still call it their favourite in the series. If you never played it, or only remember seeing it on a friend's computer, this post explains what it is, why it stood out, and whether it is worth playing now.

It is a mchezo for adults, with strong violence and language. Read the age rating section before anyone younger plays.

## Key points

- Vice City is a 2002 game from Rockstar North, set in 1986 in a fictional city based on Miami. Its PlayStation 2 release was on 29 October 2002 in North America.
- It followed Grand Theft Auto III. The hero speaks for the first time, and the 1980s setting and music became its trademark.
- You can buy it legally on phones and, in remastered form, on consoles and PC. The 2021 remaster was criticised at launch.
- It is rated for adults: ESRB Mature 17+ and PEGI 18.
- Unofficial copies are illegal and often carry malware.

## What is Vice City?

Vice City is an action-adventure game. You play Tommy Vercetti, a criminal who comes out of prison and gets caught up in a drug deal that goes wrong. He then tries to build his own criminal empire in a bright, hot city by the sea.

The game was made by Rockstar North and published by Rockstar Games. Take-Two Interactive, Rockstar's parent company, announced it on 21 May 2002 as "an entirely new game set in the '80s". Its press release for the October launch said it was the follow-up to Grand Theft Auto 3 and was available only on the PlayStation 2. The release for Europe came on 8 November 2002.

Wikipedia, which we use for the story of how the game was made, says the setting borrows from 1980s films and TV, especially Scarface and Miami Vice. Treat those details as background, not as official statements.

## What made it unique?

Vice City did not change the formula of GTA 3. It improved it and gave it a strong personality.

- **A speaking hero.** In GTA 3 the main character never talks. Tommy talks, and Wikipedia says actor Ray Liotta voiced him. That made the story easier to follow.
- **A real time and place.** The 1980s look, with neon signs, bright clothes and fast cars, is the first thing people remember.
- **Music.** Wikipedia reports about nine hours of music with more than 113 songs and adverts, plus a lot of talk radio. Many people say the radio is the best part of the game.
- **More to do.** You can ride motorcycles, buy properties and run businesses, and the city is bigger than in the earlier game.

Take-Two's press release from February 2003 said the game became the best-selling video game of 2002 and the fastest selling, highest grossing PlayStation 2 game at that time. We cannot confirm a newer sales total from an official page, so we leave it out.

## Where it fits in the series

Vice City is the second of the three games set in the same era of the series, between GTA 3 (2001) and San Andreas (2004). The stories are separate, so you do not need the others. ## How does it play today?

Be ready for a game from 2002.

**Still good:** The world feels alive, the story is easy to follow, the missions have more variety than many modern games, and the music still works. The humour is sharp. A session of an hour or two can be fun.

**Feels old:** Aiming is clumsy compared with modern games. Wikipedia notes that reviewers at the time criticised the shooting and the long loading times. Missions can be unforgiving if you fail and have to drive back. Graphics are plain by today's standards.

On phones, touchscreen controls are the weak point. Wikipedia says reviewers of the 2012 mobile version liked the visuals but found the controls the main problem. A gamepad helps if your phone supports one.

## The 2021 remaster: The Definitive Edition

The Definitive Edition is a bundle of GTA 3, Vice City and San Andreas. Rockstar's store page says it brings "brilliant new lighting and environmental upgrades", sharper textures, longer view distance and Grand Theft Auto V-style controls. It was adapted by Grove Street Games.

It was not well received at launch. Wikipedia lists Metacritic scores of 56 for Xbox Series X|S, 54 for PS5, 49 for Windows and 46 for Switch, with critics praising lighting and controls but criticising bugs and character models. Rockstar published a note after launch about the technical problems and has issued updates. Wikipedia also says more than 30 songs are missing from Vice City and San Andreas together compared with the original. If the music is why you want to play, that matters.

A later update added a "classic lighting" option, according to Wikipedia. We have not tested it.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**

- Strong style, music and sense of place.
- A clear story with a speaking hero.
- Free trial on phones lets you test before paying.

**Cons**

- Aiming and some missions feel dated.
- The remaster had a poor start and has fewer songs than the 2002 game.
- Adult themes. Not suitable for children.

## So, is it worth playing today?

This is our opinion, by type of player.

- **Curious adult newcomer.** Yes, try it, starting with the free phone trial. It is the easiest of the older games to enjoy for its style.
- **Nostalgic player.** The remaster may feel different from your memory. Check what changed before you pay full price.
- **Low-end PC owner.** The remaster needs a fairly strong PC. A phone may be a better route.
- **Young teen.** No. It is for adults.

## Common questions

### Is GTA Vice City free?

Not fully. The mobile version of the remaster gives a 30-minute free trial. After that you pay once. A "free full copy" from another site is not official.

### Are unofficial copies safe?

No. They are illegal and often contain malware or scams. Our guide to [fake downloads and scams](/post/video-games-updates/gta-6-scams-fake-downloads-and-leaks-how-to-stay-safe/) shows how to spot them.

### Do I need to play GTA 3 first?

No. The story stands alone.

### Is it good for a low-end Android phone?
