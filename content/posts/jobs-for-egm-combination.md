EGM means Economics, Geography and Mathematics as your three principal subjects in Form Five and Form Six. It is the combination for students who like numbers, maps and the question of how a country, a town or a family makes its money work. This guide is for Form Four leavers choosing a combination, Form Six leavers choosing a degree, and parents who want to understand what EGM can and cannot do.

The degree rules below come from the TCU admission guidebook for 2026/27. Each university adds its own rules, so use this as a map and confirm before you apply.

## Key points

- EGM is a strong fit for economics, statistics, actuarial science, development planning, geomatics and some teaching degrees.
- Mathematics is the subject that opens the most doors. Several programmes ask for Advanced Mathematics, sometimes with a minimum grade of D.
- For most degrees the general minimum is 4.0 points from the two subjects that define the programme.
- EGM does not lead to medicine, nursing or pharmacy, which need Physics, Chemistry and Biology.
- No place or job is guaranteed. Pay and openings change from one employer to the next.

## What the three subjects do for you

**Economics** is named in almost every business, finance and planning programme we checked. It teaches you how prices, jobs, trade, taxes and savings work.

**Geography** appears in planning, geomatics (land and map measurement), environment and disaster management programmes. It also keeps teaching doors open.

**Mathematics** is the one that carries weight. In the guidebook the A-Level subject is called Advanced Mathematics. Schools use the short word "Mathematics" in combination names, so ask your school which mathematics subject its EGM class studies. Basic Applied Mathematics appears in the guidebook as a subsidiary subject, which is a lighter extra pass.

## How points work

The guidebook scores principal passes like this for results from 2016: A = 5, B = 4, C = 3, D = 2, E = 1 and S = 0.5. Most degrees need at least 4.0 points from two subjects related to the programme.

Imagine you get C in Economics, C in Geography and D in Mathematics. For an economics degree you would count Economics and Geography, which is 3 + 3 = 6 points. That clears 4.0 on paper. But some universities receive many more applicants than places, so a higher total is safer.

## Work that follows these degrees

A degree does not equal a job, but it points in a direction. Here are the directions people usually mean, and we describe them in general terms only.

- **Economics and planning.** Analysing prices, budgets, local development and projects. Employers can include government offices, development organisations, banks and private firms.
- **Statistics and data.** Collecting and reading numbers for surveys, health, agriculture, business and public services. EASTC trains statisticians and data scientists for several African countries.
- **Actuarial and insurance work.** Measuring risk and setting prices for insurance and pensions. This path leans heavily on Mathematics.
- **Land and maps.** Geomatics and planning degrees lead toward surveying, mapping and city or district planning work.
- **Teaching.** The Mwalimu Nyerere Memorial Academy lists EGM by name for its degree in Mathematics and Information Technology with Education (Advanced Mathematics plus Economics, Geography, Physics or another listed subject, 4.0 points). Other education degrees accept Economics or Geography. Teaching jobs usually follow government or private school hiring rules.
- **Banking, tax and finance.** Economics and Mathematics are accepted for finance and tax degrees, though students from commerce combinations often fit these programmes even more closely.

We could not confirm salary figures from an official Tanzanian source, so we give none. Pay varies by employer, region and experience. Look at several real job adverts before you decide.

## A note to your heart

Choosing a combination can feel like signing a paper that decides your whole life. It does not. It decides which doors are easy to open first. Many other doors can still be opened later with a diploma, a short course or an extra year of study.

Maybe your parents or guardians are paying fees with money they worked hard for. Maybe you are the first in your family to reach Form Five. That weight is real, and it is fine to feel it. Choose with your head and your honest strengths, then give the combination steady work. Nothing here is a promise, but steady work and honest choices give you the best chance you can build for yourself.

## Where EGM does not fit

EGM is not the route into medicine, dentistry, pharmacy or nursing. Those degrees list Physics, Chemistry and Biology. Read our [PCB guide](/post/educational/study-tips/jobs-for-pcb-combination/) if that is your dream.

Most engineering degrees ask for Advanced Mathematics with Physics, so EGM students without Physics usually cannot enter them. Civil engineering at the universities we checked is an example. If engineering is your goal, look at the PCM and PGM guides before choosing.

Law is more open. Several law programmes accept two principal passes from a long list that includes Economics and Geography, though some add other rules.

## If you are in Form Four: is EGM right for you?

Check these before you pick it.

1. **Basic Mathematics.** Your O-Level grade shows whether Advanced Mathematics will feel possible. A weak grade is not a ban, but plan for extra practice from week one.
2. **Geography.** If you enjoy maps, fieldwork and reading about places, you will likely enjoy it. If you only picked it to avoid other subjects, think again.
3. **Commerce, Civics and English.** Economics asks you to read, explain and argue in English. Strong reading helps.
4. **Your school.** Not every school offers EGM. Ask which schools near you do, and which mathematics subject they teach.
5. **The careers you can see yourself in.** Write down three, then search each degree in the guidebook.

## If you are in Form Six: choosing and applying

1. Wait for your results, then add up your points using the scale above.
2. List four or five degrees from the table and read the exact subject and grade rules at each institution.
3. Apply through the university or institution channels in the current admission period. Nobody can sell you a place, and anyone who says so is a risk to your money.
4. Look at tuition and apply for a student loan if you qualify. Our guide on [how the HESLB loan works](/post/educational/study-tips/students-loan-tanzania-complete-guide/) shows the steps.
5. Read our [guide to Form Six results and points](/post/educational/results/how-to-read-acsee-results-points-and-divisions/) if the points system is new to you.

## What if your points are not enough?

1. **Choose a nearby programme.** Planning, development finance, procurement or education degrees often accept Economics or Geography and may be less crowded.
2. **Try a diploma.** NACTVET-registered colleges offer diplomas in statistics, planning, accounting and more. Check that the college and programme are accredited. Our guide on [moving from a diploma to a degree](/post/educational/career-development/diploma-to-degree-tanzania-entry-requirements/) explains the route.
3. **Improve one subject.** If Mathematics is the weak one, ask a teacher what is realistic before you decide to retake examinations.
4. **Apply again next cycle.** Some students do. It costs time, so decide with your family.

## Common questions

### Can EGM students study statistics?

Yes. Several statistics degrees accept Advanced Mathematics with Economics or Geography. Some, such as the University of Dodoma, set a minimum of D in Advanced Mathematics.

### Can an EGM student become an accountant?

Often yes. Accounting and finance degrees at several institutions accept two principal passes from a long list that includes Economics, Geography and Advanced Mathematics. Some add an O-Level Mathematics rule. The ECA combination is closer to this field.

### Is EGM a science or an arts combination?

Schools group it differently, and the guidebook does not use those labels. What matters is the subjects: Economics, Geography and Mathematics.

### Can EGM lead to teaching?

Yes. One degree in Mathematics and Information Technology with Education names EGM, and other education degrees accept Economics or Geography. Check the programme page.

### How many points do I need?

Most degrees ask for at least 4.0 points from two relevant subjects. Some institutions, such as Mzumbe University, set higher institutional minimums such as 4.5. Check each programme.

## What to do next

1. Download the TCU guidebook and search for three degrees from the table. Write down the subjects, grades and years of study.
2. If you are in Form Four, ask your school whether it offers EGM and which mathematics subject it teaches.
3. Talk to one person who works in economics, statistics or planning. Ask what a normal week looks like.
4. Keep a second plan, such as a related degree or an accredited diploma.
