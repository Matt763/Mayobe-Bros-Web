Your child asks to install a new game, and you want to say yes. You also want to know what is inside it. Many parents and guardians feel the same worry, because games arrive on phones with very little explanation.

This guide explains the age labels in plain words, shows what you will see on Google Play, and gives the steps to set limits. As checked in November 2026, these are the facts on the official pages listed at the end.

## The short answer

An age rating tells you what kind of content a game has, such as violence or strong language. It is advice for adults, and it does not tell you if the game is good, safe or cheap. Read the label, then look at the extras that ratings do not cover: chat, spending and random items.

- ESRB and PEGI are two well-known rating systems. Each uses an age label and short notes about content.
- On Google Play, many countries without their own rating body see a generic set of labels: 3+, 7+, 12+, 16+ and 18+.
- Ratings are guidance. We did not find any Tanzanian rule that makes these labels compulsory, so treat them as advice, not law.
- You can turn on content limits on Google Play with a PIN your child does not know.
- The best tool is still a talk with your child, before you lock anything.

## What do ESRB ratings mean?

The ESRB is the Entertainment Software Rating Board. It uses five main labels:

| Label | What the ESRB says |
| --- | --- |
| Everyone | Content is generally suitable for all ages |
| Everyone 10+ | Content is generally suitable for ages 10 and up |
| Teen | Content is generally suitable for ages 13 and up |
| Mature 17+ | Content is generally suitable for ages 17 and up |
| Adults Only 18+ | Content is suitable only for adults aged 18 and up |

Next to the label you may see content descriptors. These are short notes such as Blood, Violence, Language or Gambling Themes. They explain why the game got its rating.

The ESRB also shows "interactive elements". These tell you about online features: In-Game Purchases, Includes Random Items, Users Interact and Shares Location. The ESRB says these do not change the age rating. That matters, and we come back to it below.

## What do PEGI ratings mean?

PEGI is another well-known system. It has five age labels: 3, 7, 12, 16 and 18. In short:

- **PEGI 3:** suitable for everyone. Very mild, comic violence is allowed.
- **PEGI 7:** may have scenes or sounds that could frighten younger children. Violence is very mild or not realistic.
- **PEGI 12:** more graphic violence toward fantasy characters, or non-realistic violence toward human-like characters. Mild swearing and sexual hints may appear.
- **PEGI 16:** violence or sexual activity can look like real life. Stronger language, and tobacco, alcohol or illegal drugs, may appear.
- **PEGI 18:** the most serious content, including gross violence, simulated gambling and explicit sexual activity.

PEGI also uses content symbols, such as violence, bad language and fear. Its page says the age labels are about suitability by content and risks, and it does not say anything about how hard a game is.

PEGI has added more notes about online risks. These include in-game purchases, paid random items and chat that cannot be blocked or muted. Its page says some new rules apply to games submitted since June 2026, for example a higher rating for paid random items. Older games may not follow those newer rules, so always read the label on the game you are looking at.

## What will you see on Google Play in Tanzania?

Most families here meet games through the Google Play Store on an Android phone. Google Play uses a system called IARC, which works with rating bodies around the world. The developer answers a questionnaire, and the rating comes from the answers.

Google's help page says that in countries without a rating body of their own, Play shows generic IARC ratings. The labels are Rated for 3+, 7+, 12+, 16+ and 18+. We could not confirm from Google's page which exact label Tanzania sees, so check the live listing on your own phone. The Play Store also shows ratings by the viewer's country, so a friend abroad may see a different label for the same game.

Please remember that none of this is a Tanzanian law on games. It is a safety guide from companies and rating groups. We did not find an official Tanzanian source that sets age ratings for games, so we do not claim one exists.

If you name a game in a family talk, open its page and read the label yourself. A friend's memory of a game is not a rating.

## What does a rating not tell you?

A rating mostly describes content. It does not tell you about three things that often worry parents more.

1. **Chat with strangers.** The ESRB label "Users Interact" says players can meet user-made content and talk to each other. A game rated for young children can still have chat.
2. **Spending.** "In-Game Purchases" means you can buy things with real money. A game can be free to install and still ask for money again and again.
3. **Random items.** "Includes Random Items" means you pay without knowing what you will get. We explain this in [our guide on loot boxes and in-game purchases](/post/video-games-updates/loot-boxes-and-in-game-purchases-protect-your-money/).

So the label is the first step, not the last. Look at the full listing, read recent reviews, and ask what the game lets your child do with other players.

## How do you set limits on a phone, console or PC?

### On an Android phone with Google Play

Google's help page gives these steps for a family member who manages their own account:

1. Open the Google Play app and tap the profile icon at the top right.
2. Tap Settings, then Family, then Content restrictions.
3. Tap Go to Parental controls, then set up a parental controls PIN.
4. Choose a PIN your child does not know.
5. Select the types of content you want to filter, then choose how to limit access.

If your child has an account managed with the Family Link app, open Family Link, pick your child, tap Controls, then Google Play, and choose what to filter.

Know what these settings cannot do, as Google explains:

- They work only on the device where you set them. Repeat the steps on every phone or tablet your child uses.
- Restricted apps can still show in search or open through a direct link.
- Games installed before you set the limit stay on the phone. Family Link can block them on a child's device.
- Menus change, and some options are not offered in every country. If a step looks different, search Google Play Help for the current page.

### On consoles and PCs

Xbox, PlayStation, Nintendo and Windows each have their own family tools, and the menus change often. We did not open every platform page, so we do not give menu steps here. The ESRB Family Gaming Guide is a good place to start. It lists parental controls and child accounts, and it advises giving each child their own account so that limits on ratings, schedules, contacts and purchases can be set for that child. Then open the support page of your console maker and follow its current steps.

## Talk with your child before you lock things

A lock without a conversation can feel like a punishment. A child who feels trusted is more likely to come to you when something goes wrong, such as a rude message or a surprise charge.

Here is a plain way to start. Sit beside your child and ask to see the game. Ask who they play with. Ask what the game lets them buy. Then agree some house rules together, such as when the phone is put away and what you will say yes to without asking.

The ESRB guide suggests the same idea: agree on limits for screen time, spending and online chat, and set rules that match each child's age. For children under about five it suggests adult supervision. For the middle years it suggests checking ratings and contacts. For teenagers it suggests open talk, with rules that loosen as they show they can handle more.

You do not need to be a gamer to do this. You only need to be interested.

## Common questions

### Is an ESRB or PEGI label a law in Tanzania?

We did not find a Tanzanian rule that makes these labels compulsory. They are guidance from rating bodies. The Play Store shows ratings by country, and the generic labels are advice for adults.

### Does a low rating mean a game is safe?

No. A rating covers content. It does not cover chat, spending or random items. A game rated for young children can still let players talk or buy things.

### Can my child get around Google Play limits?

Content restrictions are not perfect. Google says restricted apps can still appear in search or open from a link, and old installs stay. A PIN your child does not know helps, and so does talking.

### What if I forget my parental controls PIN?

Google's page says you can clear the storage of the Play Store app to reset the PIN and the restrictions. This also removes your settings, so write them down first.

## What to do next

1. Open your child's most used game on Google Play and read its rating, its reviews and its purchase notes.
2. Set a Content restrictions PIN on each device your child uses, and keep the PIN to yourself.
3. Read the [ESRB Family Gaming Guide](https://www.esrb.org/tools-for-parents/family-gaming-guide/) and pick two house rules to try this week.
4. Sit with your child for ten minutes while they play, and ask them to show you their favourite part.

Small steps count. A calm talk tonight is worth more than a perfect setting you never explain.
