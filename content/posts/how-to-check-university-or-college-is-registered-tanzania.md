You have worked hard for years, and your family may be saving every shilling for your fees. Before any money leaves your phone, take ten minutes to check that the place you chose is real and approved. It is a small step that protects a big dream.

## The short answer

For a university, search the official list on the website of the Tanzania Commission for Universities (TCU). For a technical or vocational college, use the information from the National Council for Technical and Vocational Education and Training (NACTVET). Then check that the course itself is offered. Do this before you pay anything.

- Use only the official TCU website, typed in your browser yourself, not a link sent by a stranger.
- Look for your university in the TCU list and read its status.
- Open the programme list and look for your exact course.
- For certificate and diploma courses, follow the NACTVET process and keep the messages NACTVET sends to your phone.
- Pay fees only through the official portal of the institution.

As checked in November 2026, the TCU pages described below were open and working. Websites change, so if a page looks different, look for the same search boxes under the same menu names.

## Why check first?

A good name on a poster does not prove that a college is approved. A course that is not approved may leave you with a certificate that employers or other schools do not accept. That is a heavy loss after years of study and fees.

Checking costs nothing but a little data and time. It also helps your parents or sponsors feel calm, because you can show them the official page instead of only a promise from an agent.

TCU has warned applicants not to deal with people who say they are admission agents. According to a Daily News report on the first 2026/27 admission window, TCU says undergraduate applications and fee payments are handled electronically through official university admission portals. If someone asks you to pay them personally to "secure a place", stop and check.

## How do I check a university on the TCU list?

TCU keeps a public page called "Universities registered in Tanzania". Here is how it worked when we checked it in November 2026.

1. Open the TCU website at tcu.go.tz and go to the accreditation services, then the list of registered universities.
2. Type the name of the university in the search box. You can also filter by location (for example Dar es Salaam, Dodoma or Zanzibar) and by type, such as public or private, university or college.
3. Read the status column. Each entry shows either "Accredited" or "Accredited and Chartered". The page does not explain the difference, so if the status matters to you, ask TCU.
4. If you cannot find the name, try another spelling or the short name. Many institutions have long names and different spellings.
5. If it is still not there, do not pay. Ask TCU directly.

The page also has a link to download the full list as a PDF. The file we saw was titled "List of university institutions in Tanzania as of 30 October 2026". The PDF is large, so open it on Wi-Fi if you can. If you want to know how many universities are registered, read that number from the page on the day you check, because it changes.

## How do I check that my course is offered?

A university can be real while a certain course is not offered there. So check the programme too.

Each row on the TCU list has a "View Programmes" link. It opens the programme list for that institution. TCU also has a separate page for academic programmes offered by universities. On that page you can search by:

- programme name,
- study mode (full time or part time),
- award level, from certificate up to doctorate,
- the university institution.

The results show the programme, the institution, the award level, the length in months and the level in the qualification framework (for example "UQF 10").

One honest limit: the results table we saw did not show an accreditation status for each programme. The page links to a separate programme accreditation service on TCU's e-services. We could not confirm from the page what that service shows. If you are unsure, write to TCU or visit the office and ask them to confirm that your course is approved.

You can also read the TCU admission guidebook for the year. The Daily News report says it lists approved programmes and entry requirements.

## What about technical and vocational colleges?

Certificate and diploma courses at technical colleges are under NACTVET. We could not open the institution pages on the NACTVET site when we checked, because they loaded blank. So we describe NACTVET only through a Daily News report from June 2026 and ask you to confirm on the council's official site.

According to that report, for 2026/27:

- NACTVET publishes an Admission Guidebook that lists accredited programmes and institutions.
- Most programmes are applied for directly at the institution. Health and allied sciences programmes on the Mainland go through the Central Admission System (CAS) on the council's website.
- Successful applicants get a message from NACTVET on the phone number they used. It names the institution and gives a code to show at registration.
- After registration, you get a second message. You can then check your details through the Student Information Verification link on the council's website.
- NACTVET advises applicants to avoid brokers and to complete the process themselves.

Keep those messages. They are your proof, so do not delete them or share the code with others.

## Warning signs of a bad deal

Here are signs that should make you pause. These come from what TCU and NACTVET have said publicly, plus common sense.

- Someone asks you to pay them personally, or to send money to a personal mobile number, to "get you admitted".
- You are told you can skip the official application or the entry requirements.
- The person will not let you use the official portal yourself.
- You are pushed to pay today or lose the place.
- The institution cannot be found on the official list, and nobody can explain why.

None of these proves a college is fake, and we are not naming any institution as bad. They are reasons to check the official list before you go further.

Say a friend sends you a message saying a college offers a diploma in six weeks. Do not argue. Open the official site yourself, search the name, and check the course. If the answer is not clear, ask the regulator.

## Common questions

### Is a university on the TCU list the same as an approved course?

Not always. The list shows the institution. Use the programme search or ask TCU to confirm the course you want.

### Can I pay a registration fee to an agent?

TCU says fees are paid through the official admission portal of the university. If you are asked to pay someone else, check with the university first, then with TCU if the problem is not solved.

### I use a phone with little data. What is the easiest way to check?

Search by name on the TCU list page first. Avoid downloading the large PDF unless you have Wi-Fi or a good bundle. Our post on [saving mobile data when studying online](/post/educational/online-learning/save-mobile-data-when-studying-online/) can help.

### Where do I start if I need help paying for university?

Read our [guide to the student loan in Tanzania](/post/educational/study-tips/students-loan-tanzania-complete-guide/) so you know the steps before you apply.

## What to do next

1. Write down the full name of the institution and the exact name of your course.
2. Search the institution on the TCU list, or find the course in the NACTVET guidebook, and save a screenshot with the date.
3. Open the programme list and confirm the course is there. If you are still unsure, contact TCU or NACTVET.
4. Pay only through the official portal, and keep every receipt.

Taking this care is not suspicion. It is respect for your own effort, and you deserve a place that is real.
