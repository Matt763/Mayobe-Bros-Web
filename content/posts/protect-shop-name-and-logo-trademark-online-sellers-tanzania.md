You chose your shop name with care. You tried many logos, posted every day, and slowly people began to know you. Then one worry creeps in: what if someone copies it? This guide explains, in plain words, what protection you can get from BRELA, the Business Registrations and Licensing Agency. It is general information, not legal advice. For a particular problem, ask a qualified lawyer or BRELA.

## The short answer

A registered business name and a trade or service mark are two different things.

- A **business name** is who you trade as. BRELA says registering it gives your business a legal identity and protects the name from use by others.
- A **trade or service mark** is a sign, such as a logo, that identifies your product or service. BRELA says it gives the owner exclusive rights to use the mark.
- If you sell online with a name and a logo, you may want both.
- Registration gives you a legal right and a route to complain. It cannot stop anyone from copying you.
- Pay BRELA only against a receipt.

## Why this matters now

On 21 October 2026, at the CRDB Instapreneur forum in Dar es Salaam, the Minister of Industry and Trade, Judith Kapinga, urged entrepreneurs who sell on social media to register their logos with BRELA. BRELA's news item, dated 23 October 2026, quotes her saying that many followers alone do not protect a brand. She said people should register logos because they put great effort into creating them. BRELA also said its services are online, so you do not need to go to its office.

Followers are a good sign. They are not a legal right. That is the gap registration is meant to fill.

## What does a registered business name give me?

BRELA's page on business names says a registered name:

- gives the business a legal identity,
- protects the name from being used by someone else,
- is recognised by banks and financial institutions, for example when you open an account,
- can help you access loans from banks.

BRELA's listed requirements include that the name is legally acceptable, that the applicant is Tanzanian and at least 18, a national ID number, a personal phone number and email, and the business and home addresses. A name can be registered by one person or a group of up to 20. Fees are on a separate BRELA page, so check it.

For the full steps on starting a business, see our guide to [registering a small business with BRELA, TIN and licence](/post/business-ideas/startups/register-small-business-tanzania-brela-tin-licence/).

## What does a trade or service mark give me?

BRELA describes a trade or service mark as a unique sign that identifies a product or service. It helps businesses that make similar goods tell their products apart in the market. A trade mark is for goods, and a service mark is for services.

BRELA lists these benefits:

- it lets you identify and promote your product or service,
- it gives you exclusive rights to use the mark,
- it helps customers recognise and choose your product by its mark,
- it can help earn income if you license the mark to others.

Think of your shop name as the name on your door, and the logo as your signature. The first says who you are. The second is the picture people remember. BRELA protects them in different ways.

### What can be refused?

BRELA's criteria say a mark must:

- be unique and created by the owner,
- not look like a mark already registered by someone else in the same class (a class is a group of similar goods or services),
- not simply describe the goods or services in that class,
- not be against the law or morals.

So a logo that copies a famous brand will not pass, and it could get you into trouble. A plain description, like the word "Shoes" for a shoe shop, also cannot work as a mark. Pick something distinctive. Never use another company's logo, crest or badge. Our post on [branding basics](/post/business-ideas/marketing/branding-basics-name-logo-colours/) explains how to choose a name, logo and colours that are your own.

## How do I apply online with BRELA?

As checked in November 2026, BRELA's trade and service mark page gives these steps:

1. Go to brela.go.tz or ors.brela.go.tz.
2. Log in to your ORS account, or create one.
3. Choose the online services option.
4. Choose the trade or service mark service.
5. Choose the type of service.
6. Fill in the details of the mark.
7. Upload the attachments.
8. Pay by mobile money or bank.
9. The application is processed after payment is received.

The attachments BRELA lists are a picture or drawing of the mark and a consolidated form. If an agent applies for you, Form TM/SM 1 is also needed. BRELA's page does not state how long approval takes, and we could not confirm it, so do not plan around a date. BRELA's call centre number on the page is 0222 212 800.

Before you apply, search the internet and social media to see whether a similar mark already exists. Keep a clear picture of your logo, and know which class your goods or services are in.

## What does it cost?

BRELA's trade and service mark fee page says, as checked in November 2026:

| Item | Fee listed |
| --- | --- |
| Application to the Registrar for protection of a mark | No fee |
| Registration of one mark for goods in one class | TSh 60,000 |
| Renewal of a registration | TSh 30,000 |

The page lists other fees as well, for example for objections and hearings, which can apply in disputes. We have quoted only the lines we checked. Look at BRELA's page for the full table before you pay.

Two safety notes from BRELA's page:

- All payments go to the Registrar and you should get a receipt. BRELA urges applicants not to pay without a payment receipt.
- If an officer asks for any payment beyond the listed ones, BRELA says to report it on the phone numbers given on the page.

Never pay a "helper" who promises a quick approval. Nobody can promise it.

## What are the limits of registration?

Be honest about what a certificate can do. It gives you a legal right to your mark in the class you registered. It gives you a route to complain if someone uses a confusing copy. It does not stop copying, and it does not remove the need for evidence or, in a serious dispute, a lawyer.

BRELA's page also does not tell us how long a mark lasts, so check the renewal rules on BRELA's site.

## Everyday habits that protect a small brand

Whatever you decide, build a record.

- **Keep original files.** Save the first sketch, the design file and the final logo with their dates.
- **Keep dates.** Screenshots of your first posts, first sales and first adverts show when you started.
- **Be consistent.** Use the same name and logo everywhere so customers remember it.
- **Do not copy others.** Respect other people's names and logos. Do not use a real company's logo on your products.
- **Watch for copies.** If you see another shop using your name, save a screenshot, the link and the date.

## Common questions

### Is a registered business name enough?

It protects the name you trade under. A logo is a different thing. BRELA treats trade and service marks as a separate registration.

### Do I need to visit a BRELA office?

BRELA says its services are digital and you need not go to the office. Use the online steps above.

### How much does it cost?

As checked in November 2026, BRELA lists no fee for the application, TSh 60,000 for registering one mark in one class, and TSh 30,000 for renewal. Check the current page.

### Can registration stop people copying me?

No. It gives you a legal right and a way to complain. It does not stop someone from trying.

## What to do next

1. Write down your shop name, and keep the first logo files and screenshots with dates.
2. Check whether a similar name or logo is already used, then read BRELA's two pages.
3. Register your business name if you have not, then think about the mark.
4. Pay only against a receipt.

You put real effort into your brand, and taking a small, legal step to protect it is a sensible way to respect that work.
