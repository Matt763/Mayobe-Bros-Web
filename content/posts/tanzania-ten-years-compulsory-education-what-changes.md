Tanzania is changing how long a child must stay in school. Today the compulsory part is seven years of primary school. The government's plan is ten years, which takes every child up to Form Four. This is the elimu ya lazima change that parents, teachers and students keep hearing about. This post explains what has been announced, what is still being decided and what a family can do now. The facts were checked in November 2026, and the plan is still moving, so check the Ministry of Education for the latest.

## The short answer

- The revised Education and Training Policy (2023 edition) provides for compulsory education for ten years, ending at Form Four.
- Primary school is to become six years instead of seven. After Standard Six, children go on to secondary school.
- The first big intake under the new structure is expected in January 2028. The Education Minister told Parliament that about 3.02 million students are projected to start secondary school that year.
- The government is reviewing the Education Act of 1978 so the law matches the policy. We found no report that a new law has passed.
- Secondary schools will need many more classrooms. The Prime Minister said every village should have one.

## What is changing and why

Under the old structure, the law makes seven years of primary school compulsory. Many pupils stopped at Standard Seven, usually around age 13. The Respondent reported that under the new system, children finish compulsory education at about 16.

Education Minister Prof Adolf Mkenda explained the plan at the International Quality Education Conference in Dar es Salaam on 12 November 2026, as reported by Daily News. He described three reforms:

1. Two pathways in secondary school, general and vocational. The vocational pathway puts more weight on practical skills so graduates can join the workforce or start a business.
2. Compulsory education extended from seven to ten years, so all children continue through Form Four.
3. Primary education shortened from seven years to six, with pupils moving on to secondary school after Standard Six.

He also said the changes are already under way and there is no turning back.

## Why two groups reach Form One together

A change like this cannot happen in one year, so there is a transition. Daily News reported the minister's explanation as follows.

The new curriculum began with Standard Three. Pupils who were in Standard Three when it started will finish primary school after Standard Six next year. Pupils who were in Standard Four at that time stayed on the previous curriculum and will finish Standard Seven. That means two groups finish primary school in 2027, and both go to Form One in January 2028.

The minister told Parliament in May that 3,021,177 students are projected to start secondary school in 2028, according to The Respondent. That is a very large Form One class. The same budget speech, as reported by Daily News, set out 8 million textbooks and teachers' guides for the year, including 3,102,263 for Standard Six.

We did not find an official table that explains every year group, so if your child is in Standard Four to Standard Seven, ask the school which curriculum and which exam route applies. Do not rely on a social media message for this.

## What it means for classrooms

More students need more places. The Citizen reported on 5 September 2026 that Prime Minister Mwigulu Nchemba, speaking in Bahi District in Dodoma Region, said every village should have a secondary school. The report said the plan needs more than 16,000 classrooms and about 8,000 laboratories. It named no completion date and no dedicated funding source.

At the November conference the minister said existing village primary schools could be expanded with extra classrooms so children can reach secondary school within reasonable walking distance. He said boarding secondary schools will still be built, and planners will consider where people live and the shape of the land.

The Education Ministry works with the President's Office for Regional Administration and Local Government (PO-RALG, also called TAMISEMI). Daily News reported that the two share the work on guidelines, building money, teacher training and involving the private sector.

## The law is being reviewed

The main education law is the National Education Act of 1978. On 21 May 2026 the minister held a meeting in Dodoma to start a review of the law, The Citizen reported. He said the government found gaps that need urgent changes so the law fits the reforms. Two options were open: big amendments to the old Act, or a new law. The President decides after Cabinet discusses it.

The ministry expected a first draft to reach the Cabinet Secretariat by mid-June 2026, and after Cabinet approval, the law goes to Parliament. The Deputy Minister asked that the draft be posted on the ministry website so parents, teachers and others can comment. We could not find a report that Parliament has passed a new law, so treat the details of penalties and enforcement as not yet known.

## What this means for a family

**Fees.** We did not find a new fee announcement in the sources we read. The plan is about how long school is compulsory. If you pay any school contribution, ask the head teacher in writing what it is for, and check the ministry's circulars for what public schools may charge.

**Subjects and streams.** The textbook figures in the budget speech mention "general streams" and "vocational streams" for secondary school. Ask your child's school how streams will be chosen. If your child is thinking about combinations and careers later, our [guide to combinations and careers](/post/educational/combination-na-aina-ya-kazi-utakayofanya/) may help, though it was written for the system as it stands today.

**Exams.** We did not find an official statement in the sources we opened about what happens to the Standard Seven exam. Ask the school and watch NECTA announcements. ## Common questions

### When does the ten-year system start?

The first large Form One intake under it is expected in January 2028, according to the minister's statements reported in May and November 2026. The change has already begun in the lower primary classes through the new curriculum.

### Does my child have to go to secondary school after Standard Six?

The minister said pupils will not leave the education system after Standard Six, because they must go on to secondary school. The rule is expected to be written into law through the review. Until a new law is published, ask your local education officer how it applies to your child.

### Is there a new education law yet?

Not that we could find. A review began in May 2026 and the Cabinet and Parliament still have to act. Check the Ministry of Education website and Parliament's announcements.

### Will every village get a secondary school?

That is the Prime Minister's stated aim, and the minister said regional and local leaders have been told to make sure of it. No completion date was given, so it may take years.

## What to do next

1. Find out which curriculum your child's class follows now, and ask the head teacher what route applies to the Form One year.
2. If you plan to send a child to a private or boarding school, ask early how places will be shared out in 2028.
3. Read the draft law when it is published and send comments through the ministry's channel.
4. Keep up with the Ministry of Education and NECTA for official notices, and use our guide to [free textbooks from TIE](/post/educational/online-learning/tie-online-library-free-textbooks-tanzania/) to help your child study.
