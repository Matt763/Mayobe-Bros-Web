Tanzania is made of two parts that were once separate countries. Many people know the date, 26 April 1964, but fewer know how the Union came about. This post tells the story in order, says where sources differ, and takes no side in the debates people still have.

## The short answer

Tanganyika became independent in December 1961, and Zanzibar in December 1963. In January 1964 a revolution changed Zanzibar's government. On 26 April 1964, Tanganyika and Zanzibar joined to form one state. It was first called the United Republic of Tanganyika and Zanzibar, and later renamed Tanzania.

- Julius Nyerere led Tanganyika, and Abeid Karume led Zanzibar's revolutionary council.
- The talks were kept secret.
- Sources differ on the exact signing and approval steps.
- The new name, Tanzania, was adopted on 29 October 1964, according to the sources we checked.

## Two countries, two paths

Tanganyika became independent from Britain on 9 December 1961. You can read that story in our post on [how Tanganyika won independence](/post/world-histories/how-tanganyika-won-independence/).

Zanzibar followed. Wikipedia's article on the Union says Zanzibar became independent from the United Kingdom on 10 December 1963 as a constitutional monarchy under its Sultan. EBSCO says Britain had set 10 December as the target date.

EBSCO also reports that in the July 1963 elections the Afro-Shirazi Party won 55 percent of the vote, while the Zanzibar Nationalist Party won a majority of seats. Mohammed Shamte became prime minister.

## January 1964: the Zanzibar Revolution

On 12 January 1964, EBSCO says, a revolution broke out in Zanzibar. John Okello led it, and Abdul Rahman Mohammed Babu was a second leader. The Sultan fled, and the cabinet resigned the next day. A revolutionary council of 31 members was formed, chaired by Abeid Amani Karume.

The revolution was violent. Wikipedia says Arab and Indian people were killed in large numbers. The number of dead is disputed, and we do not give one. We mention it because a fair account must not hide it.

EBSCO also says that on 20 January 1964 a rifle corps in Tanganyika mutinied. Nyerere went into hiding until British troops from Kenya restored order. So Tanganyika's own government faced a serious test in the same weeks.

## April 1964: Nyerere, Karume and the Articles of Union

EBSCO says Nyerere met the leaders of Kenya and Uganda in Nairobi on 10 and 11 April 1964 to discuss a wider federation. When he suggested including Zanzibar, they refused.

On 23 April 1964, EBSCO says, Nyerere and Karume announced a political union between Tanganyika and Zanzibar. It says the talks had been secret. Negotiators from both sides met, and Roland Brown drafted the articles.

Pius Msekwa was the Clerk of Tanganyika's Parliament at the time. The Citizen newspaper reports his account, given at a Dodoma symposium on the Union's 58th anniversary. He said Nyerere and Karume kept talks secret to avoid sabotage. He said Nyerere's trip to Zanzibar for the signing was described publicly as a friendly tour, and the truth came out that evening in a short State House statement.

## What are the dates?

Here the sources differ, and we show you how.

| Source | What it says |
| --- | --- |
| EBSCO | Announced 23 April 1964. Ratified by Tanganyika's national assembly and Zanzibar's revolutionary council on 26 April 1964 |
| Pius Msekwa, in The Citizen | Parliament approved at an emergency sitting on 25 April, and the Union took effect the next day |
| Wikipedia, Union article | Union formed on 26 April 1964 |

All three agree on 26 April 1964 as the day of the Union. They differ on the steps just before it, and we have not found a source that settles who signed what and where. Msekwa's article also gives two timings for when the Union took effect, so it is best to treat that detail as unsettled.

## What changed? A new state, a new name and Union matters

**A new state.** EBSCO says Nyerere became president of the new republic. Karume became first vice president and kept the title President of Zanzibar. Rashidi Kawawa became second vice president.

**Union matters.** Msekwa says the Articles of Union listed 11 Union affairs. These are subjects handled by the Union government, while others stay with each side. His article is unclear on whether the list was later enlarged. Wikipedia says Zanzibar's government kept considerable local autonomy.

**An interim constitution.** According to Msekwa, Tanganyika's constitution became the interim constitution of the Union, with changes that recognised Zanzibar's government and gave Zanzibar seats in the Union Parliament. It was meant to last one year.

**A new name.** EBSCO and Wikipedia both say the name changed to the United Republic of Tanzania on 29 October 1964. The name joins "Tanganyika" and "Zanzibar". EBSCO adds that a blue stripe was added to the flag to stand for Zanzibar. We did not find a government page for this date, so please check one if you need it for an exam or official use.

## What do people still debate?

We keep this section to facts and do not say who is right.

**Why did it happen?** EBSCO reads the Union as a response to a crisis. In its view, it limited communist influence in Zanzibar without pushing Zanzibar into the Western camp, and it says the issue was "settled without a shot fired." That is one historian's interpretation. Others may weigh the reasons differently.

**The secrecy.** EBSCO says British and American diplomats learned of the Union at the last minute. Some people ask what the secrecy means for how the Union is seen. That is a matter of opinion.

**The structure.** Wikipedia says that in 1990 a Zanzibari coalition demanded a referendum on independence, citing the Union's economic effects, and that the demand was ignored. Debates about how the two governments share power continue. We do not offer a view on whether the arrangement should stay as it is or change.

## Union Day on 26 April

Tanzanians mark 26 April as Union Day. The Citizen's report on Msekwa's talk is from a symposium held in Dodoma to mark 58 years of the Union. We have not checked an official page on whether it is a public holiday, so please check the government calendar.

## Common questions

### When did Tanganyika and Zanzibar unite?
On 26 April 1964, according to the sources we checked.

### When did the name Tanzania start?
The sources we checked say 29 October 1964. Before that, the state was called the United Republic of Tanganyika and Zanzibar.

### Who were the leaders?
Julius Nyerere of Tanganyika became president. Abeid Karume of Zanzibar became first vice president.

### How many Union matters are there?
Msekwa's account lists 11 at the start. Sources differ on later changes, so check a current official source for today's list.

## What to do next

1. Learn the order: December 1961, December 1963, January 1964, April 1964, October 1964.
2. Read the EBSCO and Citizen pieces side by side and note where they differ.
3. For the present list of Union matters, use the Constitution or an official government page, not a summary.

Seeing both the agreed facts and the open questions is the best way to understand a shared history.
