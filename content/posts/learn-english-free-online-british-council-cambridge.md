Maybe you want to follow lectures more easily. Maybe you want to write a good job application, or speak with more confidence in an interview. Better English opens doors, and you do not need a lot of money to start. Two well-known organisations offer free practice online, and you can use them on a phone.

This post shows what is free, what is not, how the English levels work, and a small daily plan you can really keep.

## The short answer

Start with the free level test on the British Council LearnEnglish site, then practise at that level for about 20 minutes a day. Use the Cambridge English site for extra activities and for writing practice with feedback.

- Both sites have large free sections. Some other things on them, such as live classes and private tutoring, cost money.
- Your level (A1 to C2) tells you what to practise. Pick lessons that are slightly hard, not very hard.
- Short, regular practice works better than one long session each month.
- A free account on LearnEnglish lets you save your favourite lessons.
- Nobody can promise you a test score or fluency by a fixed date. Progress is personal and it takes time.

All details below were checked in November 2026. Websites change, so look at the official pages for the latest.

## What do the levels A1 to C2 mean?

English learning is often described with six levels. They come from a common scale used by many schools and exams around the world.

| Level | Name | Plain meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A1 | Beginner | You understand and use very simple, everyday phrases |
| A2 | Elementary | You handle short, simple talk about daily life |
| B1 | Intermediate | You cope with most common situations and can explain your opinions simply |
| B2 | Upper intermediate | You follow longer talks and write clear text on many topics |
| C1 | Advanced | You use English flexibly for study and work |
| C2 | Proficient | You understand almost everything with ease |

The LearnEnglish site has lessons for listening, reading and writing from A1 to C1. Its speaking lessons run from A1 to B2. Grammar and vocabulary lessons are grouped in pairs of levels, such as A1 to A2 and B1 to B2. The level test and level guides cover all six levels, A1 to C2.

You will often see the levels in groups. Cambridge English calls A1 and A2 "Basic", B1 and B2 "Independent", and C1 and C2 "Proficient".

## British Council LearnEnglish: what is free?

LearnEnglish is run by the British Council, the United Kingdom's organisation for cultural relations and education. As checked in November 2026, the free part of the site includes:

- Listening, reading, writing and speaking lessons, grouped by level.
- Grammar exercises, a grammar reference and vocabulary games.
- General English and Business English videos, audio, stories and series.
- A free English level test.
- Activities for children (5 to 12) and teenagers (13 to 17).
- A free account, which lets you save favourites and receive the newsletter.

The listening section is a good place to begin. The lessons come with recordings and interactive exercises, so you can check yourself right away.

There are also podcasts for learners at A2 and B1. At the time of checking there were four series. Each episode mixes short drama, jokes, quizzes and language tips. Every recording has a transcript and exercises. A transcript is helpful: listen first without reading, then listen again with the words in front of you.

### What costs money on LearnEnglish

The same site also promotes paid products. These include live online classes, private tutoring, a self-study course and IELTS preparation. Their prices were not shown on the main page, so we cannot tell you the cost. If you want one, read the course page carefully first. You can make good progress with the free lessons alone.

## Cambridge English: free activities and writing practice

Cambridge English is part of the University of Cambridge. Its learning page has free material you can use without paying. As checked in November 2026, this includes:

- Free online activities for teenagers and adults, grouped by level.
- Write & Improve, a free tool where you type your writing and get feedback within seconds.
- Free sample tests and vocabulary lists in the exam preparation section.
- An audio series called Virtually Anywhere.
- Free quizzes and word lists from Cambridge Dictionary Plus.
- Activities for young learners.

Write & Improve is useful because writing is hard to practise alone. You cannot tell what is wrong if nobody reads it. The tool gives quick feedback, and you can try again. Treat the feedback as a guide, not as a final judge. A teacher or a friend with strong English can still help.

The site also lists exams such as Cambridge English Qualifications and Linguaskill, plus official preparation products and a bookshop. Those are paid, and the page does not show prices. Taking an exam is a separate decision. You do not need one to practise.

## How do I pick my starting level?

1. Take the free level test on the LearnEnglish site. Do it in a quiet place, without a dictionary, so the result is honest.
2. Read your result as a range, not a grade. Many people are strong in reading but weaker in speaking.
3. Choose lessons at your level for reading and listening. For your weakest skill, pick one step lower so you can build confidence.
4. Test again after a few months, not every week.

If the test feels very hard, that is fine. It only tells you where to start. Starting at A1 or A2 is nothing to be ashamed of. Many learners begin there, and the early levels can move faster than you expect when you practise often.

## A 20-minute daily plan

Say you have 20 minutes after dinner, or on the daladala ride home. Here is one way to spend them. Change it to fit your day.

| Minutes | What to do | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5 | Listen to one short recording. Do not read the words yet. | LearnEnglish listening or a podcast |
| 5 | Listen again with the transcript. Underline three new words. | The same lesson |
| 5 | Do the exercises or one grammar activity | LearnEnglish or Cambridge English |
| 5 | Write three sentences using your new words | A notebook, or Write & Improve |

On two days a week, swap the last five minutes for speaking. Read your three sentences out loud. Say them to a mirror, a friend or a relative. Speaking aloud feels strange at first, and that feeling gets smaller each week.

A few small habits help:

- Keep one notebook for new words. Write the word, its meaning in simple English or Kiswahili, and your own example sentence.
- Review the notebook once a week.
- Practise at the same time every day, so the habit becomes automatic.
- If your data is limited, open the lesson page while you have a connection and look at the transcript later. Check how much data audio uses on your own phone before you rely on it.

## How do I keep going when it gets hard?

Everyone hits a flat patch. You practise for weeks and feel the same. This is normal. Understanding grows quietly before it shows.

Some ideas that help:

- Make the goal small. "Twenty minutes today" is easier to meet than "be fluent".
- Mark each day you practise on a calendar. A chain of marked days is encouraging.
- Study with a friend. You can send each other a sentence a day.
- Choose topics you already like, such as football, music, farming or business. Interest keeps you coming back.
- Forgive missed days. Start again the next day without guilt.

Every sentence you understand today was impossible for you once. That is real progress, even when it feels slow.

## Common questions

### Is it really free?

The lessons, activities and tools listed above were free to use when we checked in November 2026. Both sites also sell courses, exams and tutoring. You will see these offers, and you can ignore them.

### Can I use these sites on my phone?

Both are websites, and you can open them in a phone browser. Layout and features can differ from a computer. Try them on your own phone to see what works.

### Which site is better for me?

Neither is better for everyone. LearnEnglish has a big spread of lessons, podcasts and a level test. Cambridge English is strong for writing feedback and exam practice. Using both is fine.

### Will this help me pass an exam like IELTS?

Regular practice builds the skills those exams test, but we cannot promise any score. If you plan to take an exam, read the exam body's own information and consider asking a qualified teacher.

### How long until I improve?

It depends on your starting level, how often you practise and how you use English outside the lessons. Anyone who gives a fixed number of weeks cannot know that for you.

## What to do next

1. Open the LearnEnglish site, create a free account and take the level test this week.
2. Pick one listening lesson at your level and follow the 20-minute plan for seven days.
3. Try Write & Improve once with a short paragraph about your day.
4. Tell one person what you are doing. It is easier to continue when someone asks how it is going.

You do not need to be perfect. You only need to show up tomorrow, and the day after.
