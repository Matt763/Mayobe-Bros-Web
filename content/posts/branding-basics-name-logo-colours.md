Every small business starts as an idea in one person's head. A brand is how that idea becomes something other people recognise and remember. You do not need an agency or a big budget to start. You need a few clear choices, made once and kept.

## The short answer

A brand is what customers think of when they think of your business. Your name, logo, colours and fonts are the part people can see. Keep them simple and use them the same way everywhere.

- Pick a name that is easy to say, spell and search for.
- Choose two colours and one font, and stick with them.
- Keep the logo plain enough to read on a small phone screen and on a signboard.
- Use the same look on your sign, receipts, social pages and Google listing.
- Registering a business name with BRELA is a separate task from design.

## What is a brand, really?

The Queensland Government's small business guide describes a brand as what customers and potential customers think of when they think of your business. It includes things you can touch, like your shop, and things you cannot, like your reputation and service. It calls brand identity the part people can see, hear and experience: the business name, logo, tagline, colours, fonts, graphics and images.

So a logo alone is not a brand. If your logo looks smart but you reply slowly and give the wrong change, people remember that. The same guide says what your brand says and does should be the same.

That is good news. You can start building a brand today, because the biggest part is how you treat customers.

## How do I choose a business name?

The Queensland guide suggests a name or logo should be simple, memorable, ownable (clearly yours), enduring and versatile. Here is how to turn that into a test you can do in an afternoon.

1. **Say it.** Say the name out loud to three people. Can they repeat it?
2. **Spell it.** Ask them to write it. If they get it wrong, it may be too hard.
3. **Search it.** Type it into Google, Instagram and Facebook. Is there a business with a very similar name doing something similar? If so, people may mix you up. Choose another.
4. **Check it will still fit.** If you may add products later, avoid a name that boxes you in, like "Mama Neema Bicycle Spare Parts" if you may sell motorcycle parts next year.
5. **Think of both languages.** In Tanzania, many customers use Kiswahili and English. Check the name does not sound odd or rude in either.
6. **Keep it short.** Short names fit on signboards, receipts and phone screens.

Do not copy a famous name or a name that is very close to one. It can confuse customers, and it can cause legal trouble. For questions about trademarks and intellectual property, the Queensland guide says to always seek legal advice.

## How do I pick colours, a font and a logo?

Choose few things and repeat them.

### Two colours

Pick one main colour and one second colour. Add white or black for text and background. Look at them on a phone screen in daylight. Is the text easy to read on the background? If you have to squint, change it.

Colours are your choice, not a rule. A paint shop, a cloth seller and a pharmacy may all want different feelings. Pick what suits you and your customers, and do not copy another business's colours.

### One font

Use one clear font for your name and text. A plain, easy-to-read font is better than a fancy one that people cannot read from across the road. Check the font's licence if you plan to use it on a printed sign.

### A simple logo

A good small-business logo can be your name in your chosen font and colour, with one simple shape if you like. Test it:

- Does it look fine very small, like on a WhatsApp profile picture?
- Does it still work in one colour, for example on a stamp or a receipt?
- Is it your own work, not a copy or an edited version of someone else's logo?

Only use pictures and symbols you have the right to use. If a freelance designer makes it, agree in writing who owns the finished design.

## How do I use the same look everywhere?

This is where small businesses can beat bigger ones. The Queensland guide says to apply your brand rules to every place customers meet you: website, social media, brochures, vehicles, uniforms. It also suggests writing the rules down in a style guide.

Yours can fit on one page:

- Business name, written exactly one way.
- Two colours, with their codes if you use a phone design app.
- One font.
- Where the logo goes on a sign, a receipt and a social media profile.
- How you greet customers, in one sentence.

Then check each place:

- **Signboard or stall:** same name and colours.
- **WhatsApp Business or social pages:** same profile picture, same name.
- **Receipts and notebooks:** name and phone number at the top.
- **Bags, boxes or uniforms:** a small logo or name.
- **Phone number:** the same one everywhere.

## What name should I use on Google?

If your business appears on Google Maps, the name matters. Google's Business Profile help says your name should reflect your business's real-world name, as it appears on your storefront, website and stationery and as customers know it. It says not to add extra information, such as keywords, hours or taglines.

So if your sign says "Neema Fashions", use "Neema Fashions" on Google, not "Neema Fashions Best Cheap Tailor Mwanza Open 8am". Unneeded additions can cause problems with the listing. This is why a simple, steady name helps: you set it once.

## Is registering a name with BRELA part of branding?

No. Branding is how you look and sound to customers. Registration is a legal step. They are different jobs.

As checked in November 2026, the BRELA page on registering business names says (the page is in Kiswahili):

- One person, or a group of up to 20 people, may register a business name.
- The applicant must be Tanzanian and at least 18 years old.
- The steps are done online in BRELA's registration system.
- Payment is by mobile money or bank, and the application is processed after payment is received.

The page lists no fees and has no separate name-check step, so we cannot tell you the cost. BRELA lists trade and service mark registration as a separate service from business name registration. We could not read the trade mark page, so we do not explain it here. Ask BRELA for the current steps, fees and what each registration protects. Check brela.go.tz.

## Common questions

### Do I need a professional logo designer?

Not to begin. A clear name in a clear font and two colours can do the job. A designer can help later if your business grows. Agree on ownership first.

### Can I change my name or colours later?

You can, but people need time to learn it. Change rarely, and tell customers when you do.

### Should my name include what I sell?

It can help people understand you, but it can also limit you later. Choose based on your plans for the next few years.

### Is a brand the same as a trademark?

No. A brand is how customers know you. A trade mark is a legal registration. BRELA treats it as a separate service. Ask BRELA or a lawyer.

## What to do next

1. Write your business name on paper and run the say, spell and search test with three people.
2. Pick two colours and one font. Write them on one page.
3. Check that your sign, WhatsApp, social page and Google listing all show the same name.
4. Visit brela.go.tz or ask BRELA about registering your business name, and ask what each step costs.

Great businesses are often remembered for small, steady things done the same way every day. Start with yours.
