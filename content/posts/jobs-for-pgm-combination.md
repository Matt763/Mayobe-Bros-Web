PGM means Physics, Geography and Mathematics (Advanced Mathematics) as your three principal subjects in Form Five and Form Six. The government list of combinations, called *tahasusi* in Kiswahili, shows it as number 30. Many students pick it without knowing how many doors it opens: mapping, land, town planning, surveying, water and some engineering degrees. This guide is for Form Four leavers choosing a combination, Form Six students waiting for results, and parents helping with the decision.

We read the entry rules below in the TCU admission guidebook for the 2026/27 cycle in November 2026. Rules change every year and differ between universities, so treat this as a map and check the latest guidebook before you apply.

## Key points

- PGM keeps Physics and Advanced Mathematics, the pair that most engineering entries ask for, and adds Geography, which many land, mapping and planning degrees accept.
- Most degrees named here set a general minimum of 4.0 points from two principal passes.
- Two engineering routes stand out for PGM: mining engineering at one university, where Geography can stand in for Chemistry at A-Level, and aircraft maintenance engineering, which asks for Advanced Mathematics and Physics.
- PGM does not lead to medicine, pharmacy, nursing or the main geology degree at UDSM, which asks for Chemistry.
- A place is never guaranteed. Pay and job openings vary a lot, and we could not confirm salary figures from an official source, so we give none.

## How the points work

The Tanzania Commission for Universities (TCU) publishes a guidebook each year for Form Six leavers. It lists every degree, the subjects asked for, the minimum points, the number of places and the years of study.

For results from 2016 onward, the guidebook counts A = 5, B = 4, C = 3, D = 2, E = 1 and S = 0.5. The general minimum is 4.0 points from two principal passes in the subjects that define the programme.

Here is a hypothetical example. Say you get C in Physics, C in Geography and D in Advanced Mathematics. For a degree that asks for Physics and Advanced Mathematics, that is 3 + 2 = 5 points. For a degree that accepts any two of your three subjects, your best two are Physics and Geography, 3 + 3 = 6 points. Universities usually receive more applicants than places, so grades well above the minimum matter.

## What each PGM subject does for you

- **Advanced Mathematics** is the engine. It appears in almost every entry in the table. A student who is weak in Mathematics will find the doors narrow.
- **Physics** sits next to it in engineering, meteorology, hydrography and mining entries. It is also the subject that surveying instruments and mapping technology rely on.
- **Geography** is what makes PGM different from PCM. It appears in the lists for land valuation, planning, GIS and remote sensing, mining at UDOM, and the geology science degree at UDSM.

One practical warning. Some entries ask for a credit in Chemistry at O-Level even when Chemistry is not your principal subject. The UDOM Applied Geology entry asks for at least C in Physics and Chemistry at O-Level. If you choose PGM, do not let your Chemistry go in Form Three and Form Four.

## Jobs these degrees can lead to

We describe kinds of work, not salaries or job numbers. Pay differs between government, private and mission employers.

- **Mapping and land.** Geomatics and GIS work with measurements, satellite pictures and digital maps. Land management and valuation deal with plots, property and land records.
- **Planning.** Urban, regional and housing planning help towns grow with roads, water and services. Local councils and consulting firms employ planners.
- **Construction cost and building.** Quantity surveyors estimate and track the cost of building work.
- **Engineering, mining and geology.** Civil and mining engineering, and geology-linked degrees, lead to work in construction, minerals and energy.
- **Aircraft maintenance.** Aircraft maintenance engineering trains people to inspect and keep aircraft safe. The guidebook lists it at two institutions. Check the Chemistry condition in the entry, because PGM does not include Chemistry.
- **Weather and water.** Meteorology and hydrographic studies serve weather services, ports and water projects.
- **Teaching.** A Bachelor of Science with Education accepts Geography, Physics and Advanced Mathematics.

Several of these fields have professional boards that register graduates. For example, engineering is a regulated profession, and the Engineers Registration Board says registration is the only legal authorisation to practise engineering in Tanzania. The Architects and Quantity Surveyors Registration Board handles registration for architects and quantity surveyors. We could not confirm the exact steps for each profession, so ask your university what applies after you graduate.

## A message for the student who is afraid of choosing wrong

Imagine you are in Form Four. A friend says "take PCM, everyone does". Another says Geography is easy and you should avoid it. You are not sure whom to trust, and you know money for your education came with sacrifice.

Here is something honest. PGM is not the easy road. Advanced Mathematics and Physics ask for steady work. But it is also a combination for people who like to see the world as a map, who ask where a road should go, how land should be shared, and how water moves. If that is you, PGM can fit you well.

Nobody can promise you a place or a job. What you can do is choose with your eyes open, then give it your effort week by week, not in the last month. That effort is the part nobody can take from you.

## If you are in Form Four: is PGM right for you?

Check these before you list PGM first.

1. **Mathematics.** Do you enjoy it and score well? You will need Advanced Mathematics.
2. **Physics.** Can you explain a topic in your own words, not only repeat notes?
3. **Geography.** Do you like maps, land, climate and how places change?
4. **Chemistry.** Keep it strong anyway. Some degrees ask for a credit at O-Level.
5. **Your goal.** Write down three careers. Search each degree in the TCU guidebook and check that PGM appears.
6. **Selection rules.** The official TAMISEMI list shows PGM at number 30. Ask your school what grades Form Five selection needs in your year.

If your heart is in medicine, read our [PCB guide](/post/educational/study-tips/jobs-for-pcb-combination/) instead.

## If you are in Form Six: choosing a degree

Use our guide on [how to read your Form Six results for university entry](/post/educational/results/how-to-read-acsee-results-points-and-divisions/) to work out your points. Then:

1. Put your points next to the minimum for each degree you like.
2. List the universities that offer it. Entries differ between universities.
3. Apply only through the official university and TCU systems. Nobody can sell you a place.
4. Plan how you will pay. Our guide on [HESLB student loans](/post/educational/study-tips/students-loan-tanzania-complete-guide/) explains the process.

## Where PGM does not fit

Health degrees are the main example. Medicine, dentistry, pharmacy and medical laboratory sciences list Physics, Chemistry and Biology. Nursing asks for Chemistry and Biology. A PGM student has neither, so these routes are closed.

Geology needs care. The UDSM Bachelor of Science in Geology asks for three principal passes in Advanced Mathematics, Physics and Chemistry, so PGM does not meet it. But two other geology-linked entries do accept Geography: the UDSM Bachelor of Science with Geology and the UDOM Applied Geology degree, with the O-Level conditions noted above.

Law and business degrees are open to many subjects. The UDSM Bachelor of Laws lists Geography, Physics and Advanced Mathematics among accepted subjects, with a subsidiary or O-Level pass in History and English. PGM does not block you, but it is not built for these paths.

## What if your points are not enough?

Many students miss their first choice. You still have roads.

1. **Apply to a related degree.** If Geomatics is full, look at GIS, land management, planning or a science degree with Education. Read each entry.
2. **Take the diploma route.** Technical colleges offer diplomas in land surveying, civil engineering and more under NACTVET. Our posts on [NTA levels](/post/educational/career-development/nta-levels-explained-tanzania/) and [going from diploma to degree](/post/educational/career-development/diploma-to-degree-tanzania-entry-requirements/) explain how. Check that the college and programme are accredited before you pay.
3. **Retake.** Some students sit examinations again. This costs time and money, so ask your teachers first.

## Common questions

### What does PGM stand for in Tanzania?

PGM stands for Physics, Geography and Mathematics. The TAMISEMI list of combinations shows it as number 30.

### Can a PGM student study engineering?

Yes, in some cases. Civil engineering at UDSM asks for Advanced Mathematics and Physics, which PGM includes. Some entries add Chemistry conditions, so read each one.

### Can a PGM student become a geologist?

Partly. The main UDSM geology degree asks for Chemistry, but a science degree with Geology and the UDOM Applied Geology programme accept Geography.

### Is PGM good for surveying?

The guidebook lists Geomatics and Quantity Surveying degrees that accept PGM subjects. Land surveying at diploma level is another route. Check the entry rules.

### Which is better, PGM or PCM?

Neither. PCM suits engineering and chemistry-linked work. PGM suits land, mapping and planning. Choose by the careers you want and the subjects you do well in.

## What to do next

1. Download the latest TCU guidebook for Form Six holders and search for three degrees from the table.
2. Write down the subjects, points and years for each, and the universities that offer them.
3. Check your marks in Mathematics and Physics. Start extra practice now if one is low.
4. Keep a backup plan, such as a related degree or an accredited diploma.
