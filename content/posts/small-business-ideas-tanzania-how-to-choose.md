Many people search for "biashara zenye faida Tanzania" hoping for one winning idea. No honest source can name it, because profit depends on you, your town, your capital and your customers. What you can do is choose a business that fits your situation, check the rules, and test it before you put in your savings. This post shows how.

## Key points

- Nobody can promise that one business pays best. The same shop can do well in one street and fail in the next.
- Choose by fit: your skills, your capital, your place, the rules, the competition and your cash flow.
- Check the papers early. The steps are different for a duka, a mobile money agent and a bodaboda rider.
- Test with a small version for 30 days before you buy stock or equipment in bulk.
- Keep the shop money apart from your home money from day one.

## Six questions that decide if a business fits you

Ask these before you compare ideas. Write the answers down.

1. **Skills.** What can you already do well? A tailor starts with a head start. A person who has never kept a poultry house has a lot to learn.
2. **Capital.** How much can you lose without hurting your family? That figure, not the amount you wish you had, is your budget.
3. **Location.** Where will customers find you? Foot traffic, a bus stand, a school or a market changes everything.
4. **Rules.** Does the business need a licence, a permit or a registration with a regulator? Find out before you buy anything.
5. **Competition.** How many people already sell the same thing within a short walk? If ten others do, what will you do differently?
6. **Cash flow.** Do customers pay today, or do they take goods and pay later? A business that waits for money needs more capital.

For a one-page way to put the answers together, see our post on [writing a one-page business plan](/post/business-ideas/startups/write-one-page-business-plan-small-business-tanzania/).

## Eight common small businesses compared

This table is a thinking tool. It does not rank the businesses and it gives no income figures, because we could not find a reliable official figure for any of them. The needs and risks are described in general terms.

| Business | What you usually need | Main risk to plan for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Retail kiosk or duka | Rent, shelves, first stock, council licence | Slow stock, goods on credit, expired goods |
| Mobile money agent | A registered business, TIN, tax clearance, a bank account, float | Fake payment messages, float running out |
| Poultry (chickens) | A clean house, feed, vaccines, a vet contact | Disease, feed prices, a failed batch |
| Vegetables and fruit trading | A stall or a route, quick access to suppliers | Goods spoil fast, prices swing |
| Water vending | A safe source, containers or a cart, permits | Dry supply, quality complaints |
| Tailoring | Machine, space, skill, a customer base | Few customers at first, late payments |
| Bodaboda or transport | A motorcycle, driving licence, insurance, LATRA licence | Accidents, repairs, fuel cost, rules |
| Online selling | A phone, data, clear photos, a delivery plan | Scams, returns, building trust |

## What the rules say for three of them

Rules change, so this is a snapshot. As checked on 24 November 2026, here is what the official documents say.

**Mobile money agent.** The Bank of Tanzania's Electronic Money Regulations, 2015 say a mobile money company must appoint a retail agent that has "a business registration licence, tax identification number, tax clearance certificate and other necessary permits". The same regulations say the agent must open a bank account for the agency business, and that the agency agreement must allow the agent to serve more than one company. The company, not you, deals with the Bank of Tanzania, so ask the network for its current terms.

**Bodaboda.** LATRA's Private Hire Services (Amendment) Regulations, 2026 (Government Notice No. 6, published on 16 January 2026) list an annual licence fee of TSh 20,000 for motor cycles and TSh 30,000 for motor tricycles, plus an application fee of TSh 2,000 for motorcycles and motor tricycles. We did not find a full checklist of other documents, so ask LATRA or your local bodaboda group what else applies, and check whether the fee has changed.

**Duka, vegetables, tailoring.** For these the usual path is a TIN from TRA and a licence from the council trade office. Council fees are set locally, so ask the office and pay only through the official channel. See our guide to [registering a small business](/post/business-ideas/startups/register-small-business-tanzania-brela-tin-licence/).

## Registration and tax for a very small business

Here is the short path, as read on the official pages on 24 November 2026.

- **BRELA business name.** One person, or a group of up to 20, can register a business name. The applicant must be Tanzanian, at least 18, and have a NIN, a phone in their own name and an active email.
- **TRA TIN.** TRA says a person who starts a business should apply for a TIN within fifteen days of starting. You attach a letter from the local government authority and a lease or title deed for the business place.
- **Tax clearance and licence.** After the TIN there is an assessment interview. After you meet your tax duties you get a tax clearance, which is used to get a business licence from the council or the ministry.
- **Presumptive tax.** TRA says a resident individual whose only income is from a business in Tanzania, with turnover up to TSh 200 million, can fall under the presumptive tax. Turnover up to TSh 4,000,000 is nil. TRA also says a new taxpayer pays no tax for the first 12 months if they qualify and claim it.

Turnover means total sales, not profit. For the full table with worked examples, read our post on [small business tax in Tanzania](/post/business-ideas/startups/small-business-tax-tanzania-presumptive-tax-explained/). It is general information, not tax advice, so confirm the current table with TRA.

The Ministry of Community Development, Gender, Women and Special Groups publishes a guide to registering small traders. It describes a digital ID for small traders whose yearly sales do not exceed TSh 4,000,000, and it says the ID does not remove the local fees and taxes. Ask your council trade office if you qualify.

## A simple 30-day test before you invest

The aim is to learn cheaply whether people will buy from you at a price that leaves something after costs.

1. **Pick one business** from your six answers, not three.
2. **Set a test budget** you can afford to lose, say TSh 100,000 as an example, and spend only part of it.
3. **Sell a small version.** Buy a little stock, borrow equipment, or take a few orders by phone before you rent or build anything.
4. **Write every sale and cost.** Use a notebook. Our [cash book guide](/post/business-ideas/startups/simple-business-records-small-traders-cash-book-tanzania/) takes ten minutes a day.
5. **Count what is left** after stock, transport, fees and your own time. If the result only works on your best day, change the plan.
6. **Decide on day 30.** Grow slowly, change one thing, or stop. Stopping a test is a cheap lesson, not a failure.

## Common questions

### Which business gives the highest profit in Tanzania?
We cannot say, and anyone who names one without showing their sources should be questioned. Profit depends on your costs, prices, location and customers. Use the table above as a starting point and test.

### Do I need a lot of capital?
Not always. Some businesses start small, but each has costs that surprise people, such as rent, licences and slow weeks. Keep a cash reserve and do not spend everything on stock.

### Do I have to register before I start selling?
TRA says to apply for a TIN within fifteen days of starting a business, and most businesses need a licence. Ask the council trade office what applies to yours before you sign a lease.

### How do I avoid losing money in the first months?
Start small, record every sale, avoid goods on credit, and keep your home money separate. [Pricing](/post/business-ideas/startups/how-to-price-your-products-small-business-tanzania/) that covers all your costs matters as much as sales.

## What to do next

1. Write your answers to the six questions on one page.
2. Pick the one or two businesses from the table that match, and visit your council trade office to ask what papers and fees apply.
3. Run the 30-day test with a small budget and a notebook.

A good business is usually the one you can run well, not the one that looks best on a list. Take the first small step this week.
