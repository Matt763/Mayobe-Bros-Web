History is an essay subject. Nobody can pass it by memorising dates alone, and nobody can pass it without knowing the facts. This post is for Form Five and Form Six students, and for parents and Form Four leavers choosing a combination (tahasusi). It explains how ACSEE History is examined, which topics appear, how the newer TIE syllabus differs, and how to practise.

## Key points

- NECTA's subject is **112 History**, with two papers of three hours: 112/1 and 112/2.
- In each paper there are seven essay questions. You answer five, and Question 1 is compulsory. Each question carries 20 marks.
- NECTA's 2025 report says 68,073 candidates sat and 99.67% passed, and all 13 topics examined were rated good.
- The 2023 TIE syllabus is organised around themes: Pan-Africanism, the industrial revolution, administrative systems, threats to world peace and development in Africa.
- History is part of HGK, HGL, HGE and HKL, and is useful for law, political science and heritage degrees.

## What History is at A-Level

The NECTA exam format says the paper tests whether you can interpret events that shaped the past and present, understand the problems African societies faced, and explain how Africans resisted colonial rule and later neo-colonialism. It also tests skills: reasoning, judging evidence and communicating clearly. TIE gives the subject ten 40-minute periods a week.

## The two papers and their topics

From NECTA's 2019 format, which is the version NECTA's 2025 report says the History exam followed:

**112/1 History 1**

- Pre-colonial African societies
- Africa and Europe in the 15th century
- People of African origin in the New World
- From colonialism to the First World War (1880s to 1914)
- Colonial economy and social services after the Second World War
- The influence of external forces, and the rise of nationalism and the struggle for independence
- Political and economic development in Tanzania since independence

**112/2 History 2**

- The rise of capitalism in Europe
- The rise of democracy in Europe
- Imperialism and the territorial division of the world
- The rise of dictatorships in Germany, Italy and Japan
- The rise of socialism
- The emergence of the USA as a new capitalist superpower
- Threats to world peace after the Second World War
- Neo-colonialism and underdevelopment in Third World countries

Question 1 in Paper 1 in 2025 was on political and economic development in Tanzania since independence, and it asked candidates to show how post-colonial economic problems came from colonial policies. That is a good picture of the exam: a big idea, supported by evidence from across the topic.

### What the 2025 report says

NECTA says 13 of the 15 topics were examined in 2025. "From colonialism to the First World War" and "the rise of dictatorships" were not. The topic scores were all rated good. The highest was 96.52% for People of African Origin in the New World. The report's advice to teachers and students is plain: read widely, read each question carefully, improve English, and use group discussion and debate.

A topic left out one year can come back the next. Do not guess which ones to skip.

## Topics in the 2023 TIE syllabus

TIE's "History Syllabus for Advanced Secondary Education Form V-VI" (2023) is built from themes. Here is our summary.

**Form Five**

- Pan-Africanism: its meaning and origins in the 15th century, its growth through slavery and the Back to Africa movement, its role in anti-colonial struggles up to 1960, and its story since 1960.
- The industrial revolution: Britain compared with Belgium, France, Germany, the USA and Japan; revolutions of the 20th century in Asia and Latin America; the Fourth Industrial Revolution; and industrial development in Africa since the 1960s.
- Administrative systems of the world: ancient and indigenous systems, religion and government, slavery and feudalism, European political revolutions, the crises of capitalism and new systems, and administration in post-colonial Africa.
- A project on a historical topic, with a report.

**Form Six**

- Threats to world peace: great wars of the 17th to 19th centuries, the world wars, the Cold War and arms control, new threats such as terrorism and cyber threats, and civil and ethnic wars.
- Development in post-colonial Africa and the factors that hold it back.
- Completing the project.

Notice how the newer syllabus asks more often for comparison and judgement ("discuss", "examine"). That is the habit to build whichever syllabus you follow. The syllabus also gives 30% of the final mark to school work (including a 7% project and a 7% mock exam) and 70% to the national exam.

### Which syllabus applies to you?

NECTA's 2025 report says that year's History exam used the 2009 syllabus and the 2019 format. TIE lists the 2023 syllabus, and the 2023 curriculum for Form Five and Six also adds two compulsory subjects, Academic Communication and Historia ya Tanzania na Maadili. The Form Five rollout was postponed in 2024, according to The Citizen on 28 July 2024, and Form Five books for the improved curriculum began to be distributed on 24 June 2025. We could not find an official page that says which Form Six exam year is the first to use the 2023 syllabus. As checked on 24 November 2026, ask your school and read NECTA's current format.

## How to study History

- **Build a timeline for each topic.** Put the key dates, people and events on one page. Add causes and results.
- **Learn the essay shape.** Introduce the issue, give three or four points, explain each with evidence, and end with a short judgement.
- **Answer the verb.** "Discuss" means more than "describe". NECTA's report says misreading questions causes off-topic answers.
- **Read beyond your notes.** The report urges wide reading. Use the school library and official textbooks.
- **Practise under time.** Five essays in three hours is about 36 minutes each, including thinking. Practise writing one in 35 minutes.
- **Work on English.** NECTA names weak English as a major reason for lost marks. Learn the key words of each topic, such as colonialism, nationalism and imperialism.
- **Debate with friends.** The new syllabus itself suggests debates and group work.

## Common questions

### Is History only about memorising?

No. The marks go to explaining and judging. NECTA's report asks teachers to train students to read questions and to write on the point asked.

### Is History the same as Historia ya Tanzania na Maadili?

No. History (112) is an A-Level subject you choose in a combination. Historia ya Tanzania na Maadili is a separate compulsory subject in the 2023 curriculum.

### Can History lead to law?

It can. The TCU guidebook lists Bachelor of Laws among programmes that accept two principal passes from a list that includes History. Check the current conditions for each university.

### How many questions must I answer?

Five in each paper. Question 1 is compulsory in the format we read.

## What to do next

1. Download the TIE History syllabus and make a checklist of topics for your form.
2. Choose one topic a week, build a timeline, and write one timed essay.
3. Ask your teacher to mark one essay a fortnight and note the comments.
4. Read the NECTA History report once and underline the examiners' advice.

Good history writers are patient readers first. Read a little every day.
