When rent is due, school fees are late or the shop has run out of stock, an advert that says "get cash in minutes" can feel like a hand reaching out. That feeling is normal, and there is no shame in it. This article gives you a calm way to check a loan app before you trust it with your phone, your ID number or your money.

## The short answer

Before you borrow from any app, look the lender up on the Bank of Tanzania (BoT) website. The BoT publishes a list of licensed institutions and approved digital lenders, and it also publishes a list of digital lending platforms and apps that it has not approved. As checked in November 2026, the BoT page says the unapproved list runs up to 31 December 2025 and will be updated.

- If the lender is not on the BoT licensed list, treat it as unapproved.
- Never pay a fee to "release" or "unlock" a loan.
- Read the terms before you press Install, not after.
- Report an unlisted lender to the BoT, and report fraud to the police.
- Nothing here can promise that a loan is safe. A licence is a good sign, not a guarantee.

## Why unapproved loan apps are a risk

The BoT says that offering loans through digital platforms or apps without its approval is an offence under section 16 of the Microfinance Act of 2018 (in Swahili, Sheria ya Huduma Ndogo za Fedha). That is the law on the lender's side.

There is also a risk on your side. The BoT page says people who deal with unapproved platforms may lose the protection they would have under the Bank's financial consumer protection rules (Consumer Protection Regulations of 2019, as amended). In simple words: if something goes wrong with a lender that is not approved, the rules that normally help borrowers may not reach you.

Some "loan apps" are also not lenders at all. They only want your phone number, your ID details or an upfront "fee". We cannot tell you how common this is, because we found no official figure. What we can say is that the check below takes five minutes and costs nothing.

## How do I check a lender with the Bank of Tanzania?

Use the BoT website itself, not a link from an advert, a message or a friend's forward. Type the address in your browser.

1. Open the BoT page on digital lending, in the Bank Supervision section of www.bot.go.tz. The page is in Swahili. Ask a friend or use your browser's translate option if you need help.
2. Find the list of licensed institutions and approved digital lenders. Search for the exact name of the lender, spelled the way it appears in the app store or advert.
3. Look at the list of unapproved platforms and apps. If the name is there, do not borrow.
4. If the name is on neither list, do not assume it is fine. The BoT asks the public to report any digital lender that does not appear on the licensed list.
5. Write down the date you checked. Lists change, and an app that is missing today may be listed later.

We do not name any app in this article, as safe or unsafe. Names come and go, and only the BoT list can tell you the position on the day you look. We also did not find a second official source that lists licensed names, so please rely on the BoT pages.

## Red flags before you press Install

A licence check comes first. These signs are a second layer of care. Most of them are common sense, and where that is the case we say so.

| Red flag | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| The lender asks you to pay a fee first to "release", "insure" or "unlock" the loan | Stop. Do not send money. A real lender takes costs from the loan agreement, and you can ask to see that agreement in writing |
| The lender is not on the BoT licensed list | Do not borrow. Consider reporting it to the BoT |
| You cannot find clear terms: the amount you repay, the date, and every charge | Do not install until you can read them |
| The advert or message pushes you to hurry ("only today", "last chance") | Slow down. Hurry is how people skip checks |
| The link came in an SMS or from an unknown sender | TCRA, the communications regulator, says not to click links from unknown senders |
| The app asks for access it does not seem to need, such as contacts or photos | Think twice. This is our common-sense advice, not an official rule |
| No real business name, address or working contact details | Treat it as a warning |

The same habits protect you in other places. Our guide on [fake payment messages](/post/business-ideas/e-commerce/fake-mobile-money-payment-messages-sellers-tanzania/) and the post on the [mobile money send-back scam](/post/technology-solutions/cyber-security/mobile-money-send-back-scam-wrong-number/) show the same pattern of hurry and pressure.

## Before you borrow, ask yourself three plain questions

- How much will I repay in total, and by which date? If the app cannot tell you clearly, that is your answer.
- Can I repay it from money I already expect to receive? Say you borrow TSh 100,000 and the total to repay is TSh 120,000. You need TSh 20,000 on top of what you borrowed, so check that your plan can carry it.
- Is there a safer option, such as asking family, an employer, a savings group or a licensed institution? We do not give debt advice, and we cannot say what a fair interest rate is. A qualified adviser can help if the sums worry you.

## If you have already borrowed or are being threatened

If you have already installed an app and something feels wrong, you are not alone and you have options. Take these steps in order.

1. Stay calm and do not send more money to "fix" the problem. Extra fees often lead to more demands.
2. Keep proof: screenshots of messages, the lender's name, phone numbers, dates and any payment receipts.
3. If you were tricked or threatened, report it to a police station. TCRA says fraud is a criminal matter and the police give you an RB number when your report is registered.
4. Report the scam phone number by SMS to 15040. TCRA's pages describe the word as "Fraud" (2022) and "UTAPELI" (in its FAQ), so follow the automated reply that comes back.
5. Report the lender to the BoT, which asks the public to tell it about any digital lender that is not on its licensed list. Contact details are on the BoT website.
6. Tell someone you trust. Threats are meant to make you feel alone, and talking to a friend or relative takes some of their power away.

We cannot give legal advice. If the situation is serious, speak to a lawyer or a legal aid office.

## Common questions

### Is a loan app on the Play Store or App Store automatically safe?

No. Being in an app store does not show that a lender is licensed in Tanzania. Check the lender on the BoT lists.

### What if the app name is not on either BoT list?

Do not borrow, and do not treat "not listed as unapproved" as "approved". The BoT asks people to report digital lenders that are not on the licensed list.

### Should I pay a small fee to get my loan approved?

No. A request for money before you receive a loan is a classic warning sign. Stop and check the lender with the BoT.

### Where do I complain about a financial service?

TCRA's FAQ says non-criminal complaints about financial services go to the Bank of Tanzania. Fraud goes to the police.

## What to do next

1. Open the BoT website now and find the licensed list, so you know where it is the next time you need it.
2. Share the five-step check with one family member or friend who might be tempted by a loan advert.
3. If you are in a tight spot today, write down the real total cost and the date you can repay before you accept any offer.

Needing help with money is not a failing. Taking five minutes to check is a way of looking after yourself and the people who depend on you.
