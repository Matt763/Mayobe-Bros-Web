Many Tanzanian families still carry the memory of the Maji Maji war. It is a hard story, and it deserves to be told plainly and with respect. This post explains what the war was, why it began, how it ended, and why historians still do not agree on how many people died.

## The short answer

The Maji Maji war was an uprising against German colonial rule in German East Africa, the land that is now mainland Tanzania. It lasted from 1905 to 1907. People rose because of heavy taxes, forced labour and pressure to grow cash crops such as cotton.

- Germany ruled the colony from 1885 to 1918.
- The war began in July 1905 and was not fully put down until 1907.
- It spread over about 26,000 square kilometres and involved more than twenty ethnic groups.
- Most deaths came from famine after German forces destroyed crops and villages.
- Estimates of the dead run from about 75,000 to 300,000, depending on the source.

## Where and when did it happen?

Germany claimed this territory in the 1880s. According to World History Encyclopedia, its government took over from the German East Africa Company in 1891, and German rule ended with Germany's defeat in the First World War. Britain took over afterwards.

The rebellion took place mostly in the south and east of the colony. World History Encyclopedia says it covered about 10,000 square miles, which is about 26,000 square kilometres. Encyclopedia.com says it was organised across ethnic lines and involved over twenty different ethnic groups.

That detail matters. Communities that had different languages and different leaders joined in one movement against the same rulers. That is part of why the war is remembered as a movement across communities.

## Why did the Maji Maji war start?

There was no single cause. Several pressures arrived together, and they landed on people who already had little.

**Taxes.** World History Encyclopedia says the revolt was driven by the "extortionate taxes" of the colonial administration. A summary on Wikipedia, which cites historians, says a head tax began in 1898. A head tax is a payment due from each person. 

**Forced labour and low pay.** Both World History Encyclopedia and Encyclopedia.com name forced labour among the main causes. People were made to work on roads, plantations and other projects, and wages were low.

**Cotton.** Encyclopedia.com says that harsh demands for cotton farming, forced labour and taxation set off the rebellion in 1905. The Wikipedia summary adds that the governor ordered villages to grow cotton for export in 1902, and that cotton needs a lot of water and does not suit much of the country. Village headmen were told to meet quotas, which put them at odds with their own people.

Think about what that means for a family. Time spent on a cotton plot is time not spent on food crops. If the cotton fails, there is still a tax to pay.

**Drought.** The same Wikipedia summary says a drought in 1905 added to the strain. We could not find this point in a second source, so read it as a likely extra pressure, not a proven cause.

**Other changes.** World History Encyclopedia also lists the suppression of traditional practices as a grievance. Together, these things left many people feeling they had no way to be heard.

## What does maji mean, and who was Kinjikitile?

"Maji" is the Swahili word for water. In the name Maji Maji, it refers to a belief that spread through the movement.

World History Encyclopedia says the revolt was led by followers of Kinjikitili, a prophet, who claimed that specially prepared water could protect people from bullets. Encyclopedia.com names him as Kinjikitile Ngwale, an indigenous prophet who died in 1905.

Here is where we should be careful. What people believed is part of history, and it shows how much hope the movement carried. The water could not stop bullets, and the German response was, in the words of Encyclopedia.com, brutal. The Wikipedia summary says German forces arrested and hanged Kinjikitile. We read this in only one general source, so treat it as likely but not confirmed here.

We have also left out detailed stories about speeches and rituals. Many of those accounts come from later retellings, and we cannot confirm them.

## How did the war begin and how did it end?

The Wikipedia summary says fighters from the Matumbi hills marched on a place called Samanga on 31 July 1905 and destroyed a cotton crop and a trading post. It says the uprising then spread across the south and east.

The German response grew over time. The same summary says the governor asked for reinforcements and that about 1,000 regular soldiers arrived in October 1905. Columns then moved south. It says fighting in some areas continued until August 1907.

World History Encyclopedia gives the main reason the rebellion failed: German forces deliberately destroyed crops and created famine. When people cannot plant, harvest or store food, they cannot keep fighting, and they cannot keep their children fed.

This is the hardest part of the story. Most of the people who died were not killed in battle. They died of hunger and the illness that follows it. We will not describe it in more detail. The facts are enough.

## What changed afterwards?

World History Encyclopedia says some minor reforms followed, if only to prevent future uprisings. It also says the authorities promoted schools to train local people for the civil service, local government and the police.

So the colonial government did change some of its methods. That should not hide what came first. The reforms came after a famine in which a very large number of Africans had died.

## How many people died?

No one knows the exact number. Every source we opened gives a different figure, and they are estimates, not counts.

| Source | What it says |
| --- | --- |
| Encyclopedia.com | More than 75,000 Africans killed |
| World History Encyclopedia | As many as 250,000 African lives lost to the famine |
| France 24, reporting on experts | 200,000 to 300,000 members of the local population killed |
| Wikipedia summary, citing historians | 75,000 to 300,000, with most from famine |

Why such a wide range? Deaths from hunger and disease are hard to separate from deaths in fighting, and sources may count different areas and time periods. We could not find one agreed count in any source we opened.

The fair way to say it is this: tens of thousands at the lowest estimate, and possibly hundreds of thousands at the highest. We have not picked one number, because no source we opened can prove one.

Some historians call the German campaign genocide, and others dispute that word. We report both views. What every source agrees on is that the loss of life was enormous and that the destruction of food was deliberate.

## How is the Maji Maji war remembered?

The war is remembered in southern Tanzania, and Songea is home to the Maji Maji Museum. On 1 November 2023, France 24 reports, German President Frank-Walter Steinmeier spoke at that museum on the last day of a three-day visit.

He said he felt shame over crimes committed during German colonial rule. He said, "I would like to ask for forgiveness for what Germans did to your ancestors here." France 24 also reports that he promised to help find answers to open questions and to work with Tanzania on how the past is dealt with together.

The same report quotes John Mbano, a Tanzanian lawyer who is a descendant of Chief Songea Mbano, who was killed in the rebellion. He welcomed the words and said: "We have been crying for years, now it is the time to end our crying."

France 24 also reports that Berlin's Museum of Prehistory and Early History has studied about 1,100 skulls from German East Africa since 2017 with the aim of returning them, and that in September 2023 it said it had found living relatives in Tanzania of some of the people whose skulls were taken. Germany said it was ready to work with Tanzania on returning human remains and cultural objects.

## Common questions

### Was Maji Maji one war or many local risings?
Sources describe one movement that spread across many communities. Historians still discuss how united it really was, and the Wikipedia summary notes that later writers questioned the early view of a single plan.

### Did the water really protect people from bullets?
No. The belief shows how much hope the movement carried, but the German response was brutal and very many people died.

### Why do the death numbers differ so much?
Hunger deaths are hard to count, and sources may use different areas and years. Treat any single number with caution.

### Is it only a southern story?
The war took place mostly in the south and east of the colony, and it involved more than twenty ethnic groups.
