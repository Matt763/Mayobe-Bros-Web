You open the results page and, where you hoped to see a grade, there is a letter you do not know. For a candidate or a parent, that is a frightening moment. Take a breath. Most of these codes have a plain meaning, and several of them can be solved with a phone call or a payment receipt.

## The short answer

NECTA explains its result codes on its FAQ page. These codes are not grades. They tell you that something is missing, held back or did not happen.

- **ABS:** the candidate missed the exam.
- **X:** the candidate did not appear for one registered subject.
- **I:** the result is incomplete because continuous assessment scores are missing.
- **E:** the result is withheld until proof of payment of the exam fee.
- **W:** the result is withheld, nullified or cancelled because of proven dishonesty or irregularities.
- **FLD:** the candidate sat the exam and failed.

As checked in November 2026, these meanings are on NECTA's FAQ page. Use that page as the official source if you need the exact wording.

## Where you will see these codes

On NECTA's published school result pages, each candidate has a row. In the school pages we opened for the 2025 Form Four results, X appeared next to a subject, and ABS appeared in the division column. So a letter beside one subject points to that subject. A code in the division column points to the whole result.

Read the row slowly before you decide what it means. A single X does not mean the whole exam went wrong. It only says that one registered subject was not sat.

## Codes for missing or incomplete results

These are the quieter codes. They do not blame anyone, but they do need action.

**I means incomplete.** NECTA says this happens when continuous assessment (CA) scores are missing, for all the subjects or for only some of them. Continuous assessment means the marks a school records through the year. If they are missing, NECTA cannot complete the result. This is usually a records question. Start by asking your school's academic office whether the scores were sent, and ask NECTA if the school cannot explain.

**X means a subject was not sat.** The candidate was registered for the subject but did not appear. If you did sit that subject, tell your school and NECTA at once, with your candidate number and any proof you have, such as your examination slip.

## Codes for withheld results

**E means withheld until proof of fee payment.** NECTA's wording is that results are withheld pending proof that the candidate paid the required examination fees. This is the most fixable code. NECTA lists a service called Release of Exam Results for candidates whose results are withheld because of late payment of the exam fee. If you paid, find your payment receipt and take it to your school and to NECTA. If the fee is still unpaid, ask NECTA what is needed to release the result.

**W means withheld, nullified or cancelled for proven dishonesty.** NECTA uses this code when a candidate was proven to have been involved in dishonesty or irregularities before, during or after the exam. This is the hardest code to see, and we will not guess about any single case. If you or your child sees W and believes it is a mistake, contact NECTA directly and ask what the process is. NECTA also lists an Appeals service for candidates who disagree with their results, and the page describes it as a formal, clear and fair follow-up. Ask NECTA how to use it.

We cannot give tips on getting around a W. The only honest route is to ask NECTA and follow what it tells you.

## Absent and failed codes

**ABS means the candidate missed the exam.** It may show in the division column. If the candidate was truly absent, that is the record. If the candidate sat the exam and still sees ABS, contact the school and NECTA straight away.

**FLD means the candidate sat and failed.** This one is hard to read, but it is not the end. A failed result is still a result, and there are routes forward. Our post on [what to do after poor Form Four results](/post/educational/results/what-to-do-after-poor-form-four-results/) lays them out.

## What to do if your result is withheld

Here is a calm order of steps. You do not need to do all of them if the first one solves it.

1. Write down your candidate number and exactly what the page shows. Take a clear screenshot.
2. Match the code to the list above. This tells you whether it is about fees, scores, a missing subject or something else.
3. Gather your papers: your exam slip, school fee or exam fee receipts, and your school's contact details.
4. Ask your school first. For E and I, the school often holds the answer.
5. Contact NECTA. The CSEE page lists the email esnecta@necta.go.tz and the phone numbers 0738743381 and 0262610690. Say your candidate number, the code and what you have already checked.
6. Be patient and keep records of every call and message. Do not pay anyone who says they can "fix" or "release" a result for a private fee. NECTA's own services page lists Release of Exam Results as an official service.

## Common questions

### Is ABS the same as failing?

No. ABS means the candidate missed the exam. FLD means the candidate sat the exam and failed. They are different records.

### Does X mean my whole result is cancelled?

No. X refers to one registered subject that the candidate did not appear for. The rest of the row may still show grades.

### Can a withheld result be released?

For E, NECTA offers a Release of Exam Results service for results withheld because of late payment of the fee. For other codes, ask NECTA what applies to your case.

### What if I disagree with my result?

NECTA lists an Appeals service for candidates who disagree with results. We could not confirm the steps or any cost, so ask NECTA directly.

### Who do I contact at NECTA?

The CSEE page lists the email esnecta@necta.go.tz and the phone numbers 0738743381 and 0262610690. Include your candidate number in every message.

## What to do next

1. Find your candidate number and the code on the result page, and match it to the meaning above.
2. If it is E or I, contact your school this week and bring any receipts or records.
3. If it is W or something you do not understand, write to NECTA at esnecta@necta.go.tz with your candidate number.
4. If you want to read how a normal result is laid out, see [how to read your Form Four results](/post/educational/results/how-to-read-csee-results-divisions-points/).

A code on a screen is information, not a verdict on you. Many families find that one honest phone call sorts it out.
