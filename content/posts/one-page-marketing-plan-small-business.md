Many small owners try everything at once. A poster on Monday, a social media page on Tuesday, a flyer on Wednesday. By the end of the month they are tired, the money is gone, and they cannot say what worked.

You can do better with less. A marketing plan does not need to be a thick document. One page is enough, and a page you actually follow is worth more than a long plan in a drawer.

## The short answer

A one-page plan has five parts: one goal you can measure, one kind of customer, two ways of reaching them, a small budget with a weekly routine, and a monthly check where you change only one thing. Write it in a notebook or on one sheet of paper and keep it where you work.

- Pick one goal with a number and a date.
- Name the customer you most want to reach.
- Choose only two channels that you can keep up every week.
- Set a budget you can lose without harm, and say how you will measure it.
- Review once a month and change one thing at a time.

## Why a short plan beats a big one

Business Queensland, an Australian government service, gives plain advice in its guide to writing a marketing plan: "Don't try to do everything at once." It says to spread the work out according to the time, skills and people you have. It also says to test ideas with friendly customers before you commit, and to check regularly what is working.

That advice fits a one-person shop as well as it fits a company. We use the Queensland guide for its method only. None of its figures or rules apply to Tanzania, and all the TSh amounts below are examples we made up.

If you have not yet found out who your customers are, do that first. Our post on [how to know your customers](/post/business-ideas/marketing/know-your-customers-small-business/) takes about a week and costs nothing.

## Step 1: Write one goal you can measure

"Get more customers" is not a goal. You cannot tell when you have reached it.

Business Queensland describes a "SMART" goal. It is specific, measurable, achievable, relevant and has a time limit. In plain words: say what you want, give it a number, make sure it is possible for you, make sure it helps the business, and give it a date.

Some examples of the shape of a good goal:

- "Get 15 new customers to buy at least once in the next three months."
- "Have 8 customers ask for a repeat order by the end of December."
- "Collect 20 phone numbers of people who want to hear about new stock by the end of March."

Notice that these ask for customers and actions, not for a promise of money. You cannot control how much people spend. You can control how many people you speak to and how you follow up.

Choose one goal. If you have three, you have none.

## Step 2: Name your customer and pick two channels

Write one sentence that describes the customer you want most. For example: "Mothers who live within a short walk of my shop and buy school items." The more clearly you can picture them, the easier the next choice becomes.

Business Queensland suggests choosing target groups by how large they are, how much they could bring in, and how easy they are to reach. If you cannot reach a group cheaply, it is probably not the right one to start with.

Now pick two channels. A channel is just a way to reach people. Some common low-cost choices:

| Channel | Good for | Cost and effort |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A clear sign or painted board at your shop | People who pass by | One-time cost, little weekly work |
| Word of mouth and thanking customers | People who already trust you | Free, but needs a kind habit |
| A WhatsApp status or a social media page | People who already have your number or follow you | Free to post, needs a regular time slot |
| A flyer or notice at a place your customer visits | A local area | Printing cost, needs permission in some places |
| A simple phone call or message to past buyers | Repeat sales | Free apart from airtime |

Pick two that match your customer. If your customers are mothers near your shop, a sign and word of mouth may beat a social media page. If your customers are young people who scroll all day, a regular post may work better. Do not choose a channel only because your neighbour uses it.

Why two? Because you have limited hours. One channel done well each week beats five done once.

## Step 3: Set a small budget and a weekly routine

Start with an amount you could lose without hurting your family or your stock money. It may be small, and that is fine.

Here is an example. All the numbers are made up. Say you set aside TSh 30,000 a month:

- TSh 15,000 for printing 50 small notices
- TSh 10,000 for airtime and data to send messages and post
- TSh 5,000 kept back to test one new idea

That adds up to TSh 30,000. Check the total whenever you write a budget, because mistakes here are common.

Do not copy these amounts. Use your own figures. The point is to write them down before you spend, so you can compare the plan with what really happened.

Now add a routine. Time is a cost too. The Queensland guide says each action should have timing, a person responsible and a way of measuring it. In a one-person business, the person is you. For example:

- **Monday, 15 minutes:** Post one photo and one useful sentence about a product.
- **Wednesday, 15 minutes:** Message three past customers to say thanks and ask if they need anything.
- **Saturday, 10 minutes:** Write in your notebook how many people asked about the business and how they heard of it.

Forty minutes a week is something most owners can keep up. A routine you can keep is better than a big push that stops after two weeks.

## Step 4: Put it on one page

Draw a table in your notebook or on a sheet of paper. Here is a sample you can copy. The details are an example only.

| Part of the plan | Your answer (example) |
| --- | --- |
| Goal and date | 15 new customers who buy at least once by the end of January |
| My customer | Parents who live near the shop and buy school items |
| Channel 1 | A painted sign and 50 notices at the school gate and the church board |
| Channel 2 | A weekly photo and message on WhatsApp status |
| Monthly budget | TSh 30,000 (TSh 15,000 printing, TSh 10,000 airtime, TSh 5,000 test) |
| Weekly routine | Monday post, Wednesday messages, Saturday count |
| How I measure | Ask every new customer "How did you hear about us?" and tally the answers |
| Review date | The last Sunday of each month |

Ask yourself two questions about the page. Can I do all of this with the time I really have? Can I measure the goal without special tools? If the answer to either is no, make the plan smaller.

Measuring does not need software. A tally of tick marks in a notebook is enough. The key is to ask each new customer how they found you, and to write it down at once.

## Step 5: Check results every month and change one thing

Business Queensland suggests reviewing the plan regularly and asking what is working and what has changed in the market. Its guide mentions checking each quarter. For a small shop with a small budget, a short monthly look is easier to keep.

On your review day, answer these questions:

1. How many new customers did I get, and how did they hear about me?
2. Did I do my weekly routine, or did I skip it? Why?
3. Did I stay inside my budget?
4. Which channel brought more people?
5. What is one thing I will change next month?

Change only one thing. If you change the message, the channel and the price at once, you will not know which change mattered.

Be patient and honest. Some months will show little. That does not always mean the plan has failed. Sometimes it means the customer needs more time, or the message is unclear. We cannot promise any result, and nobody honest can. What a plan gives you is a clear way to learn from what you try.

## Common questions

### Do I need a big budget to market my business?

No. Many useful actions cost only time and airtime, such as thanking customers, asking for referrals and posting regularly. Spend only what you can afford to lose.

### Which two channels should I choose?

Choose the two that your customer already uses and that you can keep up every week. Your own notes from customer chats are the best guide.

### How long before I see results?

It varies, and nobody can promise a date. Give a channel at least a few weeks of steady effort before you judge it, and track how customers say they found you.

### Should I pay someone to run my marketing?

Not at the start. Learn what works yourself first. If you later pay someone, you will know what to ask for. Be careful with anyone who promises sales or a top place in search results.

## What to do next

1. Write your one goal with a number and a date.
2. Fill in the one-page table above using your own answers.
3. Put a reminder for your monthly review on a calendar or phone.
4. Keep going for three months before you decide the plan is not working.

You do not have to be a marketer to grow a business a step at a time. A plan this small is something you can start today.
