You open your shop's page and see 200 followers. A rival down the road has 20,000. Then a message arrives: "We can fix that for a few thousand shillings." It is easy to feel small at that moment, and easy to say yes. This post is here to slow you down for five minutes.

## The short answer

No, it is not safe to buy followers or to pay anyone who says they can promise you sales or a first place on Google. The platforms themselves say so, and nobody outside those companies controls the results.

- Meta's rules prohibit buying engagement such as likes, shares and views, and buying accounts or pages.
- Google says no one can guarantee a number one ranking.
- A big follower count does not pay your rent. Customers do.
- Honest, slow work (good photos, quick replies, happy customers who tell others) is cheaper and safer.
- If you have already paid, stop, protect your accounts and keep your records. The last section tells you how.

## Why do these offers feel so tempting?

Many small owners in Tanzania sell through WhatsApp, Instagram and Facebook. A page with few followers can look new or risky to a stranger. So a seller who says "20,000 followers by tomorrow" is selling relief from that worry.

The worry is real. The fix is not. A follower who never reads your posts will never buy your tomatoes, shoes or phone covers. Think about it this way: if you paid TSh 20,000 for numbers on a screen, what would you have in your hand next week? A bigger number, and no new customer.

The same pressure shows up in a second form. Someone promises "guaranteed sales" or "guaranteed first place on Google". It sounds like a business partner. It is usually a promise nobody can keep.

## What do Meta and Google actually say?

We checked these pages in November 2026. Policies change, so open the links in "Sources and further reading" for the current wording.

### Meta (Facebook and Instagram)

Meta's Spam policy lists, among the things it prohibits, attempting to or successfully selling, buying or exchanging for engagement, such as likes, shares and views. It also prohibits selling, buying or exchanging platform assets such as accounts, groups and pages.

That means a seller of followers is offering something the platform does not allow, and a buyer is taking part in it. Meta's policy tells you what is not allowed. We could not confirm from the policy what happens to a particular account, so we will not guess. The honest point is simple: you would be building your shop on something the platform says you may not do.

Meta's fraud and scams policy also names things like offers of unrealistic financial reward for unclear or minimal effort, and offers where returns are guaranteed or risk-free. It lists advance fees (a promise of money for a payment up front) and failing to deliver a service after you have paid. These match the patterns in most "growth" scams.

### Google

Google's own page on whether you need an SEO helper is blunt: "No one can guarantee a #1 ranking on Google." It adds that if someone guarantees that their changes will give you first place, you should find someone else.

Google also warns about companies that contact you out of the blue, about secrecy, and about claims of a special relationship with Google or a "priority submit". Nobody can buy a private door into search results.

## What are the warning signs before you pay?

Look for these. One sign is a reason to ask more questions. Two or three together is a reason to walk away.

- **A guarantee.** "Guaranteed sales", "guaranteed first page", "guaranteed 10,000 followers". Real marketing has no guarantees.
- **Unrealistic speed or size.** Big results for very little work or money.
- **Pressure.** "The price goes up tonight." "Only three places left."
- **Payment first, details later.** Especially if they want full payment by mobile money before showing any work.
- **Secrecy.** They will not explain what they will actually do.
- **A message out of the blue.** A direct message or email you did not ask for.
- **They ask for your password.** Never share it. Legitimate help does not need your login.
- **They ask for full control of your accounts.** Google says to give an SEO helper read access only to your Search Console, at the start, and not write access.
- **They want you to link to their site** or place their links on your pages.
- **No real examples.** They cannot show past work you can check yourself.

### Questions to ask any marketing helper

Google suggests asking an SEO adviser questions like these, and they work for any helper:

1. Can you show me examples of your previous work, and let me check them myself?
2. What results do you expect, and over what time?
3. What exactly will you do on my accounts and pages?
4. Will I keep full control of my accounts and my website?
5. What happens if nothing improves?

A good helper answers calmly in plain words. A scammer gets annoyed, vague or fast.

## Can I be punished for what I say in my own adverts?

This section is about your own claims, not about the sellers. It is general information, not legal advice. For your own case, ask a lawyer.

The Fair Competition Act, 2003 has rules on honest trading. In the copy published by WIPO Lex, section 15 says nobody shall, in trade, engage in conduct that is misleading or deceptive or likely to mislead or deceive. Section 16 lists false or misleading representations about goods and services, including about price, quality, and guarantees. Section 20 covers false or misleading statements about the profitability or risk of a business activity. The section numbers may differ in a later edition of the Act, so check the current text.

What does this mean for you? Do not copy a seller's tricks. Do not tell customers you have "50,000 happy customers" if the number is bought. Do not promise results you cannot control. A small, true claim ("open since 2021, delivery within Dar es Salaam") is stronger and safer.

## What can I do instead that actually works?

Slow methods cost little and build something you own.

- **Ask happy customers to share your page.** One real customer who tells a neighbour is worth more than a hundred empty accounts.
- **Post real pictures of your real products** with the price and where to find you.
- **Reply quickly.** Many buyers decide by how fast you answer a question.
- **Give your page the same name as your shop sign** so people can find you.
- **Keep a simple list of customers who agreed to hear from you.** The post on [SMS and email marketing consent](/post/business-ideas/marketing/sms-and-email-marketing-consent-tanzania/) explains how to do that fairly.
- **Be patient.** Real trust grows over weeks and months.

Say you spend TSh 20,000 on printed price cards and a clear sign instead. Customers who walk past can see what you sell and what it costs. That is a real result you can see, and no seller can take it away.

## What if I have already paid or shared a password?

Many people have done this. It is not stupid, it is human. Do these steps in order.

1. **Stop paying.** Do not send "one more fee" to fix the problem.
2. **Change your password now** on the account involved, and on any other account that uses the same password. Use a long password you have not used elsewhere.
3. **Turn on two-step login** if the app offers it.
4. **Check who has access.** Remove any person or app you do not know from your page roles and connected apps.
5. **Keep proof.** Save messages, receipts and mobile money confirmations. Take screenshots.
6. **Tell the platform** if your account is at risk, using its help pages.
7. **Ask your mobile money provider** whether they have a way to report a fraud.
8. **Report it.** Google's page says that for a company in another country, you can file a complaint at econsumer.gov. For a seller inside Tanzania, ask the police or a lawyer how to proceed.
9. **Tell a friend in business.** Scammers rely on silence.

Do not expect to get money back. We cannot promise that, and you should be careful about anyone who says they can recover it for a fee.

## Common questions

### Will buying followers help my sales?

We found no reason to think so. A follower who is not a real, interested customer will not buy. Also, it is against Meta's rules to buy engagement.

### Is it wrong to hire someone to help with my page?

No. Many honest people help small shops with photos, writing and posting. The warning signs above separate them from scammers. Start with a small, clear task and pay for work you can see.

### Can anyone really get me to the top of Google?

Google says no one can guarantee it. Honest helpers can improve how clear your pages are. Nobody can promise a place.

### I got a message offering to "manage" my page. Is it real?

Treat it as unsafe until you can check who it is. Do not give a password. Look at the warning signs above, and ask for past work you can verify yourself.

## What to do next

1. Open your page and look at it as a stranger would. Is the name clear? Are prices, location and a contact number easy to find?
2. Ask three happy customers this week to share your page or leave a kind comment.
3. If someone has already offered you "followers" or "guaranteed sales", save the message and do not reply with money.

Slow and honest is not the weaker path. It is the one that is still standing next year.
