If a child in your home keeps saying "Roblox", you are not alone in wondering what it is. It is not one game. It is a place where millions of small games, made by other people, sit side by side. Many families in Tanzania meet it on a phone first, and the Swahili word parents often use is simply "mchezo", a game. That word hides a lot.

This guide explains Roblox in plain English for parents, teachers and curious adults. It covers how children play, how money works, which safety settings exist, and what to do if something goes wrong. It also shows the good side: young people can learn to build their own games. As checked on 19 November 2026, the details below match Roblox's own pages.

## The short answer

- Roblox is a free-to-play platform of games made by its users. Roblox calls them "experiences".
- ESRB, the North American ratings board, rates Roblox T for Teen, with a note that some content may not suit all ages.
- Chat is locked until a person completes an age check. After that, chat is limited to similar age groups.
- A parent can link their own account to a child's account and manage chat, spending and blocked games.
- Roblox Studio, the building tool, is free. It is a real way to learn creating games.

## What Roblox is

Roblox is a platform, not a single game. Players open the app, pick an experience made by someone else and play it. One experience might be a racing game, another a pet shop, another an obstacle course. Roblox says it is a place where games are discovered, built and experienced.

ESRB describes it as a free-to-play platform of user-created games. It is available on phones, tablets, computers, consoles and VR headsets. Playing is free. Players can choose to buy Robux, the in-game money, or subscribe to Roblox Premium.

Because anyone can make an experience, quality and content vary a lot. Roblox says games must be rated and pass a multi-step review, including creator checks. Even so, ESRB's rating carries the label "Diverse Content: Discretion Advised". That label is honest advice: not every experience is right for every age.

## How children play

Most children start by joining a popular experience, often with friends. They pick an avatar, which is their character, and move around a shared 3D world. Others are in the same world at the same time, so play is social.

That social part is what makes Roblox fun, and it is also why adults should pay attention. ESRB lists "Users Interact" and "In-Game Purchases (including random items)" as interactive elements of the rating. In plain words: your child can talk to other people, and the game can ask for money.

You do not need to ban it to be careful. You need to know what is switched on.

## Robux and spending

Robux is the currency inside Roblox. It is bought with real money, then spent on things like clothing for an avatar or extras inside an experience. Roblox also sells Premium subscriptions.

Spending adds up quietly, especially when a child sees a small price and a bright button. Our guide on [loot boxes and in-game purchases](/post/video-games-updates/loot-boxes-and-in-game-purchases-protect-your-money/) explains why random items are tempting and how to add a purchase check on Google Play.

Roblox's parent page says a linked parent can set a monthly spending limit that covers Robux and subscriptions. The limit resets at the start of each calendar month. Parents can also choose whether to get alerts for every purchase, only for large spending, or none. Gift card redemptions are not counted in that limit, so remember this if someone gives your child a card.

Mobile money and cards are real money. Treat a child's Roblox balance like pocket money: agree an amount first, then top up.

## Age checks and chat: what changed

Roblox announced on 19 November 2025 that age checks would become required for chat. Checks started as voluntary that day. Enforcement began in a few countries in December 2025 and was planned to expand worldwide in January 2026 wherever chat is available. Roblox says chat stays locked until a user completes a check.

After a check, a user is placed in one of six groups: under 9, 9 to 12, 13 to 15, 16 to 17, 18 to 20, or 21 and over. People can chat with their own group and nearby groups. Users aged 13 or older who have passed the check can also use Trusted Connections to chat with people they know in real life.

For younger children there are extra limits. Roblox says chat inside experiences is off by default for under-9s unless a parent agrees after an age check. Chat outside experiences is restricted for users under 13.

### How the age check works

The quickest way is Facial Age Estimation. In the app, the user goes to Settings, then Account info, then Age Group, and chooses "Continue with selfie". Users aged 13 or older who have a government photo ID can use ID verification instead.

Roblox says its vendor, Persona, processes the images, and that the pictures and video are deleted after processing. It also says it does not sell image data. Readers who are uncomfortable with a selfie check should read Roblox's age estimation page themselves before deciding. The check is optional, but chat stays closed without it.

If the estimate is wrong, Roblox says a verified linked parent can correct the birthday once in Parental Controls when a child was estimated under 13.

## Two account types for younger children

Roblox's safety page describes accounts built for age. As checked on 19 November 2026, in most regions:

- **Roblox Kids (ages 5 to 8):** only games rated Minimal or Mild are available. Chat is off by default. Parents can turn on in-game chat or one-on-one chat with age-appropriate users and trusted friends.
- **Roblox Select (ages 9 to 15):** games can be rated up to Moderate, and parents can lower the limit for ages 9 to 12. Chat is on with age-appropriate users and trusted friends in the same game. One-on-one in-game chat is off unless a parent enables it.

Roblox says voice chat is not available for ages 9 to 12, and that video chat is not available in any region. It also says settings and features differ by region. Because we could not confirm exactly how every setting appears in Tanzania, open the app and look at what your child's account actually shows.

## How to set up parental controls

These steps follow Roblox's parent page. Menu names can change, so treat them as a guide.

1. **Link your account.** Roblox says a parent must verify their own age, with a government ID or a credit card, to link accounts. If you have neither, ask Roblox support what other option exists. We could not confirm one.
2. **Set chat.** You can turn Experience Chat and Direct chat on or off. You can also limit chat with friends to trusted friends, or to no one.
3. **Set content maturity.** Lower the limit so your child only sees games suited to their age. The "Allow sensitive issues" switch is off by default for under-13s. Keep it off.
4. **Block games and people.** Add a game to the blocked list in the content restrictions. Blocked games can still show in search results, but your child cannot join them.
5. **Set a spending limit** and choose your alert level.
6. **Set screen time.** Roblox says time limits are available for children under 13.

ESRB says teens aged 13 to 17 are handled differently. Traditional parental controls are not available for them, so a teen has to invite a parent to link accounts. The parent can then see connections, playtime and purchase notifications. A talk comes before a setting here.

Also use your phone's own controls. Our guide to [game age ratings and parental controls](/post/video-games-updates/game-age-ratings-esrb-pegi-and-parental-controls/) shows how.

## Safe chat habits to teach

Settings help, but habits protect more. ESRB advises that children should never share personal information. Teach these simple rules:

- Do not share your real name, school, address, phone number or photos.
- Keep talk inside the game. Do not move to another app because a stranger asks.
- Do not accept a gift, a trade or a "free Robux" offer from someone you do not know.
- Never share a password, even with a friend.
- Tell an adult if something feels strange. You will not be in trouble.

That last rule is the most important one. Children hide problems when they fear losing their phone. Say clearly, before anything happens, that you will help and you will stay calm.

## Signs of trouble

These are general warning signs. They do not prove anything on their own.

- Your child hides the screen, closes the app quickly or becomes upset after playing.
- They talk about a new "friend" you have never heard of.
- They ask for gift cards, mobile money or Robux for reasons they cannot explain.
- They receive gifts, or are asked to keep secrets.
- Their sleep, school work or mood changes. See our post on [healthy gaming habits](/post/video-games-updates/healthy-gaming-habits-when-play-becomes-a-problem/) if play is taking over.

## What to do after a bad contact

Stay calm, thank your child for telling you, and do not punish them for it.

1. **Stop the contact.** Block the user. Roblox says a blocked user cannot chat directly with your child or add them again without a parent's permission.
2. **Report.** Roblox says users can report and block other people or games when they feel unsafe. As a parent you can report a connection from Settings, Parental Controls, then Manage under Connections, using the three-dot menu next to the name.
3. **Keep evidence.** Take screenshots of messages and the username before blocking.
4. **Check money.** Look for unexpected purchases or requests.
5. **Get support.** If a child is in danger or was threatened, contact local police or a child protection service. Roblox's safety page also describes its process for reporting to law enforcement.

## For young creators

Roblox is also a way to learn. Roblox Studio is the tool creators use to build, script, test and publish experiences, and Roblox's documentation says it is free to use. It is available on Windows and Mac. Roblox lists 3 GB of memory as the minimum and 8 GB as recommended, so Studio is a computer tool, not a phone app.

Scripts are written in Luau, a language Roblox says is based on Lua 5.1. ESRB also notes that Studio lets children learn coding and game design.

We cannot promise anyone income from making games. Treat it first as a way to learn logic, patience and design. If your child likes building, you can also try [learning to make games with Godot](/post/video-games-updates/learn-to-make-games-for-free-with-godot/), a free engine that runs on a normal PC.

## Common questions

### Is Roblox safe for children?

It can be a good place when settings fit the child's age and an adult stays involved. No platform removes every risk. ESRB warns that content varies and that children can be pressured to share personal information.

### What age is Roblox for?

ESRB rates it T for Teen. Roblox itself builds accounts for ages 5 to 8 and 9 to 15 in most regions, with more limits for younger children.

### Does my child need a selfie to chat?

Roblox says chat stays locked until an age check is done. The quickest way is a selfie, and users 13 or older can use an ID instead.

### Is Roblox free?

Playing is free. Robux and Premium are optional purchases.

## What to do next

1. Sit with your child, open the app together and look at the settings.
2. Link your account, set chat and content limits and agree a monthly spending amount.
3. Agree the rule: "Tell me if something feels wrong, and you will not be punished."
4. If your child is curious about making games, try Roblox Studio on a computer together.
