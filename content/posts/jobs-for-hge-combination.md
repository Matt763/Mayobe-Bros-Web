HGE means History, Geography and Economics as your three principal subjects in Form Five and Form Six. It is one of the more open arts combinations. Because it includes Economics, it can reach degrees that HGK or HGL cannot. This guide is for Form Four leavers choosing a combination, Form Six students waiting for results, and parents who want to understand the options.

We read the entry rules below in the Tanzania Commission for Universities (TCU) 2026/27 guidebook in November 2026. Rules change each year, so check the latest edition before you apply.

## Key points

- NECTA lists HGE among the arts combinations: History, Geography and Economics. General Studies is compulsory for all candidates.
- HGE fits economics, development studies, public administration, urban and regional planning, tourism, law and arts teaching.
- Some economics degrees also ask for a maths pass. Each university words this its own way, so read each line.
- Points are counted from two principal subjects that match the programme, usually with a 4.0 minimum.
- No combination guarantees a place or a job.

## Why Economics makes HGE different

Many degree entries name Economics. Arts-only combinations cannot meet them. The University of Dar es Salaam (UDSM) Bachelor of Arts in Economics, for example, asks for Economics or Advanced Mathematics as one of the two principal passes. HGK and HGL have neither.

History and Geography still count too. Many planning, development and public service degrees accept them directly. So HGE gives you two sets of doors: arts and social science doors, and the economics door.

## Is this combination only for the weak? No

Maybe someone told you that arts students "failed science". That is false. Economics is hard. It uses graphs, careful thinking and real data. It sits behind banking, planning and government budgets.

Your parents may be paying fees with hard-earned money. You want to make that sacrifice count. Pick a combination because the guidebook shows a road you want, not because of what others say.

## How points work

The guidebook counts points for results from 2016 onward as A = 5, B = 4, C = 3, D = 2, E = 1 and S = 0.5. For most degrees you need two principal passes in subjects the programme lists, with at least 4.0 points in total.

Say you get C in Economics, D in History and D in Geography. For a programme that asks for Economics plus another subject, you could count 3 + 2 = 5 points. That meets 4.0 on paper, but many applicants will have more. UDSM lists "5 from 3 subjects" for its arts programmes, and its counting rule is different from the common 4.0, so ask the university how it works.

## Degrees HGE can reach

The table summarises 2026/27 entries where HGE subjects qualify. Each institution sets its own line.

| Degree | Institution | Subjects asked for | Minimum points |
|---|---|---|---|
| BA in Economics | University of Dar es Salaam | Two passes, one in Economics or Advanced Mathematics. Without Advanced Mathematics, a credit pass in Basic Mathematics at O-Level | 5 from 3 subjects |
| BA in Economics | Open University of Tanzania | Economics plus one of History, Geography, Kiswahili, English and others | 4.0 |
| BA in Environmental Economics and Policy | University of Dodoma | Two passes from a list including Geography and Economics. Maths rule applies (see below) | 4.0 |
| BA in Development Studies | University of Dodoma | Two passes from History, Geography, Economics and many others | 4.0 |
| BA in Political Science and Public Administration | University of Dodoma | Two passes from History, Geography, Kiswahili, English or French | 4.0 |
| Bachelor in Urban and Regional Planning | Institute of Rural Development Planning | Two passes from Economics, Geography, History and others | 4.0 |
| Bachelor of Laws | University of Dodoma | Two passes at D or above from a long list including History, Geography, Economics | 4.0 |
| Bachelor of Banking and Finance | Institute of Finance Management | History, Geography, Economics and others, with a Maths rule | 4.0 |

The urban planning degree is listed at 4 years. The others listed here are 3 years, except the Open University programme, which the guidebook shows as 3 to 6 years.

## The maths trap: read this before you choose

This is the part that surprises students. Several economics and finance lines ask for maths, even when it is not one of your three subjects.

- **UDSM Economics:** without Advanced Mathematics, you need a credit pass in Basic Mathematics at O-Level.
- **UDOM Environmental Economics and Policy:** if you have no Advanced Mathematics principal pass, you need a subsidiary pass in Advanced or Basic Applied Mathematics, or a D in O-Level Mathematics.
- **Institute of Finance Management (Banking and Finance):** without Advanced or Basic Applied Mathematics, you need a credit at O-Level.
- **College of Business Education (Economics and Finance):** a pass in Basic Mathematics at O-Level if you lack Advanced Mathematics.

If your Form Four maths was weak, start work on it now. For these degrees, a good Form Four maths grade can matter as much as your Form Six points.

## Public service, planning and tourism

Public administration and development studies accept History, Geography and Economics directly. They prepare you for work in councils, development projects and government offices. Hiring depends on the employer and the year. We could not confirm pay figures from an official source, so we give none.

Planning is a good match for Geography. The Institute of Rural Development Planning in Dodoma lists Urban and Regional Planning with Economics, Geography and History among the accepted subjects.

Tourism is open too. Our post on [Tanzania's tourism boom](/post/educational/tanzania-tourism-boom-kilimanjaro-serengeti-zanzibar-world/) explains why Geography and History give you a useful base for that field. Check the tourism line of each university, because the list of accepted subjects differs.

## Law and teaching

Law is possible. The University of Dodoma lists Law at 4.0 points, and applicants without History or English at A-level need a C at O-level. HGE students have History. Mzumbe University asks for 4.5 points plus one subsidiary, with its own English and History rule. Read the university line before you plan.

Teaching is possible too. UDSM's Bachelor of Arts with Education lists Economics, History and Geography among its subjects, and Mkwawa University College of Education lists them as well. UDOM's list names History and Geography. These degrees prepare you to teach arts subjects in secondary schools.

## If you are in Form Four: is HGE right for you?

1. **Economics:** do you like understanding why prices rise, how markets work and how money moves?
2. **Maths:** is your Form Four grade good enough for the maths rules above?
3. **History and Geography:** do you enjoy reading and explaining ideas in writing?
4. **English:** it matters for law and public service, because universities may ask for an English grade.
5. **Your plan:** write three careers and check their guidebook lines.

If you want engineering, HGE is not the road. See the [PCB guide](/post/educational/study-tips/jobs-for-pcb-combination/) for health science paths.

## A note to your heart

Think of the day you open your Form Six results. Your hands might shake. Your family is waiting. That fear shows how much this matters.

Think about what you can control now. Your grades this term. Your maths. Your reading. Your questions to teachers. No one can promise you a degree or a job. But a student who plans with the guidebook in hand is better prepared than one who guesses. You are not behind. You are early.

## If you are in Form Six: after the results

1. Work out your points from your two best matching subjects.
2. Shortlist 5 programmes. Include one difficult choice, two realistic ones and one safer option.
3. Apply only through the official systems. Nobody can sell you a place.
4. Read how to [confirm your admission under TCU multiple selection](/post/educational/study-tips/how-to-confirm-university-admission-tcu-multiple-selection/) if you receive more than one offer.
5. Start your [HESLB loan application](/post/educational/study-tips/students-loan-tanzania-complete-guide/) early.

## What if your points are not enough?

1. **Try a related programme.** Development studies, public administration and planning often have different competition from law or economics.
2. **Look at diploma routes.** Accredited colleges under NACTVET offer diplomas. TCU has a separate guidebook for diploma holders who want to move up to a degree.
3. **Fix the maths gap.** If a degree failed on maths, ask the university what the rule accepts.
4. **Plan another year.** Some students retake examinations. Ask a teacher first, because it costs time and money.

## Common questions

### What can I study with HGE combination?

You can study economics, development studies, public administration, urban and regional planning, tourism, law and arts teaching at the institutions listed in the TCU guidebook. Each one has its own entry line.

### Can HGE students study economics at university?

Yes. UDSM and the Open University of Tanzania list Economics degrees that accept Economics. UDSM also asks for a Mathematics pass at O-Level if you do not have Advanced Mathematics.

### Can HGE students study law?

Yes, at several universities. The University of Dodoma lists Law at 4.0 points. Check the English and History rule for each one.

### Is HGE better than HGK?

Neither is better. HGE adds Economics, so it reaches economics and planning degrees. HGK adds Kiswahili, so it reaches Kiswahili teaching. Choose by the careers you want.

### Can HGE students become accountants or bankers?

The Institute of Finance Management lists a Bachelor of Banking and Finance that accepts History, Geography and Economics, with a Mathematics rule. Accountancy degrees often ask for Accountancy, Commerce or Economics, so check each line.

## What to do next

1. Open the TCU guidebook and search for three programmes you like.
2. Write down the subjects, maths rules and points for each one.
3. Check your Form Four maths and English grades against those rules.
4. Talk to a teacher or a university student in the field.
