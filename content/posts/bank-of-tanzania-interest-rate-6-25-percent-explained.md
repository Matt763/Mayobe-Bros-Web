In October 2026 the Bank of Tanzania (BoT) decided to keep its main interest rate at 6.25%. That is the rate people call the Central Bank Rate, or CBR. It sounds far from daily life, but it sits behind the price of a bank loan, the pay on a savings account and the effort to keep prices steady. This post explains the decision in plain words and what it can mean for you. Figures were checked in November 2026, and the Swahili term to look for is *kiwango cha riba cha Benki Kuu*.

## The short answer

- On 8 October 2026 the BoT said its Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) kept the Central Bank Rate at 6.25% for the quarter ending December 2026.
- In July 2026 the same committee had raised the rate from 5.75% to 6.25%, because of high world prices for energy, fertiliser and transport.
- Mainland inflation was 4.2% in October 2026. The official target range is 3% to 5%.
- Banks lent to businesses and households at an average of 15.54% in September 2026, and paid an average of 9.02% on deposits, as reported from the BoT's monthly review.
- The next rate decision is due on 7 January 2027, after the committee meets on 6 January.

## What the Central Bank Rate is

The Central Bank Rate is the Bank of Tanzania's signal rate. In general, a central bank uses it to guide what banks charge each other for short loans. Banks then use that cost as a starting point when they set the rates they offer to you.

The link is not exact. Your bank, SACCOS or lender sets its own price, based on its costs and on how risky it thinks you are. But when the central bank rate goes up, borrowing tends to get dearer. When it goes down, borrowing tends to get cheaper.

You can see the link in the BoT's own market data. In a week in late October 2026, the 7-day rate that banks charged each other was 6.59%, which is close to the 6.25% policy rate and inside the BoT's corridor of 4.75% to 7.75%. That figure comes from the BoT's weekly report as written up by TanzaniaInvest.

## What happened in 2026

The committee meets once every quarter. Two meetings matter for this story.

**2 July 2026.** The MPC raised the rate by half a percentage point, from 5.75% to 6.25%. In its statement of 3 July, the BoT said the aim was to hold inflation inside the 3% to 5% target. It blamed high energy, fertiliser and transport costs on world markets, which it linked to the conflict in the Middle East.

**7 October 2026.** The committee met again and kept the rate unchanged. The BoT statement said the current tight stance was still working its way through the economy and was holding down the pressure on prices while supporting growth.

Put simply, the bank raised the rate once, saw that it was working, and chose to wait. This is why we say the rate was "held" and not "cut" or "raised" in October.

## Inflation: the number behind the decision

Inflation means how fast prices rise over a year. The National Bureau of Statistics (NBS) publishes it every month. As reported by TanzaniaInvest, the NBS said annual inflation was 4.2% in October 2026, down a little from 4.3% in September. The reading was released on 9 November.

Some parts of the basket moved more than others:

| Group | Annual change, October 2026 |
| --- | --- |
| All items | 4.2% |
| Food and non-alcoholic drinks | 3.6% |
| Transport | 14.9% |
| Energy, fuel and utilities | 8.3% |
| Services | 6.3% |

Transport stands out. The BoT said the rise in inflation since mid-year came mostly from higher world oil prices, which raised fuel and transport costs at home. If you want to see how fuel prices are set, read our post on [Tanzania fuel prices and how the caps work](/post/tanzania-news/tanzania-fuel-security-measures-navigating-current-challenges/).

Food was calmer. The BoT said food supply from the 2025/26 harvests was adequate, and the NBS data showed monthly price falls in October for maize grain, maize flour, rice and cooking oil. Next month's figure is due from the NBS on 8 December 2026.

One more thing to know: Zanzibar has its own inflation figure. The BoT said it was 5.6% in August 2026, a little above the 5% target there.

## What it can mean for your money

### If you borrow

Banks have been lending fast. The BoT said credit to the private sector grew by an average of 32.5% in the quarter ending September 2026. Reported averages for September were 15.54% for all loans and 12.03% for the best-rated, negotiated borrowers.

A hypothetical example: if you borrow TSh 1,000,000 for one year at a flat 15.54%, the interest is TSh 155,400. Your own bank may price it differently, and many loans are paid back in monthly parts, so the true cost depends on the terms. Always ask for the total amount you will repay, in shillings, before you sign.

Because the central bank rate did not rise again in October, there is no new push to make loans dearer. But the BoT has also not promised to cut. For a borrower, the safest plan is to compare offers and borrow only what your income can repay. Be careful with online lenders you cannot check; our guide on [how to check a loan app](/post/technology-solutions/cyber-security/fake-loan-apps-tanzania-how-to-check/) shows how.

### If you save

The reported average deposit rate in September 2026 was 9.02%. With inflation at 4.2%, a deposit at that average rate would, over a year, grow a little faster than prices. Your own account may pay more or less, so ask your bank for its current rate and for any fees.

### If you run a small business

Higher borrowing costs and fuel costs both reach your price list. Our post on [how to price your products](/post/business-ideas/startups/how-to-price-your-products-small-business-tanzania/) helps you add real costs, including transport, without guessing.

### If you hold dollars or send goods abroad

The BoT's weekly report showed the shilling at TSh 2,636.74 per US dollar in the week of 19 to 23 October 2026, a small fall of 0.14% from the week before. In July the BoT said it expected little effect on prices from the exchange rate, because of high export earnings from gold, tourism and farm goods in the second half of the year.

## The wider picture the BoT described

The October statement gave several other facts that help you read the news:

- Tanzania's national accounts were rebased. The base year moved from 2015 to 2019, and Mainland nominal GDP for 2025 is now TSh 234,104 billion (about USD 92.3 billion).
- Mainland growth was 6% in the first quarter of 2026 and is estimated above 6% in the second and third quarters.
- Foreign exchange reserves were above USD 6 billion, enough for 4.3 months of imports. The national minimum is four months.
- Non-performing loans, meaning loans in trouble, were 2.5% in August 2026, below the 5% level the BoT treats as tolerable.
- The committee named two risks to watch: the Middle East conflict and possible El Niño-related disruptions.

The BoT also said it will "stand ready" to respond if price pressures become more lasting and broad.

## Common questions

### Does the Central Bank Rate change my bank loan straight away?

Not by itself. Your lender sets your rate. The central bank rate affects the cost of money for banks, and the effect reaches borrowers slowly and unevenly. A loan with a fixed rate will not change until you take a new one, and a loan with a variable rate follows what your lender decides.

### Why did the BoT raise the rate when inflation was still inside the target?

In its July statement, the BoT said it wanted to keep inflation inside the 3% to 5% range. Inflation had risen from 3.2% in March to 4.2% in May 2026, and world energy, fertiliser and freight costs were going up. Raising the rate early is one way to try to stop prices from rising further.

### Where can I check the figures myself?

The BoT posts each Monetary Policy Committee statement on its website, and the NBS posts the monthly price index. Those official pages are the first place to look. Numbers for lending rates and the shilling come from BoT reports; here we read them through TanzaniaInvest, so check the BoT's own monthly review for the exact table.

### Will the rate change in January?

Nobody can say. The BoT has given the dates, 6 and 7 January 2027, but no promise about the decision. The committee said it will watch the Middle East conflict and El Niño risks.

## What to do next

1. Before you borrow, ask the lender for the full repayment amount and the date each part is due. Compare at least two lenders.
2. Check your savings account's current rate and fees. If you have idle cash, ask what safe options your bank offers.
3. If you sell goods, write down your fuel and transport costs each month and update your prices when they change.
4. Mark 8 December 2026 for the next inflation release and 7 January 2027 for the next rate decision.
