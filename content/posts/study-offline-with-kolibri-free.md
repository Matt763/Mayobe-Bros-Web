A data bundle runs out, and the lesson you wanted is still on the screen, loading. Many students in Tanzania know that feeling. There is a free tool made for exactly this problem: you go online once, load your lessons, and then study with no bundle at all.

## The short answer

Kolibri is a free, open-source learning platform that works without internet. You need internet only once to get the app and the content you want. After that, you can study offline, and you can pass the same lessons to friends on a USB drive.

- Kolibri is free to use, according to its maker, Learning Equality.
- It works without internet once the content is loaded.
- Content can be shared from person to person or on a USB drive.
- The Android app needs Android 6 or newer (as checked in November 2026).
- Download only from the official site, learningequality.org, to stay safe.

## What is Kolibri?

Kolibri is an offline-first learning platform. "Offline-first" means it was built to work where the internet is weak, slow or too costly. Learning Equality, the non-profit organisation behind it, describes it as an open-source tool for teaching and learning where there is no internet. Open source means anyone can read how it is built, and it costs nothing to use.

Think of it as a small library of lessons that lives on your own device. Lessons are grouped into "channels". A channel is a set of lessons, videos and exercises from one source. You load the channels you want, and they stay on your device.

Learning Equality says its Kolibri Library has material in more than 173 languages. We could not confirm how much of that material is available in Kiswahili. We also could not confirm which subjects or school levels you will find. So please look at the channel list yourself before you plan your revision around it. The Kolibri help pages say Swahili (Tanzania) is available as an interface language in Kolibri, which means the menus can be shown in Swahili. That is not the same as lessons in Swahili, so check the channels.

## What do I need before I start?

You need very little.

1. **One online session.** The Kolibri FAQ says you need internet to get the installer and the channels, both for the first install and for later upgrades. After that it can run on its own.
2. **A device.** Either an Android phone running Android 6 or newer, or a computer.
3. **Enough storage.** Lessons, and especially videos, take space. Check how much free space you have before you load many channels.
4. **A USB drive (optional).** This is useful if you want to share content or carry it to a place with no internet.
5. **Time on Wi-Fi or a good bundle.** The first load is the heavy part. If your school, a library or a friend has Wi-Fi, use that.

## How do I set it up on an Android phone or a computer?

We could not test every step on every device for this post, so we describe the plan and point you to the official guides for exact screens. Menus and button names can change between versions.

### On an Android phone

1. Check your Android version in your phone's settings. It must be Android 6 or newer.
2. Get the Kolibri app from the official Learning Equality site (the Kolibri download page on learningequality.org). Avoid copies from unknown websites or chat groups.
3. Open the app and follow the setup steps to create your learner account.
4. While you are online, open the channel list and choose the channels you want. Start with one or two, so your storage does not fill up.
5. When the channels finish loading, turn off your mobile data and open a lesson to make sure it works offline.

### On a computer

The Kolibri FAQ explains that Kolibri runs on a "server" device, such as a desktop or laptop (or a Raspberry Pi). Other devices with a web browser, such as tablets or other laptops, are "clients". One computer can be both server and client, so a single laptop is enough for one learner.

1. Download the installer for your computer from the official Kolibri download page while you are online.
2. Install it and start Kolibri. Open it in your web browser as the official guide explains.
3. Import the channels you want while you are online.
4. Test one lesson with the internet switched off.

For the exact steps for your device, read the official installation guide on the Kolibri documentation site.

## How can friends share lessons with a USB drive?

This is where Kolibri helps a whole group. Only one person needs a good internet session.

According to the Kolibri FAQ, you can set up Kolibri where there is internet, import channels, and export them to a removable USB drive. The drive can then be carried to a place with no internet, where the channels are imported onto another device.

Here is a simple plan for a study group:

1. One member, perhaps the one whose family has Wi-Fi, loads the channels.
2. That member exports the channels to a USB drive.
3. The others import from the drive on their own devices.
4. Everyone studies offline, and the group agrees which lessons to cover each week.

If several people sit in one room, Kolibri can also work over a local network. The FAQ says client devices must join the same local network as the server, for example through a Wi-Fi access point. Nobody needs internet for that. A school or club teacher could set it up for a whole class. For a class, it is wise to ask someone with computer skills to help.

Say a group of four friends each saves a bundle for one lesson. They would pay four times for the same download. If one friend downloads it once and shares it by USB, the other three save their bundle for something else. That is only an example, and the saving depends on what your bundle costs.

## What are the limits?

Honest limits help you plan.

- You still need internet once. Without it you cannot get the app or the channels.
- Content is chosen by the channels' makers. It may not match the Tanzanian syllabus. For the official syllabus, see the Tanzania Institute of Education (TIE), which we explain in our post on the [TIE online library](/post/educational/online-learning/tie-online-library-free-textbooks-tanzania/).
- Large videos use your phone's storage. Load a few channels at a time.
- Kolibri is a study tool, not a replacement for your teacher, your textbooks or your own notes.
- Menus and features change with new versions.

## Common questions

### Is Kolibri really free?

Learning Equality says Kolibri is free to use and open source. We saw no fee on its official page. If any site asks you to pay for the Kolibri app, treat it with care and go back to the official site.

### Can I use Kolibri on an iPhone?

The official page we read talks about Android devices and computers. We could not confirm iPhone support, so we do not give steps for it.

### Does Kolibri use data while I study?

Once the content is on your device, you can study without internet. Updates and new channels do need a connection.

### Is there Kiswahili content?

We could not confirm how much. Swahili (Tanzania) is available as a menu language. Look through the channel list to see what exists for your subject.

## What to do next

1. Check that your phone runs Android 6 or newer, or choose a computer.
2. Plan one online session on Wi-Fi, and download Kolibri only from learningequality.org.
3. Choose one or two channels that fit the subject you need, and test them offline.
4. If you study in a group, agree who will load the content and share it by USB.

You do not need a big bundle to learn something every day. A little preparation can keep your study going when the network does not.
