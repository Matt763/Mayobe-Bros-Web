In September 2025, a Tanzanian runner won the men's marathon at the World Athletics Championships in Tokyo by three hundredths of a second. His name is Alphonce Simbu, and the win was the first global title for any athlete from Tanzania, according to World Athletics. In 2026 he kept running at the top level. This post sets out his record, explains how big marathons work, and shows what young runners can learn from his career.

The results below were checked in November 2026 against World Athletics and news reports. Times in a marathon are written as hours, minutes and seconds, so 2:02:47 means two hours, two minutes and 47 seconds.

## The short answer

- On 15 September 2025 in Tokyo, Simbu and Germany's Amanal Petros were both given 2:09:48. A photo finish gave the gold to Simbu.
- World Athletics called it the smallest winning margin ever in a marathon at a global championships.
- On 20 April 2026 in Boston, Simbu finished second in 2:02:47, behind Kenya's John Korir (2:01:52).
- On 30 August 2026 in Sydney he finished fourth in 2:04:57, with the same time as the third-place runner.
- He was fifth at the 2016 Olympics in Rio de Janeiro and seventh at the Tokyo Olympics.

## The Tokyo win

World Athletics describes a race that came down to the last few metres. Petros moved ahead with about 200 metres to go, and Simbu chased him. Simbu kicked in the final 30 metres, and the judges needed a photo finish to separate them. Both were credited with 2:09:48. Italy's Iliass Aouani took bronze in 2:09:53.

The winning margin was 0.03 seconds. That is far less than the blink of an eye. For Tanzania it was a historic first, because World Athletics says Simbu became the first athlete from the country to win a global title.

## 2026: Boston and Sydney

In Boston on 20 April 2026, John Korir won in 2:01:52, Simbu was second in 2:02:47 and Kenya's Benson Kipruto was third in 2:02:50, three seconds behind Simbu (Business Insider Tanzania). Wikipedia lists 2:02:47 as a Tanzanian national record, and other outlets reported it that way. We did not find the national athletics federation confirming it, so treat "record" with care. The report also gave prize money of US$75,000 for second place.

In Sydney on 30 August 2026, Simbu crossed the line with the same time as Lesotho's Tebello Ramakongoana, 2:04:57. Ramakongoana was ahead at the line, so he took third and Simbu was fourth (The Citizen). The winner, Ethiopia's Addisu Gobena, ran 2:04:42, a course record. Simbu was also inside the old record of 2:06:06.

When two runners record the same time, the official order is the order in which they crossed the line. That is why a tie in time can still give different places.

## What those times mean

Numbers like 2:02:47 are hard to picture, so here is simple arithmetic. It is our own calculation from the published time, not a figure from a report.

A marathon is 42.195 kilometres. The time 2:02:47 is 7,367 seconds. Divide 7,367 by 42.195 and you get about 175 seconds per kilometre, or roughly 2 minutes 55 seconds for every kilometre. That is an average speed of about 20.6 kilometres an hour, held for more than two hours.

In Tokyo the pace was slower, because world championship races are decided by position and not by the clock. The winning time there was 2:09:48, or about 3 minutes 5 seconds per kilometre.

## A career built over many years

Simbu's record shows steady progress, not one lucky day. According to Wikipedia, he ran the marathon at the 2015 World Championships in Beijing, finished fifth at the 2016 Rio Olympics, and won bronze at the 2017 World Championships in London in 2:09:51. World Athletics also refers to his 2017 world bronze. He finished seventh at the Tokyo Olympics, held in 2021, in 2:11:35, then won gold in 2025.

The photo above shows him in the 2017 London Marathon. The pattern is clear: ten years of consistent racing at the highest level before a world title. If you want a model for patience, this is one. ## How big marathons work

- A marathon is 42.195 kilometres. The distance is the same everywhere.
- The "World Marathon Majors" are a group of the biggest city races. Boston, which was first held in 1897, is one of them.
- Prize money exists in city races. Do not treat it as a normal income for runners, because only a small number of athletes earn it.

## If you want to start running

Most people who read about a champion do not plan to run a marathon, and that is fine. If you want to start, a few plain rules apply. These are general safety points, not a training plan, and they do not come from Simbu's coaches.

- Start with walking and short, easy runs. Add distance a little at a time.
- Wear shoes that fit and are made for running. Old, worn shoes can cause pain.
- Drink water, and avoid running in the hottest hours of the day.
- Stop and rest if you feel dizzy, have chest pain or sharp pain in a joint. See a doctor or a clinic if it continues.
- Run with a friend or tell someone your route, especially early in the morning or in the evening.

An elite marathoner trains with coaches, medical support and years of base fitness. Copying that plan on your own can cause injury.

## Common questions

### Is Simbu the first Tanzanian world champion?

World Athletics says he is the first athlete from Tanzania to win a global title, in the marathon in Tokyo in September 2025.

### Did he break a record in Boston?

He ran 2:02:47, which is his best time. Wikipedia lists it as a Tanzanian national record. Check with the Tanzanian athletics federation for the official record list.

### Why did he finish fourth in Sydney with the same time as third place?

Both runners were timed at 2:04:57. The official results use finishing order when times match, and Ramakongoana finished just ahead.

### How long is a marathon?

It is 42.195 kilometres, which is the same in every city.
