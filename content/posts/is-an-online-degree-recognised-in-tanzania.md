Studying online can save travel costs and let you keep your job. But a degree is a big investment of money and years, so one question matters before you pay: will it be accepted in Tanzania?

The honest answer depends on what kind of online study you mean. This post explains the three cases, what the Tanzania Commission for Universities (TCU) says in its public documents, and where those documents stay silent. This is general information, not legal advice.

## The short answer

An online degree from a Tanzanian university must follow the approval rules of that university and TCU. A degree from a foreign university needs TCU recognition if you want to work or study further in Tanzania. TCU's public pages do not say whether online foreign awards are handled differently, so ask TCU before you pay.

- Check that the university is registered with TCU, and that the programme is real and approved.
- For a foreign award, ask TCU about recognition before you enrol, not after you graduate.
- A short online certificate is not a degree, even if the website uses big words.
- Recognition of a qualification does not by itself allow you to practise a regulated profession.
- Keep emails and replies in writing.

All of this was checked in November 2026. Rules change, so confirm on the official pages.

## What are the three kinds of online study?

People use "online degree" for very different things. Separate them first.

1. **An online or blended programme from a Tanzanian university.** The university is in Tanzania and TCU regulates it.
2. **A degree from a university in another country, studied online.** You may never travel there. The award is foreign.
3. **Short online certificates and courses.** These are often from learning platforms or companies. They are useful for skills but are not university degrees.

Each case has a different check. Mixing them up is how people lose money.

## Online programmes from Tanzanian universities

TCU published Guidelines for Online and Blended Delivery Modes of Courses for University Institutions in Tanzania in February 2022. The guidelines apply to universities in Tanzania.

Some points from the document that matter to a student:

- Online and blended programme curricula must follow the approval processes of the university and of TCU.
- The guidelines define "blended" as part of the content online and part face to face. A fully online course is one where 100% of the content is delivered online.
- Universities should explain which parts of a course are online and how they are delivered.
- For final online exams, the guidelines expect software to confirm who is taking the exam, to check for copied work, and to supervise the test. Online exam systems need special approval from the university senate and TCU.

What does that mean for you? If a Tanzanian university offers a programme online, that programme is supposed to have been approved in the normal way. You can ask the university for proof of approval for the exact programme, and you can ask TCU.

Do not assume that a university being registered means every programme it advertises is approved. Check the programme itself.

### How to check the university

TCU has a page listing universities registered in Tanzania. For each one it shows the name, head office location, whether it is public or private, and its status. The status is either "Accredited" or "Accredited and Chartered". There is a "View Programmes" link for each institution, and you can filter the list by name, location or type.

Use that page like this:

1. Find the university by name.
2. Click to see its programmes.
3. Look for the exact programme you want.
4. If you cannot find it, ask the university and TCU in writing.

## Foreign online degrees and TCU recognition

TCU's page on recognition of foreign qualifications says that people who obtained a university education abroad need TCU recognition if they want to work or study further in Tanzania. Applications are made through the online Foreign Award Assessment System (FAAS). TCU assesses the award against academic standards and principles taken from conventions, regional agreements and national policies. If you disagree with a decision, you can appeal.

Here is the part you must read carefully. That page does not mention online or distance awards at all. We could not find, in the pages we read, any statement saying that a foreign online degree is accepted, or that it is rejected, or that it is treated differently from a degree studied on campus.

So we will not guess. We also will not say that any named foreign online university is, or is not, recognised. Only TCU can tell you how it would treat the award you are thinking about.

What you can do:

- Write to TCU at es@tcu.go.tz. Give the university name, the programme and the way it is delivered. Ask whether such an award can be recognised and what documents are needed.
- Keep the reply. If the answer is unclear, ask again until you understand.
- Do this before you pay fees.

### Recognition is not a licence to practise

TCU says that recognition of academic qualifications alone does not guarantee permission to practise. Some professions need a separate licence or registration from the professional body. Think of engineering, medicine, nursing or architecture. If your goal is a regulated profession, ask that body too, before you enrol.

## Short certificates are not degrees

A certificate from an online course shows that you finished that course. It can still help you: you learn a skill, and you can show it to an employer. But it is not a university degree, and no recognition process turns it into one.

Be careful when a course advert uses words like "degree-level", "equivalent to a degree" or "fast-track bachelor". Ask who awards the qualification, and whether a university or accreditation body stands behind it. ## Checklist before you pay

Print this or save it on your phone.

1. **Who awards the qualification?** A university, a training company or a platform?
2. **Where is that university?** If it is in Tanzania, look it up on the TCU registered list. If it is abroad, find its country's official list of recognised universities, and ask TCU about recognition.
3. **Is this exact programme approved?** Ask for it in writing.
4. **How are exams done?** Online, in a centre, or both? Are there any in-person parts you must travel for?
5. **What is the total cost?** Ask for tuition, exam fees, registration, books and any currency charges. Compare it with what you can afford.
6. **What will you do with the award?** Job application, further study, a regulated profession? Ask each body that matters.
7. **Is anyone pressuring you?** Real universities do not need you to pay today. Walk away from "last chance" offers.
8. **Never pay anyone to "arrange" recognition or a certificate.** Official processes go through the official channels. Anyone who offers a sure outcome for a fee is a warning sign.

Say you are a working parent, and a Dar es Salaam friend sends you a link to a foreign online degree for TSh 8,000,000 a year. Follow the checklist before sending a single shilling. An hour of emails costs nothing, and it could save you years of hard work that no employer accepts.

## Common questions

### Can I just study online and check later?

It is risky. If recognition is a problem, you will find out after paying fees and spending time. Ask first.

### Is an online degree weaker than a campus degree?

We cannot say that from the sources we read. TCU's guidelines say that online delivery should not lower the quality or validity of higher education, which is why it sets standards. What matters for you is whether the programme is approved and whether the award is accepted for your goal.

### Which universities in Tanzania offer online programmes?

The TCU list of registered universities has a link to each university's programmes. Check each university's own pages for how a programme is delivered.

### Who can I contact at TCU?

TCU's headquarters are in Dodoma, and it has an office in Dar es Salaam. Its recognition page lists the email es@tcu.go.tz. Check the page for current phone numbers.

### Does recognition guarantee me a job?

No. Employers choose their own staff, and some jobs need extra registration.

## What to do next

1. Decide what you want the degree for, and write it down.
2. Look up the university and programme on the TCU registered list, or write to TCU about a foreign award.
3. Ask the university for approval details and a full cost list in writing.
4. Wait for replies before paying. If someone rushes you, treat it as a warning.

Asking questions now is a kind of care for your future self. Take your time, and choose a path that opens doors rather than closes them.
