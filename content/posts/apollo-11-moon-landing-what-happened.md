In July 1969, three men flew to the Moon, and two of them walked on it. Hundreds of millions of people watched on television. If you have ever looked up at the Moon and wondered how it was done, this post tells the story in plain words, using NASA and the Smithsonian as our main sources.

## The short answer

Apollo 11 was the first mission to land people on the Moon. It lifted off on 16 July 1969, the lunar module Eagle landed on 20 July, and the crew splashed down in the Pacific Ocean on 24 July 1969.

- The crew was Neil Armstrong (commander), Edwin "Buzz" Aldrin (lunar module pilot) and Michael Collins (command module pilot).
- Armstrong and Aldrin landed in the Sea of Tranquility. Collins stayed in orbit.
- They spent 21 hours and 36 minutes on the surface, and the moonwalk itself lasted a little over two and a half hours.
- They left a plaque and other items behind and brought back 47 pounds (21.5 kilograms) of Moon rock and soil.

## Why did the United States try to reach the Moon?

The Moon race grew out of the Cold War, the rivalry between the United States and the Soviet Union. The Smithsonian's National Air and Space Museum says the Soviet Union sent the first person into space, Yuri Gagarin, on 12 April 1961. Days later, President John F. Kennedy asked Vice President Lyndon Johnson to find a space programme the United States could win. On 25 May 1961, Kennedy told Congress that the country should aim to explore the Moon with people.

It took eight years and a huge rocket to do it. NASA lists the launch vehicle as a Saturn V.

## Who were the crew?

- **Neil Armstrong**, commander. He was the first person to walk on the Moon.
- **Buzz Aldrin**, lunar module pilot. He walked on the Moon after Armstrong.
- **Michael Collins**, command module pilot. He flew the spacecraft Columbia and stayed in orbit around the Moon while the others went down.

Collins's role is often forgotten, yet the mission needed him in orbit. NASA names three backup astronauts: James Lovell, Fred Haise and William Anders.

## What happened from launch to landing?

The mission ran for eight days. Here are the main steps, based on NASA's mission overview.

1. **16 July 1969.** The Saturn V rocket lifted off from Launch Pad 39A in Florida at 9:32 a.m. Eastern time.
2. **Journey to the Moon.** The crew travelled in Columbia, joined to the lunar module Eagle.
3. **20 July.** Armstrong and Aldrin flew Eagle down to the Moon. It landed in the Sea of Tranquility, about four miles from the planned spot and about a minute and a half early, says NASA. The Smithsonian gives the distance as about 6 kilometres, which is the same distance in different units.
4. **Manual flying.** The Smithsonian says Armstrong flew the last minutes by hand to avoid a crater about 180 metres wide.
5. **First steps.** Armstrong stepped onto the surface about 109 hours and 42 minutes after launch. Aldrin followed about 20 minutes later.

NASA prints Armstrong's first words from the surface as "...one small step for a man, one giant leap for mankind." Some people discuss whether he said "a man" or just "man". We use NASA's wording and leave that debate aside.

The Smithsonian estimates that about 650 million people watched around the world. It is an estimate, as nobody counted exactly.

## What did they do on the Moon?

Be careful with two different times, because they are often mixed up.

- **Time on the surface:** 21 hours and 36 minutes. This is how long Eagle stayed on the Moon.
- **Moonwalk:** more than two and a half hours. This is the time the two men spent outside.

During the moonwalk, Aldrin set up the Early Apollo Scientific Experiments Package. The men also collected samples, and both walked up to about 300 feet from Eagle. NASA says Aldrin was outside for 1 hour and 33 minutes before going back in, and Armstrong followed 41 minutes later.

After the walk, they rested and then took off to meet Collins in orbit. The three flew home. The command module splashed down on 24 July 1969 at 12:50 p.m. Eastern time, about 13 miles from the recovery ship USS Hornet. NASA says the landing point was moved about 250 miles because of weather. The whole flight lasted 195 hours, 18 minutes and 35 seconds.

## What did they leave, and what came back?

Left on the Moon, according to NASA:

- A plaque on the lunar module's descent stage. NASA's mission page says it was signed by President Richard Nixon and the three astronauts. Its words are: "Here men from the planet Earth first set foot upon the moon, July 1969 A.D. We came in peace for all mankind."
- Medallions honouring the three Apollo 1 astronauts and two Soviet cosmonauts who died in accidents.
- A small silicon disk, 1.5 inches wide, with goodwill messages from leaders of 73 countries.

Also left was a laser reflector, an array of 100 small mirrors in a 10 by 10 grid, put down on 21 July 1969, according to NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory. It sends light straight back to where it came from.

Brought back: 47 pounds (21.5 kilograms) of lunar rock and soil, says NASA. Other NASA and Smithsonian pages give slightly different weights, such as 21.7 or 21.8 kilograms, because of how samples are counted. All are close to the same amount.

## Myths and facts about the landing

Some people say the landing was staged. We do not list every claim, but one fact is easy to check. The laser reflector Apollo 11 left is still used. The JPL says observatories such as McDonald Observatory in Texas and one near Grasse in France fire lasers at it. NASA's 2020 article says research with these Apollo-era reflectors continues. People in many countries can measure the returning light, so this evidence does not rest on NASA alone.

We do not credit Apollo with inventing everyday products, because we found no NASA page that says so.

## Why does Apollo 11 still inspire students?

The mission shows what many people can do when they work together on a hard problem. The three astronauts were the most visible people, but a mission like this needs many kinds of skill on the ground as well as in space.

You do not need a rocket to start. Maths, physics and careful reading are the first steps. If you are a student in Tanzania who likes science, the same subjects open many doors, from engineering to data work. We cannot promise any particular career, and you should check what each course requires. Yet the habit of asking a big question and working on it step by step is open to everyone.

## Common questions

### Who was the first person to walk on the Moon?

Neil Armstrong. Buzz Aldrin followed about 20 minutes later, according to NASA.

### How long did Apollo 11 take?

From launch on 16 July to splashdown on 24 July 1969. NASA gives the total flight time as 195 hours, 18 minutes and 35 seconds.

### Did anyone stay in the lunar module the whole time?

No. Armstrong and Aldrin went down in Eagle while Collins stayed in orbit in Columbia.

### How many people watched?

An estimated 650 million, according to NASA and the Smithsonian. It is an estimate.
