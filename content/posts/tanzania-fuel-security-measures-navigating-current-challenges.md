Tanzania imports almost all its petrol and diesel, so changes in world oil prices reach local pumps quickly. In April 2026 the price caps jumped by about a third. This post explains what happened, how the monthly price caps are set, what the government said it was doing, and how you can check the price you should be paying.

We checked these facts in November 2026 against news reports and the EWURA website. Fuel prices change every month, so treat the figures as examples from the dates given, not as today's price.

## The short answer

- On 1 April 2026, EWURA's cap price for petrol in Dar es Salaam rose from TSh 2,864 to TSh 3,820 a litre, about 33 percent (The Citizen, via other outlets). Diesel went from TSh 2,858 to TSh 3,806.
- The regulator blamed the conflict involving the US, Israel and Iran, which began in late February 2026, and its effect on world oil supply and prices.
- By July 2026 caps came down a little. In Dar es Salaam, petrol was TSh 3,990, diesel TSh 4,182 and kerosene TSh 4,443 from 1 July.
- EWURA's website lists an "October 2026 caps" notice dated 6 October. Check that notice for the current price.
- Stations must show their prices and give electronic receipts.

## How fuel reaches your tank

Tanzania does not refine crude oil for its own market in any large way. Most fuel is imported by ship, mainly through Dar es Salaam, with smaller flows through Tanga and Mtwara. It is then moved by road tanker to depots and stations around the country.

That is why the cap prices differ by place. For example, in April the Dar es Salaam price was lower than in Tanga and Mtwara, because of transport and logistics costs. In July, the petrol cap was TSh 3,990 in Dar es Salaam, TSh 4,051 in Tanga and TSh 4,083 in Mtwara.

## How EWURA sets cap prices

EWURA (the Energy and Water Utilities Regulatory Authority) publishes cap prices for petrol, diesel and kerosene, normally at the start of each month. A cap is a maximum retail price. A station can sell for less, but not for more.

EWURA said the April 2026 prices followed higher world oil prices and exchange-rate changes. The detailed formula is set by EWURA, so we describe it only in general terms. In general, a cap includes the cost of the imported fuel, shipping, taxes and levies, and the margins of the businesses along the chain.

## What happened in 2026

| Date | Dar es Salaam petrol cap | Note |
|---|---|---|
| March 2026 | TSh 2,864 | Before the jump |
| 1 April 2026 | TSh 3,820 | Up about 33 percent |
| May 2026 | About TSh 4,115 | Reported by The Citizen |
| June 2026 | TSh 4,086 | Reported by The Citizen |
| 1 July 2026 | TSh 3,990 | First fall; still above March |

Say a bodaboda rider fills 5 litres. In March that cost 5 x 2,864 = TSh 14,320. At the April cap it was 5 x 3,820 = TSh 19,100, which is TSh 4,780 more per fill-up. This is only an example, since stations round and prices differ by place.

Reasons given in July were easing world oil prices, diplomatic efforts and reopening of shipping routes. The July report also noted that prices were still above levels before the conflict.

## What the government said and did

We are careful here, because many claims about "fuel security" online are not backed by sources. What we found:

- The Ministry of Energy said fuel reserves are not only tanks inside the country. They include fuel in transit and contracts with international suppliers (Daily News). It did not publish reserve volumes in the page we read.
- Prime Minister Mwigulu Nchemba said authorities would meet to keep fuel affordable and prevent a rise in living costs (AllAfrica).
- Energy Minister Deogratius Ndejembi told Parliament on 26 June that the government was working to keep fuel supplies stable and look for ways to ease prices (The Citizen).
- EWURA urged consumers to use fuel efficiently.
- For fares, LATRA reviewed bus fares after the fuel rise. See our post on [Tanzania's new transport fares](/post/tanzania-news/tanzania-new-transport-fares/).

Some claims that circulate online, such as the size of a national strategic petroleum reserve, have no public source, so we do not repeat them.

## How the price affects you

Fuel affects more than drivers. Transport costs feed into food prices, since farm produce moves by truck. A business that uses diesel generators or pumps will see its costs rise with diesel prices. The Daily News report on the fare dispute said fuel is about 40 percent of transport costs, from LATRA's 2022/23 report.

Practical ways to cope, all general advice:

- Plan trips to combine errands.
- Keep tyres at the right pressure and service your vehicle, since this helps fuel use.
- Buy only at stations that display the price.
- For cooking, compare the cost of charcoal and gas per month before switching. We do not have a verified price comparison, so do your own sums with local prices.

## Common questions

### How do I find the official price today?

Open EWURA's website, www.ewura.go.tz, and look for the latest cap price notice. News outlets such as The Citizen also report the new caps at the start of each month.

### What if a station charges more than the cap?

Ask to see the price board and the receipt. EWURA lists a toll-free line, 0800 110 030, and the email info@ewura.go.tz. Write down the station name, time, fuel type and price charged.

### Will prices keep rising?

We cannot know. Prices depend on world oil prices and the exchange rate, which both move. Prices fell in July. Check EWURA's October notice to see whether caps rose again.

### Is there a fuel shortage?

We found no official statement of a national shortage in the sources we checked. Ministry statements spoke of keeping supply stable.

## What to do next

1. Save EWURA's website and the toll-free line on your phone.
2. Check the cap at the start of each month, and make sure the station's board matches it.
3. If you run a small business, record your fuel spending each month so you can adjust prices with facts.
4. Use [our post on transport fares](/post/tanzania-news/tanzania-new-transport-fares/) if you want to compare how fares followed fuel prices.
