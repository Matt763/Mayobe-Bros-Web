In Tanzania, your combination (the three principal subjects you study in kidato cha tano and kidato cha sita, Form Five and Form Six) decides which university programmes you can apply for. It does not decide your whole life, but it does close some doors and open others. This guide is for Form Four leavers, their parents and teachers. It shows how combination and career connect, using the 2026/27 admission guidebook from the Tanzania Commission for Universities (TCU).

Schools in Tanzania use the English word "combination" even when speaking Kiswahili. Other Swahili terms, such as kidato cha tano (Form Five), are explained where they appear.

## Key points

- Universities ask for specific principal passes (the subjects you study at A-Level). Your combination limits which degrees you can choose.
- Many degrees accept a long list of subjects. Health degrees and engineering are the strictest.
- The general minimum for most degrees is 4.0 points from two relevant subjects. Health degrees often ask for 6 points from three.
- A diploma or short course is another route. A combination is not a life sentence.
- Pick your combination after reading the guidebook, not after asking only your friends.

## What the TCU guidebook is

TCU publishes a guidebook every year for people with Form Six results. It lists every approved degree, the subjects each requires, the minimum points, the places available and the years of study. We read the 2026/27 edition (published in 2026) for this article.

Points work like this for recent results: A = 5, B = 4, C = 3, D = 2, E = 1, S = 0.5. For most degrees the guidebook sets a general minimum of 4.0 points from two subjects related to the programme.

Individual universities often add their own rules, such as minimum grades or a subsidiary pass. That is why you must read the entry for your exact university.

## How combinations map to degrees

Below are examples taken from the guidebook. They are not complete lists. A programme may be offered by many universities, each with slightly different wording.

| Area | Examples of degrees | What the guidebook asks for (examples) |
|---|---|---|
| Medicine and health | Doctor of Medicine, Pharmacy, Dental Surgery | Three principal passes in Physics, Chemistry and Biology, minimum 6 points |
| Nursing | BSc Nursing | Chemistry, Biology and Physics, Advanced Mathematics or Nutrition, minimum 6 points |
| Engineering | Civil Engineering | Advanced Mathematics and Physics (one university lists Advanced Mathematics with Physics, Chemistry or Geography) |
| Architecture | Bachelor of Architecture | Two principals from Advanced Mathematics, Physics, Chemistry, Biology, Geography, Computer Science or Fine Arts, plus a subsidiary in mathematics |
| Teaching, science | BSc with Education | Two principals from Physics, Chemistry, Biology, Advanced Mathematics, Computer Science or Geography |
| Teaching, arts | BA with Education | Two principals from History, Geography, Kiswahili, English and similar subjects |
| Law | Bachelor of Laws | Two principals from a long list of arts, business and science subjects, plus an English requirement (a subsidiary pass, or a grade at O-Level, depending on the university) |
| Accounting and finance | BSc Accounting and Finance | Two principals from a long list; often a mathematics requirement at subsidiary or O-Level |
| Veterinary medicine | Bachelor of Veterinary Medicine | Chemistry and Biology principals with a subsidiary pass (Sokoine University listing) |

Two things stand out. First, health programmes are narrow, because they depend on Physics, Chemistry and Biology. Second, law, accounting and many teaching degrees are wide, because they accept many subject mixes.

## Common combinations and where they lead

Schools in Tanzania use short names such as PCM or PCB. The meaning depends on your school's list, so ask the headmaster or academic master (the teacher in charge of subjects and exams). The ones below are widely used.

### PCM: Physics, Chemistry, Mathematics

PCM is the usual science route into engineering and architecture-related degrees, and also into many science and computing programmes. Civil engineering in the guidebook asks for Advanced Mathematics and Physics, which PCM provides. PCM does not meet the three-subject rule for medicine, which asks for Biology.

### PCB: Physics, Chemistry, Biology

PCB is the standard route for medicine, dentistry, pharmacy and medical laboratory sciences. We wrote a separate guide on [jobs and degrees for the PCB combination](/post/educational/study-tips/jobs-for-pcb-combination/).

### CBG: Chemistry, Biology, Geography

CBG is a science and environment combination. It can lead to degrees in agriculture, environmental subjects, food science and science teaching. It does not satisfy the Physics, Chemistry, Biology rule for medicine, so check before you pick it if you want a health degree. Some nursing and environmental health entries accept Chemistry and Biology with another subject, so look at each one.

### EGM: Economics, Geography, Mathematics

EGM suits economics, statistics, planning, and many business and social science degrees. Mathematics gives you an advantage in subjects such as statistics, where the guidebook lists a mathematics requirement.

### Arts combinations

Combinations built on History, Geography, Kiswahili, English or Literature lead to teaching (BA with Education), law, journalism, public administration and language degrees. The guidebook lists these subjects for arts teaching degrees, and the minimum for such degrees is 4.0 points from two subjects at several universities.

## Mathematics matters more than many students think

Advanced Mathematics appears in many entries: engineering, architecture, statistics, computer science at some universities and parts of accounting. Even where it is not required as a principal pass, a subsidiary or O-Level mathematics pass is often asked for.

If you avoid mathematics only because it feels hard, you may reduce your options without knowing it. If you do struggle, get help early from a teacher, a study group or a tutor. Our [study techniques post](/post/educational/study-tips/10-proven-techniques-to-study-effectively-for-students/) has practical methods.

## Does your combination decide your job?

Not completely. Here is a realistic view.

- **Some jobs are tied to a degree.** A doctor, pharmacist or engineer needs the right degree and professional registration. These need the right subjects at A-Level.
- **Many jobs are not tied to one subject.** Business, administration, sales, journalism, farming, ICT support and many technical roles accept people from different backgrounds.
- **Skills matter.** Writing, speaking, basic computer use, honesty and punctuality help in any combination. Our guide on [building a career from scratch](/post/educational/career-development/how-to-build-a-career-from-scratch/) covers this.
- **Technical colleges exist.** After Form Four or Form Six, you can study for certificates or diplomas at accredited colleges. VETA, the Vocational Education and Training Authority, offers vocational training and short courses.

No combination guarantees a job. The job market depends on the economy, your skills, and where you live.

## When to start thinking about it

Many students first think about this in the weeks after Form Four results, when selections for Form Five are announced. That is late. Better to begin in Form Three, when you can still change how you study.

In Form Three and Four, look at which subjects you enjoy and which you score well in. Ask your teachers honestly. A student with strong marks in Chemistry and Biology but weak Mathematics should know that before choosing a combination, not after.

If you did not get the combination you wanted, ask the school what is possible, and talk to the education office in your district. Do not pay anyone who promises to change your selection quietly.

## How to choose, step by step

1. **List three careers you find interesting.** Do not choose only because a relative did.
2. **Find each career's degree.** Search the TCU guidebook for the programme name.
3. **Write down the required subjects and points.** Note differences between universities.
4. **Compare with your Form Four results.** If you are weak in a subject, ask whether you can improve it before registration.
5. **Ask your school.** Not every school offers every combination. Ask which ones your school runs and how many students they take.
6. **Talk to people who do the work.** A nurse, an accountant or a teacher can tell you what a normal week looks like.
7. **Pick a backup.** Choose a second degree that your combination also allows.

## Three hypothetical examples

These are made-up cases to show how the rules work. They are not real people.

**A student who wants to be a civil engineer.** The guidebook entries we checked ask for Advanced Mathematics with Physics. PCM fits. PCB does not. The student should choose PCM, and work on Mathematics from Form Three.

**A student who likes biology and wants to work in health.** PCB opens medicine, pharmacy and medical laboratory sciences. If Physics is a struggle, CBG still allows some agriculture and food degrees, though not medicine. The student should decide which matters more, health degrees or a safer subject mix.

**A student who wants to be a lawyer.** The guidebook lists a long set of subjects for law, including arts, business and sciences. English matters, either as a principal subject or through a subsidiary or O-Level pass. A student with History, Geography and Language, or Economics, Geography and Mathematics, could both qualify on paper. Strong English and reading habits help far more than the label.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Choosing the combination your friends choose.** Friends may have different goals and grades.
- **Choosing only the "easy" one.** An easy combination that does not lead anywhere you want is not helpful.
- **Dropping Mathematics without checking.** See the section above.
- **Ignoring parents' concerns.** Parents often worry about school fees and jobs. Show them the guidebook entries. A calm talk with facts works better than an argument.
- **Believing anyone who offers to "buy" a place.** Admission goes through university systems and TCU. Paying an agent for a seat is a scam risk.

## Common questions

### Can I change my career after I have chosen a combination?

Yes, within limits. You can choose a different degree that accepts your subjects, study a diploma, or later take a conversion course. Switching to medicine without PCB is hard.

### Is a science combination better than an arts one?

No. They lead to different work. Law, teaching, journalism and administration come mostly from arts and business subjects.

### What if my school does not offer the combination I want?

Ask the education officer in your district about other schools. Compare the cost, distance and boarding before you transfer.

### Do I need Mathematics for business degrees?

Often yes, at least at a lower level. Some accounting programmes in the guidebook ask for a subsidiary or O-Level mathematics pass.

### How many points do I need?

For most degrees the general minimum is 4.0 points from two subjects. Health degrees often ask for 6 points from three subjects. Popular programmes often need more than the minimum, so check the university's own guidance.

## What to do next

1. Download the latest TCU guidebook for Form Six holders.
2. Pick two or three careers and write down the subjects needed.
3. Ask your school which combinations it offers and what grades are needed to join them.
4. Share your list with a parent or teacher and ask for their view.
