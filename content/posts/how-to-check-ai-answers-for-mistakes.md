An AI assistant writes smoothly. It never says "um" and it rarely says "I am not sure". That calm voice can make a wrong answer feel right. If you are about to send a message, hand in an assignment or spend money because of what a chatbot said, take two minutes to check it first.

## The short answer

Do not treat a chatbot as the final word. The companies that make them say so themselves. Check the parts that could hurt you if they are wrong.

- Look for specifics: names, numbers, dates, fees, laws and quotes.
- Ask for sources, then open them yourself.
- Confirm important facts on an official website.
- Be strictest with health, law, money and anything about real people.
- If you cannot confirm a fact, leave it out or say you are unsure.

## Why can an AI sound sure and still be wrong?

Anthropic, the maker of Claude, says on its help page that Claude can write things that look correct but are mistaken, which is often called "hallucinating". It also says the tool's training information can be out of date, and that it can show quotes that look authoritative or convincing but are not grounded in fact. Anthropic says you should not rely on Claude as your only source of truth.

Google says something similar. Its Gemini help page says Gemini Apps may sometimes produce inaccurate or inappropriate information, and that its answers about people in particular need double-checking. It also says not to rely on Gemini answers as medical, legal, financial or other professional advice.

You do not need to know how the machine works to use this. The lesson is simple: a confident tone tells you nothing about whether the answer is true. We checked these pages in November 2026. Other assistants will have their own warnings, and we did not open every one.

## Five quick checks you can do on a phone

### 1. Underline the checkable facts

Read the answer and pick out anything you could prove right or wrong: a name, a figure, a date, a fee, a law, a quote, a web address. These are the parts to check. Opinions and general advice are less risky than hard facts.

### 2. Ask where it came from, then open the source

You can ask, "What sources support this? Give me links." Then open the links. Anthropic's page says that when Claude searches the web you should check the cited sites, because original pages may hold context that the summary leaves out.

Be careful: a chatbot can name a book, article or web page that does not exist, or one that does not say what it claims. A link that does not open, or a page that does not mention the fact, is a red flag.

Google's Gemini page says Gemini may show a Sources button with related links, but not every response includes sources. So when there is no source, you must check another way. As checked in November 2026, that is what Google's page says. Features and menus change, so look at the current app.

### 3. Check the fact on an official or original site

Search for the fact yourself. Look for the site of the body that owns the information, and not a blog that repeats it. Open the original page for any quote.

### 4. Be stricter with numbers, dates and names

Small errors in these can cause big damage: a wrong deadline, a wrong fee, a wrong spelling of a person's name. If the chatbot gives a figure, find the same figure on the official page. If two sources disagree, believe the official one and note the date you checked.

### 5. Ask the assistant to test itself, then decide

You can ask, "Which parts of your answer are you least sure about?" or "What might be out of date?" This sometimes shows weak points. But it does not prove anything, because the assistant can also be wrong about itself. Treat it as a hint about where to look, never as a check by itself.

## Where do I check facts about Tanzania?

Go to the body that is responsible for the thing you asked about. Here is a starting table. Always use the official website you can confirm is real.

| What you want to check | Where to look |
| --- | --- |
| University admission, programmes, accreditation | Tanzania Commission for Universities (TCU) |
| Student loans | Higher Education Students' Loans Board (HESLB) |
| Exam results and codes | National Examinations Council of Tanzania (NECTA) |
| Taxes and TIN numbers | Tanzania Revenue Authority (TRA) |
| Business registration | BRELA |
| Bus and transport fares | LATRA |
| Personal data rules | Personal Data Protection Commission (PDPC) |
| A quote from a book or article | The original book or page |
| Health questions | A qualified health worker or the Ministry of Health |

Fees, dates and rules change, so a chatbot may give you last year's figure. Our own posts say "as checked in" a month for that reason, and you should do the same in your notes.

## When should I not rely on AI alone?

Some choices are too important for a chatbot answer on its own:

- **Health.** Do not use it to decide what is wrong with you or what medicine to take. Ask a health worker.
- **Law.** Do not use it to decide your rights in a contract or a dispute. Speak to a qualified person.
- **Money.** Do not move money, sign up to an investment or send mobile money because a chatbot said it was safe. Scammers also use AI-written messages.
- **Real people.** Google warns that answers about people can be wrong. Never repeat a claim about a named person without proof.
- **School and work.** You are responsible for what you hand in. See [using AI to study without cheating](/post/technology-solutions/ai-tools/using-ai-to-study-without-cheating/).

For a related warning about money scams, see [fake job offers in Tanzania](/post/jobs-and-internships/fake-job-offers-tanzania-warning-signs-never-pay/) if it fits your case.

## A short example

Say you ask a chatbot, "What is the deadline to apply for my course?" It gives a date. Do this:

1. Mark the date as the fact to check.
2. Open the university's own admissions page.
3. Compare. If they agree, good. If not, trust the university's page.
4. If you cannot find the date, phone or email the admissions office.

This is a made-up example, but it shows the habit. It takes about two minutes and can save you a missed deadline.

Checking does not guarantee you are right. It lowers the chance of a mistake. For high-stakes choices, still ask a qualified person.

## Common questions

### Can I just ask the chatbot, "Are you sure?"

You can, but the answer is not proof. It may repeat the mistake or change a correct answer. Use outside sources.

### Are answers with links always right?

No. A link shows where to look. Open it and make sure the page really says what the chatbot says.

### Does a better question reduce mistakes?

It can help the answer fit your needs. It does not make facts safe. See [how to write a good AI prompt](/post/technology-solutions/ai-tools/how-to-write-a-good-ai-prompt/), and still check.

### What if I find a mistake?

Tell the tool. Both Google and Anthropic describe a thumbs-down button for feedback on answers. Then correct your own notes.

## What to do next

1. Before you use your next AI answer, mark every name, number and date.
2. Check each on an official site, and write down when you checked.
3. Remove or soften anything you could not confirm.
4. Keep the habit for your next assignment, message or business decision.

You do not have to distrust the tool or be afraid of it. You only have to stay the person in charge, and you can do that in two minutes.
