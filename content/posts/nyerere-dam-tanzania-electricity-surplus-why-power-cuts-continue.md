In August 2026 Tanzania formally opened the Julius Nyerere Hydropower Project on the Rufiji River. The government says the country now makes far more electricity than it uses. But many people still lose power at home or at the shop, and on 27 June 2026 the whole national grid failed for hours. This post explains the difference between making electricity and delivering it, and what you can do when the lights go off (kukatika kwa umeme). We checked the facts in November 2026.

## The short answer

- The Nyerere plant has a capacity of 2,115 megawatts (MW). Tanzania's total capacity is now 4,646 MW, while peak demand is 2,271 MW, according to the Energy Minister.
- Making power is only the first step. It must also travel on high-voltage transmission lines and then on local lines and transformers to your home.
- The June 2026 blackout was a grid failure, not a shortage of power, according to the Energy Minister.
- TANESCO's toll-free line is 180. Use it to report a fault.
- We did not find an announced change to household electricity prices in the sources we opened. Check EWURA, the regulator, for the latest tariffs.

## What opened in August 2026

Reuters reported that President Samia Suluhu Hassan commissioned the plant on 22 August 2026. It cost about TSh 7.45 trillion (about US$2.84 billion) and construction began in 2019. One news report says the plant has a 131-metre concrete dam and nine turbines of 235 MW each. Nine times 235 gives the 2,115 MW total.

The Energy Minister, Deogratius Ndejembi, gave the numbers in the table below, as reported by Reuters.

| Item | Figure |
|---|---|
| New plant | 2,115 MW |
| Total national capacity | 4,646 MW |
| Peak demand | 2,271 MW |
| Spare capacity | 2,375 MW |

Reuters also said hydropower now gives about 60 percent of Tanzania's electricity, with natural gas providing a large share of the rest. Talks are going on to sell power to neighbours, including Kenya and Zambia.

The project has critics. Conservation groups say the dam affects the Rufiji River, which flows through the Selous Game Reserve. The president answered that the country had "no way to abandon" the project because of its importance.

## Why a surplus does not end power cuts

Think of electricity as water. The dam is the tank. Transmission lines are the big pipes, and the local network is the small pipes in your street. A full tank does nothing if a pipe breaks.

An analysis by African Sustainability Matters, published on 1 September 2026, says spending on high-voltage lines and substations has lagged behind spending on generation. It also points to losses on local lines and to the need for better grid investment. That is one outlet's analysis and it cites no named experts, so read it as a view, not a measured fact.

The June blackout shows the same point in a real event.

### The blackout of 27 June 2026

According to The Chanzo, the grid failed at about 7 pm, during the evening peak. Power was back by midnight. TANESCO's managing director, Lazaro Twange, said three transmission lines had faults. He said the grid's automatic protection then shut parts of the system down to prevent damage.

The reports differ on one detail. One says a grid substation also failed, and another quotes Twange as saying a power station developed a fault. We do not know which is right, so we mention both.

The Permanent Secretary of the Energy Ministry, Felchesmi Mramba, said the first faults were on lines going to Dodoma and then spread to the Chalinze to Dar es Salaam line. The minister said early findings showed a grid failure and that "generation had no challenges whatsoever." He set up an investigation team and told TANESCO to make the grid able to cut off a faulty part while the rest keeps power. He also told TANESCO to speed up the Chalinze to Dodoma and Chalinze to Kinyerezi to Mkuranga lines. We did not find the investigation team's final report.

### The new line to Dodoma

The 400-kilovolt Chalinze to Dodoma line is 345 km long and is paid for by the government, at about TSh 514 billion. On 28 July 2026, TANESCO said it was 94 percent complete and due to finish by 21 August 2026. To finish the work, TANESCO planned temporary outages in parts of the Central, Lake, Northern, Western and South-Western zones, from 30 July to 4 August and from 11 to 17 August. Later reports on whether the line is now in service were not found in the sources we opened.

This is a useful example of something every reader can expect: planned cuts for maintenance are announced. TANESCO said it would give updates through the media and social media so customers can plan.

## What this means for you

For households, the main effect of the dam may come slowly. More supply helps, but only if the lines and transformers near you can carry it. If your street has frequent cuts, the cause could be a local problem rather than a national shortage.

For small businesses that depend on power, such as welders, salons, cold-room sellers and print shops, a surplus is good news only when the supply at your door is steady. A cut at the wrong time can spoil stock or stop work. Keep a record of the date, time and length of each cut. It helps if you need to complain or ask for a fault to be fixed.

## How to report a fault

TANESCO launched a toll-free call centre number, 180, in March 2025, after complaints that callers had to pay. The Guardian reported that the call centre handles up to 15,000 calls a day and up to 50,000 at peak times, so you may wait. When you call:

1. Say your area, street and the nearest landmark.
2. Give your meter number if you have it.
3. Say when the cut began.
4. Ask for a reference number, and write it down with the date.

We could not verify other channels, such as an app or WhatsApp number, so we do not list them. Use only contact details that appear on TANESCO's official site or on its official social pages.

## Prices and the next steps for the grid

Tariffs are set by EWURA, not by TANESCO alone. We looked for a 2026 tariff order and did not find one in the sources we opened. Some websites show different prices per unit, and they do not agree, so we do not print a figure here. Check EWURA's site or your TANESCO notice before you plan a budget.

Looking ahead, the Energy Minister's statements point to three things to watch: the Chalinze to Dodoma line, the Chalinze to Kinyerezi to Mkuranga line, and any export deals with neighbours. Exporting power needs both lines and customers, so it will take time.

## Common questions

### Did the Nyerere dam end power cuts?

Not by itself. The dam raised supply, but cuts can also come from faults on transmission lines, local lines or transformers. The June 2026 blackout happened when the country already had more capacity than demand.

### Will electricity get cheaper?

We did not find an official announcement of lower prices. Tariffs are decided by EWURA. Check its website for any notice.

### How do I report a power cut?

Call TANESCO's toll-free number, 180. Give your location, meter number and the time the cut began, and note the reference number.

### Are planned power cuts announced?

Yes. For the July and August 2026 work on the Chalinze to Dodoma line, TANESCO said it would publish schedules through the media and social media. Watch for notices in your area.

## What to do next

1. Save 180 in your phone as "TANESCO".
2. Write down each long cut with the date, time and length.
3. Follow TANESCO's official pages for planned outages near you.
4. Check EWURA's site for tariff notices before you set a budget.
