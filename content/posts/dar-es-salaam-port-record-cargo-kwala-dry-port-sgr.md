Most of what Tanzanians buy from abroad, from cooking oil to spare parts, arrives through the port of Dar es Salaam. In 2025/26 the port handled a record amount of cargo, and the government is trying to move more of it inland by train instead of by lorry. This post explains the new numbers, what the Kwala dry port is, and what it could mean if you import goods or sell them. We checked the facts in November 2026 (bandari ya Dar es Salaam in Swahili).

## The short answer

- The port handled 33.7 million tonnes in the 2025/26 financial year, up from 27.7 million tonnes the year before, according to the Daily News.
- In March 2026 the government said over 90 percent of cargo leaving the port went by road, and it wants 70 to 80 percent of cargo for rail-linked places to go by SGR or the older metre-gauge railway (MGR).
- Kwala dry port, in Kibaha District, opened on 31 July 2025. It is linked to the SGR.
- Faster clearing is now the main goal. The head of the port authority said growth must be matched by faster cargo clearance.
- If you import, ask your clearing agent where your container will be cleared and how it will travel inland.

## How big the growth is

The port director, Abed G. Abed, told a meeting in Bagamoyo that the port handled 33.7 million tonnes in 2025/26. The Daily News reported this on 16 July 2026. Before the recent changes, yearly growth was 1 to 1.5 million tonnes. Growth was 4 million tonnes in 2024/25 and 6 million in 2025/26.

The Tanzania Ports Authority (TPA) credits better efficiency and more private firms running parts of the port. A longer view comes from a March 2026 report. It says cargo rose from 16.269 million tonnes in 2020/21 to 27.764 million tonnes in 2024/25.

A monthly report shows what is moving. The Citizen reported on 10 June 2026 that in May 2026:

| Cargo type | May 2026 | Change from May 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Vehicles and rolling cargo (RoRo) | 30,442 units | up 88% |
| Containers at the DP World terminal | 44,001 TEUs | up 57% |
| Dry bulk | 267,560 tonnes | up 27% |
| General cargo | 204,978 tonnes | down 2% |

A TEU is a "twenty-foot equivalent unit", the standard way to count containers. A 40-foot container counts as two. The Daily News reported in August 2026 that the port had handled a record 138,000 containers in one month, against 50,000 to 60,000 in 2021. We could not find the underlying TPA statement, so treat that figure with care.

## The old problem: lorries and double handling

Over 90 percent of cargo leaving the port goes by road, The Citizen reported in March 2026. Lorries create traffic jams in Dar es Salaam and damage roads. The Transport Minister, Prof Makame Mbarawa, said that moving cargo to rail would reduce lorry numbers, accidents and road damage. He also admitted that many inland container depots (ICDs) had been licensed in the middle of the city, which made traffic worse. Residents near some of them had protested.

Another cost was "double handling". Until recently, containers had to go first to the Pugu inland container depot and then be loaded on to SGR wagons. TPA's Director General, Plasduce Mbossa, said this raised operating costs. In March 2026 the minister said cargo would soon move directly from the port onto the SGR. In August 2026 the Daily News said a link between the port and the SGR allows cargo to move straight from ships to trains.

## What Kwala dry port is

A dry port is a cargo yard far from the sea where containers are cleared and collected. Kwala is in Kibaha District in the Coast Region, about 90 to 105 km from Dar es Salaam. TanzaniaInvest reported these details of the launch on 31 July 2025:

- It covers over 1,000 hectares, with a first phase of 5 hectares.
- It handles about 820 containers a day and is designed for 300,000 containers a year.
- It has a concrete road to the Dar es Salaam to Morogoro highway and a rail link to the SGR.
- The government said it could take up to 30 percent of the cargo that goes through the port.

SGR electric freight trains began commercial service on 27 June 2025, running between Dar es Salaam and Dodoma. The government bought 1,430 new wagons for TSh 330.2 billion, including 200 container wagons.

The government plans to close or shrink town-based ICDs and move container handling to Kwala and other sites outside the city. Other reports say some containers were cleared at the port but then stuck in a second round of paperwork at Kwala. We saw this only in commentary, not an official statement, so treat it as a risk to ask about, not as proven fact.

## What it means for importers and traders

If you buy goods in bulk from abroad, the changes could affect time and cost. We cannot give a price, because the sources we found give no cost per container for rail compared with road. Here are the practical points.

- **Where will your container go?** Your clearing agent should tell you whether it will be cleared at the port, at Kwala or at another depot.
- **Who clears it?** Clearing needs the Tanzania Revenue Authority (TRA), the port and the railway to work together. A digital single window called TanESWS is meant to help with this. Ask your agent how it affects your paperwork.
- **How is it moving inland?** Rail goes to Morogoro, Dodoma and other central places. The older MGR still serves Kigoma and Zambia routes, according to the minister.
- **What are the storage charges?** Delays usually cost money. Ask for the daily storage rates before the ship arrives.

If you buy small quantities, for example through a freight forwarder or a seller abroad, you may not deal with the port directly. ## What could still go wrong

Record volume has pressure points. The Citizen quotes Mbossa as saying the next focus is faster cargo clearance. Industry leaders told The Citizen the next challenge is moving cargo efficiently from the port to inland and neighbouring markets. The government says it will add three new berths and expand yards, but these are plans, not finished projects.

Prices on the shelf depend on many things, so do not expect cheaper goods only because the port is busier. We found no data linking the port's growth to shop prices.

## Common questions

### What is the Kwala dry port?

It is a large cargo yard in Kibaha District, Coast Region, linked by road and by the SGR to Dar es Salaam. It opened on 31 July 2025 to ease congestion at the port and in the city.

### Does the SGR carry goods?

Yes. Electric freight trains have run between Dar es Salaam and Dodoma since 27 June 2025, and Kwala is a stop for containers coming from the port.

### Will goods now arrive cheaper?

We found no official figure that shows lower costs for importers. The government says rail should cut logistics costs. Ask your agent for quotes for road and rail.

### Does this affect travellers?

Not directly. Passenger trains are a separate service. Our post on [the SGR passenger train, fares and tickets](/post/tanzania-news/sgr-train-tanzania-dar-es-salaam-dodoma-fares-tickets/) covers that.

## What to do next

1. If you import, ask your clearing agent where your container will be cleared and by which route it will go inland.
2. Ask for the daily storage rates and the expected clearing time in writing.
3. Keep copies of all customs and transport documents.
4. Follow TPA and TRA notices for changes to procedures.
