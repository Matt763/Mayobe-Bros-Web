AI tools can draft text, summarise long documents, write down meetings and tidy up your writing. Used well, they save time on routine work. Used carelessly, they can give wrong answers or leak private information.

This list covers ten tools that exist today, what each one is good for, and what we could check about free plans. Prices and limits change often, so check each tool's own website before you rely on them. We checked what we could in November 2026.

## The short answer

- Pick one general assistant first (ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude or Microsoft Copilot). Most people do not need all ten tools.
- AI can sound sure and still be wrong. Check names, numbers and dates yourself.
- Do not paste private work data, client details, passwords, ID numbers or exam material into an AI tool unless your employer or school allows it.
- Free plans exist for some tools, but limits are small and change. We state a free plan only where we checked it on the tool's own site.
- AI is a helper. You are still responsible for what you send to your boss, lecturer or customer.

## General assistants

These are chat tools. You type a question or a task and they reply in text. They can explain ideas, draft emails, outline essays, suggest formulas for spreadsheets and help you plan.

### 1. ChatGPT (OpenAI)

A chat assistant from OpenAI. Good for drafting, rewriting, brainstorming and explaining hard topics in simple words. Ask it to "explain this like I am in Form Two" and it will adjust.

Free plan: plans and limits change often, so check chatgpt.com for what is free today.

### 2. Google Gemini

Google's AI assistant. Google describes it as a tool for summarising documents, coding and brainstorming. It can be handy if you already use Gmail, Google Docs and Google Drive.

Free plan: Google's overview page did not state this clearly, so check the Gemini app in your country.

### 3. Claude (Anthropic)

Another chat assistant. People often use it for long reading, such as summarising a report or comparing two documents, and for careful writing.

Free plan: plans and limits change often, so check claude.ai for what is free today.

### 4. Microsoft Copilot

Microsoft's assistant, built into Microsoft 365 apps such as Word, Excel and PowerPoint on paid plans. Microsoft's page for individuals lists Personal at US$9.99 per month (as checked in November 2026) and says Copilot features are part of those paid plans. It does not clearly list a free version.

Useful if your office or school already runs on Microsoft apps. Ask your IT team before using it with company files.

## Research and notes

### 5. Perplexity

An AI search tool. You ask a question, it searches the web and replies with links to its sources. Perplexity's own help page says each answer includes citations so you can check the original pages.

That makes it a better starting point for research than a plain chatbot, but you must still open the links. Free plan: check perplexity.ai for what is free today.

### 6. Notion AI

Notion is a notes and project tool. Its AI features sit inside your pages and databases. Notion's own page says it can help improve writing, fill in database fields and transcribe and summarise meetings.

Free plan: Notion says the Free plan includes a trial of Notion AI. Notion's page says AI is included in its Business plan with a monthly credit allowance, so regular use may need a paid plan. Check the current terms.

### 7. Otter.ai

A tool that records and transcribes meetings and then makes a summary. Handy for lectures, interviews and team calls, as long as everyone knows they are being recorded.

Free plan: Otter's pricing page describes a Basic plan as "free forever". Limits we saw in November 2026 include 300 transcription minutes per month and a 30-minute cap per conversation. Limits change, so look again before you depend on it.

## Writing and design

### 8. Grammarly

A writing checker that finds spelling, grammar and tone problems. Useful if English is your second language and you write emails, CVs or assignments in English.

Free plan: Grammarly's plans page lists a Free plan with grammar and spelling checks, tone visibility and 100 AI prompts per month (as checked in November 2026). More advanced rewrites need a paid plan.

### 9. Canva (with its AI tools)

Canva is a design tool for posters, flyers, social media posts and presentations. It has added AI features, called Magic Studio, for things like generating images and resizing designs.

Free plan: which plan includes which AI features changes often, so check canva.com.

## Automation

### 10. Zapier

Zapier connects apps so one action triggers another. For example, when someone fills a Google Form, Zapier can add a row to a spreadsheet and send you an email. It also has AI features for building these workflows.

Free plan: yes. Zapier's pricing page lists a Free plan with 100 tasks per month and simple two-step Zaps (as checked in November 2026). It is enough to test an idea, not to run a busy business.

## Protect your data when using AI tools

When you paste text into an AI tool, it goes to a company's computers, often in another country. The company's privacy policy decides how long it keeps the text and whether it can use it to improve its systems. Many tools let you change this in settings.

Simple rules that cost nothing:

1. Never paste passwords, bank or M-Pesa details, NIDA numbers or medical records.
2. Remove names and phone numbers from client messages before asking for help.
3. Do not upload exam papers, internal contracts or confidential company reports unless your employer approves it.
4. Look for a setting about "chat history" or "using your data to improve the model" and read what it says.
5. If your employer gave you a company AI account, use that one for work, not your personal account.

Tanzania also has a Personal Data Protection Act (Act No. 11 of 2022). It sets rules for organisations that collect and handle other people's personal data. If your job involves customer data, ask your manager what the policy is. This is general information, not legal advice.

## Common questions

### Which AI tool should I start with?

Start with one general assistant that works well on your phone and your data plan. Try the same task in two tools and keep the one that answers more clearly.

### Can AI do my assignment for me?

You should not submit AI text as your own work if your school forbids it. You may also be caught and penalised. Use AI to understand a topic or check your grammar, then write in your own words. Read your institution's rules first.

### Are AI answers always correct?

No. These tools can invent facts, names and links. Check anything important against an official source.

### Do I need the internet all the time?

Yes, for all ten tools. If bundles are expensive, do your AI tasks in one session and keep prompts short.

## What to do next

1. Pick one task you do often, such as writing emails or summarising notes, and try one tool on it this week.
2. Open that tool's settings and read its data and privacy options.
3. Note how much time it really saved. If it saved none, drop it.
