The FIFA Club World Cup brings together the champion clubs of each continent to decide the best club team in the world. It is different from the men's World Cup, which is for national teams. This guide explains how the tournament began, lists every edition, and shows what changed in 2025.

All facts were checked as of 24 November 2026 on FIFA's own pages. Some plans for the future can still change, so we say clearly what is confirmed.

## Key points

- FIFA held the first Club World Cup in Brazil in 2000. Corinthians won.
- After 2000 there was a four-year wait. The tournament returned in Japan in 2005 and ran most years after that.
- European clubs have won 17 of the 21 editions up to 2025. South American clubs have won the other four.
- In 2025 the tournament grew to 32 teams and was played in the United States. Chelsea beat Paris Saint-Germain 3-0 in the final.
- FIFA's Council said the 32-team tournament will be played every four years.
- None of the four African clubs in 2025 got past the group stage.

## What the tournament is

The Club World Cup is a FIFA competition for clubs that won their continental title. Africa's place goes to the winner of the CAF Champions League, Europe's to the winner of the UEFA Champions League, and so on. FIFA's six confederations are AFC (Asia), CAF (Africa), CONCACAF (North and Central America), CONMEBOL (South America), OFC (Oceania) and UEFA (Europe).

The tournament has often been called the "world club championship". The winner is called a world club champion.

## How it began

FIFA says the first Club World Cup was held in Brazil in 2000, with eight clubs playing in Rio de Janeiro and Sao Paulo. The six continental champions took part. Two more teams were invited: Real Madrid, who had won the older Intercontinental Cup, and the host club Corinthians.

The eight clubs were split into two groups of four. The group winners met in the final. Corinthians and Vasco da Gama, both from Brazil, drew 0-0 after extra time, and Corinthians won 4-3 on penalties.

Then came a gap. FIFA's own history says there was a four-year wait before the next tournament, in Japan in 2005.

### The Intercontinental Cup connection

Before this, a match between the European and South American champions was played for decades. It was called the Intercontinental Cup, and from 1980 it was played in Japan as the Toyota Cup. From 2005 the Toyota Cup merged into the FIFA Club World Cup, and the tournament has been run by FIFA since.

In 2017 the FIFA Council went further. It recognised the European and South American winners of the Intercontinental Cup from 1960 to 2004 as club world champions. This is why you may see different "world title" counts for a club, depending on whether the old Intercontinental Cup is counted. The table below lists only the FIFA Club World Cup editions.

## Who dominates

Looking at the table, European clubs have won 17 of 21 finals. South American clubs have won four: Corinthians twice, Sao Paulo and Internacional. No club from Africa, Asia, North America or Oceania has won.

Real Madrid have won the most titles. FIFA called their win in February 2023 a record-extending fifth. Barcelona have three, and Corinthians, Bayern Munich and Chelsea have two each.

## The 2025 edition in the United States

FIFA announced the new format in December 2023. The 2025 tournament had 32 teams from all six confederations, was held in the United States from 15 June to 13 July 2025, and used a new qualification system based on continental titles and results over four seasons.

- **Groups:** eight groups of four teams, each team playing the others once.
- **Knockouts:** the top two in each group went to the round of 16. From there, every round was a single match up to the final.
- **Numbers:** FIFA reports 48 group matches, 144 goals and a total attendance of 1,667,819 in the group stage.
- **Prize money:** FIFA's Council announced USD 1 billion in prize money for the clubs.

The final was played at MetLife Stadium in the New York and New Jersey area on 13 July 2025. Chelsea beat Paris Saint-Germain 3-0. Cole Palmer scored in the 22nd and 30th minutes and Joao Pedro in the 43rd.

## How African clubs have done

African clubs have reached the final twice. TP Mazembe of DR Congo lost 3-0 to Inter Milan in 2010. Raja Casablanca of Morocco lost 2-0 to Bayern Munich in 2013.

Al Ahly of Egypt have also done well. FIFA coverage lists their bronze medals in 2006, 2020, 2021 and 2023.

In 2025, four African clubs took part: Al Ahly, Espérance de Tunis, Mamelodi Sundowns and Wydad Casablanca. None got past the group stage. Sundowns finished with four points, the most of the four, after beating Ulsan 1-0 and drawing 0-0 with Fluminense. Al Ahly drew 4-4 with Porto, which was one of the tournament's notable matches. ## What changes next

In March 2023 FIFA's Council said the 32-team Club World Cup will be played every four years from June 2025. That puts the next one in 2029. Reports say FIFA has told the confederations it will be played in the summer. We could not find an official announcement of the host, so check FIFA's website for news. The yearly Intercontinental Cup continues in the years between.

## Common questions

### Is the Club World Cup the same as the World Cup?

No. The World Cup is for national teams. The Club World Cup is for clubs.

### How many teams played in 2025?

Thirty-two, from all six confederations.

### Which club has won the most titles?

Real Madrid, with five in the FIFA Club World Cup, according to FIFA.

### Has an African club ever won?

No. Two African clubs have reached the final and lost: TP Mazembe in 2010 and Raja Casablanca in 2013.

## What to do next

1. Bookmark FIFA's Club World Cup page and look for the 2029 host announcement.
2. Watch for the Intercontinental Cup, which is played each year.
3. If you follow African football, track the CAF Champions League. Its winner earns a place in the next Club World Cup.

Every great club once started small. A trophy like this is a long road, and the stories on the way are worth following.
