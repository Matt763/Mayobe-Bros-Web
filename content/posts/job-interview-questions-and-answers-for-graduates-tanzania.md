The invitation has arrived, and now the nerves start. Your heart beats faster, and you wonder what they will ask. Almost every first interview uses the same few questions, so you can prepare for them, and this post shows you how.

The advice here is general advice from career services in the UK and the US. Ways of interviewing differ between employers in Tanzania, so use it as a base and adjust to the place you are going to.

## The short answer

You do not need perfect answers. You need honest, prepared answers that show you understand the job and can do it. Practise out loud, bring the right papers, arrive early, and send a short thank-you afterwards.

- Read the advert again and learn the main duties.
- Prepare answers to the common questions below, with real examples from your life.
- Use a simple structure (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for "tell me about a time" questions.
- Prepare four or five questions to ask them.
- Plan your journey, your clothes and your papers the day before.
- Send a thank-you message within about two days.

## Before the interview: research and practise

Prospects, the UK graduate careers service, suggests learning about the employer's work, its recent projects and its culture. Reread the job advert and your own application. If you can find out who will interview you, learn their names and roles.

Then practise. Do at least one mock interview with a lecturer, a friend or a family member who will be honest. Say your answers out loud. You can also record yourself on your phone and listen back. Hearing yourself is uncomfortable, and it works.

If the interview is by phone or video, test your phone, data bundle and charger beforehand. Find a quiet place. Tell the people at home when you will be busy.

## The questions you will probably hear

These eleven come from the Prospects list of common questions. The advice under each one is our own summary.

1. **"Tell me about yourself."** Keep it short. Start with your qualification, then the strongest things you have done, then the skills that fit the job. If you have little work history, talk about what you studied and any projects, volunteering or part-time work.
2. **"Why do you want to work here?"** Show that you researched them. Link something real about the organisation to something in the advert.
3. **"What are your strengths?"** Pick three or four qualities the job asks for. Give one example for each.
4. **"What are your weaknesses?"** Name a real weakness that you are working on, and say what you do about it. Avoid stock answers such as "I work too hard".
5. **"How do you prioritise your work?"** Describe how you handle several tasks and deadlines, such as exams plus an assignment plus a part-time job.
6. **"How would you improve what we do?"** Offer one or two polite, practical ideas that fit the role. Do not criticise them harshly, and do not say nothing needs to change.
7. **"Tell me about a difficult situation you handled."** Pick an example where you stayed calm and found a way forward.
8. **"What is your greatest achievement?"** Choose something that shows teamwork, effort or determination. Prepare a few options. Prospects notes that graduating alone is too common to stand out, unless you overcame real difficulty to do it.
9. **"What are your goals?"** Give realistic short and long-term goals that fit the role and the organisation.
10. **"What are your salary expectations?"** If the advert states a salary, start from that. Check what similar jobs pay, and give a range rather than one figure. Show that you are open to talk.
11. **"Do you have any questions for us?"** The answer is always yes. See the section below.

Write short notes for each answer, not a full script. If you memorise word for word, you will freeze when they ask it differently.

## How to answer "tell me about a time" questions

Some questions start with "Tell me about a time when..." or "Give me an example of...". The University of Washington career centre teaches the STAR method for these. It has four parts:

- **Situation:** the background, in a sentence or two.
- **Task:** what you had to achieve.
- **Action:** what you personally did. This should be the longest part of your answer.
- **Result:** what happened, with a number if you can.

Here is a made-up example, to show the shape. Say the question is "Tell me about a time you worked in a team."

*"In my final year, four of us had to present a group project in three weeks (Situation). I was asked to organise the work (Task). I made a simple plan on paper, shared the tasks by each person's strength, and checked progress with the group every Friday (Action). We handed in on time, and our lecturer said the work was well organised (Result)."*

This is only an example of the structure. Use something that really happened to you, and never exaggerate. Interviewers often ask follow-up questions, and a true account is easy to explain.

Prepare three or four stories from study, group work, volunteering, a job, a family business or community work. One story can answer several questions.

## What to wear, what to bring and when to arrive

**Clothes.** Dress clean, neat and suited to the workplace. Prospects says it is safer to be a little too smart than too casual. If you are unsure, you can politely ask the employer about the dress code.

**What to bring.** Prospects suggests a pen and notebook, your CV, the invitation, relevant certificates or work samples, photo ID, water, and money for travel and food. In Tanzania, bring the originals of your certificates and a few copies, and keep them in a clean folder.

**Getting there.** Plan the journey the day before. Check the route, the fare and how long it takes in traffic. For a Dar es Salaam interview, for example, allow extra time for jams. Aim to arrive well before the start, so you can sit down, breathe and read your notes.

**Nerves.** Sleep as well as you can, eat before you go, and drink water. Prospects points out that the worst outcome is simply not getting the job. If you need a moment to think before you answer, pause. A pause looks calm.

## Questions you can ask them

Prospects advises preparing at least four or five questions, because some will be answered during the interview. Good topics are the work itself, the team, training and what happens next. Examples:

- What does a typical day in this role look like?
- How could someone do really well in the first three months?
- What training or progress is possible in this team?
- Can you tell me more about the team I would join?
- What happens next, and when can I expect to hear from you?

Save questions about pay and leave for when you have an offer. Do not ask what you could have found on their website.

## After the interview: thank-you and follow-up

Colorado College's career centre suggests sending a thank-you letter or email within 48 hours. Mention one or two specific things from the conversation and keep it short. Check the spelling carefully. If you met several people, write to each one.

Here is a short example you can adapt.

*Subject: Thank you for the interview*

*Dear Mr or Ms [surname of your interviewer],*

*Thank you for meeting me on Tuesday about the sales assistant position. I enjoyed hearing how your team plans the week, and it made me more interested in the role. Please contact me if you need any more information. My number is 07XX XXX XXX.*

*Kind regards,*
*Your name*

If the interviewers said when you would hear back, wait until that date has passed. If they did not, many career services suggest waiting a week or two before asking. This is common practice and not a rule. Then send one polite message asking about the timeline.

A delay does not always mean no. Colorado College puts it this way: no news does not necessarily mean bad news. Waiting is hard, and our guide on [what to do after a job rejection](/post/jobs-and-internships/job-rejection-what-to-do-next-tanzania/) can help whichever way the answer goes.

## Common questions

### How long should my answer to "Tell me about yourself" be?

Prospects says to keep it under five minutes. For a first job, shorter is fine. Stop when you have covered your studies, your best experience and why you want this job.

### What if I have no work experience?

Use what you have: study projects, group work, volunteering, helping in a family business, sports or community roles. Say what you did and what you learned.

### What if I do not know the answer to a question?

Do not invent one. Pause, and say that you have not done that exactly, then explain how you would find out or what you have done that is close.

### Should I talk about salary first?

No. Wait for them to bring it up, or for an offer. If they ask what you expect, give a realistic range based on the advert and on similar jobs.

## What to do next

1. Write answers in note form for the eleven questions above.
2. Choose three true examples from your life and shape each one with the four STAR parts.
3. Do one practice interview with someone you trust this week.
4. The day before, pack your papers, plan the journey and prepare your clothes.

You have already done the hard part, which was being invited. Prepare calmly, and let them see the person who earned the invitation.
