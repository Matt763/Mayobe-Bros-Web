Few things hurt like a data bundle that is gone before the week ends. For a student, a bundle is real money. A game can use it in ways you do not see, and one big update can empty it in minutes.

This post shows where a game uses data, and three settings that give you control. The menu names come from Google's help pages, and your phone may use different names.

## The short answer

Stop automatic updates on mobile data, turn on Data Saver, and set a data warning and a data limit. Download and update games on Wi-Fi when you can. Check which apps use the most data once a week.

- Set Google Play to update apps over Wi-Fi only.
- Turn on Data Saver so apps stop using mobile data in the background.
- Set a data warning, and a data limit if you want a hard stop.
- Prefer offline games when your bundle is small.
- Look at the per-app data list to find what really uses your bundle.

## Where does a game use your data?

A game can use data in five main ways.

1. **The first download.** Some games are large. We do not quote any size here, because sizes change with each update.
2. **Updates.** Games update often. Each update downloads new files, and a big one can be a large download.
3. **Online play.** Matches against other players send data all the time. Lag also makes you retry, which can cost more.
4. **Ads and extras.** Free games that show ads load them from the internet, and some games download extra content while you play.
5. **Background use.** A game or app can use data when you are not looking at it, for example to check for news or to sync.

We cannot give an honest figure of megabytes per hour for any named game, because it depends on the game, the version and what you do. The reliable way is to measure your own phone, and we show how below.

## How do I stop updates from eating my bundle?

Google Play can update apps by itself. If it does this on mobile data, a big game update can quietly use your bundle. Google's help page gives these steps for the Play Store:

1. Open the **Google Play Store** and tap your profile picture at the top right.
2. Go to **Settings**, then **Network preferences**, then **Auto-update apps**.
3. Choose **Update over Wi-Fi only**, or **Don't auto-update apps** if you want full control.

The page also lists a choice to update with a limited amount of mobile data. If you do not understand it, pick Wi-Fi only.

You can also change this for a single game. Open the Play Store, tap your profile picture, go to **Manage apps & device**, then **Manage**, and open the game's page. Tap the three dots and turn **Enable auto update** off or on.

If you choose Wi-Fi only, you must find Wi-Fi from time to time to keep your games up to date. A school, a library or a trusted friend's home network can help. Be careful on public networks: avoid logging in to important accounts on them.

## How do I use Data Saver and per-app limits?

Data Saver is a switch that stops most apps from using mobile data in the background. Apps that are already open can still use data, so it does not stop a game you are playing. It helps with the quiet use that you do not notice.

On a Google Pixel phone, the steps are:

1. Open **Settings**.
2. Tap **Network & internet**, then **Data Saver**.
3. Turn it on. A small icon appears in the status bar.

If an app must keep working in the background, go to **Data Saver**, then **Unrestricted data**, and switch that app on.

Other phone makers use other names. Phones from Samsung, Tecno, Infinix and Xiaomi can place these settings in different menus and give them different names. We did not check those menus, so if you cannot find a setting, use the search box at the top of Settings and type "data".

## How do I set a data warning and a data limit?

A warning tells you when you are near the end. A limit turns mobile data off when you reach it. Google's Pixel help gives these steps, and they may differ on your phone.

**Check which apps use data**

1. Open **Settings**, then **Network & internet**, then **SIMs**.
2. Tap **App data usage**.
3. Scroll below the graph to see how much each app used. You can change the time period.

**Set a warning**

1. In the same SIMs menu, tap **Data warning & limit**.
2. Turn on **Set data warning**, then tap **Data warning**.
3. Enter a number, choose MB or GB, and tap **Set**.

**Set a limit**

1. In **Data warning & limit**, turn on **Set data limit** and tap **Ok**.
2. Tap **Data limit**, enter a number, and tap **Set**.
3. When you reach the limit, mobile data turns off by itself.

To turn data back on, return to the same menu and switch off **Set data limit**.

### A worked example with made-up numbers

Say you have a bundle of 3 GB for the month, and you want to keep 1 GB for study and messages. This is only an example, and not a price or a real offer. You could set the data warning at 2 GB. If you want a hard stop, set the limit at 2.5 GB, so that you still have a small reserve to switch back if you need it.

Now say a game update is 500 MB. Over Wi-Fi it costs you nothing from the bundle. On mobile data, it takes one sixth of the 3 GB (500 divided by 3,000). The point is simple: big downloads belong on Wi-Fi.

Your mobile operator may count data differently from your phone. Google says to check with your carrier for its figures and your plan limits. For current bundle prices, look at your own operator's page or app, because we do not quote any here.

## Which games suit a small bundle?

Games that work offline use no data while you play. Look for the "Offline" tag on the Play Store page. Some games are only partly offline, so read the description. Our post on [free games for low-end Android phones](/post/video-games-updates/free-games-for-low-end-android-phones/) shows how to read these labels.

If you play online, keep the matches for times when you are on Wi-Fi. Also see the ideas in our post on how to [save mobile data when studying online](/post/educational/online-learning/save-mobile-data-when-studying-online/). Many of them work for games too.

## Common questions

### Does Data Saver stop my game from using data?

Not while you are playing. Google says Data Saver limits most apps' background use over mobile data, and apps already active can still use it. Use the data limit if you want a hard stop.

### Is Wi-Fi only safe for updates?

It is a good habit for your bundle. Install updates from the Play Store on a network you trust.

### Can I find out how much data one game used?

Yes. Open the app data usage list in your phone's data settings. It shows how much each app used in the time period you choose. The menu name may be different on your phone.

### Will an operator give me a special games bundle?

We could not confirm that any operator in Tanzania offers a bundle for games or for the Play Store today. Ask your own operator, and be careful with offers that come from strangers by SMS or social media.

## What to do next

1. Set Google Play to update over Wi-Fi only.
2. Turn on Data Saver, and set a warning at a number that suits your bundle.
3. Open the app data usage list this week, and see what uses the most.
4. Keep one or two offline games for days when the bundle is low.

Small habits protect a small bundle, and you do not have to give up the games you enjoy.
