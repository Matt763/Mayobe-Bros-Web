If you already sell on your ordinary WhatsApp, you know the problem. Customers ask the same questions all day, your photos are lost inside chats, and a serious buyer cannot tell if you are a real business.

WhatsApp has a separate app for this, called WhatsApp Business. This post shows how to set it up step by step. Menus change with each app version, so use this as a map and compare it with what you see on your own phone.

## The short answer

Install the WhatsApp Business app, fill in a clear business profile, add your best products with photos and names, and set up a greeting so people get an answer when you are busy. Then follow the selling rules, because Meta can remove listings that break them. None of this brings sales by itself. Customers still need to trust you and find you.

- Fill in four things first: business name, category, description and contact details.
- Add 5 to 10 products with clear photos before you tell customers.
- Set a greeting and an away message so nobody waits in silence.
- Check each tool inside your own app. Features differ by country and version.
- Never list items that the Meta rules ban.

## What is the difference between WhatsApp Business and normal WhatsApp?

WhatsApp Business is a separate app. WhatsApp's own business page says it is built with the small business owner in mind (as checked in November 2026). It lets you chat one to one with customers and gives you tools such as a business profile, ways to show your products, and replies when you are away.

Normal WhatsApp has none of these. Everything lands in a personal profile.

We could not confirm the app's cost on the official pages we opened, because the product page does not state it. The app is downloaded from Google Play or the App Store, so look at the store listing for the current terms. WhatsApp's help pages also describe a different product, the Business Platform, which is for medium and large businesses. That is not what this post covers, and it has its own pricing. If anyone offers to sell you "business broadcasts" or bulk messaging, check the official pages first. Such features can be limited and may carry charges in some countries, so we do not promise them here.

Use a separate number for the business if you can, so your family chats stay private. Ask WhatsApp's help centre how a number can be used before you change anything.

## How do I build a profile people trust?

WhatsApp's page says a business profile gives customers helpful information in one place. Treat it like your shop sign.

1. **Name.** Use the name customers know from your shop or stall.
2. **Category.** Pick the closest one, such as clothing, food or electronics.
3. **Description.** Write one or two plain sentences: what you sell, where you work, who you serve.
4. **Address and hours.** Add them if you have a fixed place. If you sell only from home, think about whether you want to show a full address.
5. **Contact details.** Add a second way to reach you, like a phone number or website, if you have one.
6. **Profile picture.** Use your shop front, your products or a plain logo you own. A clear picture works better than a blurry one.

Do not claim things you cannot prove, like "best prices in Dar" or "100% genuine". Our post on [earning the trust of customers as a new online seller](/post/business-ideas/e-commerce/earn-customer-trust-new-online-seller-tanzania/) explains what builds trust honestly.

## How do I make a product catalogue?

A catalogue is a list of your products inside your profile. People can browse it without scrolling through old chats.

WhatsApp's help page (we read the Swahili version) says each catalogue item has a unique name. It can also have a price, a description, a website link and a product code. Prices are optional, and the page says that adding prices is available only in some countries. We could not confirm whether it works in Tanzania, so check inside your own app. If the price field is not there, write the price in the item description or in your reply to the customer.

The same page says customers can add items to a cart and send it to the chat with the business, and then talk about payment options.

To add an item, open the app, look for the catalogue or Tools menu, and choose to add a product. Names can change, so follow the screen on your phone.

Tips for good listings:

- Take photos in daylight, near a window, on a plain background. Show the real item, not a picture from the internet.
- Use a name a buyer would type, like "Black school shoes, size 38", not "Special offer".
- Put the size, colour, what is included and how long delivery takes in the description.
- Only list what you have or can really supply. Remove sold-out items.
- If you do show prices, check them against your cost first. Our post on [how to price your products](/post/business-ideas/startups/how-to-price-your-products-small-business-tanzania/) shows how.

## How can I answer customers faster?

The WhatsApp page says you can greet people the moment they start a conversation and respond to messages even when you are away. In most versions of the app these tools sit in a Tools or Business tools menu. Look there for greeting message, away message and quick replies. We could not confirm quick replies or labels on the official pages we opened, so check whether your version has them.

Write short messages:

- **Greeting:** "Hello, thank you for contacting [shop name]. We will answer within an hour. Open: Monday to Saturday, 8:00 to 18:00."
- **Away message:** "We are closed now. Leave your question and we will reply tomorrow morning."
- **Quick replies:** answers to questions you get every day, such as where you are, how delivery works and how to pay.

Do not copy a long script. Customers can feel when a message is automatic. Keep it kind, short and true. If you promise "within an hour", keep that promise.

## How do I take orders and payment safely?

Be calm and clear in the chat. Confirm the item, the amount, the place and the time. Ask the customer to pay only to the number or merchant number you have given. WhatsApp's help page says the cart lets customers and the business talk about payment options, so the payment is arranged between you. We did not find an official page describing in-app payments for Tanzania, so we do not describe any.

For mobile money, confirm the payment on your own phone or portal before handing over goods. Our post on [mobile money for business](/post/business-ideas/startups/mobile-money-for-business-lipa-kwa-simu-tanzania/) explains merchant numbers and how to guard against fake messages.

If you deliver, send the customer a photo of the item and tell them when the rider will arrive. Write each order in your cash book. Our post on [simple business records](/post/business-ideas/startups/simple-business-records-small-traders-cash-book-tanzania/) shows how.

## What are Meta's selling rules?

Meta's Commerce Policies cover selling on Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp and Messenger, including WhatsApp Business catalogues (as checked in November 2026). The policy lists many categories that cannot be sold or promoted, including adult products, alcohol, tobacco, weapons, prescription drugs, medical and healthcare products, gambling and stolen goods. Goods that infringe other people's rights are also not allowed. Read the full list on the Meta page before you list anything unusual.

The policy says that violations can lead to rejected or removed listings, and to restrictions on commerce features. If you break the rules again and again, it can lead to more action on your account. So list only items that you are sure are allowed and that you have a right to sell.

## Common questions

### Do I need a shop to use WhatsApp Business?

No. Many sellers work from home or a small stall. Be honest about where you are and how customers get their goods.

### Can I use the same number on both apps?

Check WhatsApp's help pages for how a number can be used. Many sellers prefer a separate number for the business.

### Will the catalogue bring me customers?

No tool can promise that. A catalogue makes it easier for people who already know you to order. You still need to tell people about it, for example with your shop sign or by word of mouth. Our post on [low-cost online business ideas](/post/business-ideas/e-commerce/low-cost-online-business-ideas-tanzania/) covers other ways to start.

### Is my customer list safe to use for messages?

Only message people who have contacted you or agreed to hear from you. See our post on [consent for SMS and email marketing](/post/business-ideas/marketing/sms-and-email-marketing-consent-tanzania/).

## What to do next

1. Download the WhatsApp Business app and compare each screen in this post with your own.
2. Finish your profile and add five products with clear photos.
3. Set a greeting and an away message you can keep.
4. Read the Meta Commerce Policies for your type of goods.

Start small and keep every promise to your customers. That is how a small WhatsApp shop earns a good name.
