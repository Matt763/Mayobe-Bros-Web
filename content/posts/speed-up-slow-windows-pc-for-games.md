An old or low-cost laptop can feel like it is fighting you. The game stutters, the fan screams and you wonder if it is worth the trouble. Often a few free changes help. Sometimes the honest answer is that the machine has reached its limit, and this post helps you tell which case you are in.

The steps come from Microsoft's own help pages. The menu paths are for Windows 11, with Windows 10 differences noted.

## The short answer

First find out how much memory (RAM) and what processor your PC has. Then restart, turn off startup apps, free disk space, set the power mode, install updates, and lower the settings inside the game. Microsoft warns that on older hardware these steps may not help much, and a newer PC may be the real fix.

- Check your specs in Settings, System, About.
- Disable startup apps in Task Manager.
- Free disk space and remove apps you do not use.
- Set the power mode to best performance while you play.
- Lower the game's own graphics settings.
- Do not use cracked software or "booster" tools.

## How do I check what my PC has?

You cannot judge a slow PC until you know what is inside. Microsoft's help page gives this route.

1. Right-click the **Start** button and choose **Settings**.
2. Select **System**, then **About**.
3. Under **Device specifications**, read the **Processor**, the **Installed RAM** and whether Windows is **32-bit or 64-bit**.
4. Under **Windows specifications**, see which Windows version and edition you have.

Now compare these numbers with the system requirements on the game's own store page or its maker's website. Every game lists what it needs. If your PC is clearly below the minimum, no setting will fix it, and you should choose a lighter game instead.

## What are the quick wins: restart, startup apps and disk space?

Start with the free and safe steps. Microsoft lists all of these.

1. **Restart the PC.** Select Start, then Power, then Restart. A PC that has run for days often gets slow.
2. **Close what you do not need.** Press **Ctrl + Shift + Esc** to open Task Manager. On the **Processes** tab, close apps you are not using. Browser tabs count too.
3. **Turn off startup apps.** In Task Manager, select **Startup apps**, right-click an app you do not need at start-up and choose **Disable**. This does not remove the app. It only stops it from opening each time the PC starts.
4. **Uninstall apps you do not use.** Go to **Start, Settings, Apps, Installed apps**, open the menu (three dots) next to the app and choose **Uninstall**. On Windows 10 it is Settings, Apps, Apps & features.
5. **Free disk space.** Go to **Settings, System, Storage**. Run **Storage Sense**, and open **Temporary files** to remove what you do not need. Microsoft says that low disk space causes slowdowns, so keep some free.
6. **Turn off unneeded notifications.** Go to **Settings, System, Notifications**.

Take care when you disable startup apps. If you do not know what an item is, leave it alone or look it up first. Do not disable your security software.

## What about power mode, visual effects and updates?

These three settings are also on Microsoft's list.

**Power mode.** On Windows 11, go to **Settings, System, Power & battery**, and choose **Best performance** in the Power mode list. Microsoft warns that this uses more power, which can drain a laptop battery faster and make it run warmer. Plug the laptop in when you play, and keep it on a hard, flat surface so that air can reach the vents. Windows 10 has no such list. Use **Settings, System, Power & sleep, Additional power settings**, then choose **High performance**, and select "Show additional plans" if you do not see it.

**Visual effects.** Search the Start menu for "Adjust the appearance and performance of Windows". On the **Visual Effects** tab, choose **Adjust for best performance**. The desktop looks plainer, and the PC has less to draw.

**Updates and drivers.** Go to **Settings, Windows Update, Check for updates**. For optional driver updates, open **Advanced options, Optional updates**. Download over Wi-Fi if your bundle is small, because updates can be large. See our guide on [saving mobile data](/post/educational/online-learning/save-mobile-data-when-studying-online/) if you use a phone hotspot.

**A security scan.** Open **Settings, Privacy & security, Windows Security, Virus & threat protection**, and run a **Quick scan**. Keep your antivirus switched on. Never turn it off to make a game run faster.

Microsoft also lists optimizing drives, which is under "Defragment and Optimize Drives" in the Start menu. Select a drive and choose Optimize.

## Which game settings cost the most?

Microsoft's page does not cover game settings, so this part is general knowledge, not an official source. We give no frame-rate figures, because they depend on your PC and the game.

In most games, the settings below ask the most from the PC. Lower them one at a time, and test after each change.

- **Resolution.** A lower resolution means fewer pixels to draw.
- **Shadows and lighting.** These are often costly. Set them to low or off.
- **Effects and detail.** Reduce effects, view distance and texture quality.
- **Full screen.** Many games run best in full screen, with other windows closed.

If the game has a "low" or "performance" preset, begin there.

## When is the PC too old, and what does Windows 10 ending mean?

Microsoft says that on older hardware, upgrading may be the best long-term fix. Signs that your PC has reached its limit: it is below the game's minimum requirements, it is slow even after the steps above, or it has very little RAM.

There is one more point about age. Microsoft states that Windows 10 support ended on 14 October 2025. That means no more security updates, and no technical support from Microsoft. The PC still runs, but it is at greater risk from viruses and malware. As checked in November 2026, Microsoft's page lists these options:

- Upgrade to Windows 11 if your PC meets the requirements.
- Buy a new PC with Windows 11.
- Join the consumer Extended Security Updates programme, which covers Windows 10 until 12 October 2027.

We do not list the Windows 11 hardware rules here. Check Microsoft's own page for them. If you must keep an older PC online, be extra careful with downloads, emails and links, and play only games from official stores and publishers.

Buying a used PC? Check its specs first, and compare them with the games you want. Cheaper is not better if the machine cannot run them. Our post on [game age ratings](/post/video-games-updates/game-age-ratings-esrb-pegi-and-parental-controls/) can help you choose games that suit the player.

## What should I not do?

Some "fixes" make things worse or break the law.

- **Registry cleaners and "PC booster" programs.** Microsoft's list does not include them, and they can cause harm. Skip them.
- **Cracked Windows or cracked games.** They are illegal and a common way to catch malware.
- **Switching off antivirus.** It exposes you to the risk you were trying to avoid.
- **Overclocking.** It can damage hardware and is not for beginners.

## Common questions

### Will more RAM make games faster?

It can help if your PC runs out of memory, but we could not confirm a figure that suits every game. Check the game's requirements and ask a trusted repair shop before you buy.

### Is it safe to disable startup apps?

Yes for most apps you recognise, because disabling only stops them from opening at start-up. Leave security software and anything you do not know.

### Can I still play on Windows 10?

The PC still runs. Microsoft says it no longer gets security updates, so the risk of viruses is greater. Look at the upgrade options above.

### Do these steps work on Windows 10?

Most do. Startup apps, visual effects and Task Manager are the same. Menu names for updates, power and storage differ, and Microsoft gives the Windows 10 paths on its page.

## What to do next

1. Open Settings, System, About, and write down your processor and RAM.
2. Restart, disable startup apps and free disk space.
3. Set the power mode, plug in the laptop and lower the game's settings.
4. If it is still too slow, choose lighter games, or plan an upgrade.

An old PC can still bring you a lot of fun if you pick the right games, and there is no shame in knowing its limits.
