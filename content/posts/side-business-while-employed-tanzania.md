Many salaried people in Tanzania think about a second source of money, a biashara ukiwa umeajiriwa. It is a sensible wish, but a side business can put your job at risk if you skip the rules, or put your savings at risk if you rush. This guide shows the order that protects both: rules first, then time, then a small test.

## Key points

- Read your employment rules before you spend anything. Public servants have written rules on private work, and private employers usually put limits in the contract.
- Never run the side business in work hours or with your employer's phone, car, office or time.
- Choose a business that fits a few hours a week and does not depend on you being present all day.
- Keep a separate record and, if you can, a separate account for the business.
- Be careful with schemes that promise guaranteed returns. Salaried workers are common targets.
- Test for 30 days with a small budget before you decide to grow.

## Step 1: Check what your job allows

This is the step most people skip. It is also the one that can cost you your post.

### If you are a public servant

The Standing Orders for the Public Service, 2009 (third edition) set out rules on outside interests. As read on 24 November 2026, they say a public servant shall not:

- engage in any private occupation or undertaking during official hours;
- engage in any activity that would impair his usefulness as a public servant; or
- engage in any occupation or undertaking that might conflict with the interests of his organisation or be inconsistent with his position.

Another Standing Order says a public servant shall not render professional assistance to, or accept payment from, private persons or firms without the written permission of the employer. The text adds that such permission is not usually given unless it is to the public advantage.

The Public Service Regulations, 2022 (Government Notice No. 444 of 2022) add more. Their Code of Ethics says a public servant shall not run unofficial activities or projects during official hours, in the office or with public property, and shall tell superiors when a conflict of interest arises. The list of offences also includes engaging in an activity outside official duties that is likely to lead to taking improper advantage of one's position.

We are not lawyers and these rules are read here in summary. They may be amended, and your institution, such as a teachers' or local government service, may have its own code. So do this: ask your HR office, in writing, what is allowed for you and whether you need permission. Keep the reply.

### If you work in a private company or an NGO

Look at your contract and the staff handbook. Search for words such as "outside work", "conflict of interest", "competing business", "confidential information" and "exclusive service". Two common limits are working for a competitor and using company information or clients for your own gain. If the text is unclear, ask HR. A short email is cheaper than a dispute.

## Step 2: Count your real time and money

A job takes your best hours. Be honest about what remains.

- Write your week: work, travel, family, sleep. Find the hours that are really free.
- Many side businesses fail because the owner promised customers more time than they had.
- Decide your loss limit. Use only savings that your family can spare. Do not borrow for a first test.

If you earn a salary, you may be tempted to put a large part of it into stock in one month. Resist that. A small start teaches you more per shilling.

## Step 3: Pick a business that fits a few hours a week

We give no income figures, because results differ a lot. The question is fit.

- **Selling online or through WhatsApp.** You can answer messages in the evening. You still need time for delivery and for dealing with returns. See our guide to [low-cost online business ideas](/post/business-ideas/e-commerce/low-cost-online-business-ideas-tanzania/) and the post on [WhatsApp Business](/post/business-ideas/e-commerce/whatsapp-business-app-sell-products-tanzania/).
- **A service you already know.** If you have a skill, such as bookkeeping, tutoring, design or repairs, you can take a few jobs on weekends. Check that it does not compete with your employer.
- **A business someone else runs for you.** A small duka or poultry unit can run with a trusted helper, but then your biggest risk is trust and stock control. Visit often and count stock.
- **Freelancing.** It can fit evenings, but fees, payment and scams need care. Read our post on [freelancing online from Tanzania](/post/educational/freelancing-online-from-tanzania-platforms-fees-scams/).

Avoid a business that needs you at a fixed place during office hours. Also avoid any business that uses your employer's clients or information.

## Step 4: Keep the money, records and papers in order

- **Separate money.** Use a different mobile money line or bank account for the business. Pay yourself a set amount.
- **Records.** A notebook is enough. See our [cash book guide](/post/business-ideas/startups/simple-business-records-small-traders-cash-book-tanzania/).
- **Registration and tax.** TRA says to apply for a TIN within fifteen days of starting a business, and most businesses need a council licence. TRA's presumptive tax table lists as a condition that all of the person's income for the year comes from a business, so it may not fit someone with a salary. Ask TRA how your salary and your business income are treated together. Our posts on [registering a small business](/post/business-ideas/startups/register-small-business-tanzania-brela-tin-licence/) and [small business tax](/post/business-ideas/startups/small-business-tax-tanzania-presumptive-tax-explained/) explain the steps.

## Step 5: Protect your job and your name

- Do the side work only in your own time and on your own phone and equipment.
- Do not sell to your own employer, to colleagues' clients, or to anyone you deal with at work, unless the rules allow it.
- Do not advertise the business at the office in a way that causes problems.
- If a conflict could arise, tell your superior early.

## Scams that target people with a salary

A regular salary makes you a target for people who want your savings.

- **Promises of safe, high returns.** If someone offers a fixed or very high return with no risk, treat it as a warning. A joint public notice from the Bank of Tanzania and the Capital Markets and Securities Authority on one scheme said that promoting or joining any pyramid scheme is an offence under the Penal Code.
- **Recruit to earn.** If you make money mainly by bringing in other people, that is the pyramid pattern.
- **Fake loan apps and fake agents.** See our post on [fake loan apps in Tanzania](/post/technology-solutions/cyber-security/fake-loan-apps-tanzania-how-to-check/).
- **Pressure.** "Invest today or lose the chance" is a sales trick.

The Bank of Tanzania keeps a Financial Consumer Alert page, a list that helps the public tell licensed providers from those only presumed to be licensed. Check it, and the Bank's list of supervised institutions, before you hand over money.

## A simple 30-day test plan

1. **Week 1.** Get the written answer from HR or read your contract. Choose one idea. Write your loss limit.
2. **Week 2.** Make a small version: a few items, a few customers, one price list. Start the notebook.
3. **Week 3.** Sell in your free hours only. Note which hours work and which questions customers ask.
4. **Week 4.** Add up sales and costs. Count your own time too. Decide: continue the same, change one thing, or stop.

## Common questions

### Can I start a business while employed?
Often yes, but it depends on your employer and your rules. Public servants have written limits and may need permission. Ask in writing.

### When should I leave my job for the business?
There is no safe number we can give. A sensible sign is steady results over many months, a cash reserve, and a plan for the months when sales are low.

### Should I tell my employer?
If your rules ask for it, or if the business could touch your work, yes. If you are unsure, asking is safer than hiding.

### How much of my salary should I use?
Only what you can afford to lose. Do not borrow or sell important assets for a first test.

## What to do next

1. Ask HR in writing what is allowed, or read your contract this week.
2. Choose one small idea that fits your free hours and write your loss limit.
3. Run the 30-day test and keep a notebook.

A steady job is a strong base. Use it to take careful steps, not big risks.
