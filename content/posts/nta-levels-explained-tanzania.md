If you finished Form Four and your marks are not what you hoped for, you may be told that college is "second best". It is not. Technical colleges in Tanzania teach a ladder of qualifications, and each step has a number. Those numbers are the NTA levels.

This post explains what the numbers mean, who they are for, and how the ladder joins a bachelor's degree. We use the Ministry of Education's own framework, not blogs, because blogs often disagree about the names.

## The short answer

NTA stands for National Technical Award. The Tanzania Qualifications Framework, published by the Ministry of Education, Science and Technology, gives each award a level from 4 to 10.

- NTA Level 4 is the Basic Technician Certificate.
- NTA Level 5 is the Technician Certificate.
- NTA Level 6 is the Ordinary Diploma.
- NTA Level 7 is the Higher Diploma.
- NTA Level 8 is the Bachelor's Degree, then Level 9 is the Master's Degree and Level 10 is the Doctorate.

Each level normally leads to the next one. These names are as checked in November 2026 in the Ministry framework.

## What does NTA stand for and who sets the levels?

NTA means National Technical Award. It is the technical education line of the national framework, which has ten levels for all of education, from pre-primary school at the bottom to a doctorate at the top.

The framework is a Ministry of Education, Science and Technology document. The copy we read is 64 pages and was published in October 2024. It shows several lines side by side: school education, vocational awards, technical awards, university awards and professional awards. The idea is that a learner can move up, and sometimes across, without starting again.

The technical awards (NTA) are the line most people meet at colleges such as technical institutes and business colleges. The university line uses its own names for the same levels, which can confuse people. We keep to the NTA line here.

## NTA Levels 4, 5 and 6 in plain words

These three levels are where most school leavers begin. Here is what the framework says about each one.

| NTA level | Award | Who it is meant for (per the framework) | Minimum credits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 4 | Basic Technician Certificate | Form Four leavers, and some holders of a National Vocational Award III with a Certificate of Secondary Education | 120 |
| 5 | Technician Certificate | Holders of NTA 4 or an equivalent such as the Advanced Certificate of Secondary Education (Form Six) | 120 |
| 6 | Ordinary Diploma | Holders of NTA 5 or an equivalent | 120 |

A credit is a unit of learning. The framework sets the minimum you must earn at each level. Your college tells you how many years that takes, because it depends on the course and the college. We have not listed durations for that reason.

### What can you do with each one?

The framework describes the purpose of each level in broad terms. In our own words:

- **Level 4** prepares you to work in an area of a profession and to keep learning.
- **Level 5** gives you basic practical knowledge and skills, so you can handle problems in a specific field with little supervision.
- **Level 6** gives you broader facts and theory in one field of work or study. It is the Ordinary Diploma, which is the qualification the TCU diploma guidebook names when it talks about entry to a bachelor's degree.

You can start work after any of these levels. You can also continue. That is the point of a ladder.

## How do Levels 7 to 10 fit above them?

Above the Ordinary Diploma, the framework lists these awards.

- **Level 7, Higher Diploma.** The framework says you need 240 credits. It is meant for holders of an Ordinary Diploma or an equivalent, and also for people with an Advanced Certificate of Secondary Education.
- **Level 8, Bachelor's Degree.** The framework says 360 credits. It describes this level as meant for Higher Diploma holders.
- **Level 9, Master's Degree.** For people who already hold a bachelor's degree. A Postgraduate Certificate or Postgraduate Diploma can also sit at this level.
- **Level 10, Doctorate.** For people who already hold a master's degree.

We will not guess how long the higher levels take. Ask the institution for the exact programme length.

## How can a diploma lead to a degree?

On paper, the technical ladder goes 4, 5, 6, 7, 8. In real life, there is a second route that many people use. Tanzania's university regulator, the Tanzania Commission for Universities (TCU), runs an "equivalent entry" scheme. Under it, a holder of an Ordinary Diploma (NTA Level 6) can apply for a bachelor's degree, if the grades are high enough.

In the 2026/27 TCU guidebook for diploma holders, the general rule is a Grade Point Average (GPA) of at least 3.0 for an Ordinary Diploma, plus the O-Level passes the guidebook asks for. Different programmes can ask for more. We explain this step by step in our post on [diploma to degree entry requirements](/post/educational/career-development/diploma-to-degree-tanzania-entry-requirements/).

The framework and the guidebook describe two connected ideas. The framework describes the levels. The guidebook says who each degree programme accepts this year. If they seem to say different things, the guidebook for the current admission year and the institution's own page decide your application.

## Why does this matter for a school leaver?

Say you finished Form Four with passes, not top grades. A certificate course at NTA Level 4 can feel small. But on the framework's chart, it is the first step of a line that ends at a doctorate. Nothing on that chart marks a college route as a dead end.

That does not mean every step is easy or free. Fees, places and entry marks differ by college, and you need to work hard at each stage. We cannot promise a job or a place. We can say the path exists, it is official, and you can plan it one step at a time.

## How do I choose a level to start at?

Start from the qualification you hold today. If you have Form Four, the framework names NTA Level 4 as the level intended for you. If you have Form Six, it names NTA Level 5 as an equivalent starting point, and Level 7 for people who also hold the Advanced Certificate of Secondary Education.

Then think about where you want to be in five years. If you want a bachelor's degree, you can plan the climb backwards. Ask what the degree you want asks for, then pick a diploma that fits it. Our post on changing course, [wrong course or wrong university](/post/educational/career-development/change-university-course-transfer-tanzania/), covers what to do if you start in the wrong place.

## Common questions

### Is NTA Level 4 the same as Form Four?

No. Form Four is a school level. NTA Level 4 is a technical award at a college. The framework says NTA 4 is intended for Form Four leavers, so it is often the next step after Form Four.

### Do I have to do every level in order?

Often yes, because each level lists who it is meant for. But the framework also lists equivalents. For example, Form Six (the Advanced Certificate of Secondary Education) is named as an equivalent entry for Level 5 and Level 7. Ask the college which of your qualifications it accepts.

### Is a diploma enough to get a degree place?

It can be, under TCU's equivalent entry scheme, but you need to meet the minimum GPA and any programme-specific rules. Admission is decided by the institution you apply to, not by TCU.

### Where do I check that a college is real?

Use official lists. Our post on [how to check if a university or college is registered](/post/educational/study-tips/how-to-check-university-or-college-is-registered-tanzania/) shows how.

## What to do next

1. Write down your current qualification and find its place on the framework's chart (Figure 1 in the framework).
2. Pick the next step on that line and check which colleges teach it.
3. Ask each college how many years the course takes, what it costs and what it needs for entry.
4. If you want a degree later, read the TCU diploma guidebook early, so you choose a diploma that opens the door you want.

Every ladder is climbed one rung at a time. You are allowed to start on the first one.
