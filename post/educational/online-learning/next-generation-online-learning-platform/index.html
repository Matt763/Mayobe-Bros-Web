How to Choose and Use an Online Learning Platform

A practical guide to Coursera, edX, Khan Academy and Google's free skills programmes, what is free, what costs money, and how to finish what you start.

Online learning platforms let you study courses from universities and other organisations on a phone or laptop. Some are free, some charge for certificates, and many people start a course and never finish it. This guide helps you pick a platform, understand what you pay for, and build a routine that works with limited data and time.

The facts below come from each platform's own website, checked in November 2026.

The short answer

  • You can learn a lot for free. Khan Academy is free, and many edX courses can be audited for free.
  • You usually pay when you want a certificate or graded work.
  • Coursera and edX both have options for learners who cannot afford fees, but you must apply and the rules can change.
  • A certificate helps only if the skill behind it is real. Employers care more about what you can do.
  • Choose one course, set small weekly goals, and finish it before starting another.

What these platforms offer

An online learning platform is a website or app that hosts video lessons, reading, quizzes and sometimes projects. Here is what each of four well-known options says it offers.

Platform by platform

Khan Academy

Khan Academy describes itself as a non-profit whose mission is to give a free education to anyone, anywhere. Its site says it has over 840 free courses, covering maths, science, English, economics, computing, life skills and test preparation.

It fits best for school-level subjects, revision and filling gaps. If you struggled with Form Two algebra or want to practise for an exam, start here. It is less suited to getting a job-ready certificate.

Coursera

Coursera was launched in 2012 and partners with universities and companies. A course there usually has videos, readings and quizzes. Some are free to view, while certificates and graded assignments often need payment.

Coursera's help pages describe a financial aid process. You apply from the course page by answering questions about your education, goals and finances. In what we read, review can take about two weeks, and approved aid lasts a limited time (180 days in the help material), so check the current terms. Starting a free trial of a paid subscription while you apply can cancel the application.

edX

edX offers courses from universities and other institutions. Its support pages explain two tracks. In the audit track you can read materials for free for a limited time, but you get no certificate and cannot do graded work. In the verified track you pay, do graded work and can earn a certificate. One support result said the verified track starts around US$50 and depends on the course, so check the price on the course page.

Not every edX course offers an audit option, so look before you enrol.

Google's free skills programmes

Google's Grow with Google site lists Career Certificates in areas such as IT support, data analytics and UX design, plus introductory digital skills courses and AI training. The page we opened did not state prices for these, and it did not mention Digital Garage, an older Google learning brand. If you are looking for Digital Garage, search Grow with Google first and read what is currently offered.

How to choose a platform

Ask these five questions before you sign up.

  1. What do I want at the end? A school grade, a skill for a job, a certificate for your CV, or just knowledge. Your answer decides the platform.
  2. Is it really free? Read for words like "audit", "free trial", "certificate fee" and "subscription". A free trial that renews can charge you.
  3. Does it work with my data and device? Check whether you can download lessons or watch at low quality. Short lessons are easier on a small bundle.
  4. Who made the course? A university, a known company or a non-profit is easier to trust than an unknown seller.
  5. Will it be recognised? A certificate from an online course is not a degree. Do not assume an online certificate counts for university admission or a promotion. Ask the institution or employer, and for formal recognition of qualifications, ask TCU.

For help paying for a degree rather than short courses, see our guide to HESLB student loans.

Using online learning with real-life limits

Many learners in Tanzania deal with slow networks, expensive bundles and power cuts. Planning around them matters more than choosing the "best" platform.

Data and power

  • Download or watch lessons on Wi-Fi at a campus, library or cafe when you can.
  • Lower the video quality. Audio-heavy lessons need less data than high-definition video.
  • Charge your phone before study time. Keep a power bank if load shedding is common where you live.

Time

Say you have 30 minutes on weekday evenings. That is 2.5 hours a week. A course that says "10 hours" would take you about four weeks. Break big courses into small goals like this, and you will see progress.

Staying motivated

Studying alone is hard. Join a study group on WhatsApp, or find a classmate to follow the same course. Our post on helping university students learn without limits and the guide on study techniques that work have more ideas.

Common mistakes

  • Starting too many courses. Pick one and finish it.
  • Watching without practising. Pause the video and try the exercise yourself.
  • Paying before you try. Audit or preview first. Pay only if you need the certificate.
  • Trusting "guaranteed job" promises. No course can guarantee employment. Be careful with sellers who ask for money to "secure" a job.
  • Sharing private details. Use a strong password and do not share your login with others. Never send your bank or mobile money PIN to anyone.

Common questions

Are online certificates accepted by Tanzanian employers?

It depends on the employer. Some value skills shown in projects more than certificates. Ask the employer, or check with TCU if you need the qualification recognised for study.

Can I really learn for free?

Yes, to a point. Khan Academy is free by its own description, and edX has an audit track for most courses. You may need to pay for certificates and graded work.

How can I get a Coursera course if I cannot afford it?

Look for the financial aid link on the course page, and read the current rules. Apply well before you need the course, because review takes time.

Is AI making online learning better?

Some platforms add AI helpers for answers and practice. We have not verified what they can do, so we make no claims about them here. If you use AI tools to study, check their answers, and see our list of AI tools for cautions about privacy.

What to do next

  1. Decide your goal in one sentence, such as "learn spreadsheet basics for my shop records".
  2. Pick one course and read its page for cost, length and certificate rules.
  3. Block two or three study slots in your week and treat them like a class.
  4. After four weeks, check what you can now do that you could not before.
