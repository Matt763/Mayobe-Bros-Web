Freelancing Online from Tanzania: Platforms, Fees, Payments and Scams

How freelance platforms work, what they charge, how Tanzanians can receive payment, and how to spot scams, without any income promises.

Freelancing means you do work for clients as a self-employed person, not as an employee. Online, the client can be in another country, and you may be paid in US dollars. Platforms such as Upwork and Fiverr connect freelancers and clients and handle the payment.

This post explains how it works, what to expect from fees and payments, and how to avoid scams. We give no income figures. Many beginners earn little at first, and some earn nothing. What you earn depends on your skill, your portfolio, your clients and the time you put in.

The short answer

  • A platform is a middleman. It lists jobs or services, holds the client's payment, and takes a fee from you.
  • Fees, payment methods and rules change often. Check each platform's own help pages before you sign up.
  • You need a real skill, samples of your work, and good written English for most clients.
  • Use only the platform's own messages and payment system until you trust a client.
  • If anyone asks you to pay to get work, it is a scam.

How freelance platforms work

There are two main styles.

Bidding platforms. Clients post a job, and freelancers send proposals with a price. Upwork works mainly this way. You apply for jobs, the client chooses, and you agree on a price or hourly rate.

Service marketplaces. You create a listing describing what you will do for a set price, and clients buy it. Fiverr works mainly this way. Buyers find you through search, so your title, pictures and reviews matter.

On both, the usual steps are:

  1. Create a profile with your skills, experience and samples.
  2. Find work, through proposals or listings.
  3. Agree on the job, deadline and price in writing.
  4. Do the work and deliver it through the platform.
  5. The client approves, and the platform releases the payment, minus its fee.

New freelancers often struggle because they have no reviews. Many start with small jobs at lower prices to build a record, then raise prices later.

Skills that people sell

Common skills include writing, translation, graphic design, video editing, web development, customer support, bookkeeping help, social media management and data entry. Demand changes, and AI tools have changed some kinds of work. Pick a skill where you can show proof, such as samples, a short course certificate or a small portfolio website.

If you are unsure where to start, our post on low-cost online business ideas compares several options. You may also find our list of AI tools for productivity useful for saving time, but always check the quality of any AI-assisted work, and read each client's rules about its use.

Fees: what the platform takes

Platforms keep part of what you earn. On Upwork, the freelancer fee is set per contract and can differ between contracts. Percentages change, so read the current numbers before you decide. Search for "Upwork freelancer service fees" and "Fiverr fees" on their official help centres and read the current numbers.

Also look for:

  • Withdrawal fees. Moving money out of the platform can cost a fixed amount or a percentage.
  • Payment service fees. A service such as Payoneer may charge a fee and an exchange margin when you withdraw to a bank.
  • Currency conversion. If your bank converts dollars to shillings at a poor rate, you lose more.
  • Connects or bids. Some platforms charge to send proposals.

A simple example with made-up numbers: say a platform keeps 10% and a payment service charges a flat 2 dollars to withdraw. On a 100 dollar job, the platform takes 10, leaving 90. After the 2 dollar withdrawal fee, you get 88 dollars before currency conversion. Do this sum for every price you set, so you know what you really receive.

Getting paid in Tanzania

Payment methods vary by platform and country, and they change. Here is what we could confirm and what you must check.

Payoneer. Payoneer says it integrates with marketplaces including Fiverr and Upwork, and that you can withdraw funds to bank accounts in more than 190 countries by adding your local bank account. Its page does not list fee amounts, so check its current fee page for Tanzania. Third-party Tanzanian guides say bank withdrawals carry a fee and an exchange margin, but we could not verify those numbers.

Bank transfer (wire). Some platforms can send to your bank account directly. Ask your bank (for example CRDB, NMB or another) about incoming foreign payments, their fees, and what documents they need.

Other services. Wise, PayPal and local dollar account providers may or may not work for your situation. Availability in Tanzania can change, so check each one's own country page before you rely on it.

Mobile money. Some payment services and clients can pay out to M-Pesa, Tigo Pesa or Airtel Money, but availability depends on the service. Ask the platform or payer before assuming.

Whatever you use, keep your payment records. If you earn regularly, ask the Tanzania Revenue Authority (TRA) about registering and paying tax on your income.

Scams to avoid

The US Federal Trade Commission describes warning signs for job scams. They apply to freelancing too:

  • You must pay to get hired. Real clients do not charge you for jobs, training kits or "verification".
  • Huge pay for little work. If the pay sounds too good, it probably is.
  • Cheque or overpayment tricks. Someone sends you a cheque or payment, tells you to keep part and send the rest on, then the original payment fails and you owe the money.
  • Reshipping. You are asked to receive packages and forward them. This is never a real job.
  • Moving the chat off the platform. Scammers often ask you to continue on Telegram or WhatsApp so the platform cannot protect you.
  • Fake company details. You cannot find a real website, address or reviews.

Other practical protections:

  1. Search the client's name with the words "scam" or "complaint".
  2. Never share your passwords or the code from your phone.
  3. Do not buy platform accounts, or use someone else's identity. This breaks the rules and can lead to a ban.
  4. Do not do big unpaid "tests". Offer a small paid sample instead.
  5. Report suspicious jobs to the platform.

Practical problems

Slow internet and power cuts can cost you work. Keep a backup plan: a second data bundle, a power bank, and a place with steady power. Some clients are also slow to trust freelancers from Africa, so clear communication, quick replies, and good samples matter.

Common questions

Can a student freelance?

Many people do, though platforms usually require you to be at least 18 years old. Check each platform's terms. Balance the work with your studies, because deadlines are strict.

Do I need a laptop?

Many tasks need one. Some, like simple writing or social media work, can be done on a phone, but it is slower and limits what you can offer.

How long until my first job?

There is no reliable number. It can take weeks or months. Keep applying, improve your profile, and learn from rejected proposals.

Is it legal to be paid from abroad?

Receiving payment for work is normal, but you must follow banking and tax rules. Ask your bank and TRA for current requirements.

What to do next

  1. Choose one skill, and make three samples of your work.
  2. Read the official fee and payment help pages of one platform, and write the numbers down.
  3. Check which payout method works with your bank and your phone, and test it before you rely on it.
  4. Apply for small jobs, ask for honest feedback, and track your time and earnings.
