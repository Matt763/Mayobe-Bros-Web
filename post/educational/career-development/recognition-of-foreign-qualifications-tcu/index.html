Studied Abroad? How to Get Your Degree Recognised in Tanzania

Back home with a degree from abroad? Learn what TCU recognition is, how to apply online through FAAS, and what else some professions need.

You worked hard for your degree, maybe far from home and far from family. Now you are back, and an employer or a university asks one more question: has Tanzania recognised it? This post explains the steps in plain words. It is general information, not legal advice.

The short answer

Anyone who studied at university level abroad and now wants to work or study in Tanzania needs the Tanzania Commission for Universities (TCU) to recognise the qualification. You apply online. TCU checks the award, and you wait for its decision.

  • Recognition is requested through the Foreign Award Assessment System (FAAS), an online system run by TCU.
  • Recognition covers the academic qualification. Some professions also need a licence from their own authority.
  • If you disagree with the decision, you may appeal.
  • The TCU service page does not list fees, waiting times or documents. Do not trust anyone who gives you exact figures without showing an official source.

As checked in November 2026, this is what the TCU service page says. We write below where it says nothing.

Who needs recognition?

The TCU page says that a person who got a university education outside Tanzania and wants to work or study in the country must have the qualification recognised by TCU.

Say you finished a bachelor's degree in another country and a Dar es Salaam company offers you a job. The company may ask for proof that TCU recognises your degree. Say you want a master's place at a Tanzanian university. The same step can apply.

There is one small exception on the page. A foreign student who applies to study in Tanzania does not need to apply separately. The receiving university can send the application to TCU during admission.

What recognition does not cover

TCU deals with university education. A certificate from a foreign secondary school is a different matter, and we did not find an answer for it on the TCU pages. The National Examinations Council of Tanzania (NECTA) and the National Council for Technical and Vocational Education and Training (NACTVET) work in those levels. Ask them directly if your case is a secondary or college certificate.

How do I apply? The online route

The TCU page says you apply through FAAS. The TCU home page links to it under its e-services, as "Recognition of Foreign Qualifications".

  1. Open the TCU website (tcu.go.tz) and follow the link to the Foreign Award Assessment System.
  2. Read TCU's own procedures document before you start. The home page links a document called "Procedures for admission of applicants holding foreign qualifications into academic programmes in universities in Tanzania".
  3. Fill in the online application with the details the system asks for.
  4. Follow your application through the system and answer any request from TCU.

Here is the honest part. The service page does not list individual steps, a document checklist, a fee or a time limit. We could not confirm any of these, so we leave them out. Your degree papers, transcripts and identity details will probably matter, but the system and the procedures document tell you exactly what to upload. If something is unclear, write to TCU at es@tcu.go.tz.

How TCU decides

The page says TCU assesses a foreign award against academic standards and principles drawn from conventions, regional agreements and national policy. It does not give a list of countries or universities. Do not assume a degree is "automatically fine" because the university is famous. Apply and wait for the result.

Some professions need a second approval

This is the step many returning graduates miss. TCU recognition is about the academic qualification. The TCU page says some professions need a separate licence or professional registration before you can practise, and it links a list of the professional authorities.

Think of two different questions:

  • Is my degree recognised as a university qualification in Tanzania? TCU answers this.
  • May I practise this profession here? The professional authority answers this.

Say you studied a profession that is regulated, such as one in health or engineering. You may need both. Check with the authority for your field early, because it may have its own rules, exams or fees. Ask it what it needs, and whether it wants proof of TCU recognition first.

If you disagree with the decision

The TCU page says an applicant who is not happy with a decision may appeal. It points to a document called Appeal Procedures and says the appeal must come within a set timeframe. The page does not state how long that is.

So read the decision letter carefully the day it arrives. Look for the appeal route and any deadline. Keep every message and every date. If you cannot find the time limit, ask TCU in writing straight away rather than waiting.

Where to find TCU

The service page gives these contacts, as checked in November 2026:

  • Headquarters in Dodoma: 2 TCU Street, Nzuguni, P.O. Box 2600
  • Dar es Salaam office: Ministry of Education, Science and Technology building, 7 Magogoni Street, P.O. Box 6562
  • Email: es@tcu.go.tz
  • Phone: +255 (0)26 2161560 or +255 (0)26 2161561

Check the website for current details before you visit or call. Offices and numbers can change.

Common questions

Does an online degree from abroad count?

TCU's page does not say. Online study is a separate question with its own risks. Our post on whether an online degree is recognised in Tanzania explains how to think about it. Ask TCU about your case.

How long will recognition take and what does it cost?

We could not confirm either. The TCU service page lists no fee and no time. Check FAAS and the procedures document, or email TCU, and be careful with anyone who asks for money outside the official system.

Is my university registered where I studied?

Recognition starts with a real, accredited institution. For Tanzanian institutions you can learn how to check whether a university or college is registered. For a foreign university, the awarding country's own education authority is the place to look.

Do I need TCU if I only want a job in another country?

The TCU page speaks about working or studying in Tanzania. If you plan to work elsewhere, ask that country's authority what it requires.

What to do next

  1. Collect your degree certificate, transcript and passport or national ID in a safe folder, with clear scans.
  2. Open the TCU site, find FAAS and the procedures document, and read both before you apply.
  3. If you work in a regulated profession, contact its professional authority now and ask what it needs.
  4. Keep a record of every date and message. If the answer is not what you hoped, remember that an appeal route exists.

You earned that degree. A careful, patient application is a fair way to make it count at home too.
