How to Read TCU Entry Requirements for a Degree Course

Turn your Form Six grades into points, compare them with a programme line in the TCU guidebook, and see which health courses ask for more than 4.0.

Waiting for the next step after Form Six can feel heavy. A good way to ease the worry is to check, with your own numbers, whether you meet the minimum for the course you want. This post shows you how.

The figures are as checked in November 2026, in the TCU guidebook for 2026/27. Read the edition for your own year before you apply.

The short answer

The guidebook for Form Six leavers sets a general minimum: two principal passes with a total of at least 4.0 points. Points come from your grades. Each programme then lists its own subjects, and some health programmes ask for much more.

  • A = 5, B = 4, C = 3, D = 2, E = 1, S = 0.5 for A-Level studies from 2016 onwards.
  • General minimum: two principal passes (grade E and above) with at least 4.0 points in the two subjects the programme names.
  • Medicine and similar health courses: three principal passes in the named subjects with at least 6 points, and minimum grades in each.
  • Meeting the minimum is not a promise of a place.

Where do I find the current guidebook?

The Tanzania Commission for Universities (TCU) publishes it on its website under undergraduate admission guidebooks. There are separate books for Form Six leavers and for diploma holders. Take the one for secondary school qualifications if you finished Form Six.

TCU says you apply directly to each institution's online system. Questions about your application go to that institution. Our post on how to read the TCU admission guidebook shows the layout of the whole book, and this post focuses on the numbers.

How do ACSEE grades become points?

For people who completed A-Level from 2016 onwards, the guidebook gives this scale.

GradeABCDES
Points543210.5

The guidebook has different scales for earlier groups. People who finished A-Level before 2014 use a similar one, and those who finished in 2014 and 2015 use another scale. If that is you, read your own row in the guidebook. We do not use those scales here.

For a deeper look at your result slip, see how to read your Form Six results for university entry.

What is the 4.0-point general minimum?

The general table says you need two principal passes (grade E and above), with a total of 4.0 points in the two subjects that define admission to the programme you want.

The guidebook also says that applicants without at least two principal passes totalling 4.0 points are advised not to apply, because they do not meet the minimum.

Here are three made-up examples, only to show the sums.

  • A C and a D: 3 + 2 = 5 points. That is above 4.0.
  • A D and a D: 2 + 2 = 4 points. That is exactly 4.0.
  • A D and an E: 2 + 1 = 3 points. That is below 4.0.

Notice that the points count for the two subjects the programme names, not just any two. A student with an A in a subject the programme does not list cannot use it as one of the two.

What are the special rules for medicine, pharmacy and nursing?

Health programmes have their own table, and it asks for three principal passes. Here are some lines from the 2026/27 guidebook.

ProgrammeSubjects and pointsMinimum grades
Doctor of Medicine, Doctor of Dental Surgery, Bachelor of PharmacyPhysics, Chemistry and Biology, at least 6 pointsAt least D in each of the three
Bachelor of Medical Laboratory SciencesPhysics, Chemistry and Biology, at least 6 pointsAt least C in Chemistry, D in Biology and E in Physics
Bachelor of Science in NursingChemistry, Biology and one of Physics, Advanced Mathematics or Nutrition, at least 6 pointsAt least C in Chemistry, D in Biology and E in the third subject

These are the general lines. Other health programmes are listed in the same table, so look up yours by name. Also check the institution's own page, because the institution runs the admission.

A worked example with invented grades

Say a student has Physics C, Chemistry B and Biology C. That is 3 + 4 + 3 = 10 points, with three principal passes.

  • Medicine: needs at least 6 points and at least D in each. The sums fit. Each grade is above D.
  • Nursing: needs at least 6 points, C in Chemistry, D in Biology and E in a third subject. The Chemistry grade is B, Biology is C and Physics is C. The sums fit.

Now say another student has Chemistry D, Biology D and Physics E. That is 2 + 2 + 1 = 5 points. That is below 6, so these health programmes would not accept it, even though each grade is a pass.

These examples only show the arithmetic. They are not predictions. Nobody can tell you what points will win a place in a given year, and you should not trust anyone who claims to know a "sure cut-off".

How do I read one programme line?

When you find a programme in the institutional tables, read across the row in this order.

  1. The programme name and code, so you are sure it is the right course.
  2. The admission requirements, which list the subjects where you need principal passes.
  3. The minimum points, which you compare with your own sum.
  4. The capacity, which is the number of places.
  5. The duration, which is how many years the course takes.

Then look for the word "or". Some programmes accept more than one route, for example a foundation programme or a diploma. Write down the route that fits you. If the row is unclear, ask the institution, because it makes the admission decision.

Common questions

Do the names on my application have to match my exam records?

Yes. The guidebook says your names and index numbers in the application must match those used in your examinations. A mismatch can cause problems, so check before you submit.

Is 4.0 points enough to be admitted?

It is the general minimum to be considered. The programme's own line can ask for more, and the number of places is limited.

What if I finished A-Level before 2016?

The guidebook lists a scale for your group. Read that row and use it, because the grade values differ for some groups.

What if I was admitted to more than one institution?

The guidebook says you must confirm the programme and institution you prefer most.

What to do next

  1. Write your grades and convert them to points with the table above.
  2. Open the current guidebook for secondary school qualifications and find three programmes you like.
  3. Compare each row with your points and your subjects.
  4. If you are close to the minimum, ask the institution before you apply.

You do not have to understand the whole guidebook in one day. One programme line at a time is enough.
