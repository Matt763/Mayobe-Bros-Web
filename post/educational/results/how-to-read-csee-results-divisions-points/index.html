How to Read Your Form Four Results: Divisions and Points

Learn what CNO, AGGT, DIV and the subject grades mean on a NECTA Form Four result page, and why a lower aggregate is better.

Waiting for results is hard. When the page finally opens, you may see a row of letters, numbers and short codes that nobody ever explained. This post helps you read that row calmly, one column at a time, so you know what you are looking at before you decide what to do next.

The short answer

Each candidate gets one row on the NECTA school result page. The row shows your candidate number, sex, an aggregate number, a division, and a grade for each subject. On the 2025 pages we opened, a smaller aggregate meant a better division.

  • CNO is your candidate number (it starts with your school code).
  • AGGT is the aggregate, a total made from your subject results.
  • DIV is the division: I, II, III, IV or 0.
  • DETAILED SUBJECTS lists each subject with its grade.
  • Codes such as ABS, X and I are not grades. They mean something is missing.

NECTA does not print the grading rules on the result page itself. We say below what we could confirm and what we could not.

What each column means

The 2025 Certificate of Secondary Education Examination (CSEE) results are published school by school on NECTA's online results site. Each school page has a small summary table at the top and a long list of candidates below it. As checked in November 2026, the list has these columns.

ColumnWhat it shows
CNOYour candidate number, for example a school code and a number
SEXF for female, M for male
AGGTThe aggregate number, or a dash if none could be given
DIVYour division, or a code such as ABS
DETAILED SUBJECTSEach subject followed by its grade

The summary table at the top counts how many girls, boys and the total fell into each division at that school. It is about the school, not about you.

Find your own row by your candidate number. Read across slowly. Check that the number and the sex match what you registered. If something is wrong with your details, that is worth knowing early.

Grades and points per subject

Each subject on your row has a letter grade. Grades are letters. A is the top grade and F is a fail.

Here we need to be honest. The result page has no key that explains the letters, and we did not find an official NECTA page in plain text that lists the marks for each grade or the points each grade is worth. Some websites publish tables of marks and points. Many of them mix old rules with new ones, and the rules were different in earlier years. We do not repeat any of those tables here.

What you can do is use the grades on your row and the aggregate shown. NECTA has already added up your result into the AGGT number. You do not need to work it out yourself to know your division.

If you want the exact rule, ask NECTA. The CSEE page gives the contact email, esnecta@necta.go.tz, and the phone numbers 0738743381 and 0262610690.

How many subjects are counted?

Every CSEE candidate sits seven compulsory subjects: Civics, History, Geography, Kiswahili, English Language, Biology and Basic Mathematics. A candidate can add up to two more subjects from groups such as Physics, Chemistry, Commerce, Book Keeping and technical subjects. One optional subject can be added from a list that includes Bible Knowledge, Elimu ya Dini ya Kiislamu, Fine Art, Information and Computer Studies and Additional Mathematics. No one may register for more than ten subjects in one sitting.

So two candidates in the same school may show different numbers of subjects on their rows. That is normal.

Division I to IV and Division 0

The DIV column shows a Roman numeral from I to IV, or the number 0. Division I is the highest and Division 0 is the lowest. Division 0 is the last group, after IV.

NECTA's result pages do not define these divisions in words. What we can report is what the published 2025 pages show. In a sample of school pages that we read, the aggregates for each division fell in these ranges:

DivisionAggregate seen
I7 to 17
II18 to 21
III22 to 25
IV26 to 33
034 to 35

These ranges are what we saw in NECTA's 2025 published results. They are not an official rule, they come from a sample of pages and not every school, and they could change in another year. Treat them as a rough guide to the pattern. A lower aggregate gives a better division.

Notice what this means in practice. The difference between two divisions can be only a few points in the total. A single subject moving by one grade can change a division. That is a gentle reminder that a division is not a measure of your worth or your future. It is a summary number from one exam week.

Why some candidates show no points

Not every row has an AGGT number. Some rows show a dash instead, and the DIV column may show a code such as ABS. This usually means the candidate missed the exam, or the result was held back for a reason.

On the pages we opened, ABS appeared in the DIV column, and X appeared beside subjects. NECTA's FAQ page explains these codes. ABS means the candidate missed the exam. X means the candidate did not appear for a registered subject. I means incomplete, because continuous assessment scores are missing. E means withheld until proof of payment of the exam fee. W means the result was withheld, nullified or cancelled because of proven dishonesty.

We explain these in more detail in our post on NECTA result codes. If your row has a code instead of a number, read that post first.

Common questions

Does a lower aggregate mean a better result?

Yes. On the pages we read, Division I had the smallest aggregate numbers and Division 0 the largest. So lower is better.

Where do I find my result?

NECTA publishes results for each school on its online results site. Open the CSEE results for the year, choose your school by its code, and look for your candidate number. Do not pay anyone to "check" or "change" your result. No one can legally change it for you.

Can I work out my division myself?

You do not need to. NECTA gives the aggregate and the division on your row. Because the official grading text is not on the result page, a home calculation from a website table may be wrong.

What if I think my result is wrong?

NECTA lists an Appeals service for candidates who disagree with their results. The page describes it as a formal and fair follow-up when there is disagreement. Ask NECTA how to use it and what it costs, as we could not confirm the steps.

What does Division 0 mean for my future?

It is the lowest group on the page. It does not close every door. Our post on what to do after poor Form Four results lays out real options.

What to do next

  1. Find your row by candidate number and read it from left to right. Write down your aggregate, your division and each grade.
  2. If you see a code instead of a number, look it up on NECTA's FAQ page before you worry.
  3. If you are planning for Form Five, college or training, keep a clear copy of your result and read the entry rules of the place you want, as each one sets its own.
  4. If the result is not what you hoped for, give yourself a day, then read the options. One exam week is not the end of your story.

Whatever your row says, you have done a hard thing by finishing four years of school, and that counts.
