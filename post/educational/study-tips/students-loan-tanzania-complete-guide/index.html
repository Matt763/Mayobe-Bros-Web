HESLB Student Loans in Tanzania: How to Apply and Repay

How the HESLB student loan works in Tanzania: who it is for, how to apply through OLAMS, what to prepare, and what to expect when you start repaying.

The Higher Education Students' Loans Board (HESLB) gives loans and some grants to Tanzanian students who need help paying for higher education. This guide explains the basic steps: where to apply, what to prepare, and how repayment works. It is written for Form Six leavers, diploma students and parents who are trying to understand the process for the first time.

One warning first. HESLB changes its rules, dates and forms from year to year. This guide was checked against the official website in November 2026, but the final authority is always the current guideline on heslb.go.tz.

The short answer

  • HESLB is the government body that lends money for higher education to students who qualify. A loan is not free money. You repay it after you finish studying and start earning.
  • Applications are made online through HESLB's OLAMS system (Online Loan Application and Management System). Do not pay anyone to apply for you.
  • Each academic year has its own application window and its own guideline document. As checked in November 2026, applications for the 2026/2027 year were closed.
  • Loan size depends on your need and on the programme you are admitted to. Not every applicant gets the full amount they ask for.
  • Repayment is usually taken from your salary once you are employed. Keep your contact and employer details up to date with HESLB.

What HESLB does

HESLB is a Tanzanian government board. Its main office is in Dodoma (PSSSF House, Makole Road, as listed on its contact details in November 2026). It lends to students who are admitted to eligible programmes and who can show that they need financial help.

The idea is simple. Money that graduates pay back goes into a fund that can lend to the next group of students. That is why HESLB follows up on repayments, and why it works with employers.

For current figures on how many students HESLB has helped, check HESLB's own reports on its website.

Who can apply

The exact rules are in the guideline for your level of study. HESLB publishes separate guidelines for diploma, bachelor's degree, postgraduate diploma in legal practice and several named scholarships. As checked in November 2026, the bachelor's and diploma guidelines were both posted in June 2026.

In general, you should expect these basics to matter:

  • You are a Tanzanian applicant.
  • You have been admitted, or are applying for admission, to a programme at a recognised institution. Admission in Tanzania is handled by bodies such as the Tanzania Commission for Universities (TCU) for degrees. Check the guideline for the list of what is covered.
  • You can show financial need. HESLB looks at your family situation and the information you give in your application.
  • You give true information and the right documents.

The full list of conditions is in the guideline for your programme, so download the PDF that matches it and read the eligibility part before you do anything else. The files are large (the bachelor's guideline is about 41 MB), so use Wi-Fi or a good data bundle.

How to apply

The official application route is OLAMS. The steps below are the general pattern. Details such as the dates, the documents and the form fields are set each year in the guideline.

  1. Read the current guideline. Go to the HESLB application guideline page and download the guideline for your level (diploma, bachelor's and so on).
  2. Check the application window. HESLB shows an open or closed notice on its home page. Dates differ each year. Do not wait for the last week.
  3. Prepare your documents. Typical needs include your national ID or birth certificate details, your NECTA results, and your admission information. Your guardian's details may also be needed. The guideline has the exact list. Scan or photograph each document clearly.
  4. Open OLAMS. Use the link on the HESLB website. Create your account with an email address and phone number you can really access and will keep.
  5. Fill in the form carefully. Names must match your documents. A small spelling difference can cause delays.
  6. Submit, then save proof. Keep a screenshot or printout of the confirmation, and write down your application number.
  7. Watch for the results. HESLB publishes allocation results online. You can also use the contact details on its site: phone +255 736 665 533 or info@heslb.go.tz, Monday to Friday, 8am to 4pm (as listed in November 2026).

Safety tips

  • Only use the official site, heslb.go.tz, and the OLAMS links shown there.
  • HESLB does not need a "broker". Be careful with anyone at a cyber cafe or on social media who promises a bigger loan for a fee.
  • Never share your OLAMS password, even with a friend or relative.

What the loan is meant to cover

HESLB loans are meant to help with the costs of study, such as tuition and living costs. The exact parts and amounts depend on the guideline for the year and on your programme. Amounts change each year, so we do not list any here. The guideline for your year and programme has them.

A practical tip: when your loan result comes out, compare it with your university fee structure. If there is a gap, plan how you will cover it before the semester starts. Ask the university's student finance office what is paid directly to the university and what comes to you.

Repaying the loan

You repay after you finish your studies and begin earning. Many graduates repay through their employer, who deducts money from salary and sends it to HESLB. HESLB has an employer repayment portal for this, and a page for beneficiaries to log in.

The size of the deduction has changed over the years. The Citizen newspaper reported in 2017 that an amendment to the HESLB Act required employers to deduct 15 percent of a beneficiary's gross salary, up from 8 percent, from February 2017. Reports differ on whether the rate applies to gross or basic pay, and rules have been reviewed since then. HESLB's repayment pages and your payslip show the rate that applies to you today, so confirm it there before you plan around it.

If you are self-employed or between jobs

Do not wait for HESLB to find you. Log in to your beneficiary account, update your details and ask what payment options are open to you. HESLB's repayment section lists obligations and FAQs. Missing repayments can bring extra charges and legal steps, so tell HESLB early if your situation changes.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Starting too late and missing the window.
  • Using an email or phone number you later lose.
  • Uploading blurry or wrong documents.
  • Believing someone who says they can "guarantee" a loan.
  • Forgetting about the loan after graduation. Repayment starts whether or not you remember it.

Common questions

Is a HESLB loan the same as a scholarship?

No. A loan must be repaid. HESLB also manages some scholarships and grants, such as the Samia Scholarship and the Mwl. Julius K. Nyerere Memorial Masters' Degree Scholarship, which have their own guidelines.

When can I apply?

The window changes every year. As checked in November 2026, applications for 2026/2027 were closed. Watch the home page of heslb.go.tz and your school or university notices for the next opening.

Do private university students qualify?

Possibly, but rules differ by programme and level. Read the guideline for your level and check with HESLB.

Can a parent apply for me?

Parents can help gather documents, but the application should be in the student's name with the student's details. Follow the guideline.

Who do I contact if I have a problem?

Use the contact page on heslb.go.tz. Keep your application number ready.

What to do next

  1. Open the HESLB home page and see whether the application window is open.
  2. Download the guideline for your level and read the eligibility and documents sections.
  3. Gather and scan your documents now, so you are ready when the window opens.
  4. If you are a graduate, log in to your beneficiary account and confirm your employer and contact details.
