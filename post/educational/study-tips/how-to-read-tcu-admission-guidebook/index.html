How to Read the TCU Admission Guidebook Before You Apply

A plain walk through the TCU undergraduate guidebook: the two pathways, entry points, how to read a programme table, and how to build a shortlist of five.

You finished Form Six, or you hold a diploma, and now you want a place at university. The Tanzania Commission for Universities (TCU) publishes a guidebook that lists the degree programmes you can apply for and what each one asks. It is long and full of tables, so many people skip it. Here is how to use it without feeling lost.

The short answer

Open the TCU guidebook for your pathway, check the general entry rule, then go to the table of the institution you like and read each programme row. Pick programmes whose subjects and points you meet, and apply directly to the institution online.

  • There are two guidebooks, one for secondary school qualifications and one for diploma holders.
  • Applications are made directly to the universities, not to TCU.
  • Your name and index number in the application must match your exam records.
  • A programme row shows its code, subject requirements, minimum points, number of places and years of study.
  • Aim for a shortlist of about five programmes that you truly qualify for.

All details here are as checked in November 2026. Dates, rules and programme lists change every year, so always confirm on the TCU website before you apply.

What is the guidebook and who is it for?

The guidebook is TCU's yearly document for applicants, parents, sponsors and universities. According to the 2026/27 edition, it gives information on institutions that admit students in that cycle, the minimum entry requirements, the places each programme can take and how long each programme lasts.

It is for you if you are a Form Six leaver, a holder of an ordinary diploma or an equivalent qualification, or someone planning to apply in a coming year and wanting to understand how things work.

Where do I find the right guidebook?

TCU keeps the guidebooks on its website, under the undergraduate admission guidebooks page. As checked in November 2026, the page listed guidebooks from 2016/2017 up to 2026/2027, and 2026/2027 was the latest edition. Each year has two documents:

  1. Holders of Secondary School Qualifications (older editions call this Form Six applicants).
  2. Ordinary Diploma or equivalent applicants.

Choose the one that matches your own qualification, and choose the edition for the year you want to join. An old guidebook can show programmes that no longer exist or rules that have changed.

How does the application work?

The TCU page on undergraduate admissions says that you apply directly to the institutions you choose. Each institution announces its own list of admitted applicants and handles questions about your application. The 2026/27 guidebook says the same, and adds some points worth remembering.

  • Applications are made online through the websites of the institutions.
  • Check that you have enough information on the programmes you choose, including how many places they have.
  • Make sure the names and index numbers in your application match what you used in your secondary school or college examinations.
  • If you are admitted by more than one institution, you must confirm your most preferred programme and institution.
  • Send questions about your application or admission to the institution you applied to.

Be careful with anyone who offers to get you a place for money. TCU has warned people about fake admission agents, and applications and fee payments go through official university portals. We cover more about this in our post on what to do if you were not selected yet.

How do entry points and principal passes work?

This is the part that worries most people, so go slowly.

In the 2026/27 guidebook for secondary qualifications, the general rule is that an applicant needs at least two principal passes in the Advanced Certificate of Secondary Education Examination, with a total of at least 4.0 points. The guidebook advises applicants who do not meet this not to apply, because they do not meet the minimum requirement.

For A-Level studies from 2016 onwards, the points for each principal grade are:

GradeABCDES
Points543210.5

Here are two small examples with made-up results, only to show the arithmetic.

  • Say you have a C and a D in the two subjects that a programme asks for. That is 3 + 2 = 5 points. This is above 4.0.
  • Say you have a D and an E. That is 2 + 1 = 3 points, below 4.0.

That is only the general minimum. The tables for each programme show their own "minimum institutional admission points", and those can be different. Some health programmes ask for more. The guidebook says, for example, that medicine needs three principal passes in Physics, Chemistry and Biology with at least 6 points. The guidebook's health table lists several other programmes, so look up yours by name.

Nobody can tell you in advance what points will be enough in a year. The number of places and the number of applicants both change. Do not trust anyone who gives you a "sure cut-off". Use the guidebook for the minimum, and the institution's website for its updates.

How do I read a programme table?

After the general sections, the guidebook has a long part where each institution has its own tables. The section is called Institutional Programme Requirements. Each row is one programme and the columns are the same every time:

ColumnWhat it tells you
Programme codeA short code you may need when you apply
Admission requirementsThe subjects in which you need principal passes
Minimum institutional admission pointsThe least points the programme accepts
Admission capacityHow many places the programme has
Programme durationHow many years it takes

Below is an invented row to show how to read one. It is not a real programme.

ProgrammeCodeRequirementsPointsCapacityYears
Bachelor of Example StudiesEX001Two principal passes in Geography, Economics or Commerce4.01203

You would read it like this. "This three-year programme has 120 places. I need two principal passes from the three listed subjects, and at least 4.0 points."

Some real programmes list many subjects, and some say "OR" for other routes, such as a foundation programme. Read the whole row, not only the first line. Capacity matters too. A programme with 400 places and one with 30 are different when many people apply.

Tip: the guidebook is a large PDF and some pages are hard to read on a phone, because they are image-heavy. Use the search tool in your PDF reader and type the name of the institution or programme.

How do I make a shortlist of five?

A shortlist keeps you from applying in a rush. Try this method.

  1. Write down your principal subjects and grades from your results slip.
  2. Think about what you want to study, and write down three to five areas you would enjoy. If you are unsure, our post on jobs and degrees you can study with the PCB combination shows how one combination opens many doors.
  3. In the guidebook, search for programmes in those areas and note the code, subjects, points, capacity and years.
  4. Remove any programme where you do not meet the subject rule or the points.
  5. Look at cost and place. Think about fees, rent, and how you would travel. For help with fees, read about HESLB student loans.
  6. Check that the institution is registered, using our guide to checking a university or college.
  7. Keep five programmes. Put them in order from the one you want most to the one you would still be happy with.

Mix your list. Have some programmes you really want, and some where you are safely above the minimum. Ask a teacher or a career counsellor to look at your list. Then read each institution's own page for its application steps.

Common questions

Do I apply through TCU?

No. As checked in November 2026, TCU's page says you apply directly to the institutions. TCU publishes the guidebook and coordinates the system, and institutions announce their own admitted lists.

Is the quality assurance fee a form of admission payment?

No. The guidebook explains that TCU set a quality assurance fee of TSh 20,000 that every student enrolled at a university pays, whatever the programme. It is not a fee to get a place. Pay only through official institution channels.

I have a diploma. Which book do I read?

Use the guidebook for Ordinary Diploma or equivalent applicants. It is a separate document on the same TCU page. Its rules and tables can differ from the Form Six book, so read the one that fits you.

What if I do not meet the points for my dream programme?

Look at related programmes in the guidebook where you do qualify, and think about routes such as a diploma first. Do not give up on the field you love. There are often several ways in.

What to do next

  1. Download the correct 2026/2027 guidebook, or the newest one, from the TCU website and save it on your phone.
  2. Read sections 1.1 to 1.3 first, so you understand the rules and the points.
  3. Build your shortlist of five using the steps above.
  4. Visit each institution's website for its application steps and dates.

Taking time to read the guidebook is a real step toward your future. You are choosing carefully, and that is a good way to begin.
