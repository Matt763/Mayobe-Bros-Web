CBN Combination: Nutrition, Nursing and Food Science Degrees

CBN (Chemistry, Biology, Food and Human Nutrition) can lead to nursing, dietetics, public health and food science in Tanzania. See TCU rules and limits.

CBN means Chemistry, Biology and Food and Human Nutrition as your principal subjects in Form Five and Form Six. NECTA lists it among the natural science combinations of the ACSEE. It points to nursing, clinical nutrition and dietetics, public health, environmental health and food science. It does not lead to medicine, dentistry or pharmacy.

This guide is for Form Four leavers choosing a combination, Form Six students waiting for results, and parents. We read the entry rules in the TCU admission guidebook for 2026/27 in November 2026. Rules change every year, so treat this as a map and confirm before you apply.

Key points

  • NECTA's ACSEE page lists CBN as Chemistry, Biology and Food and Human Nutrition. The N is for that subject.
  • The TCU nursing rule lists Chemistry, Biology and one of Physics, Advanced Mathematics or Nutrition, with at least 6 points. CBN fits it. CBG, which has Geography instead, does not.
  • Clinical Nutrition and Dietetics, Environmental Health Sciences and Public Health also accept Nutrition as the third subject.
  • Food science, human nutrition and food processing degrees ask for Chemistry plus a related subject, often with only 4.0 points.
  • Medicine, dentistry, pharmacy and medical laboratory sciences ask for Physics. CBN cannot enter them.
  • No place or job is guaranteed. We could not confirm official salary figures, so we give none.

What the subjects give you

Chemistry and Biology are the core of almost every health and food degree. Food and Human Nutrition adds what you eat, how the body uses it, and how food is grown, kept and cooked.

The guidebook writes the subject in two ways. Some entries say "Nutrition" and some say "Food and Human Nutrition". NECTA's combination page uses the longer name. If you have this subject, ask the university whether both names are treated as the same for your chosen degree.

If you have read our guide to CBG, the difference is clear. CBG leans toward land, forests and environment. CBN leans toward food, bodies and community health.

How points work

TCU, the Tanzania Commission for Universities, publishes a guidebook each year. It lists each degree, the subjects needed, the minimum points, the places and the years of study.

For results from 2016 onward the scale is A = 5, B = 4, C = 3, D = 2, E = 1 and S = 0.5. Many degrees ask for two principal passes with at least 4.0 points in total. Health degrees ask for three passes and more points.

Here is a hypothetical example. Say you get C in Chemistry, D in Biology and E in Nutrition. That is 3 + 2 + 1 = 6 points, which matches the rule for nursing, Clinical Nutrition and Dietetics, and Environmental Health Sciences. Another student with D, D, D also has 6 points but would miss the rule that asks for a C in Chemistry. Grades per subject matter as much as the total.

The harmonised health rules also set minimum grades. For nursing the guidebook asks for at least a C in Chemistry, a D in Biology and an E in Physics, Advanced Mathematics or Nutrition. Public Health at Zanzibar University asks for D in Chemistry and Biology and E in the third subject.

Some universities are stricter than the common rule. One example is MUHAS: its nursing entry asks for 8 points in Physics, Chemistry and Biology, so CBN does not fit there. This is why you must read each university's own line.

What kind of work follows

A degree is not a job. These are the kinds of work the programmes above prepare you for.

  • Nursing. Care in hospitals, clinics and communities. After a nursing degree, you need to register with the professional council before you practise, so ask your university what applies.
  • Nutrition and dietetics. Helping patients and communities eat in ways that fit their health, in hospitals, health programmes and public services.
  • Public and environmental health. Disease prevention, clean water, safe food and sanitation in districts and towns.
  • Food science and processing. Quality control, food safety and making products such as juice, flour and dried fruit.
  • Teaching. Nutrition and catering with education prepares you to teach food and nutrition subjects.

We could not confirm salary figures from an official source, and pay differs between government, private and mission employers. Look at real job adverts and ask people in the field.

A heart-to-heart before you choose

Imagine you are the child who watched a neighbour become ill because there was not enough good food, or who saw a parent worry about what to cook that week. Maybe you simply like cooking and wonder if that counts as a reason. It does.

Nutrition is quiet work. You may not hear the same applause as a surgeon gets. But what a child eats in the first years of life shapes their strength and their schooling. A nurse, a nutritionist or a food technologist helps whole families, often without being thanked.

You may feel nervous because CBN is not as famous as PCB. Fame is not the same as fit. If this combination matches your interests and your marks, it can lead to honest, useful work. We cannot promise a job, but we can say this: your effort in Form Five and Form Six matters more than the three letters.

If you are in Form Four: is CBN right for you?

Check these before you choose.

  1. Chemistry and Biology marks. Both are needed in every CBN entry. Health rules ask for a C in Chemistry and a D in Biology at A-Level.
  2. Interest in food and health. Do you enjoy reading about food, cooking, or how the body works?
  3. Basic Mathematics. Some entries ask for a pass or a D in Basic Mathematics at O-Level. Do not skip it.
  4. Physics. If you want medicine, pharmacy or medical laboratory sciences, you need PCB. CBN does not lead there.
  5. School options. Not every school teaches CBN. Check what your school offers, because Form Five places are allocated by the government.

Our overview of how your combination affects your career gives the wider picture.

If you are in Form Six: what to do after your results

  1. Read your points with our guide to reading your Form Six results.
  2. Open the current TCU guidebook and search for each programme. Write down the subjects, minimum grades and years.
  3. Apply through the university websites in the dates TCU announces. Nobody can sell you a place.
  4. Plan fees early. Our guide to HESLB student loans explains how to apply.
  5. Choose a second and third course that also accept CBN, for example one nursing, one nutrition and one food science.

What if your points are not enough?

  1. Move to a related programme. Food science, human nutrition and public health usually ask for fewer points than nursing and dietetics, though competition differs each year.
  2. Think about a diploma. Colleges offer diplomas in nursing, nutrition and food technology. NACTVET regulates them, so check that the college and course are accredited.
  3. Plan the diploma-to-degree route. Read our guides on going from diploma to degree and NTA levels.
  4. Try again. Some students retake examinations privately. It costs time and money, so ask your teachers first.

Where CBN does not fit

  • Medicine, dentistry, pharmacy, medical laboratory sciences. They ask for principal passes in Physics, Chemistry and Biology.
  • Engineering. It normally asks for Advanced Mathematics, usually with Physics.
  • Nursing at universities that ask for PCB. MUHAS is one example, so read each university's entry.

Other combinations worth comparing

  • CBG suits agriculture, forestry, wildlife and environment, but it does not meet the nursing rule.
  • PCB opens medicine, pharmacy and laboratory degrees, if your Physics is strong.
  • PCM leads to engineering and physical sciences. It is a different path from CBN.

Common questions

Is CBN a real combination in Tanzania?

Yes. NECTA's ACSEE page lists CBN as Chemistry, Biology and Food and Human Nutrition. Check with your school whether it teaches it.

Can a CBN student study nursing?

Often yes. The TCU harmonised nursing rule lists Chemistry, Biology and Physics, Advanced Mathematics or Nutrition, with at least 6 points. Some universities set stricter rules, such as MUHAS, so read each entry.

Can CBN lead to medicine or pharmacy?

No. Medicine and pharmacy ask for Physics, Chemistry and Biology. For these you need PCB.

What is the difference between CBN and CBG?

CBN has Nutrition and CBG has Geography. CBN fits nursing and dietetics. CBG fits agriculture, forestry and wildlife. Both fit Environmental Health Sciences and Public Health.

Can I teach with CBN?

Some programmes prepare teachers in food and nutrition, such as the Nutrition and Catering with Education degree at SUA. Check the current entry.

What to do next

  1. Download the latest TCU guidebook for Form Six holders and search for nursing, nutrition and food science.
  2. Write down the subjects, minimum grades and years for three degrees you like.
  3. Ask a teacher, nurse or student in the field what the work is really like.
  4. Keep a backup: a related degree and an accredited diploma.

Sources and further reading

  • Admission Guidebook for Holders of Secondary School Qualifications 2026/27, Tanzania Commission for Universities
  • Advanced Certificate of Secondary Education Examination (ACSEE), National Examinations Council of Tanzania
