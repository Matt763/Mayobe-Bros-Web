How the United Nations Began: From War to the 1945 Charter

How the United Nations began, from the Atlantic Charter of 1941 to the San Francisco Charter of 1945, and how Tanganyika was linked to it before 1961.

The United Nations was born out of a war that left families everywhere in grief. Its founders wanted a place where countries could argue with words instead of weapons. This post follows how that idea grew, step by step, and ends with the part of the story that touches Tanzania.

The short answer

The United Nations was planned during World War II. Its Charter was signed in San Francisco on 26 June 1945 and came into force on 24 October 1945, which is why 24 October is United Nations Day.

  • The first ideas came in 1941 and 1942, while the war was still going on.
  • Four powers, the United States, the United Kingdom, the Soviet Union and China, drafted the first proposals in 1944.
  • Fifty nations signed the Charter in 1945, and Poland signed later that year, making 51 original members.
  • Tanganyika was a UN trust territory under British administration before it joined the UN on 14 December 1961.

Why did leaders want a new organisation?

Before the UN there was the League of Nations, created after World War I. It did not stop the next world war. By 1945 many leaders agreed that a stronger body was needed.

The Charter itself shows the mood. Its opening words, in the UN's summary, say the peoples of the United Nations are determined to save future generations from war, which brought great suffering twice in one lifetime. We do not quote any figure for the dead, because estimates differ and we have not confirmed one from a UN page. It is enough to say that the loss was enormous, and the founders knew it.

If you want the background to the earlier war, read our post on what caused World War I.

How did the idea grow, from 1941 to 1943?

The UN's own history pages give this timeline.

  • 12 June 1941. Britain, its Dominions and several governments in exile signed the Declaration of St James's Palace in London. It called for cooperation among free peoples after the war.
  • 14 August 1941. US President Franklin Roosevelt and British Prime Minister Winston Churchill issued the Atlantic Charter. Its eighth paragraph spoke of a future "wider and permanent system of general security".
  • 1 January 1942. Twenty-six states at war with the Axis Powers endorsed the Atlantic Charter's principles in the Declaration by United Nations. This was the first official use of the term "United Nations". The UN says Roosevelt coined it.
  • 18 October to 1 November 1943. At the Moscow Conference, the United States, United Kingdom, Soviet Union and China called for a general international organisation to keep peace and security. The UN calls it the first official document to do so.
  • 28 November to 1 December 1943. Roosevelt, Churchill and Stalin met in Tehran and agreed to keep working together during and after the war.

So the name came first as the name of the wartime alliance. Later it became the name of the organisation.

What happened at Dumbarton Oaks, Yalta and San Francisco?

Dumbarton Oaks, 21 August to 7 October 1944. Representatives of the four powers drafted proposals for the new organisation. The final document was issued on 9 October 1944 and became the working paper for the later conference.

Yalta, 4 to 11 February 1945. The leaders agreed on how voting in the Security Council would work. They also called a conference in San Francisco for 25 April 1945.

San Francisco, 25 April to 26 June 1945. The UN says 50 nations sent delegates. Forty-six were invited at first and four more were invited later: the Byelorussian SSR, the Ukrainian SSR, Denmark and Argentina. There were 850 delegates. With advisers and staff the number was 3,500, and more than 2,500 press, radio, newsreel people and observers also came.

The Charter was adopted unanimously on 25 June at the Opera House. It was signed the next day at the Herbst Theatre.

Why do some sources say 50 and others say 51?

Both numbers are right, and each one counts something different. Fifty nations took part in the conference and signed on 26 June. Poland had no representative there, because its new government was announced too late, and a space was left open for it. A Polish government was announced on 28 June, and Poland signed on 15 October 1945. That made 51 original Members.

What happened on 24 October 1945?

A treaty only starts when enough countries approve it at home. The Charter came into force on 24 October 1945, after the five permanent members of the Security Council and a majority of the other signatories deposited their ratifications. The UN says this date is now observed as United Nations Day.

The purposes in Article 1 include keeping international peace and security, building friendly relations between nations, working together on economic, social, cultural and humanitarian problems, and promoting human rights for everyone without distinction of race, sex, language or religion. The UN today lists 193 member countries.

How is Tanzania linked to the story?

Tanganyika did not sign in 1945, because it was not independent. It was a colony under British rule. After World War I it had been a mandate, and after World War II it became a trust territory. The UN's research guide lists Tanganyika as a Trust Territory administered by the United Kingdom, with the trusteeship agreement approved by the General Assembly on 13 December 1946.

The Charter's chapter on trusteeship set out aims. These include helping the people of a territory make progress towards self-government or independence, as suited to the territory and the wishes of its people. It also called for respect for human rights without distinction of race, sex, language or religion.

Tanganyikans used this system to speak for themselves. According to The Citizen, a Tanzanian newspaper, Julius Nyerere presented Tanganyika's case to the UN Trusteeship Council in New York in March 1955 and again in November 1956. The same article says that visiting Trusteeship Council members suggested in 1954 a path of 20 to 25 years to independence, which the nationalist party TANU thought too slow. The article credits Nyerere's arguments with shortening it to about seven years. That is the newspaper's view. We could not confirm from a UN page how much his arguments changed the timetable, so read it as one account.

Tanganyika became independent on 9 December 1961, according to the same article. The UN's list of members gives the admission date of the United Republic of Tanzania as 14 December 1961.

To read more of that story, see how Tanganyika won independence and Julius Nyerere and ujamaa.

Common questions

When was the United Nations founded?

The Charter was signed on 26 June 1945 and came into force on 24 October 1945. The UN says it officially came into existence on that second date.

Who coined the name "United Nations"?

The UN says Roosevelt coined it. It was first used officially in the Declaration by United Nations on 1 January 1942.

What was a trust territory?

It was a territory placed under the UN trusteeship system, run by an administering country and watched by the Trusteeship Council. Tanganyika was one, administered by the United Kingdom.

When did Tanzania join the UN?

The UN member list gives 14 December 1961 as the date of admission.

What to do next

  1. Mark 24 October on your calendar as United Nations Day, and read the Charter's opening words on the UN website.
  2. Compare the dates in this post with your school notes, and check them against the sources below.
  3. Read about Tanganyika's road to independence to see how the trusteeship system fit into it.

History like this reminds us that even after great loss, people sat down and tried to build something better, and you can learn from that.
