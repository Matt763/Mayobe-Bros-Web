How Tanganyika Won Independence on 9 December 1961

A clear timeline from German rule and the British mandate to TANU in 1954, the elections, and independence on 9 December 1961, won by talks and votes.

Every 9 December, Tanzanians remember the day their country became free of colonial rule. The date is simple to remember. The road to it was longer, and it is worth knowing step by step. This post gives you a timeline you can trust, with the sources at the end.

The short answer

Tanganyika became independent from Britain on 9 December 1961. It got there through a political party, TANU, led by Julius Nyerere. TANU argued for independence, spoke to the United Nations, and won elections. The sources we opened describe a party, talks and elections, and none describes an armed struggle.

  • 1954: TANU is formed on 7 July.
  • 1955 and 1956: Nyerere speaks to the UN Trusteeship Council in New York.
  • 1958 and 1960: TANU wins elections.
  • 9 December 1961: independence.
  • 1962: Tanganyika becomes a republic.

What was Tanganyika before independence?

For about three decades, the land was part of German East Africa. World History Encyclopedia says Germany ruled the colony from 1885 to 1918 and lost it by defeat in the First World War. EBSCO says that after the war the territory went to the League of Nations, which gave Britain the mandate to rule it. A mandate is a duty to govern a territory on behalf of an international body.

After the Second World War, the arrangement changed. The United Nations research guide says Tanganyika became a Trust Territory under United Kingdom administration, with the trusteeship agreement approved on 13 December 1946.

So by the 1950s, Britain was administering Tanganyika under a UN trusteeship agreement.

7 July 1954: TANU is formed

EBSCO says the Tanganyika African National Union was formed on 7 July 1954, with Julius Nyerere as its president.

TANU's goal was independence. Its route, as the sources describe it, was political: appeals to the UN and elections.

Speaking to the United Nations

EBSCO says Nyerere travelled to New York to address the UN Trusteeship Council twice, in March 1955 and in December 1956.

Think about what that means. The leader of a political party in a trust territory went to New York to make the case for self-rule in front of an international body.

We did not find, in the sources we opened, the exact wording of what he said, so we do not quote it.

1958 to 1960: elections and self-government

EBSCO says TANU won the Legislative Council elections of September 1958 "overwhelmingly". In August 1960 came a general election, after which Nyerere was elected as prime minister of the new government. Wikipedia's article on the territory says TANU won both the 1958 and 1960 elections.

Titles in these years differ between sources. Some call the leader "chief minister" before "prime minister". We have not confirmed the exact dates of each title, so we use the safer wording: by independence, Nyerere was leading the government as prime minister.

We also left out seat counts, because we could not check them in a source we opened.

9 December 1961: independence day

EBSCO says Tanganyika achieved independence on 9 December 1961. The Wikipedia article on the Union of Tanganyika and Zanzibar gives the same date and says Nyerere was Prime Minister.

Other countries moved quickly. The US Office of the Historian says President John F. Kennedy sent a congratulatory message to Nyerere on 9 December 1961, and that the US recognised Tanganyika and set up diplomatic relations that day. The UN's list of members shows that Tanzania, the state that Tanganyika later became part of, was admitted on 14 December 1961. That is five days after independence.

You may have heard a story about why the date was fixed as 9 December. The accounts we saw were one person's memory and did not agree with a UN record on the original date, so we leave it out.

Three words worth knowing

Mandate. A task given to a country by the League of Nations to govern a territory.

Trust Territory. The name the United Nations used for such a territory after 1946. Tanganyika was one, and the UK was its administering authority.

Legislative Council. The law-making body of the territory before independence. EBSCO says TANU won its 1958 elections overwhelmingly.

After independence: the republic and the Union

EBSCO says that in 1962 the country became a republic and Nyerere became president. Wikipedia gives the date of the republican constitution as 9 December 1962. In 1964, Tanganyika joined with Zanzibar. You can read that story in our post on how Tanganyika and Zanzibar became Tanzania.

Why does the peaceful road matter?

Compare it with the earlier chapter. In 1905 to 1907, many communities rose in arms against German rule in the Maji Maji war, and a very large number of people died, mostly from famine. You can read that in our post on the Maji Maji war.

Independence in 1961 came by a different path: organising, speaking and voting. We do not claim that nobody suffered under colonial rule, or that no one was ever hurt in the years before. We only say that the sources we read describe elections and talks, not a war.

Common questions

When did Tanganyika become independent?

On 9 December 1961, according to EBSCO and the other sources below.

When was TANU formed?

On 7 July 1954, with Julius Nyerere as president.

Who ruled Tanganyika before independence?

Germany until the First World War, then Britain, first under a League of Nations mandate and then as the administering authority of a UN Trust Territory.

Is 9 December the same as Union Day?

No. Union Day is on 26 April and marks the 1964 union with Zanzibar.

What to do next

  1. Write the five key dates on one page: 1954, 1955, 1958, 1960 and 1961.
  2. Read the EBSCO summary on Nyerere to see the same story in more detail.
  3. Ask an older relative where they were on 9 December 1961, and note down their story with their name and the date.

Learning how this was done, step by step, helps us value it.
