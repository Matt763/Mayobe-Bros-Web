Kilwa Kisiwani: The Rich Swahili City Ibn Battuta Admired

How a small island off southern Tanzania grew rich on the gold and ivory trade, what its coral-stone ruins show, and why Kilwa declined after 1500.

Long before Tanzania had its present borders, a city on a small island in the south was trading with Arabia, Persia, India and China. Its name was Kilwa. Its ruins are still there, and they show that the East African coast was part of a wide world of trade centuries ago.

The short answer

Kilwa Kisiwani was a Swahili island city off what is now southern Tanzania. It grew rich by sitting between two sets of traders: African gold and ivory sellers to the south and inland, and ship owners from across the Indian Ocean. It reached its height in the 1300s. The Portuguese attacked it in 1505, and trade moved north.

  • Kilwa was an independent city-state, ruled by its own sultans.
  • It made its own copper coins.
  • Gold came to it through Sofala, in what is now Mozambique.
  • A traveller named Ibn Battuta visited around 1331 and praised it.
  • Its ruins are a World Heritage Site.

Where is Kilwa Kisiwani, and who lived there?

Kilwa Kisiwani means "Kilwa on the island". It lies off the coast of southern Tanzania, in Lindi Region, near the town of Kilwa Masoko. Small boats link the island to the town. Distances from Dar es Salaam differ between sources, so we simply say it is several hundred kilometres to the south.

World History Encyclopedia describes Kilwa as the southernmost of the major Swahili Coast trading cities. "Swahili" here means the coastal people and towns of East Africa who share the Swahili language and a long history of trade across the sea.

The same source says Shirazi merchants from the Persian Gulf arrived from the 12th century, and that they took over rule of Kilwa by about 1200. Medieval Arab writers described this as peaceful. We have not used the old founding legends here, because we did not open a source we could check them against.

How did Kilwa grow so wealthy?

The answer is location and trade.

Gold from the kingdom of Great Zimbabwe reached Sofala, a port south of Kilwa. World History Encyclopedia says this gold helped make Kilwa the most prosperous city on the Swahili coast, overtaking Mogadishu. Sofala was founded perhaps around 1300 to reach the resources of southern Africa's interior.

Great Zimbabwe was a large stone city inland, in what is now Zimbabwe. World History Encyclopedia says it was lived in from about 1100 to about 1550.

Kilwa also exported ivory, copper, timber, incense and rhino horn. In return it imported Chinese Ming porcelain, Indian cloth and Persian glassware.

Kilwa also made its own copper coins. World History Encyclopedia says this began in the 11th or 12th century, and Wikipedia says Kilwa minted coins for about 500 years. Own coins mean one thing: the city had a ruler strong enough to run its own money.

We use the wording "one of the richest cities on the East African coast". No source we opened says it was the richest city in all of Africa, so we do not claim that.

What do the ruins show?

The old buildings were made of coral stone. Three places are usually named.

The Great Mosque. Also called the Friday Mosque, it is larger and grander than most Swahili mosques. World History Encyclopedia says it has 30 arched bays and columns cut from single pieces of coral stone. Sources differ about its age. World History Encyclopedia says it was begun by al Hasan ibn Suleiman in the 14th century and finished by Suleiman al Adil, who ruled from 1412 to 1442, and that it includes parts of an older mosque. Wikipedia says the earliest part is probably from the 12th century, with a big expansion in the early 14th. The safe summary is that it grew over several centuries.

Husuni Kubwa. The name means "Large Fort", but it was a palace and trading building on a sandstone ridge outside the main town. World History Encyclopedia says it covers almost 10,000 square metres, about one hectare. An inscription names al Hasan ibn Suleiman, who ruled from 1320 to 1333, as the ruler who ordered it built. Wikipedia says it was left unfinished.

Songo Mnara. A smaller stone town lies on a nearby island. Wikipedia says it was lived in from the 14th to the 16th century, with several mosques, cemeteries and stone houses. UNESCO lists it together with Kilwa Kisiwani as one World Heritage Site, which Wikipedia says was inscribed in 1981.

What did Ibn Battuta say about Kilwa?

Ibn Battuta was a Moroccan traveller who lived from 1304 to about 1368. World History Encyclopedia says he visited Kilwa around 1331 and called it "one of the most beautiful towns in the world".

That is a short line, but it matters. It is a written view from an outsider who had seen many cities, and it matches what the ruins suggest: a city with stone mosques, a palace and a busy harbour.

Why did Kilwa decline?

There were several reasons, and they built up over time.

Quarrels at home. World History Encyclopedia says disputes within the ruling family weakened Kilwa before the Portuguese came.

The Portuguese. Vasco da Gama's voyage of 1498 to 1499 opened a sea route that brought the Portuguese to the region. They attacked Kilwa in 1505, and many buildings were left in ruins. They built forts at Sofala in 1505 and at Mozambique Island in 1507.

Trade moved. After Portuguese control spread, traders from the interior shifted their business to ports further north, such as Mombasa.

We have left out some later events that only one weak source mentioned. The key point is that Kilwa lost its place at the centre of the gold trade, and with it much of its wealth.

Protecting the ruins today

Coral stone and sea air wear buildings down. Wikipedia says the site was put on the World Heritage in Danger list in 2004 and was later removed, with 2014 given as the end of that period. We could not open UNESCO's own page, so please check the current status with UNESCO before you rely on this.

If you visit, go with a local guide and ask permission before you climb on walls.

Common questions

Was Kilwa the richest city in Africa?

We cannot say so. Sources describe it as one of the richest and most prosperous Swahili coast cities, which is as far as we can safely go.

Who ruled Kilwa?

Sultans, in a series of ruling families. World History Encyclopedia names al Hasan ibn Suleiman, who ruled from 1320 to 1333, and Suleiman al Adil, who ruled from 1412 to 1442.

Can I visit Kilwa Kisiwani today?

People do visit, by boat from Kilwa Masoko. Plan the trip with a local operator, and check conditions and fees before you go.

What to do next

  1. Read the World History Encyclopedia page on Kilwa to see the sources side by side.
  2. Check UNESCO's site listing for the current protection status of the ruins.
  3. Look at a map and trace the gold route from Great Zimbabwe, through Sofala, to Kilwa.

Our coast has a proud history of trade and building, and learning it is a good start.
