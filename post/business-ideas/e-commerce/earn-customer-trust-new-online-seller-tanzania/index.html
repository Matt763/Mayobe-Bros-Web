How a New Online Seller Can Earn Customer Trust in Tanzania

No reviews yet? Learn simple, honest habits that help strangers trust a new online shop in Tanzania, from clear photos to receipts and fast replies.

Starting a shop with no reviews and no followers can feel lonely. You post a good product and nobody replies. That silence is normal. Strangers do not know you yet, and many have been cheated before, so they hold back.

You cannot make people trust you in one day. You can give them many small reasons to relax. This post lists those reasons, one by one, using advice from official pages and from the platforms sellers use.

The short answer

Trust grows from small habits that you repeat every day. Show who you are, describe your goods honestly, let buyers feel safe about payment and delivery, give receipts, and answer quickly and fairly. None of this costs much. No habit here guarantees sales, but each one removes a reason for a buyer to say no.

  • Put your name, place and opening hours where buyers can see them.
  • Use your own photos and say the real size, colour and price.
  • Offer payment after the buyer has seen the item, where you can.
  • Give a receipt and keep a record of every sale.
  • Reply fast, stay calm with complaints, and ask for feedback politely.

Why buyers are careful

Online fraud is a daily worry in Tanzania. The Tanzania Communications Regulatory Authority (TCRA) runs public campaigns to warn people about scam messages and about sending money to strangers. As checked in November 2026, it advises people not to send money from their phone to someone they do not know, and to check the message and the number before paying.

So when a stranger looks at your shop, they are not judging you. They are protecting their money. Think of each habit below as an answer to a question the buyer is asking in their head: "Is this person real? Is this item real? Will I be safe?"

Show who you are

A shop with no face is hard to trust. Give buyers one place where they can learn the basics.

If you use WhatsApp Business, fill in the business profile. WhatsApp's own page for the Business app says a business profile can give helpful information for your customers in one place, and that this helps you make a good first impression. Add your shop name, what you sell, your area (for example Kariakoo, Mwenge or Kisutu), and your opening hours.

On other apps, do the same in the bio. Use the same name and the same phone number everywhere. If your name changes from page to page, buyers wonder why.

Use a clear profile picture. A photo of your real shelf or your real products works better than a stock picture or a copied one.

Does a registered business help?

The Business Registrations and Licensing Agency (BRELA) lists benefits of holding a business licence on its website. One of them, in Swahili, is "kuaminika kwa wateja", which means being trusted by customers. Others are recognition by banks and a better chance of getting loans.

We did not find a page saying that a small seller on social media must be registered before selling. Rules depend on your kind of business and where you work, so check the BRELA and TRA pages for your own case. Our guide to registering a small business in Tanzania explains the steps. If you register, you can say so in your profile. Do not claim a licence you do not hold.

Say exactly what you sell

Most disputes begin with a gap between what the buyer imagined and what arrived. Close that gap before the sale.

  • Take photos in daylight, from more than one side. Show the real item, not a picture from a catalogue.
  • Write the size, colour, material and condition. If the item is used, say so and show any scratch or mark.
  • State the price in TSh, and say what it includes. If delivery costs extra, give that price too.
  • Say how long delivery takes in your area, and be honest. Two days promised and four days delivered hurts more than four days promised and four delivered.
  • If you do not have an item in stock, say so.

Never use another seller's pictures and never claim a brand you do not sell. A copied photo is easy to spot and quickly ends the conversation.

Let buyers feel safe about payment and delivery

The biggest fear is paying first and receiving nothing. Jiji, the online marketplace, tells its buyers to check a seller's feedback, to avoid paying upfront, to look at the item before paying, and to meet sellers face to face in a public place. That list tells you what careful buyers will want from you.

You can meet them halfway:

  1. Offer pay after inspection. Jiji's advice to sellers is not to ask for payment upfront, because buyers are wary of sending money before they see the item. It suggests delivery services that let buyers pay after they inspect the product.
  2. Meet in a public place when the buyer collects the item, such as a busy shop front or a well-known bus stop.
  3. Be clear about mobile money. Give the number and the registered name, and let the buyer see that the name matches. Never ask a buyer to send "a small deposit to hold the item" unless you are ready to explain it and return it if they change their mind.
  4. Do not rush the buyer. Pressure ("pay now, only one left") looks like the scam messages people already know.

If you sell items that cost a lot, you can expect more questions. That is fine. Answer each one patiently.

Protect yourself too

Trust runs both ways. Fake payment messages are a risk for sellers. Check that the money has really arrived in your account before you hand over the goods, and do not rely on a screenshot or a message alone. TCRA advises people to check the message and the number, and to call their provider on 100 if they are unsure.

Give receipts and keep records

A receipt tells the buyer that you are a real business that expects to be found again. It also protects you when someone says "I never got it".

The Tanzania Revenue Authority (TRA) prints a slogan on its website: "Whenever you sell, issue receipt. Whenever you buy, demand receipt." As checked in November 2026, TRA says that a person who supplies goods or services and has an annual turnover assessed at TSh 11 million or more must use an electronic fiscal device (EFD) to issue fiscal receipts. A very small seller may be below that line, but you may still give a simple receipt.

A simple receipt can show:

  • your shop name and phone number,
  • the date,
  • what was sold, the quantity and the price,
  • how it was paid,
  • a line saying how to reach you if there is a problem.

Keep a copy. A notebook or a photo of each receipt on your phone is enough to start. Records also help you see which items sell and which come back. Ask TRA or a tax adviser what applies to your turnover, because tax rules can change.

Reply fast and handle complaints fairly

Speed shows respect. Jiji tells sellers not to make buyers wait too long for a reply. If you cannot answer at once, set a short message such as "Thank you, I will reply by 6 pm." Then do it.

When something goes wrong:

  1. Listen to the whole complaint before you answer.
  2. Say sorry if it is your fault, in one plain sentence.
  3. Offer a fix you can really deliver: a replacement, an exchange, or a refund.
  4. Do it quickly and tell the buyer when it is done.

Tanzanian consumer law may give buyers rights on returns and refunds. We could not confirm the details for this post, so check the Fair Competition Commission, which handles consumer protection, for the current rules. Whatever the law says, a clear return rule written in your profile (for example, "Tell me within 24 hours if the item is damaged") is easier on everyone.

Asking for feedback

Jiji says that inviting buyers to share their experience after a sale makes it more likely that new buyers will choose you, because your rating depends on the feedback you get. Ask once, politely, after the item has arrived. Never write reviews for yourself and never pay for fake ones. Our post on asking customers for reviews honestly has wording you can use, and the one on fake followers and "guaranteed sales" offers explains why shortcuts hurt.

Common questions

How long does it take to build trust?

There is no fixed time. It depends on your product, your area and how often you sell. Expect the first sales to come mostly from people who already know you or who ask questions first and see that you answer well.

Should I ask for money before I deliver?

Many buyers do not like this, and Jiji advises sellers against it. Where you can, let the buyer pay after seeing the item. If you must take a deposit, for example for something made to order, explain why in writing and give a receipt.

Do I need a website to be trusted?

No. A tidy WhatsApp Business profile or social page with real photos, a phone number and clear prices is a fine start. A website can come later.

What if a buyer leaves an unfair complaint?

Stay polite, give the facts once, and offer to fix what is truly wrong. Other buyers read how you respond, and a calm answer says a lot about you.

What to do next

  1. Write your shop profile today: name, products, area, hours and phone number. Use the same text on every page.
  2. Retake photos of your five best items in daylight and write honest descriptions.
  3. Print or write a simple receipt format and use it for the next sale.
  4. Write your return and delivery rules in two or three lines and pin them where buyers can see them.

Every kept promise is a small brick, and a shop with no name today can have one in a year. Start with the next order.
