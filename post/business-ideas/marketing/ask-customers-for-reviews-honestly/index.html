How to Ask Customers for Reviews Honestly, and Handle Bad Ones

How to ask customers for reviews with a link or QR code, why you must never pay for them, and how to reply calmly to good and bad reviews.

You work hard to serve people well. Then one day you read a review that says something unkind, and your stomach drops. Or the opposite happens: weeks pass, you serve hundreds of people, and not one of them writes a word.

Both feelings are normal. This post helps with both. It explains how to ask for reviews in a fair way, what you must never do to get them, and how to reply to a bad review without making things worse.

The short answer

Ask every customer, politely, to share their honest experience, and make it easy with a link or a QR code. Never pay, give gifts or trade favours for a review, and never write fake ones. Reply to each review in a calm, short and honest way. Ask permission before you show a customer's words, name or chat to others.

  • Ask in person or by message, right after a good moment of service.
  • Give a simple link or QR code from your profile.
  • Do not offer discounts, free items or any reward for a review.
  • Reply to good and bad reviews politely, and never share private details.
  • Use customer words in your own posts only with their clear permission.

Why do reviews matter so much?

When you buy a phone cover at a stall, you can pick it up and look at it. When you order from a shop you have never visited, you cannot. You have only the pictures, the price and what other people say. A review is a stranger's way of saying "this shop did what it promised" or "it did not".

We cannot say that reviews raise your rank on Google or lift your sales, because we have no firm source for those claims. What we can say is that people often look for them, and that an honest review gives a new customer a reason to trust you. It also gives you something just as useful: a picture of what you do well and where you can improve.

How do I ask: a link, a QR code and a simple message

Google's help pages say you can ask customers to leave a review by sending them to a Google link or having them scan a QR code, and that you can create either one from your Business Profile. If you do not have a profile yet, read our post on how to claim a free Google Business Profile for your shop.

Know this before you ask: customers need to be signed in to a Google Account to post a review. Google says they can create one with an email address that is not Gmail. Some customers will not have an account or will not want to make one. That is fine. Do not push.

Here are some simple ways to ask:

  • At the counter. After a good conversation or a smooth sale, say, "If you were happy with us, would you share a few words online? It helps other people find us. Here is a code you can scan."
  • By message. Send a short, friendly note with the link a day or so after the sale.
  • On a small card or sign. Put a printed QR code near the till or on your receipts.

Here is sample wording you can adapt. It is an example for you to copy, not a real customer comment:

Thank you for buying from us today. If you have a minute, we would be glad to hear how it went, good or not so good. Your honest words help us improve. Here is the link: (your link).

Notice that it asks for honesty, not for five stars. This is our opinion, not a Google rule: the fair habit is to ask everyone, not only the customers who look happy. If you ask only the happy ones, the picture people see becomes less true. Google's pages we read do not name this practice, so check its current rules yourself.

Do not ask a customer who is upset or in a hurry. Fix the problem first. If it is fixed and they want to share what happened, that is their choice.

What must I not do?

This part matters most, because breaking these rules can harm your business.

Do not pay for reviews or reward them. Google's help page is blunt. It says offering free or discounted goods or services in return for reviews is "strictly prohibited". The same applies to offering a reward to change a review, or to get a negative review removed. Google treats that as fake and misleading content. So "Leave us five stars and get 10% off" is not allowed.

Do not write reviews for your own shop, or ask friends to write about visits that never happened. Google says reviews must reflect a genuine experience.

Do not buy reviews, likes or comments from someone else. Other platforms have similar rules. Meta's spam policy, for example, bans trading for engagement and offering anything of value in return for it.

Do not give false information about your goods or services. Tanzania's Fair Competition Act of 2003 has a section against misleading or deceptive conduct in trade (section 15) and a section against false or misleading representations about goods and services (section 16). We read these headings in the copy on the WIPO Lex website. This is not legal advice. If you are unsure whether something you plan to say about your products is allowed, ask a qualified adviser.

Fake praise also hurts you in a quiet way. Once a customer feels a review is fake, they stop trusting every review you have.

How do I reply to a good review and to a bad one?

Replies are public. Anyone who reads reviews can read what you wrote back. Google asks that replies be polite, professional, short and relevant. It also says not to use replies to promote deals.

A good review

A thank-you that says something real is better than a copy-paste line. Google suggests avoiding generic thanks and sharing something useful instead, such as an answer to a question, or an invitation to come back. For example:

Thank you, (name). We are glad the repair was done the same day. Come back any time you need help.

Use your own words each time. If you have no time, a short real reply is better than a long stale one.

A bad review

This is the hard part. Take a breath. Do not answer while you are angry. Leave it for an hour or until the next morning.

Google's advice for negative reviews is practical:

  1. Reply promptly, but calmly. Use the reviewer's name and sign your reply.
  2. Be honest. If you made a mistake, say so. Apologise if an apology is right.
  3. Explain what you can and cannot do. Do not make promises you cannot keep.
  4. Never share private details or attack the person. For a complex problem, invite them to continue by phone or email.
  5. If a review breaks Google's content policies, you can flag it. For example, if it is clearly fake or abusive.

Here is a sample reply. It is only an example of wording, not a real review or answer:

We are sorry the delivery was late, (name). That is not the service we want to give. Please call us on (number) so we can see what went wrong and put it right if we can.

Do not try to hide or delete critical reviews, and do not offer a gift in return for removing one. Google calls that fake and misleading behaviour. A negative review that gets a calm, kind reply can tell other readers more about you than a page of stars. People understand that mistakes happen. They notice how you respond.

Can I share customer words and chat screenshots?

Only with permission. A kind message in your WhatsApp inbox belongs to the person who wrote it. Before you post their words, their photo or a screenshot of the chat:

  • Ask them clearly, and explain where you will show it.
  • Take out names, phone numbers and any details they did not agree to share.
  • Never change what they said.
  • Take it down if they ask you to.

Tanzania has a Personal Data Protection Act. We have not studied it for this post, and this is not legal advice. Asking permission and showing the least personal detail is a respectful habit in any case.

Common questions

How many reviews do I need?

There is no fixed number. Focus on asking every customer fairly and replying to each review. A few honest reviews are better than many bought ones.

What if a customer gives only a low star rating with no words?

You can still reply. Thank them for the feedback, say you would like to learn what went wrong and give a phone number or email. Do not ask them to change the rating.

Can I offer a small gift to thank people for reviewing?

No. Google's help pages say that giving free or discounted goods or services in return for reviews is strictly prohibited. You can thank customers warmly, but do not attach a reward.

Can I remove a review I do not like?

Not just because it is unkind or hurts. If it breaks Google's content policies, for example if it is fake or abusive, you can flag it. Otherwise, reply politely and move on.

What to do next

  1. Create your review link or QR code from your profile, and test it on your own phone.
  2. Write one friendly sentence for asking, and use it with every customer for two weeks.
  3. Write down your rules for replies: stay calm, wait before answering, never share private details.
  4. Read all your reviews once a month and note one thing to improve.

A shop is built from many small acts of trust. One honest review at a time is a good way to earn it.
