SMS and Email Marketing in Tanzania: Consent Rules for Small Shops

Can you text or email customers about offers? What the Electronic Transactions Act says about consent, who you are, and opting out, in plain words.

Every day at the till, a customer gives you a phone number. It is a small act of trust. This post is about how to keep that trust when you want to tell them about a new offer.

This is general information, not legal advice. For your own case, ask a lawyer.

The short answer

Yes, you can send promotional SMS or emails, but the law sets conditions. Section 32 of the Electronic Transactions Act, 2015 says you may not send an unsolicited commercial message about goods or services unless three things are true.

  • The person agreed (consented) to get it.
  • The message says at the start who it is from and why it was sent.
  • The message gives a way to say no to further messages (an opt-out).
  • A fine of not less than TSh 10,000,000, or prison of not less than one year, or both, is the penalty for breaking the rule.
  • Customer phone numbers and emails are also personal data, which has its own rules.

What does the law say, in plain words?

We read the text of the Act as published in the Government Gazette supplement (Act No. 13 of 2015). Section 32 is titled "Unsolicited goods, services or communications". The numbering and wording may differ in a revised edition of the laws, so check the current text before you rely on a section number.

Subsection (1) says a person shall not send an unsolicited commercial communication on goods or service unless:

  1. the consumer consents to the communication;
  2. at the beginning of the message, it states who is sending it and its purpose; and
  3. the message gives an opt-out option to reject further messages.

Subsection (3) says a person who breaks the section commits an offence. On conviction the penalty is a fine of not less than ten million shillings, or imprisonment for not less than one year, or both. That is a serious amount for a small shop, which is a good reason to keep to the three conditions.

The text talks about a "commercial communication". It does not list SMS or email by name. The sensible reading for a shop is to treat both as covered. We did not find official guidance on other apps such as WhatsApp, so we do not say whether this section covers them. Ask a lawyer if you plan to use them for bulk offers.

When can consent be taken as given?

Subsection (2) says consent is deemed to be met only when all four of these are true:

  1. You collected the contact details yourself, in the course of a sale or while negotiating a sale.
  2. You send only promotional messages about your similar products and services.
  3. You offered the person the chance to opt out, and they did not opt out.
  4. You offer an opt-out with every later message.

Say a customer buys a bag of rice from you and gives their number for delivery. A message about your rice and cooking oil offers could fit that pattern, if you offered an opt-out and give one every time. A message about your cousin's new hair salon does not. It is not your similar product, and the person did not agree.

Numbers from other places are different. A list you bought, a list copied from a group chat or a number you found online has no consent from the person. Do not use it.

What should each message include?

Keep it short and clear. A message that follows the three conditions might look like this (an example, not a template to copy blindly):

Mama Neema Shop: New offer on rice this week, TSh 3,200 per kg. To stop messages, reply STOP.

The shop name comes first, so people know who sent it. The purpose is plain. The way to stop is written in the message.

An opt-out only works if you act on it. Keep a simple list of numbers that said stop, and remove them before the next send. Do it the same day if you can.

Is my customer list personal data?

Yes. A name with a phone number or an email address is personal data. Tanzania has the Personal Data Protection Act, 2022, and the Personal Data Collection and Processing Regulations, 2023. The Personal Data Protection Commission (PDPC) links both on its website.

The PDPC registration page says a person shall not collect or process personal data without being registered as a data controller or data processor. Its notice says public and private institutions, companies, organisations and non-governmental organisations must register on its system. For a private organisation, the page lists a BRELA certificate, a TIN certificate and an audited financial year report as the documents. The page also says one staff member is appointed as the Data Protection Officer to register.

What the page does not say is whether a very small shop, such as a kiosk run by one person, must register. We could not confirm that, and we do not want to guess. Write to the PDPC helpdesk at helpdesk@pdpc.go.tz and ask.

Whatever the answer, care for the list as you would care for cash:

  • Keep it in one place, on a phone or computer with a lock.
  • Do not share it with other businesses.
  • Collect only what you need (a name and a number is often enough).
  • Delete numbers when a person asks.

Good habits at the till

Making consent part of your normal service is easier than fixing it later.

  1. Ask out loud. "May we message you about offers? You can say stop at any time."
  2. Write it down. A page in a notebook with name, number, date and a tick for "yes" is a record. A digital form works too.
  3. Keep the promise. If you said "once a week", send once a week.
  4. Do not message at night. This is courtesy, not law, but it earns goodwill.
  5. Make every message useful. A real price, a real date, a real reason.
  6. Respect "stop" right away.

Think about how you feel when a stranger texts you about something you never asked for. Your customers feel the same. A short list of people who want to hear from you is worth more than a long list of people who are annoyed.

How should I choose a bulk SMS provider and sender name?

Bulk SMS companies often explain sender names, registration and costs on their own websites. Some of those pages describe rules about sender names. We could not find those rules on an official TCRA page. The TCRA rules page we opened in November 2026 lists six rules, and none of the titles mention bulk SMS or sender names. So we cannot tell you what the sender-name rules are.

What you can do:

  • Check tcra.go.tz for current rules and notices.
  • Ask any provider, in writing, what they require from you and how long approval takes.
  • Ask what they do when someone replies "stop".
  • Compare the total cost in TSh before you buy.
  • Do not pay a large sum before you have those answers.

Common questions

Do I need consent if the person is my regular customer?

Section 32 says consent can be taken as given only in the narrow case described above. Being a regular customer does not by itself change that. The safest path is to ask.

Does section 32 apply to emails as well as SMS?

The wording covers commercial communications in general and does not name a channel. Treat emails the same way as SMS. For a firm answer, ask a lawyer.

What if someone replies "stop" and I forget?

Sending after a "stop" is the kind of mistake that breaks the third condition. Keep a stop list and check it before every send.

Must I register with the PDPC?

The PDPC says a person shall not collect or process personal data without being registered. We could not confirm if tiny shops must register. Email helpdesk@pdpc.go.tz.

What to do next

  1. Open your customer list. Next to each name, note how you got the number and whether the person agreed.
  2. Write one sentence you will say at the till to ask for consent, and one line that goes into every message to stop it.
  3. Email helpdesk@pdpc.go.tz to ask about registration for your size of business.
  4. Read the section yourself in the PDF below, and ask a lawyer if you have questions.

A customer who gives you their number is saying "I trust you". Treat that gift with care and they will keep coming back.

As checked in November 2026. Laws and rules can change, so confirm with the official sources.
