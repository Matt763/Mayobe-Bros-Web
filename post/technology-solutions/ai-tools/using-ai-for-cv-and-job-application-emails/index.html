Use AI to Improve Your CV and Job Emails, Honestly

A four-step way to use an AI chatbot to tidy your CV and job email without inventing anything. Includes prompts you can copy and a list of things AI must never add.

Writing about yourself is hard, especially when you have never had a job. You may have a good heart, real skills and a lot of hope, but the page stays empty. An AI chatbot can help you find the words. It cannot give you a past you do not have, and it should not try. This post shows a simple, honest way to use it.

The short answer

Write your true facts first. Then ask the AI to organise and polish them. Then check every line, change it into your own voice and read it aloud. The facts must be yours at the start and at the end.

  • AI is good at layout, clearer wording and spotting spelling mistakes.
  • AI is not allowed to add jobs, grades, skills, courses or referees you do not have.
  • Write a new, short email or letter for each job.
  • Keep your phone number, address and ID numbers out of the chat.
  • You are responsible for the final result. Google's own advice on prompts says "the final output is yours."

What can AI do for a job application, and what can it not?

A chatbot is a program that writes text in answer to what you type. It works by guessing likely words, which makes it fluent. It does not know you, and it does not know the truth about your life. If you want to understand why, read how AI chatbots work in plain words.

Good uses:

  • Putting your notes into clear CV sections.
  • Turning "I helped customers at my uncle's shop" into a clear, honest line.
  • Shortening a long paragraph.
  • Checking spelling and grammar.
  • Suggesting a first draft of a short email that you then rewrite.

Bad uses:

  • Asking it to "make me sound more experienced."
  • Letting it write a whole letter that you send without reading.
  • Copying the same letter to many employers.
  • Pasting private data, such as your NIDA number.

Employers want to meet the real person. A claim you cannot explain at an interview can end your chances there, and a false claim can cost you a job after you start.

A safe four-step workflow

Say you are a Form Six leaver or a new graduate in Dodoma. You have your certificates, a few weeks of volunteering and a good school record. This is an example, not a real person. Here is how you could work.

Step 1: List your true facts

Open a notes page and write, in your own plain words:

  • Your education: school or college, course, years, results you can prove.
  • Work, placements and volunteering: where, when, and what you did.
  • Skills you really use: for example, typing, Excel basics, Swahili and English, selling in a shop.
  • Something you did that made a difference, with a real number only if you know it.

Do this before you open any AI tool. If a fact is not in your notes, it does not go in your CV.

Step 2: Ask the AI to organise it

Use the prompts in the next section. Ask for a layout that follows trusted CV advice. The UK National Careers Service, a government careers service, suggests these parts: contact details, a short introduction, education, work history and optional references. It suggests describing each job in two or three lines and putting the most recent first. It also suggests you include placements, volunteering and paid work.

That is UK advice. If a Tanzanian employer or portal asks for a particular form or format, follow theirs. For help with a CV that suits local employers, see our post on how to write a CV for your first job in Tanzania.

Step 3: Check every line against your notes

Read the AI's answer slowly. Put a tick next to each line that matches your notes. Delete anything that does not. Look for these traps:

  • A job title you never had.
  • A number you did not give ("increased sales by 40%").
  • A skill or course you never mentioned.
  • A school grade that is higher than yours.

Step 4: Make it sound like you

Change phrases you would never say. Read the CV and the email aloud. If you stumble, rewrite the sentence. Ask a friend, teacher or relative to read it for spelling and sense. The National Careers Service also suggests that someone else checks your CV and that you use the same clear style throughout.

Prompts you can copy

Replace the words in capital letters with your own facts. Do not type your phone number, home address, NIDA number or exam number into a chatbot. Add those yourself in the final document.

To organise a CV

You are a careers adviser. Below are my true facts. Put them into a one-page CV with these headings: Introduction, Education, Work and volunteering, Skills. Use only what I wrote. Do not add any job, grade, skill, course or number. If something is missing, tell me what is missing instead of inventing it. My facts: [PASTE YOUR NOTES WITHOUT CONTACT DETAILS]

To improve a line

Here is one line from my CV: "[YOUR LINE]". Make it clearer in simple English. Keep it truthful. Do not add numbers or tasks I did not mention. Give me three options.

To draft a short application email

Write a short email of about 120 words applying for the job of [JOB TITLE] at [EMPLOYER]. Here is the part of the advert that matters: [PASTE TWO OR THREE LINES FROM THE ADVERT]. Here are my true facts that fit the job: [TWO OR THREE FACTS]. Use simple, polite English. Do not invent anything.

To check for mistakes

Check this text for spelling, grammar and unclear sentences. Do not change the meaning and do not add new facts: [PASTE TEXT]

Google's guide to writing prompts says to give the AI a role, a task, background information and the format you want. You do not need all four every time, but the more you give, the better the first answer tends to be. If the answer is poor, ask again with more detail. For more help, see our post on how to write a good AI prompt.

Never let the AI add these

Keep this list next to you. If a draft contains any of these and they are not true, delete them.

  • Jobs, employers or job titles.
  • Grades, divisions, degree classes or awards.
  • Skills, languages, software or certificates.
  • Referees. Only name a person who has agreed.
  • Dates, numbers, targets or "results".
  • Reasons for gaps in your history that are not true.

Mistakes that cost interviews

  • One email for every job. The National Careers Service says to write a new cover letter for each job. Use words from the advert, but only where they are true for you.
  • A letter that is too long. It suggests about three to five paragraphs. For an email, shorter is fine.
  • Copied AI phrases. Lines like "I am a passionate and dynamic professional" tell the reader nothing. Say what you did.
  • Wrong names. Check the employer's name, the job title and the person you are writing to. AI can mix them up.
  • Personal details a CV does not need. The same service says to leave out your age, date of birth, marital status and nationality. Follow the employer's own form if it asks.
  • Spelling and layout. Use a clear font in a readable size, and keep the same style throughout.
  • Not keeping a copy. Keep what you sent. You may be asked about it at the interview.

We do not claim that employers reject CVs written with AI, and we did not find a reliable source for such a figure. The honest risk is simpler: a CV full of claims that you cannot explain. Nobody can promise that a good CV will get you a job, either.

Watch out for paid shortcuts

Some people offer to "fix" your CV for a fee, or to get you a job for money. A real employer in Tanzania does not charge you to be considered. If anyone asks you to pay for a job, an interview or a place on a list, read our post on fake job offers in Tanzania and walk away.

Common questions

Is it cheating to use AI for my CV?

Using it to tidy your own true facts is like asking a friend to read your CV. Making up facts is dishonest, with or without AI. Be ready to explain every line in your own words.

Should I tell the employer I used AI?

An advert or portal may have its own rules, so read them. Otherwise, the main point is that the content is true and that you wrote and checked it.

Can I paste my whole CV into a chatbot?

Take out your phone number, address, ID numbers and referees' details first. You can add them back later. Different tools handle your chats differently, so check the tool's privacy settings.

What if I have no work experience?

Use what you have: school, projects, volunteering, helping at a family business, a skill you taught yourself. Be plain about it. A clear, true CV from a beginner is better than a long, false one.

Can the AI write my cover letter for me?

It can give you a first draft, but it is not enough alone. The employer wants to hear why you want this job, in your voice. Rewrite it with your own words and facts.

What to do next

  1. Write your true facts in a notes page today, without contact details.
  2. Use one prompt from this page, then check every line against your notes.
  3. Ask one person to read the final CV and email before you send them.
  4. Read about building a career from the beginning and plan your next small step.

Every strong career starts with one honest page. Yours can begin today.
