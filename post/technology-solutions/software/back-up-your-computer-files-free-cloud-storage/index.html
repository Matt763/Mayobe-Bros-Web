Back Up Your Files: Free Cloud Storage and the 3-2-1 Rule

How to keep school and family files safe with the 3-2-1 rule, free Google and OneDrive storage, Windows Backup and a second copy on a drive.

Few feelings are worse than a laptop that will not turn on the week before a deadline. Your final-year project is inside it. So are the photos of people you love. A back-up is how you make sure that one broken machine is a bad day and not a disaster.

This post is about files on a computer: school work, documents and photos. It is not about phones. For phones, read how to back up your phone and move to a new one.

The short answer

Keep three copies of every file that matters, on two kinds of storage, with one copy away from home. A simple version is the original on your laptop, a copy on an external drive, and a copy in free cloud storage. Then check once in a while that you can open the copy.

  • Your laptop is copy one.
  • An external hard drive or flash disk is copy two.
  • Cloud storage, such as Google or OneDrive, is copy three and it is away from your room.
  • Set it to run by itself when you can, because people forget.
  • Test a restore: open a file from the back-up and check it works.

What is the 3-2-1 rule?

The US cyber agency CISA recommends this rule. It says to keep three copies of important files, to store them on two different types of storage (for example a hard drive and the cloud), and to keep one copy off-site, away from where you work.

Why three? One copy can break, and so can a second. Why two types? A hard drive can fail in a way a cloud account does not, and the other way round. Why one away from home? Fire, flood and theft hit the whole room at once. The cloud counts as off-site.

CISA also says a back-up that runs on its own, on a regular schedule, protects against hardware failure and damage. It also says you should practise restoring, so that you know it works before the day you need it.

How much free cloud storage is there?

Free storage is a good third copy for school work and key photos. Here is what the providers say. As checked in November 2026, these figures can change, so read the live pages before you rely on them.

ProviderFree amountGood to know
Google accountup to 15 GBShared by Gmail, Drive and Google Photos
Microsoft account (OneDrive)5 GBNeeds a free Microsoft account

Google says the 15 GB is shared. Your emails count, including those in Spam and Trash, and so do files in Drive and photos in Google Photos. If you go over the limit, you can still sign in, but you cannot upload new files to Drive or back up photos to Google Photos. Gmail may also stop sending and receiving. Google also says content may be removed if an account stays over the limit for two years or more, and it says it will warn you well before that.

So keep an eye on the number. Delete big files you do not need, and keep documents (small) separate from videos (big).

15 GB is room for many documents and a good number of photos, but not for hours of video. If your work is big, tell yourself which files matter most and back those up first: school work, ID scans, family photos.

How do I back up with Windows Backup and OneDrive?

Microsoft's Windows Backup app is included in Windows 10 and Windows 11. You can find it by searching "backup" in the Start menu. It can back up files and settings. With OneDrive folder back-up you turn on your Desktop, Documents, Pictures, Videos or Music folders, and they are copied to your OneDrive. You can then open them on any PC where you sign in with the same account.

Some things to know, from Microsoft's page:

  • It needs a personal Microsoft account (for example an outlook.com address). Work or school accounts will not work.
  • A free Microsoft account has 5 GB of OneDrive storage. You can buy more, but you do not have to.
  • To check the status, go to Settings, then Accounts, then Windows backup.
  • To restore, sign in with the same account on a new PC or after you reinstall Windows, and choose to restore. You can also sign in to OneDrive to get your files.

Microsoft says Windows 10 support ended on 14 October 2025, with no more free updates or security fixes. If your laptop still runs Windows 10, back up first, and then read Microsoft's page about moving to Windows 11. Our guide on speeding up a slow Windows PC may also help an older machine.

If you do not use Windows, or do not want to put your files into OneDrive, simply copy your important folders into Google Drive in your browser. The idea is the same.

How do I keep a second copy on a drive?

A drive gives you a copy that does not need the internet. This matters when data is expensive or the network is down. CISA also says to make sure your critical files can be reached without internet.

Here is a plain method that needs no special tool:

  1. Plug in your external hard drive or a large flash disk.
  2. Make a folder on it named "Backup" and the date, for example "Backup 2026-11".
  3. Copy your important folders into it by dragging them in File Explorer.
  4. Wait until the copy finishes, then eject the drive safely.
  5. Keep the drive somewhere safe, not in the same bag as the laptop.

We did not open Microsoft's steps for File History, so we do not describe it here. Copying by hand works fine if you do it on a regular day, such as the first Sunday of every month.

Flash disks are small and easy to lose, and they can get a virus from other computers. Do not make one flash disk your only copy. We do not recommend any paid back-up product here, and you do not need one to follow this plan.

How do I know my back-up works?

A back-up you have never opened is only a hope. Once a month, do a quick test.

  • Open the Backup folder or your cloud account.
  • Pick one file and open it. Check that it is the latest version.
  • Look at the date of the newest file. If it is old, something has stopped.

Say you save your project in a folder called "Project". If the copy in the cloud shows last month's file, you have found the problem while it is still small.

Common questions

Is a flash disk a back-up?

It can be one copy, but not your only one. Flash disks break, get lost and carry viruses. Use it with a cloud copy.

Is the cloud safe?

Cloud services keep your files away from your room, which helps against loss. You still need a strong password on the account. Choose a long password that you use nowhere else, and keep the recovery details for the account up to date.

What if I run out of free space?

Delete what you no longer need, or choose which files matter most. You can buy more space, but you do not have to. Never pay a stranger who offers "storage".

How often should I back up?

Back up whenever you finish something you cannot make again. For school work, that can be every time you finish a session. For other files, once a month is a good habit.

What to do next

  1. Today, copy your most important folder to a flash disk or hard drive.
  2. Sign in to Google Drive or OneDrive and upload that same folder, or turn on OneDrive folder back-up.
  3. Set a reminder on your phone for the first day of every month to check the copy.
  4. Check the live pages of Google and Microsoft for the current free storage.

You are protecting the work you have done and the memories you care about. That is worth one hour of your time.
