How to Remove a Photo Background: Free Tools on Phone and PC

Remove or change a photo background on iPhone, Samsung, Windows 11, Adobe, Canva or a web tool. What is free, what is paid, and what to protect.

You took a good photo, but the background is messy. Maybe it is a product photo for your shop, a profile picture, or a poster for a school event. Removing the background used to need hours of careful work in Photoshop. Today most phones and PCs can do a decent job in one tap.

This guide covers the tools that already exist on your phone or Windows PC, then Adobe, Canva and a web tool. It says clearly what is free and what is not. Free claims below were checked on 24 November 2026, and plans change, so look at the maker's page before you rely on one.

Key points

  • An iPhone can lift a person or object out of a photo by touch and hold. Many Samsung Galaxy phones can do something similar in the Gallery app.
  • Windows 11 has background removal in the Photos app. Microsoft also added it to Paint.
  • Adobe Firefly's background remover is free with an Adobe account, with a daily allowance. Photoshop and Canva's remover need a paid plan.
  • A free web tool may give you only a small, low-quality file. It also receives your photo, so never upload ID cards, school papers or photos of children.
  • A clean result starts with a good photo: sharp, well lit, and a subject that stands out from the background.

Before you start: what happens to your photo

Some tools work on your device. Others send your photo to a company's computers. This matters for privacy.

Microsoft says that in the Photos app, the AI that separates the background works locally on your device, so the data does not leave it. A web tool is different. When you upload a photo to a website, a copy goes to that company. Read its privacy page. We give one example below.

A simple rule: do not upload anything you would not want a stranger to see. That means national ID cards, passports, school or exam papers, bank messages, and photos of children.

On an iPhone

Apple's iPhone User Guide describes a feature called lifting a subject from the background. You can use it in Photos, Safari, Quick Look and other places. The steps:

  1. Open a photo in full screen. You can also pause a video on one frame.
  2. Touch and hold the subject. An outline appears around it.
  3. Choose what to do: Copy (or Copy Subject), Add Sticker, Share, or Look Up.

If you tap Copy, you can paste the cut-out into a message, note or email. It is the subject without its background. Apple's guide shows this page for iOS 16 and newer versions. Apple publishes a list of which iPhone models support it, so check your model there if the outline does not appear.

The original photo is not changed. You get the cut-out only when you paste it somewhere.

On a Samsung Galaxy phone

Samsung's support page for the Gallery app (the page we opened is in Russian, last updated 5 September 2025) describes the same idea.

  1. Open the Gallery app and pick the photo.
  2. Touch and hold the object you want to cut out.
  3. When Gallery has found the object, a small bar appears. You can copy it or share it. The three-dot menu lets you save it as a sticker or as an image, or edit the outline.

Samsung says the feature needs Android 14 or newer and is not on every model. If you save it as an image, Samsung says the file goes to a folder called Pinned images (the name may differ in your language). That is where to look if you cannot find it.

We did not find an official Google page with steps for Google Photos cut-outs, so we do not give steps for it. Some news sites report that Google Photos has a sticker feature, but check Google's own help for your phone.

On Windows 11: the Photos app

Microsoft's support page for editing in the Photos app gives these steps for the Background tool:

  1. Open the picture in Photos.
  2. Select Edit image.
  3. Open the Background panel. Windows selects the background automatically.
  4. Choose Blur, Remove or Replace.
  5. Select Remove, then Apply.

If the edge is wrong, turn on the background brush. Use Add mask to include more areas as background, and Remove mask to take parts out of it. You can change the brush size and softness. Replace lets you pick a plain colour as the new background.

The Photos app also has a tool called Generative Erase for taking small objects out of a photo. That is a different job from removing the whole background.

On Windows 11: Paint

In September 2023 Microsoft announced background removal in the Paint app, first for Windows Insiders. The announcement says you paste or import a picture, then select the new remove background button in the toolbar. It also says you can first use the rectangle select tool to choose an area. We did not find a Microsoft support page that gives the exact menu names today. If your Paint shows no such button, update it from the Microsoft Store and look again. For steady, documented steps, the Photos app above is the safer choice.

With Adobe: Firefly and Photoshop

Adobe Firefly (online)

Adobe's page calls it a free image background remover. The steps are to open Firefly, upload a photo, select Remove Background, and download. The file comes as a PNG with a transparent background. Adobe says uploads must be JPEG, PNG or WEBP.

Here is what Adobe says about cost. You need an Adobe account, which is free to create. Free users get free daily generations that they can use to remove backgrounds. Unlimited use needs a paid Adobe Firefly plan. The page does not say how many free uses you get each day, so check when you sign in.

Photoshop (desktop)

Photoshop has a Remove Background action. On Adobe's page, you open the image and unlock its layer, then select Remove Background in the Properties panel under Quick Actions, or in the Contextual Task Bar. It uses Subject Select to find the main subject, adds a layer mask, and deletes the rest. You can then fix small leftovers with the Brush tool and drag in a new background.

Photoshop is sold through Adobe plans. Adobe's page offers a free trial and shows plan prices, which change often, so we do not quote one. For a single photo, Firefly or the Photos app is likely enough.

With Canva

Canva's help page says that Background Remover is included with paid plans. In other words, a free Canva account does not have it. The steps for those who do:

  1. Select the photo you want to edit.
  2. On the editor toolbar, select BG Remover.
  3. Wait while Canva processes it.

Canva lists limits: the tool works only for photos under 9 MB, it is not available for vector images, and a photo over 10 megapixels is reduced to 10 megapixels after the background is removed. Canva also says the tool uses AI but counts as an editing tool.

If you only have a free Canva account, Canva may still be useful for adding a new background after you remove the old one in another tool.

A free web tool: remove.bg

Remove.bg is a well-known web tool. Its help page says it is free for low-resolution images (up to 0.25 megapixels) processed on the website and for personal use. It gives 625 by 400 pixels as an example of that size. That is small, about the size of a thumbnail. For full size, up to 50 megapixels, and for business use, Remove.bg sells plans and credits.

Look at its privacy policy before you upload. The operator is Canva Austria GmbH. The policy says files are uploaded, processed, offered for download and then deleted shortly after, with no exact time given. Server logs are kept for up to 3 months. The policy also says that uploads and other content may be analysed to train its algorithms. It also has an option to choose whether a particular image can be used for improvements.

That is a reason to be careful. If privacy matters, use a tool that works on your own device, such as the Photos app or your phone's Gallery.

How to get a clean cut-out

The tool matters less than the photo. These habits help:

  • Use a sharp, well-lit photo. Blur and shadows confuse the tools.
  • Pick a plain background. A wall, a sheet or a sky is easier than a crowd or a market.
  • Keep contrast. A dark shirt on a dark background is hard to separate. A light background behind dark clothes is easier.
  • Watch hair, glass and thin parts. Fine hair, glasses and straps often show rough edges. Zoom in and use the brush or mask tool to fix them.
  • Save as PNG if you want a transparent background. JPEG cannot hold transparency, and it shows white or black instead.
  • Keep the original. Work on a copy, so you can start again.

For shop photos, our guide to product photos with your phone has lighting and background ideas that make cutting out easier.

A word on passport and ID photos

Rules for official photos differ by country and by office. We did not find an official Tanzanian source for background colour or size rules, so we do not give any here. Ask the office that will take the photo, or check its official page, before you edit a picture for an application.

Common questions

Which method is truly free?

The ones built into your device are free. These are the iPhone lift-subject feature, the Samsung Gallery cut-out on supported phones, and the Windows Photos app. Adobe Firefly is free with an Adobe account and a daily limit.

Is it safe to upload photos to a web tool?

It sends a copy to that company. Read the privacy page. Avoid ID cards, school documents and photos of children.

Why is my downloaded picture so small?

Many free web tools give a low-resolution file and charge for the full size. Remove.bg says its free output is up to 0.25 megapixels.

Can I put a new background behind the person?

Yes. In the Photos app, use Replace to pick a colour. In Photoshop, drag a new image behind the cut-out layer. Other editors also let you place the PNG cut-out on a new picture.

What to do next

  1. Try the tool already on your device: the Photos app on Windows 11, or touch and hold on an iPhone or a supported Samsung phone.
  2. If the edge looks rough, retake the photo with better light and a plainer background.
  3. Before you use a web tool, read its privacy page and keep sensitive pictures off it. If you also edit video, see our comparison of free video editors.

Uploads and downloads use mobile data. Our guide to saving mobile data on your phone can help if your bundle is small.
