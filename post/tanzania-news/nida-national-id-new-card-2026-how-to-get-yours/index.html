NIDA National ID: The New 2026 Card and How to Get Yours

Tanzania launched a new NIDA card in November 2026. Here is how to get your NIN and card, what old cards can do, and what a replacement costs.

The National Identification Authority (NIDA) has launched a new version of the Tanzanian national ID card. The kitambulisho cha taifa is now needed for a SIM card, a bank account, a student loan and many other services, so a lot of people want to know what the new card means for them. This post explains what was announced, how to get your national ID number (NIN) and card, what a replacement costs and what to do if your card is not ready. The facts were checked in November 2026. Official steps can change, so confirm at a NIDA office.

The short answer

  • A 2026-edition NIDA card was launched in Kibaha, Pwani Region, by Prime Minister Mwigulu Nchemba. The Chanzo reported it on 19 November 2026.
  • Your old card stays valid. Officials said people who hold a valid card have no reason to swap it.
  • To apply, start at NIDA's online pre-registration, then go to your district NIDA office to give fingerprints and a photo.
  • A number alone is not always enough. NIDA and officials urge people to collect the printed card from the office where they registered.
  • Replacing a lost or worn card costs TSh 20,000 the first time, according to NIDA's website.

What was launched

The Chanzo reported that the Prime Minister launched the 2026-edition card, a new card-production system and 10 vehicles for NIDA registration officers. The card is meant to carry more information and to last longer. Home Affairs Minister Patrobas Katambi said earlier cards were below standard, with photographs and text that faded.

The government says the new card is meant to cut the number of separate IDs people carry, such as the national ID, voter card, driving licence and health insurance card. The Prime Minister said it will consolidate many other IDs over time. Do not throw away your other cards yet. We found no date from which one card replaces the others.

Officials also listed a new mobile app for ID services. The Chanzo said the ten vehicles will register people with a focus on rural areas. Tanzania also plans a second phase of its digital ID project that would add iris and facial recognition, according to Biometric Update.

Do you need to change your old card?

No. The Chanzo reported that officials said the new card does not make the old one obsolete. If you hold a valid card, you have no reason to swap it. Replacement would carry a fee, and holders may later be told how to switch when cards wear out, as with passports. We did not find a switch-over date.

How many people have a number, and how many have a card

The figures differ between reports, so here is what each said.

  • At the launch, figures presented showed 27,648,911 people registered and 23,735,907 IDs issued. Of those, 19,683,142 cards had reached their holders (The Chanzo).
  • In October 2026, a NIDA official told Parliament that 23.7 million national ID numbers had been issued, with 21.526 million cards produced and 21.515 million reported as distributed (Biometric Update).
  • In September, Katambi said about 2.02 million printed cards were waiting for collection at NIDA offices.

The numbers do not match, partly because "ID issued" means a number and "card distributed" means the plastic card in a person's hand. The Chanzo noted this difference too. The message is the same in every report: many finished cards are never collected.

How to get your NIN and card

This follows the guide on NIDA's own website. NIDA says there are two ways to register, online and in the usual way at an office.

  1. Start online. NIDA offers online pre-registration at eonline.nida.go.tz for citizens and resident foreigners, to save time. It is not the whole process.
  2. Fill in the form. The paper form is called the National Identification application form 1A. Use black ink and capital letters. It is available at NIDA district offices, at some local government offices and on NIDA's website.
  3. Get your local office to stamp it. The form must be signed and stamped at the local government office where you live (Serikali ya Mtaa), which also writes a letter confirming where you live.
  4. Bring your documents. The required ones are your birth certificate, the residence letter from your local office and a birth certificate or national ID of one parent. Extra documents that help include school certificates, a passport, a driving licence, a TIN, a health insurance card or a Zanzibar resident ID.
  5. Go to the NIDA district office. There an officer takes your photo, your electronic signature and your fingerprints. NIDA advises bringing the original documents as well as copies.
  6. Wait for the checks and printing. NIDA describes steps for data entry, checking, a public period where people may object to wrong details, and finally printing.
  7. Collect your card. NIDA says to bring the receipt you were given when your fingerprints were taken, and to collect at the district office or as announced locally.

NIDA gives some tips for the photo. Avoid white, grey, pale blue, pink or shiny clothes, and avoid hats, clothes with logos or writing, and henna on the palms, because it makes fingerprints harder to read.

The page we read lists a payment only for replacements. We did not see a fee for a first registration. If someone asks you for money to "speed up" a first ID, report it to NIDA and do not pay.

Already registered: check your number and your application

NIDA's website lists online services for two common problems:

  • Find your NIN. You fill in a short form with your details at services.nida.go.tz and the system tells you your number.
  • Track your application. NIDA has a service for applicants to see the status of their request and whether any step is needed.

NIDA customer service is on 023-2210500, Monday to Friday, according to its website. Its email is info@nida.go.tz. Use these official routes instead of a stranger on social media who offers to "find" your number.

Lost, worn or wrong details

Lost card. Take a police loss report to the NIDA office where you registered, or the nearest one. You are given a government control number. NIDA's website lists a payment of TSh 20,000 at NMB, CRDB, NBC or PBZ, then you give the receipt to NIDA so a new card can be printed. A second replacement costs TSh 30,000 and a third TSh 50,000.

Worn card. Take the worn card to NIDA, get a control number and pay the same fees. NIDA says the same amounts apply: TSh 20,000 the first time, 30,000 the second and 50,000 the third.

Wrong details. NIDA has guidelines for changing details. It lists names, date and month of birth, residence, marital status and some other details as things that can be changed with proof. It lists parents' details, place of birth and year of birth among details that can be changed only with special approval. A phone number can be changed once the officer is satisfied it is registered under your NIN.

If your phone line or your money has been affected by someone using your identity, read our guide to SIM swap fraud.

What your NIN is used for

NIDA's list of uses includes registering a SIM card, getting a TIN, registering a business with BRELA, applying for an e-passport or a driving licence, opening a bank account, applying for a higher education loan and getting health services. Our guides on registering a small business and the HESLB student loan both ask for it. The health insurance scheme also asks for it: see our explainer on Universal Health Insurance and Fahari Afya.

The Chanzo reported that 72 public and 85 private institutions are linked to the NIDA system. A missing card can therefore cost you access to services at those bodies.

Common questions

Is a NIN the same as the card?

No. The NIN is your number. The card is the printed ID. Many services accept the number for checks, but officials say a number alone is not always enough and urge people to collect the card.

Can I get my first ID online only?

No. Online registration is a first step to save time. You still need to go to the NIDA district office for fingerprints and a photo, and bring the listed documents.

What happens if I do not collect my card?

Officials have complained about uncollected cards. Biometric Update reported that NIDA had earlier threatened to suspend use of numbers for people who ignored collection notices, and The Chanzo reported the Prime Minister directed NIDA to prepare a legal mechanism, including penalties, for people who do not collect cards. We found no penalty in force yet. The Chanzo also said no storage charge has been announced.

Will my data be safe?

The minister said NIDA must handle personal data in line with the Personal Data Protection Act of 2022, according to The Chanzo. As a user, only enter your details on NIDA's own websites, which end in nida.go.tz.

What to do next

  1. Check whether you have a NIN. If you are not sure, use NIDA's "find your NIN" service.
  2. If a card is waiting for you, take your receipt and collect it from the office where you registered.
  3. Keep a photo of your NIN somewhere safe, and never share your ID or fingerprints with a stranger.
  4. If your card is worn or lost, follow the replacement steps and pay only through the bank control number.
